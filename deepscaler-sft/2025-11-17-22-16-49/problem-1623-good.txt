To solve this problem, let's start by understanding the properties of a hyperbola and its asymptotes.

The given hyperbola is:

\[
\frac{x^2}{a^2} - \frac{y^2}{b^2} = 1
\]

The equations of the asymptotes of this hyperbola are given by:

\[
y = \pm \frac{b}{a} x
\]

The slopes of the asymptotes are $\frac{b}{a}$ and $-\frac{b}{a}$. The angle between the asymptotes can be determined using the formula for the angle between two lines. Let's say the angle between the lines with slopes $m_1$ and $m_2$ is $\theta$, then:

\[
\tan \theta = \left| \frac{m_1 - m_2}{1 + m_1 m_2} \right|
\]

In our case, the slopes of the asymptotes are $\frac{b}{a}$ and $-\frac{b}{a}$. So, we substitute these into the formula:

\[
\tan \theta = \left| \frac{\frac{b}{a} - \left( -\frac{b}{a} \right)}{1 + \left( \frac{b}{a} \right) \left( -\frac{b}{a} \right)} \right| = \left| \frac{\frac{2b}{a}}{1 - \frac{b^2}{a^2}} \right| = \left| \frac{\frac{2b}{a}}{\frac{a^2 - b^2}{a^2}} \right| = \left| \frac{2ba^2}{a (a^2 - b^2)} \right| = \left| \frac{2b a}{a^2 - b^2} \right|
\]

Since the angle between the asymptotes is given as $60^\circ$, we know that $\theta = 60^\circ$, and thus $\tan 60^\circ = \sqrt{3}$. Therefore, we have:

\[
\left| \frac{2b a}{a^2 - b^2} \right| = \sqrt{3}
\]

Without loss of generality, we can drop the absolute value because the relationship will hold either way. So we have:

\[
\frac{2ba}{a^2 - b^2} = \sqrt{3}
\]

Rearrange this equation to solve for $\frac{a}{b}$:

\[
2ba = \sqrt{3} (a^2 - b^2)
\]

Divide through by $b^2$ and introduce the variable $k = \frac{a}{b}$:

\[
2k = \sqrt{3} \left( k^2 - 1 \right)
\]

This is a quadratic equation in terms of $k$:

\[
2k = \sqrt{3} (k^2 - 1)
\]

\[
2k = \sqrt{3} k^2 - \sqrt{3}
\]

\[
\sqrt{3} k^2 - 2k - \sqrt{3} = 0
\]

We can solve this quadratic equation using the quadratic formula $k = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}$, where $a = \sqrt{3}$, $b = -2$, and $c = -\sqrt{3}$:

\[
k = \frac{-(-2) \pm \sqrt{(-2)^2 - 4 (\sqrt{3}) (-\sqrt{3})}}{2 (\sqrt{3})} = \frac{2 \pm \sqrt{4 + 12}}{2 \sqrt{3}} = \frac{2 \pm \sqrt{16}}{2 \sqrt{3}} = \frac{2 \pm 4}{2 \sqrt{3}}
\]

This gives us two solutions:

\[
k = \frac{2 + 4}{2 \sqrt{3}} = \frac{6}{2 \sqrt{3}} = \frac{3}{\sqrt{3}} = \sqrt{3}
\]

and

\[
k = \frac{2 - 4}{2 \sqrt{3}} = \frac{-2}{2 \sqrt{3}} = \frac{-1}{\sqrt{3}} = -\frac{\sqrt{3}}{3}
\]

Since $a > b$, we must have $\frac{a}{b} > 1$, so we discard the negative solution. Thus, the solution is:

\[
\frac{a}{b} = \sqrt{3}
\]
