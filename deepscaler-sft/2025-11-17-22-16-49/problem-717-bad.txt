To solve this problem, we need to use properties of circles and their arcs.

### Step-by-Step Solution:

1. **Understand the Problem:**
    - We have two concentric circles centered at point \( P \).
    - An arc on the smaller circle formed by a 45-degree angle at \( P \) has the same length as an arc on the larger circle formed by a 36-degree angle at \( P \).

2. **Arc Length Formula:**
    - The length of an arc of a circle is given by the formula: \[ \text{Arc Length} = \frac{\theta}{360} \times 2 \pi r \]
      where \( \theta \) is the angle in degrees subtended by the arc at the center, and \( r \) is the radius of the circle.
    - For the smaller circle with radius \( r_s \) and a 45-degree angle, the arc length is: \[ \text{Arc Length}_{\text{small}} = \frac{45}{360} \times 2 \pi r_s = \frac{1}{8} \times 2 \pi r_s = \frac{\pi r_s}{4} \]
    - For the larger circle with radius \( r_l \) and a 36-degree angle, the arc length is: \[ \text{Arc Length}_{\text{large}} = \frac{36}{360} \times 2 \pi r_l = \frac{1}{10} \times 2 \pi r_l = \frac{\pi r_l}{5} \]
    - According to the problem, these two arc lengths are equal: \[ \frac{\pi r_s}{4} = \frac{\pi r_l}{5} \]

3. **Set Up the Equation:**
    - We can cancel \(\pi\) from both sides of the equation:
      \[ \frac{r_s}{4} = \frac{r_l}{5} \]
    - Cross-multiply to get:
      \[ 5 r_s = 4 r_l \]
    - Rearrange to express \( r_s \) in terms of \( r_l \):
      \[ r_s = \frac{4}{5} r_l \]

4. **Find the Ratio of the Areas:**
    - The area of a circle is given by the formula \( \text{Area} = \pi r^2 \).
    - The area of the smaller circle is \( \pi r_s^2 \).
    - The area of the larger circle is \( \pi r_l^2 \).
    - We want to find the ratio of the area of the smaller circle to the area of the larger circle:
      \[ \text{Ratio} = \frac{\pi r_s^2}{\pi r_l^2} = \frac{r_s^2}{r_l^2} \]
    - Substitute \( r_s = \frac{4}{5} r_l \) into the ratio:
      \[ \text{Ratio} = \frac{\left( \frac{4}{5} r_l \right)^2}{r_l^2} = \frac{\frac{16}{25} r_l^2}{r_l^2} = \frac{16}{25} \]

So, the ratio of the area of the smaller circle to the area of the larger circle is \(\frac{16}{25}\).

eq = sp.Eq((45 / 360) * 2 * sp.pi * r_s, (36 / 360) * 2 * sp.pi * r_l)

# Solve the equation for r_s in terms of r_l
solution = sp.solve(eq, r_s)[0]

# Find the ratio of the areas
