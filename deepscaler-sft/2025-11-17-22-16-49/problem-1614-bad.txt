First, we need to identify all two-digit integers such that the integer is equal to the sum of its first digit, its second digit, and the product of its first and second digits. After identifying these integers, we will then compute their arithmetic mean.

Let's denote a two-digit number as \( n = 10a + b \), where \( a \) is the tens digit (ranging from 1 to 9) and \( b \) is the units digit (ranging from 0 to 9).

According to the problem, the number \( n \) should satisfy the condition:
\[ n = a + b + a \cdot b \]
Substituting \( n = 10a + b \) into the equation, we get:
\[ 10a + b = a + b + a \cdot b \]
This simplifies to:
\[ 10a = a + a \cdot b \]
\[ 10a = a (1 + b) \]
We can divide both sides by \( a \) (since \( a \neq 0 \)):
\[ 10 = 1 + b \]
\[ 9 = b \]
So, \( b = 9 \). Substituting \( b = 9 \) back into the equation, we get:
\[ 10a + 9 = a + 9 + 9a \]
\[ 10a = a + 9a \]
\[ 10a = 10a \]
This equation is always true for any \( a \) as long as it satisfies 1 ≤ \( a \) ≤ 9 (since \( a \) is a digit of a two-digit number). Hence, any number of the form 19, 29, 39, 49, 59, 69, 79, 89, 99 should satisfy the condition if we check each of them:

So, the numbers that satisfy this condition are 19, 29, 39, 49, 59, 69, 79, 89, and 99. Next, we will compute their arithmetic mean.

The arithmetic mean of a set of numbers is given by the sum of the numbers divided by the count of the numbers.

Let's write the Python code to confirm these numbers and compute their arithmetic mean.
```python
# List to store the numbers that satisfy the condition
valid_numbers = []

# Check each two-digit number to see if it meets the condition
for n in range(10, 100):
    a = n // 10  # tens digit
    b = n % 10   # units digit
    
    if n == a + b + a * b:
        valid_numbers.append(n)

# Calculate the arithmetic mean of the valid numbers
mean = sum(valid_numbers) / len(valid_numbers)
```
