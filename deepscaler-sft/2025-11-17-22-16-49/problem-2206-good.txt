To solve this problem, we need to calculate how the loan amount grows with compound interest over different periods. We start with an initial loan of \$1000 and apply a monthly interest rate of 5%, compounded monthly. We then need to determine the smallest integer number of months after which the loan amount has more than doubled.

We'll use the formula for compound interest:
\[ A = P \left(1 + \frac{r}{n}\right)^{nt} \]
where:
- \( A \) is the amount of money accumulated after n periods, including interest.
- \( P \) is the principal amount (the initial amount of money), which is $1000.
- \( r \) is the annual interest rate (decimal). Since the interest is compounded monthly at 5%, the monthly interest rate is 0.05.
- \( n \) is the number of times interest is compounded per unit t. Since it is compounded monthly, \( n = 1 \), and the formula simplifies to:
  \[ A = P \cdot (1 + r)^t \]
  where t is the number of months.
- We want to find the smallest integer \( t \) such that the loan amount \( A \) is more than twice the initial loan amount, i.e.,
  \[ A > 2 \cdot 1000 = 2000 \]
  Substituting the values, we get:
  \[ 1000 \cdot (1 + 0.05)^t > 2000 \]
  \[ (1.05)^t > 2 \]

We can solve this inequality by iterating over the values of \( t \) until we find the smallest integer that satisfies this condition.
Let's implement this step-by-step in Python.
```python
# Initialize variables
principal = 1000
interest_rate = 0.05
target_amount = 2000
months = 0

# Loop until the amount owed is more than twice the borrowed amount
while True:
    months += 1
    amount_owed = principal * (1 + interest_rate) ** months
    if amount_owed > target_amount:
        break

print(months)
```
Code execution result: 15

Thus, the least integer number of months after which Kimberly will owe more than twice as much as she borrowed is \(\boxed{15}\). This is our final answer.
