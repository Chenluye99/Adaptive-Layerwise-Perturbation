1. **Understand the Problem:**
    - The gorge is 80 feet wide.
    - The heights of the cliffs at the start and end of the trestle are 172 feet and 112 feet, respectively.
    - The train is crossing the gorge and we need to determine the height of the train above the bottom of the gorge when the front of the train has traveled \(\frac{3}{4}\) of the trestle's length.

2. **Set Up the Problem:**
    - Let's assume the length of the trestle is \( L \). The height that the trestle descends from the higher cliff (172 feet) to the lower cliff (112 feet) can be described by a linear function if we assume a straight path between the cliffs.
    - The change in height over the width of the gorge is \( 172 - 112 = 60 \) feet, over a width of 80 feet. So, to find the height as a function of the distance from the starting cliff, we can form a linear relationship for the height \( h \) at a distance \( d \) from the starting cliff:
      \[
      h(d) = 172 - \left( \frac{60}{80} \right) d = 172 - \frac{3}{4} d
      \]
    - We need to determine the height when the train has traveled \(\frac{3}{4}\) of the trestle's length, i.e., \(\frac{3}{4} L \).

3. **Formulate the Solution:**
    - To solve this, let's assume the train travels at a uniform rate along the trestle, so the distance traveled by the train when it has covered \(\frac{3}{4}\) of the trestle's length is \(\frac{3}{4} L \).
    - The height of the front of the train when it has traveled \(\frac{3}{4} L\) would be \( h \left( \frac{3}{4} L \right) \). Since the problem doesn't specify the length of the trestle directly, we can consider it simply as "the full length of the trestle connecting the two points," but the essence is to maintain the ratio of \(\frac{3}{4}\) of the trestle's length for the distance traveled.

```python
# Define the function to get the height at any distance d along the trestle
def get_height_along_trestle(distance):
    # The height function derived is h(d) = 172 - (3/4) * d
    return 172 - (3 / 4) * distance

# We need to find the height when the train has traveled 3/4 of the trestle's length, which corresponds to the distance 3/4 * 80 from the starting point
```
