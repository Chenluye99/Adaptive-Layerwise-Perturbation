To solve this problem, we need to consider the constraints that at least one multiple-choice question and at least one open-ended question must be selected when choosing 3 questions from the test paper. The test paper consists of 12 multiple-choice questions, 4 fill-in-the-blank questions, and 6 open-ended questions, so there are a total of 22 questions.

Let's break down the problem step-by-step:

1. **Calculate the Total Number of Ways to Select 3 Questions:**
   The total number of ways to select 3 questions out of 22 is given by the combination formula \( \binom{22}{3} \).

2. **Calculate the Number of Ways That Do Not Meet the Constraints:**
   - **No Multiple-Choice Questions:** All 3 questions are either fill-in-the-blank or open-ended. The total number of fill-in-the-blank and open-ended questions is \( 4 + 6 = 10 \), so the number of ways to choose 3 questions from these 10 is \( \binom{10}{3} \).
   - **No Open-Ended Questions:** All 3 questions are either multiple-choice or fill-in-the-blank. The total number of multiple-choice and fill-in-the-blank questions is \( 12 + 4 = 16 \), so the number of ways to choose 3 questions from these 16 is \( \binom{16}{3} \).
   - **No Multiple-Choice and No Open-Ended Questions:** All 3 questions are fill-in-the-blank. The total number of fill-in-the-blank questions is 4, and there are no ways to choose 3 fill-in-the-blank questions because \( \binom{4}{3} = 4 \) and it's obvious that 3 questions can only be chosen if there are at least 3 fill-in-the-blank questions, but here it's limited to only 4, so we won't consider this overlap separately because it's already included in the "No Multiple-Choice Questions" or "No Open-Ended Questions" cases.

3. **Use the Principle of Inclusion and Exclusion:**
   The number of ways to select 3 questions such that at least one multiple-choice question and at least one open-ended question are selected is given by:
   \[
   \binom{22}{3} - \binom{10}{3} - \binom{16}{3}
   \]
   We don't need to subtract the overlap because it won't be double-counted in the combinations considered above.

Let's compute these values using Python to ensure accuracy.
```python
import sympy as sp

# Calculate combinations
C_22_3 = sp.binomial(22, 3)
C_10_3 = sp.binomial(10, 3)
C_16_3 = sp.binomial(16, 3)

# Calculate the number of ways to select 3 questions such that at least one multiple-choice and at least one open-ended question are selected
result = C_22_3 - C_10_3 - C_16_3
print(result)
```
