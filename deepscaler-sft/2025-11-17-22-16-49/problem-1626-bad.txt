First, let's state the problem clearly. We have a triangle in the Cartesian plane with vertices at \((x_1, y_1)\), \((x_2, y_2)\), and \((x_3, y_3)\). 

The sum of the \(x\)-coordinates of the vertices of the triangle is given as:
\[ x_1 + x_2 + x_3 = \sqrt{13} \]

Next, we need to find the sum of the \(x\)-coordinates of the midpoints of the sides of this triangle. 

The midpoints of the sides of the triangle are:
1. Midpoint of the side between \((x_1, y_1)\) and \((x_2, y_2)\): \(\left( \frac{x_1 + x_2}{2}, \frac{y_1 + y_2}{2} \right)\)
2. Midpoint of the side between \((x_2, y_2)\) and \((x_3, y_3)\): \(\left( \frac{x_2 + x_3}{2}, \frac{y_2 + y_3}{2} \right)\)
3. Midpoint of the side between \((x_3, y_3)\) and \((x_1, y_1)\): \(\left( \frac{x_3 + x_1}{2}, \frac{y_3 + y_1}{2} \right)\)

The \(x\)-coordinates of these midpoints are:
1. \(\frac{x_1 + x_2}{2}\)
2. \(\frac{x_2 + x_3}{2}\)
3. \(\frac{x_3 + x_1}{2}\)

So, the sum of the \(x\)-coordinates of the midpoints of the sides of the triangle, \(S\), is:
\[ S = \frac{x_1 + x_2}{2} + \frac{x_2 + x_3}{2} + \frac{x_3 + x_1}{2} \]

Let's combine and simplify this expression:
\[ S = \frac{(x_1 + x_2) + (x_2 + x_3) + (x_3 + x_1)}{2} = \frac{2(x_1 + x_2 + x_3)}{2} = x_1 + x_2 + x_3 \]

We know that \( x_1 + x_2 + x_3 = \sqrt{13} \), so:
\[ S = \sqrt{13} \]

We need to find \( S^2 \):
\[ S^2 = (\sqrt{13})^2 = 13 \]

Let's confirm this with Python to ensure our solution is accurate.
```python
import sympy as sp

# Given sum of the x-coordinates of the vertices of the triangle
sum_x_coords = sp.sqrt(13)

# The sum of the x-coordinates of the midpoints of the sides of the triangle
S = sum_x_coords

# Calculate S^2
S_squared = S ** 2
print(S_squared)
```
Code execution result: 13

Thus, the final answer is \(\boxed{13}\). This confirms that our reasoning and solution are correct.
