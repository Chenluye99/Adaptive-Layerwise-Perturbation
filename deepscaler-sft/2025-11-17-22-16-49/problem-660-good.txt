1. Let's denote the pure imaginary root as \( z = bi \), where \( b \) is a real number and \( i \) is the imaginary unit.
2. Substitute \( z = bi \) into the equation \( z^2 + z + \omega = 0 \):

\[ (bi)^2 + bi + \omega = 0 \]
\[ -b^2 + bi + \omega = 0 \]

3. Separate this equation into its real and imaginary parts:
   - The real part: \(-b^2 + \Re(\omega) = 0 \)
   - The imaginary part: \( b + \Im(\omega) = 0 \)

4. From the imaginary part, we get:
   \[ \Im(\omega) = -b \]

5. From the real part, we get:
   \[ \Re(\omega) = b^2 \]

6. Since \( |\omega| = 1 \), we have:
   \[ |\omega| = \sqrt{(\Re(\omega))^2 + (\Im(\omega))^2} = 1 \]
   Substituting \(\Re(\omega) = b^2\) and \(\Im(\omega) = -b\):
   \[ \sqrt{(b^2)^2 + (-b)^2} = 1 \]
   \[ \sqrt{b^4 + b^2} = 1 \]
   \[ b^4 + b^2 = 1 \]

7. Let \( u = b^2 \). Then the equation becomes:
   \[ u^2 + u - 1 = 0 \]
   
8. Solve this quadratic equation using the quadratic formula \( u = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a} \):
   \[ u = \frac{-1 \pm \sqrt{1^2 - 4 \cdot 1 \cdot (-1)}}{2 \cdot 1} = \frac{-1 \pm \sqrt{1 + 4}}{2} = \frac{-1 \pm \sqrt{5}}{2} \]

9. Since \( u = b^2 \) and \( b^2 \) must be non-negative, we take the positive root:
   \[ u = \frac{-1 + \sqrt{5}}{2} \]
   So,
   \[ b^2 = \frac{-1 + \sqrt{5}}{2} \]

10. Now, we have \(\Re(\omega) = b^2 = \frac{-1 + \sqrt{5}}{2}\) and \(\Im(\omega) = -b \). To find \( b \), we use the fact that \( b^2 = \frac{-1 + \sqrt{5}}{2} \), but we don't actually need the value of \( b \) to find \(\omega + \overline{\omega}\).

11. Note that \(\overline{\omega}\) (the complex conjugate of \(\omega\)) has \(\Re(\overline{\omega}) = \Re(\omega) = b^2\) and \(\Im(\overline{\omega}) = -\Im(\omega) = b\). So,
    \[ \omega + \overline{\omega} = (b^2 - bi) + (b^2 + bi) = 2b^2. \]

12. Substituting the value of \( b^2 \),
    \[ \omega + \overline{\omega} = 2 \cdot \frac{-1 + \sqrt{5}}{2} = -1 + \sqrt{5}. \]
