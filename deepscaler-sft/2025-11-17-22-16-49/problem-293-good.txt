To solve the problem, we need to find how many positive integers less than 900 are either perfect cubes or perfect squares. 

1. **Identify Perfect Squares Less Than 900:**
    - A number is a perfect square if there exists an integer \( n \) such that \( n^2 < 900 \).
    - The largest integer \( n \) that satisfies \( n^2 < 900 \) is \( \lfloor \sqrt{899} \rfloor \).

2. **Identify Perfect Cubes Less Than 900:**
    - A number is a perfect cube if there exists an integer \( m \) such that \( m^3 < 900 \).
    - The largest integer \( m \) that satisfies \( m^3 < 900 \) is \( \lfloor \sqrt[3]{899} \rfloor \).

3. **Identify Numbers That Are Both Perfect Squares and Perfect Cubes (i.e., Perfect Sixth Powers) Less Than 900:**
    - A number that is both a perfect square and a perfect cube is a perfect sixth power.
    - A number is a perfect sixth power if there exists an integer \( k \) such that \( k^6 < 900 \).
    - The largest integer \( k \) that satisfies \( k^6 < 900 \) is \( \lfloor \sqrt[6]{899} \rfloor \).

4. **Use the Inclusion-Exclusion Principle:**
    - Use the principle of inclusion and exclusion to find the count of numbers that are either perfect squares or perfect cubes.
    - The formula is: 
      \[
      |A \cup B| = |A| + |B| - |A \cap B|
      \]
      where:
      - \( |A| \) is the number of perfect squares less than 900,
      - \( |B| \) is the number of perfect cubes less than 900,
      - \( |A \cap B| \) is the number of perfect sixth powers less than 900.

```python
import math

# Find the count of perfect squares less than 900

count_either_squares_or_cubes = count_squares + count_cubes - count_sixth_powers
```
