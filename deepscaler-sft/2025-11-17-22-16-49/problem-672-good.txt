To solve this problem, we need to consider both the intra-school and inter-school games being played.

1. **Setup and Definitions:**
    - There are three schools, let's call them School A, School B, and School C.
    - Each school has 4 players.
    - There are 4 players in School A, 4 players in School B, and 4 players in School C, so there are a total of 12 players in the tournament (i.e., 4 + 4 + 4 = 12).

2. **Games Played Within Schools:**
    - Each player plays one game against each of the other players from their own school.
    - There are 4 players in each school, so the number of ways to choose 2 players from 4 for a game within a school is given by the combination formula \( \binom{4}{2} = \frac{4!}{2!(4-2)!} = 6 \). Since there are 3 schools, and each of the 4 players plays a game against each of the other 3 players in their school, each school will have 4 * 3 / 2 = 6 intra-school games per school. Therefore, with 3 schools, the total number of intra-school games is \( 3 \times 6 = 18 \).

3. **Games Played Between Schools:**
    - Each player plays three games against each player from the other schools.
    - For a given player from School A, there are 4 players in School B and 4 players in School C, so that player will play \( 4 \times 3 = 12 \) games against players from School B and School C combined.
    - Since there are 4 players in School A, the total number of inter-school games involving School A's players is \( 4 \times 12 = 48 \).
    - The same logic applies to School B and School C. So, the total number of inter-school games considering all schools is \( 48 + 48 + 48 = 144 \).

4. **Total Games:**
    - The total number of games played in the tournament is the sum of intra-school games and inter-school games: \( 18 + 144 = 162 \).
