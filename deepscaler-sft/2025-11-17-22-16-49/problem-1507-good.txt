To solve this problem, we need to calculate the angles formed by the hour and minute hands of a clock at 6:44.

1. **Calculate the Angle of the Minute Hand:**
   - The minute hand moves 360 degrees in 60 minutes. Therefore, it moves \( \frac{360}{60} = 6 \) degrees per minute.
   - At 44 minutes, the minute hand is at \( 44 \times 6 = 264 \) degrees.

2. **Calculate the Angle of the Hour Hand:**
   - The hour hand moves 360 degrees in 12 hours, so it moves \( \frac{360}{12} = 30 \) degrees per hour.
   - Additionally, the hour hand moves \( \frac{30}{60} = 0.5 \) degrees per minute.
   - At 6:00, the hour hand is at \( 6 \times 30 = 180 \) degrees. After 44 minutes, the hour hand will have moved an additional \( 44 \times 0.5 = 22 \) degrees.
   - Therefore, at 6:44, the hour hand is at \( 180 + 22 = 202 \) degrees.

3. **Calculate the Angle Between the Hands:**
   - The absolute difference between the angles of the minute and hour hands at 6:44 is \( |264 - 202| = 62 \) degrees.
   - Since we need the acute angle (i.e., the smallest angle formed by the two hands of the clock, which is always less than or equal to 180 degrees), we take 62 degrees, as it is already less than 180 degrees and is the acute angle formed by the clock hands at 6:44.

Let's confirm this by implementing it in Python.
```python
# Function to calculate the angle of the minute hand
def minute_hand_angle(minute):
    return minute * 6

# Function to calculate the angle of the hour hand
def hour_hand_angle(hour, minute):
    return hour * 30 + minute * 0.5

# Get the angles for 6:44
minute_angle = minute_hand_angle(44)
hour_angle = hour_hand_angle(6, 44)

# Calculate the absolute difference between the two angles
angle_difference = abs(hour_angle - minute_angle)

# The smaller angle between the two hands (the acute angle)
acute_angle = min(angle_difference, 360 - angle_difference)

print(acute_angle)
```
