So, the area of the circle that overlaps with the square is \( \frac{1}{4} \times 100\pi = 25\pi \).

However, since the problem context mostly considers the parts within or on the boundary of the square, we do not need to account for any overlaps that fall outside of the square's boundaries in this context because the circle's quarter only lies exactly within the square and no further accounting is needed for areas beyond the square's limits for this particular problem setup.
    - Therefore, the area of the union is just the sum of the area of the square and the area of the quarter circle, as there is no overlap subtraction needed outside these boundaries for union calculation in this geometric context as described.

Thus, the area of the union of the regions enclosed by the square and the circle is \( 100 + 25\pi \).
