First, let's establish the details and the task:

1. **Inner Cylinder Details:**
    - Radius (\(r_1\)) = 3 inches
    - Height (\(h\)) = 8 inches

2. **Outer Cylinder Details:**
    - Radius (\(r_2\)) = 3 + 1 = 4 inches
    - Height (\(h\)) = 8 inches

3. **Objective:**
    - Find the surface area of the space between the two cylinders.

To get the surface area of the space between the two cylinders, we need to compute the lateral surface area of both the inner and outer cylinders and then find the difference between them, excluding the top and bottom surfaces since they do not form part of the space between the cylinders in this context.

### Step-by-Step Process:

1. **Lateral Surface Area Calculation:**
    - The lateral surface area of a cylinder is given by the formula \(2 \pi r h\).
    - Lateral surface area of the inner cylinder: \(2 \pi r_1 h = 2 \pi \cdot 3 \cdot 8 = 48 \pi\).
    - Lateral surface area of the outer cylinder: \(2 \pi r_2 h = 2 \pi \cdot 4 \cdot 8 = 64 \pi\).

2. **Surface Area of the Space Between the Cylinders:**
    - The surface area of the space between the two cylinders is just the lateral surface area of the difference in dimensions of the two cylinders, as the top and bottom surfaces of the cylinders are the same and don't contribute to the surface area of the "space" between the cylinders.
    - Therefore, the surface area of the space between the two cylinders is simply the lateral surface area of the outer cylinder minus the lateral surface area of the inner cylinder in terms of the additional surface area due to the larger radius of the outer cylinder.

To get the accurate surface area of just the "shell" or "space" between the two cylinders, we need to consider that the space's surface area includes the outer surface of the inner cylinder and the inner surface of the outer cylinder, covering the annular (ring-shaped) region when sliced vertically.

However, because this problem only asks for the surface area of the space formed when viewed as the inner and outer surfaces of the annulus or the "shell" along their heights and considering only the curved surface (lateral surface area), our calculation would just be the lateral surface area of the outer cylinder because the inner surface isn't part of the material of the outer cylinder.

Hence, the surface area of the space between the two cylinders is essentially just the lateral surface area of the outer cylinder as it encompasses the annular surface when visualized as a cylindrical shell.

Therefore, the final answer is just the lateral surface area of the outer cylinder:

\[ 64 \pi \]

Let's confirm this with Python code for completeness, iterating if needed.
```python
import sympy as sp

# Given dimensions
r1 = 3  # radius of the inner cylinder in inches
r2 = 4  # radius of the outer cylinder in inches
h = 8   # height of the cylinders in inches

# Compute the lateral surface area for both the inner and outer cylinders
lateral_surface_area_inner = 2 * sp.pi * r1 * h
lateral_surface_area_outer = 2 * sp.pi * r2 * h

# The surface area of the space between the two cylinders will be the lateral surface area of the outer cylinder as it forms the boundary of the space
surface_area_between_cylinders = lateral_surface_area_outer

print(surface_area_between_cylinders)
```
