1. **Properties of Regular Polygons:**
    - A regular pentagon has interior angles of \(108^\circ\).
    - A regular hexagon has interior angles of \(120^\circ\).

2. **Shared Side and Configuration:**
    - The pentagon and hexagon share a common side \(\overline{AD}\).
    - Let's position the pentagon and hexagon in the coordinate plane for simplicity.

3. **Position of Points:**
    - For a regular pentagon, if we place one vertex at the origin, the vertices can be expressed using complex numbers or coordinates based on rotation by \( \frac{2\pi}{5} \).
    - For a regular hexagon, placing one vertex at the origin, the vertices can be expressed using complex numbers or coordinates based on rotation by \( \frac{2\pi}{6} = \frac{\pi}{3} \).

4. **Geometric Configuration:**
    - Let's place the regular pentagon and hexagon such that they are aligned in a way that makes it easiest to solve the problem. We can use standard placements where each side of the shapes is horizontal or vertical initially or based on rotations.

Since we need to find \( \angle BAC \), we should look at the angular relationships between points \( B \), \( A \), and \( C \) in the configuration. To solve it precisely, let's consider how these shapes are typically placed when sharing a common side and how the angles align.

5. **Finding Angle \( \angle BAC \):**

1. **Properties of Regular Polygons:**
    - The internal angle of a regular pentagon is \(\frac{(5-2) \cdot 180^\circ}{5} = 108^\circ\).
    - The internal angle of a regular hexagon is \(\frac{(6-2) \cdot 180^\circ}{6} = 120^\circ\).

2. **Identifying Angle \( \angle BAC \):**
    - When a regular pentagon and a regular hexagon share a common side (let's say \(\overline{AD}\)), if we place them properly, the angle \(\angle BAC\) will be the difference between the exterior angle of the hexagon and the exterior angle of the pentagon at point \(A\).
    - The exterior angle of a regular pentagon is \(180^\circ - 108^\circ = 72^\circ\).
    - The exterior angle of a regular hexagon is \(180^\circ - 120^\circ = 60^\circ\).
    - Angle \(\angle BAC\) would be the difference or sum, depending on the configurations, but in such cases, it is often derived by analyzing these angles around point \(A\) in terms of the
