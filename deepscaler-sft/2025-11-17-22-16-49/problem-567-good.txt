To solve this problem, we will follow these steps:

1. **Understand the Problem:**
    - The equation of the hyperbola is given as \(\frac{x^2}{a^2} - \frac{y^2}{b^2} = 1\). The foci of this hyperbola are located at \(F_1 = (-c, 0)\) and \(F_2 = (c, 0)\), where \(c = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2}\).
    - Point \(P\) is on the hyperbola such that \(PF_2\) is perpendicular to the x-axis. This means that the x-coordinate of \(P\) is \(c\). Substituting \(x = c\) into the equation of the hyperbola, we get:
      \[
      \frac{c^2}{a^2} - \frac{y^2}{b^2} = 1
      \]
      Since \(c^2 = a^2 + b^2\), we substitute \(c^2\) and get:
      \[
      \frac{a^2 + b^2}{a^2} - \frac{y^2}{b^2} = 1 \implies 1 + \frac{b^2}{a^2} - \frac{y^2}{b^2} = 1 \implies \frac{b^2}{a^2} = \frac{y^2}{b^2} \implies y^2 = \frac{b^4}{a^2} \implies y = \pm \frac{b^2}{a}
      \]
      So, the coordinates of point \(P\) are \((c, \frac{b^2}{a})\) or \((c, -\frac{b^2}{a})\).

2. **Calculate Distances:**
    - We need to calculate the distances \(PF_1\) and \(F_1F_2\).
    - The distance \(F_1F_2 = 2c\).
    - The distance \(PF_1 = \sqrt{(c - (-c))^2 + \left(\frac{b^2}{a} - 0\right)^2} = \sqrt{(2c)^2 + \left(\frac{b^2}{a}\right)^2} = \sqrt{4c^2 + \frac{b^4}{a^2}}\).
    - The distance \(PF_2 = \sqrt{(c - c)^2 + \left(\frac{b^2}{a} - 0\right)^2} = \frac{b^2}{a}\).

3. **Use the Given Condition:**
    - We are given that \(\sin \angle PF_1F_2 = \frac{1}{3}\). In the right triangle \(PF_1F_2\), \(\sin \angle PF_1F_2 = \frac{PF_2}{PF_1}\), so:
      \[
      \frac{\frac{b^2}{a}}{\sqrt{4c^2 + \frac{b^4}{a^2}}} = \frac{1}{3}
      \]
      Which simplifies to:
      \[
      \frac{b^2}{a} = \frac{1}{3} \sqrt{4c^2 + \frac{b^4}{a^2}}
      \]
      Squaring both sides, we get:
      \[
      \left(\frac{b^2}{a}\right)^2 = \frac{1}{9} \left(4c^2 + \frac{b^4}{a^2}\right)
      \]
      \[
      \frac{b^4}{a^2} = \frac{1}{9} \left(4c^2 + \frac{b^4}{a^2}\right)
      \]
      Multiplying through by 9 to clear the fraction:
      \[
      9 \cdot \frac{b^4}{a^2} = 4c^2 + \frac{b^4}{a^2}
      \]
      Rearrange to isolate \(\frac{b^4}{a^2}\):
      \[
      9 \cdot \frac{b^4}{a^2} - \frac{b^4}{a^2} = 4c^2 \implies 8 \cdot \frac{b^4}{a^2} = 4c^2 \implies 2 \cdot \frac{b^4}{a^2} = c^2
      \]
      So, we have:
      \[
      c^2 = 2 \cdot \frac{b^4}{a^2}
      \]
      We know that \(c^2 = a^2 + b^2\), so we substitute this in:
      \[
      a^2 + b^2 = 2 \cdot \frac{b^4}{a^2}
      \]
      To solve this equation, let's introduce a variable \(k = \frac{b^2}{a^2}\), then \(b^2 = ka^2\). Substituting this into the equation, we get:
      \[
      a^2 + ka^2 = 2 \cdot \frac{(ka^2)^2}{a^2} \implies a^2 (1 + k) = 2 \cdot k^2 a^2 \implies 1 + k = 2k^2
      \]
      This is a quadratic equation in terms of \(k\):
      \[
      2k^2 - k - 1 = 0
      \]
      We solve this quadratic equation using the quadratic formula \(k = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}\), where \(a = 2\), \(b = -1\), and \(c = -1\):
      \[
      k = \frac{-(-1) \pm \sqrt{(-1)^2 - 4 \cdot 2 \cdot (-1)}}{2 \cdot 2} = \frac{1 \pm \sqrt{1 + 8}}{4} = \frac{1 \pm 3}{4}
      \]
      This gives us two solutions:
      \[
      k = \frac{1 + 3}{4} = 1 \quad \text{or} \quad k = \frac{1 - 3}{4} = -\frac{1}{2}
      \]
      Since \(k = \frac{b^2}{a^2}\) and \(k\) must be non-negative, we choose:
      \[
      k = 1 \implies \frac{b^2}{a^2} = 1 \implies b^2 = a^2 \implies b = a
      \]
      Substituting \(b = a\) back into the equation \(c^2 = a^2 + b^2 = a^2 + a^2 = 2a^2\), we get:
      \[
      c = \sqrt{2} a
      \]
      The eccentricity \(e\) of the hyperbola is given by \(e = \frac{c}{a}\):
      \[
      e = \frac{\sqrt{2} a}{a} = \sqrt{2}
      \]
