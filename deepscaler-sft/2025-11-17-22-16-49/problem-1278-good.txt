To solve the problem, let's start by recalling some properties of polygons:

1. The sum of the interior angles of a pentagon is given by the formula \((n-2) \times 180^\circ\) where \(n\) is the number of sides. For a pentagon, \(n = 5\), so the sum of the interior angles is \((5-2) \times 180^\circ = 3 \times 180^\circ = 540^\circ\).
2. We are given that two of the interior angles of this pentagon are right angles, i.e., each of these angles is \(90^\circ\). So, the sum of these two right angles is \(90^\circ + 90^\circ = 180^\circ\).
3. The remaining three interior angles are congruent, so let the measure of each of these angles be \(x\). Therefore, the sum of these three congruent angles is \(3x\).
4. According to the property of polygons, the sum of all the interior angles of the pentagon is \(540^\circ\). Hence, we can set up the following equation:
   \[
   90^\circ + 90^\circ + 3x = 540^\circ
   \]
5. Simplifying the left-hand side, we get:
   \[
   180^\circ + 3x = 540^\circ
   \]
6. To isolate \(3x\), we subtract \(180^\circ\) from both sides of the equation:
   \[
   3x = 540^\circ - 180^\circ = 360^\circ
   \]
7. Dividing both sides of the equation by 3, we get:
   \[
   x = \frac{360^\circ}{3} = 120^\circ
   \]

Thus, the measure of one of the three congruent interior angles is \(120^\circ\). Let's confirm this by coding the solution in Python.
```python
# Calculating the measure of the congruent angles based on the reasoning explained above

# The sum of the interior angles of a pentagon

# The measure of the two right angles
right_angle_measure = 90

# Sum of the two right angles
sum_of_two_right_angles = 2 * right_angle_measure

# The remaining measure to be accounted for by the three congruent angles
remaining_measure = sum_of_interior_angles_of_pentagon - sum_of_two_right_angles

# Each of the three congruent angles
measure_of_each_congruent_angle = remaining_measure / 3
```
