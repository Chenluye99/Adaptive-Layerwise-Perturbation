To solve this problem, let's start by denoting the initial set of numbers as \(a_1, a_2, \ldots, a_{100}\). Define the sum of the squares of these numbers as:

\[ S = \sum_{i=1}^{100} a_i^2 \]

### Step-by-Step Solution:

1. **First Increase:**
   Each of the numbers is increased by 1. So, the new set of numbers becomes \(a_1 + 1, a_2 + 1, \ldots, a_{100} + 1\). The sum of the squares of these new numbers is:
   \[
   S_1 = \sum_{i=1}^{100} (a_i + 1)^2 = \sum_{i=1}^{100} (a_i^2 + 2a_i + 1) = \sum_{i=1}^{100} a_i^2 + \sum_{i=1}^{100} 2a_i + \sum_{i=1}^{100} 1 = S + 2 \sum_{i=1}^{100} a_i + 100
   \]
   We know that after the first increase, the sum of the squares of the numbers was not changed, i.e., \(S_1 = S\). This gives us the equation:
   \[
   S = S + 2 \sum_{i=1}^{100} a_i + 100
   \]
   Simplifying this, we get:
   \[
   0 = 2 \sum_{i=1}^{100} a_i + 100 \implies 2 \sum_{i=1}^{100} a_i = -100 \implies \sum_{i=1}^{100} a_i = -50
   \]

2. **Second Increase:**
   Each of the numbers from the previous step is increased by 1 again. So, the new set of numbers becomes \((a_1 + 1) + 1 = a_1 + 2, (a_2 + 1) + 1 = a_2 + 2, \ldots, (a_{100} + 1) + 1 = a_{100} + 2\). The sum of the squares of these new numbers is:
   \[
   S_2 = \sum_{i=1}^{100} (a_i + 2)^2 = \sum_{i=1}^{100} (a_i^2 + 4a_i + 4) = \sum_{i=1}^{100} a_i^2 + \sum_{i=1}^{100} 4a_i + \sum_{i=1}^{100} 4 = S + 4 \sum_{i=1}^{100} a_i + 400
   \]

   \[
   S_2 = S + 4(-50) + 400 = S - 200 + 400 = S + 200
   \]
   So, the sum of the squares of the numbers increased by 200 after the second increase. Thus, the change in the sum of the squares of the numbers after the second increase is 200.
