To solve this problem, let's start by understanding the properties of polynomial degrees:

1. **Degree of a Polynomial Product:**
    - If \( p(t) \) is a polynomial of degree \( n \) and \( q(t) \) is a polynomial of degree \( m \), then the degree of the product \( p(t) \cdot q(t) \) is \( n + m \).
    - In this problem, both \( p(t) \) and \( q(t) \) are seventh-degree polynomials, so they each have a degree of 7.

2. **Finding the Degree of \( p(t) \cdot q(t) \):**
    - Given that \( p(t) \) and \( q(t) \) are both of degree 7, the degree of the product \( p(t) \cdot q(t) \) would be \( 7 + 7 = 14 \).

So, the degree of \( p(t) \cdot q(t) \) is 14. We can confirm this by coding a simple check in Python, but this is more of a mathematical property check rather than a coding exercise. Nevertheless, we can create a small Python script to simulate it if needed. For now, let's just confirm this by coding a simple function that demonstrates this property, but in a more general way if desired.

Let's create a small Python script for verification although it's more of a theoretical concept rather than a practical one in code.
```python
import sympy as sp

# Define two 7th-degree polynomials
t = sp.symbols('t')
p = sum(sp.symbols(f'c_{i}') * t**i for i in range(7)) + sp.symbols('c_7') * t**7
q = sum(sp.symbols(f'd_{i}') * t**i for i in range(7)) + sp.symbols('d_7') * t**7

# Get the product of the polynomials
product = sp.expand(p * q)

# Get the degree of the product polynomial
degree_of_product = sp.degree(product, t)
print(degree_of_product)
```
Code execution result: 14

This confirms our reasoning. The degree of the product of two 7th-degree polynomials is 14. Thus, the final answer is \(\boxed{14}\). This is our final answer.
