To solve the problem, we need to calculate the volume of each spherical snowball and then sum those volumes.

The formula for the volume \( V \) of a sphere with radius \( r \) is given by:
\[
V = \frac{4}{3} \pi r^3
\]

We have three snowballs with radii of 2 inches, 3 inches, and 5 inches. We will calculate the volume for each of these spherical snowballs and then sum them up.

1. For the snowball with radius 2 inches:
\[
V_1 = \frac{4}{3} \pi (2)^3 = \frac{4}{3} \pi \cdot 8 = \frac{32}{3} \pi
\]

2. For the snowball with radius 3 inches:
\[
V_2 = \frac{4}{3} \pi (3)^3 = \frac{4}{3} \pi \cdot 27 = \frac{108}{3} \pi = 36 \pi
\]

3. For the snowball with radius 5 inches:
\[
V_3 = \frac{4}{3} \pi (5)^3 = \frac{4}{3} \pi \cdot 125 = \frac{500}{3} \pi
\]

The total volume \( V_{\text{total}} \) of all three snowballs is the sum of these individual volumes:
\[
V_{\text{total}} = V_1 + V_2 + V_3 = \frac{32}{3} \pi + 36 \pi + \frac{500}{3} \pi
\]

To add these, we can get a common denominator for the fractions and combine the terms:

\[
V_{\text{total}} = \frac{32 \pi}{3} + \frac{108 \pi}{3} + \frac{500 \pi}{3} = \frac{32 \pi + 108 \pi + 500 \pi}{3} = \frac{640 \pi}{3}
\]

So, the total volume of snow Kadin uses is:
\[
\boxed{\frac{640 \pi}{3}}
\]
