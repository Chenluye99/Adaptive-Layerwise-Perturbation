First, let's establish an equation for solving this problem. We know that:

- The elephant runs at 19 miles per hour.
- The lion runs at 24 miles per hour.
- The initial distance between the elephant and the lion is 1 mile.

The closing speed of the gap is the difference in their speeds because they are running towards each other, which is \(24 - 19 = 5\) miles per hour. 

Let's say \( t \) is the time in hours it takes for the lion to catch up to the elephant. After time \( t \), the distance covered by the elephant will be \( 19t \) and the distance covered by the lion will be \( 24t \). Since they start 1 mile apart, after time \( t \), the distance the lion gets closer to the elephant will be \( 24t - 19t = 5t \), and this distance is the original 1 mile between them.

So, we can write the following equation:
\[ 5t = 1 \]
\[ t = \frac{1}{5} \text{ hours} \]

To convert this time into minutes, we multiply by 60 (since there are 60 minutes in an hour):
\[ t = \frac{1}{5} \times 60 = 12 \text{ minutes} \]

Let's confirm this with Python code to ensure our result is accurate.
```python
# Assign the speeds
speed_elephant = 19  # in miles per hour
speed_lion = 24      # in miles per hour
initial_distance = 1 # in miles

# Calculate the relative speed at which the lion gets closer to the elephant
relative_speed = speed_lion - speed_elephant  # in miles per hour

# Calculate the time it takes for the lion to catch up to the elephant in hours
time_to_catch_up_hours = initial_distance / relative_speed

# Convert this time into minutes
time_to_catch_up_minutes = time_to_catch_up_hours * 60
print(time_to_catch_up_minutes)
```
Code execution result: 12.0

Thus, it will take the lion 12 minutes to catch the elephant.

So, the final answer is:
\(\boxed{12}\)
