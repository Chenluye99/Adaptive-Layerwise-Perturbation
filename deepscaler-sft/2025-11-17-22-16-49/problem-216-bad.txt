To ensure the function \( f(x) \) is continuous, we need to ensure that there are no jumps or breaks at the points where the definition of the function changes, i.e., at \( x = 2 \) and \( x = -2 \).

1. **Continuity at \( x = 2 \):**
    - For \( x > 2 \), \( f(x) = 2ax + 4 \).
    - For \( -2 \le x \le 2 \), \( f(x) = x^2 - 2 \).
    - The condition for continuity at \( x = 2 \) is:
      \[
      \lim_{{x \to 2^+}} f(x) = \lim_{{x \to 2^-}} f(x) = f(2)
      \]
      So, we need:
      \[
      2a(2) + 4 = 2^2 - 2
      \]
      Simplifying both sides, we get:
      \[
      4a + 4 = 4 - 2 \implies 4a + 4 = 2 \implies 4a = -2 \implies a = -\frac{1}{2}
      \]

2. **Continuity at \( x = -2 \):**
    - For \( -2 \le x \le 2 \), \( f(x) = x^2 - 2 \).
    - For \( x < -2 \), \( f(x) = 3x - c \).
    - The condition for continuity at \( x = -2 \) is:
      \[
      \lim_{{x \to -2^+}} f(x) = \lim_{{x \to -2^-}} f(x) = f(-2)
      \]
      So, we need:
      \[
      (-2)^2 - 2 = 3(-2) - c
      \]
      Simplifying both sides, we get:
      \[
      4 - 2 = -6 - c \implies 2 = -6 - c \implies 8 = -c \implies c = -8
      \]

3. **Find \( a + c \):**
    - We have \( a = -\frac{1}{2} \) and \( c = -8 \).
    - Therefore, \( a + c = -\frac{1}{2} - 8 = -\frac{1}{2} - \frac{16}{2} = -\frac{17}{2} \).
