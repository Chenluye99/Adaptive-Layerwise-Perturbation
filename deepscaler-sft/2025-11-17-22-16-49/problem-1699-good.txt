First, let's analyze the problem. We have a right-angled triangle XYZ with a right angle at X, angle Y equal to \(45^\circ\), and the side opposite the \(45^\circ\) angle (ZX) is labeled as \(12\sqrt{2}\).

Since it is a right-angled triangle with a \(45^\circ\) angle, it is an isosceles right triangle. In an isosceles right triangle, the legs are of equal length, and if the legs are each of length \(a\), then the hypotenuse is \(a\sqrt{2}\).

In this triangle, the side opposite the \(45^\circ\) angle (which is ZX) is given as \(12\sqrt{2}\). This side is one of the legs of the isosceles right triangle. Therefore, if the length of each leg of the triangle is \(a\), then:

\[ a \cdot \sqrt{2} = 12 \sqrt{2} \]

By canceling \(\sqrt{2}\) from both sides, we get:

\[ a = 12 \]

So, the lengths of both legs of the triangle (XY and YZ) are 12. We need to find the length of the hypotenuse (YZ), which is \(XY\) in this context. The hypotenuse of an isosceles right triangle with legs of length \(a\) is \(a \sqrt{2}\). Therefore:

\[ XY = 12 \sqrt{2} \]
