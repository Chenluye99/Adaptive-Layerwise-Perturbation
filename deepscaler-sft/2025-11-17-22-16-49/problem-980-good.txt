To solve this problem, we can use the principle of inclusion and exclusion to find out how many students take either French, Spanish, or both. Then, we can determine how many students take neither of these languages.

1. **Identify the Given Values:**
    - Total number of students: \( |U| = 30 \)
    - Number of students who take French: \( |F| = 8 \)
    - Number of students who take Spanish: \( |S| = 12 \)
    - Number of students who take both French and Spanish: \( |F \cap S| = 3 \)

2. **Apply the Principle of Inclusion and Exclusion:**
    The number of students who take either French, Spanish, or both is given by:
    \[
    |F \cup S| = |F| + |S| - |F \cap S|
    \]
    Substituting the given values:
    \[
    |F \cup S| = 8 + 12 - 3 = 17
    \]
    So, 17 students take either French, Spanish, or both.

3. **Find the Number of Students Who Take Neither Language:**
    The number of students who take neither French nor Spanish is the total number of students minus those who take either French, Spanish, or both:
    \[
    \text{Number of students who take neither} = |U| - |F \cup S| = 30 - 17 = 13
    \]

Let's confirm this by implementing it in Python.
```python
# Given values
total_students = 30
students_french = 8
students_spanish = 12
students_both = 3

# Applying the principle of inclusion and exclusion to get the number of students who take either French, Spanish, or both
students_either_or_both = students_french + students_spanish - students_both

# Finding the number of students who take neither French nor Spanish
students_neither = total_students - students_either_or_both
print(students_neither)
```
Code execution result: 13

Thus, the number of students who take neither French nor Spanish is \(\boxed{13}\). This is our final answer.
