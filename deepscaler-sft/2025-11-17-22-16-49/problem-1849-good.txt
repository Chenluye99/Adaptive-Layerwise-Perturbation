The problem requires us to compute the product of all numbers from \(2^1\) to \(2^{100}\), and then find the product of the tens and ones digits of the resulting integer.

First, let's calculate the product:

\[
P = 2^1 \cdot 2^2 \cdot 2^3 \cdot \ldots \cdot 2^{99} \cdot 2^{100}
\]

Notice that this is equivalent to:

\[
P = 2^{1 + 2 + 3 + \ldots + 99 + 100}
\]

The sum of the exponents from 1 to 100 can be calculated using the formula for the sum of the first \(n\) natural numbers:

\[
\text{Sum} = \frac{n(n + 1)}{2}
\]

For \(n = 100\):

\[
\text{Sum} = \frac{100 \cdot 101}{2} = 5050
\]

So, we have:

\[
P = 2^{5050}
\]

Next, we need to determine the last two digits (i.e., the tens and ones digits) of \(2^{5050}\). To do this, we will compute \(2^{5050} \mod 100\).

We can use Python to compute this efficiently. Let's get started by calculating \(2^{5050} \mod 100\) and then extract the tens and ones digits.
```python
# Calculate 2^5050 % 100 to get the last two digits of 2^5050
last_two_digits = pow(2, 5050, 100)

# Split the last two digits into tens and ones place
tens_digit = last_two_digits // 10
ones_digit = last_two_digits % 10

# Calculate the product of the tens and ones digits
product_of_digits = tens_digit * ones_digit
print(product_of_digits)
```
Code execution result: 8

Thus, the product of the tens digit and the ones digit of \(2^{5050}\) is \(\boxed{8}\). This concludes our solution.
