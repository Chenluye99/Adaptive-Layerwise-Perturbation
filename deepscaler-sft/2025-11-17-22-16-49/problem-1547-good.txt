To solve this problem, let's begin by understanding the problem statement and how each function would behave based on the given rates.

1. **Problem Understanding:**
    - Water flows into the birdbath at a rate of 20 milliliters per minute.
    - Water drains from the birdbath at a rate of 18 milliliters per minute.
    - Therefore, the net rate at which the volume of water in the birdbath is increasing is \(20 - 18 = 2\) milliliters per minute.

2. **Analyzing the Graphs:**
    - **Graph A:** Water level starts increasing, but the rate of increase is constantly high and doesn't show a consistent pattern of a constant net rate of increase of 2 mL/min after the system has stabilized.
    - **Graph B:** Initially, the water level increases, but it doesn't show a consistent rate of increase consistent with a net 2 mL/min, and the slope seems to get steeper over time instead of being constant as it would if it were just a constant net inflow.
    - **Graph C:** Initially, the water level increases at a very high rate, indicating more water coming in than going out initially, then it levels off but does not show a constant rate of 2 mL/min increase. It seems to either show a leveling-off point prematurely or begins to stabilize after some time rather than continually increasing at a consistent rate.
    - **Graph D:** Initially, the water level increases, then after some point, the graph levels off. This suggests that the inflow and outflow have reached equilibrium, but it doesn't maintain a steady state volume but rather an overflowing state where the inflow continues to add volume that is being drained at the same rate, but there's an initial period of increasing volume before the graph levels off. This implies some delay before overflow starts, but before this stabilization, it should show an increase (as water is initially being added faster than drained).
    - **Graph E:** The water level starts to increase and maintains a steady and consistent rate of increase. It matches our condition where the net inflow should be 2 mL/min.

From reasoning above, only Graph E consistently shows a steady and consistent rate of increase in volume, matching our requirement that water should be added at a net rate of 2 mL/min.

Let's now confirm this by coding a solution to check if Graph E fits the criteria of increasing volume at a net rate of 2 mL/min. We can simulate this by plotting a graph that has a slope of 2, indicating a net inflow of 2 mL/min and verifying it against the description above.
```python
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

# We will simulate the volume over time starting from t=0 and up to t=10 minutes as an example.
# Let's initialize the volume and simulate it from t=0 to t=10.
times = list(range(11))  # Time in minutes from 0 to 10
volume = [0]  # Starting volume at 0 milliliters

# Now simulate the volume change over the time period
for t in range(1, 11):
    volume.append(volume[t-1] + (20 - 18))  # Add net inflow of 2 milliliters each minute

# Plotting the graph for verification
plt.plot(times, volume)
plt.xlabel("Time (minutes)")
plt.ylabel("Volume (milliliters)")
plt.title("Volume of Water in Birdbath Over Time")
plt.grid(True)
plt.show()

print(((times, volume)))
```
