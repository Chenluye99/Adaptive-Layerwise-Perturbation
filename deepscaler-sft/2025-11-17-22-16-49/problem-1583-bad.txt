To solve the problem, we need to express \(\sqrt{53 + 20 \sqrt{7}}\) in the form \(a + b \sqrt{c}\) where \(a, b, c\) are integers, and \(c\) has no factors which is a perfect square of any positive integer other than 1.

Let's assume:
\[
\sqrt{53 + 20 \sqrt{7}} = a + b \sqrt{c}
\]
Square both sides of the equation to obtain:
\[
53 + 20 \sqrt{7} = (a + b \sqrt{c})^2
\]
Expanding the right-hand side, we get:
\[
53 + 20 \sqrt{7} = a^2 + 2ab \sqrt{c} + b^2 c
\]
For the above equation to hold, the rational and irrational parts on both sides must match separately. Therefore, we can separate the equation into two parts:

1. The rational part: \(a^2 + b^2 c = 53\)
2. The irrational part: \(2ab \sqrt{c} = 20 \sqrt{7}\)

From the irrational part, we can equate the coefficients of \(\sqrt{7}\):
\[
2ab \sqrt{c} = 20 \sqrt{7} \implies 2ab = 20 \text{ and } \sqrt{c} = \sqrt{7} \implies 2ab = 20 \implies ab = 10 \quad \text{and} \quad c = 7
\]

Now we substitute \(c = 7\) into the rational part of the equation:
\[
a^2 + b^2 \cdot 7 = 53 \implies a^2 + 7b^2 = 53
\]

We need to find pairs of integers \((a, b)\) such that \(ab = 10\) and \(a^2 + 7b^2 = 53\). We can check the possible factor pairs of 10: \((1, 10)\), \((-1, -10)\), \((2, 5)\), \((-2, -5)\), \((5, 2)\), \((-5, -2)\), \((10, 1)\), and \((-10, -1)\).

We will check each pair to see which one satisfies both equations.
