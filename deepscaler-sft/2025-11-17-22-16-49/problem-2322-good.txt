### Step-by-Step Solution:

1. **Understand the Asymptotes:**
    - The rational function has a horizontal asymptote of \( y = 0 \). This tells us that the degree of \( p(x) \) is less than the degree of \( q(x) \).
    - The rational function has a vertical asymptote of \( x = -1 \). This means that \( q(x) \) must have a factor of \( (x + 1) \).

    - Given that \( q(x) \) is quadratic, we can write \( q(x) = a(x + 1)(x - r) \) for some constants \( a \) and \( r \).
    - For the horizontal asymptote to be \( y = 0 \), the degree of \( p(x) \) must be less than the degree of \( q(x) \). Since \( q(x) \) is quadratic (degree 2), \( p(x) \) must be a linear polynomial (degree 1). So, we can write \( p(x) = bx + c \).

3. **Using Given Conditions:**
    - We are given that \( p(2) = 1 \), so substituting \( x = 2 \) into \( p(x) \), we get: 
      \[
      p(2) = b(2) + c = 2b + c = 1.
      \]
    - We are also given that \( q(2) = 3 \), so substituting \( x = 2 \) into \( q(x) \), we get:
      \[
      q(2) = a(2 + 1)(2 - r) = 3a(2 - r) = 3 \implies a(2 - r) = 1.

    - The rational function is given by \(\frac{p(x)}{q(x)} = \frac{bx + c}{a(x + 1)(x - r)}\). 5. **Solving for Constants:**

      \[
      p(2) = b(2 - 1) = b = 1.

    - Now, we have \( p(x) = x - 1 \) and \( q(x) = x^2 - 1 \). Therefore,
