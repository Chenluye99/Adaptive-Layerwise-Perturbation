1. **Set Up the Problem:**
    - We need to find the slope \( k \) of a line that intersects the parabola \( y^2 = 4x \) at points \( A \) and \( B \), and also is tangent to the circle \((x - 5)^2 + y^2 = 9\) at the midpoint of segment \( AB \).

2. **Equation of the Line:**
    - The equation of a line with slope \( k \) can be written as \( y = kx + c \).
    - Substitute this line equation into the parabola equation \( y^2 = 4x \):
      \[
      (kx + c)^2 = 4x \implies k^2 x^2 + 2kc x + c^2 = 4x \implies k^2 x^2 + (2kc - 4) x + c^2 = 0
      \]
    - This is a quadratic equation in \( x \). Let the roots of this quadratic equation be \( x_1 \) and \( x_2 \), which correspond to the \( x \)-coordinates of points \( A \) and \( B \). By Vieta's formulas, we have:
      \[
      x_1 + x_2 = -\frac{2kc - 4}{k^2}
      \]
    - The corresponding \( y \)-coordinates of points \( A \) and \( B \) are given by substituting \( x_1 \) and \( x_2 \) into the line equation \( y = kx + c \):
      \[
      y_1 = kx_1 + c, \quad y_2 = kx_2 + c
      \]

3. **Midpoint of \( AB \):**
    - The midpoint \( M \) of segment \( AB \) has coordinates:
      \[
      M \left( \frac{x_1 + x_2}{2}, \frac{y_1 + y_2}{2} \right)
      \]
    - Substituting the expressions for \( x_1 + x_2 \), we get:
      \[
      x_M = \frac{x_1 + x_2}{2} = \frac{-\frac{2kc - 4}{k^2}}{2} = \frac{4 - 2kc}{2k^2} = \frac{2 - kc}{k^2}
      \]
      \[
      y_M = \frac{y_1 + y_2}{2} = \frac{kx_1 + c + kx_2 + c}{2} = \frac{k(x_1 + x_2) + 2c}{2} = \frac{k \left( -\frac{2kc - 4}{k^2} \right) + 2c}{2} = \frac{-2kc + \frac{4k}{k^2} + 2c}{2} = \frac{-2kc + \frac{4}{k} + 2c}{2}
      \]
      \[
      y_M = \frac{-2kc + \frac{4}{k} + 2c}{2} = \frac{-2kc + \frac{4}{k} + 2c}{2}
      \]

4. **Tangency Condition with the Circle:**
    - The circle is given by \((x - 5)^2 + y^2 = 9\), which has center \((5, 0)\) and radius \(3\).
    - For the line \( y = kx + c \) to be tangent to the circle, the perpendicular distance from the center of the circle to the line must be equal to the radius of the circle.
    - The distance \(d\) from the center \((5, 0)\) to the line \( y = kx + c \) (or \( kx - y + c = 0 \)) is given by:
      \[
      d = \frac{|5k - 0 + c|}{\sqrt{k^2 + 1}} = \frac{|5k + c|}{\sqrt{k^2 + 1}}
      \]
    - Since this distance must be equal to the radius of the circle, we have:
      \[
      \frac{|5k + c|}{\sqrt{k^2 + 1}} = 3 \implies |5k + c| = 3 \sqrt{k^2 + 1}
      \]
      This gives us two possible equations:
      \[
      5k + c = 3 \sqrt{k^2 + 1} \quad \text{(1)}
      \]
      \[
      5k + c = -3 \sqrt{k^2 + 1} \quad \text{(2)}
      \]

5. **Midpoint Lies on the Circle:**
    - We know that the midpoint \( M \left( \frac{2 - kc}{k^2}, \frac{-2kc + \frac{4}{k} + 2c}{2} \right) \) lies on the circle \((x - 5)^2 + y^2 = 9\). Substituting the coordinates of \( M \) into this equation, we get:
      \[
      \left( \frac{2 - kc}{k^2} - 5 \right)^2 + \left( \frac{-2kc + \frac{4}{k} + 2c}{2} \right)^2 = 9
      \]
