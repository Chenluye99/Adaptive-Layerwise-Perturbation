### Part (1): Find the General Equation of Curve \( C \) and the Rectangular Coordinate Equation of Line \( l \)

1. **Find the General Equation of Curve \( C \):**
   - Given the parametric equations of curve \( C \):
     \[
     \begin{cases}
     x = 2 + 3 \cos \alpha, \\
     y = 3 \sin \alpha
     \end{cases}
     \]
   - We can eliminate the parameter \(\alpha\) by using the identity \(\cos^2 \alpha + \sin^2 \alpha = 1\):
     \[
     \left( \frac{x - 2}{3} \right)^2 + \left( \frac{y}{3} \right)^2 = 1 \implies \frac{(x - 2)^2}{9} + \frac{y^2}{9} = 1 \implies (x - 2)^2 + y^2 = 9
     \]
   - So, the general equation of curve \( C \) is \((x - 2)^2 + y^2 = 9\).

2. **Find the Rectangular Coordinate Equation of Line \( l \):**
   - Given the polar coordinate equation of the line \( l \):
     \[
     2 \rho \cos \theta - \rho \sin \theta - 1 = 0
     \]
   - We know that in Cartesian coordinates, \(\rho = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2}\), \(\cos \theta = \frac{x}{\sqrt{x^2 + y^2}}\), and \(\sin \theta = \frac{y}{\sqrt{x^2 + y^2}}\). Substituting these into the polar equation, we get:
     \[
     2 \frac{x}{\sqrt{x^2 + y^2}} \cdot \sqrt{x^2 + y^2} - \frac{y}{\sqrt{x^2 + y^2}} \cdot \sqrt{x^2 + y^2} - 1 = 0 \implies 2x - y - 1 = 0
     \]
   - So, the rectangular coordinate equation of line \( l \) is \( 2x - y - 1 = 0 \).

import sympy as sp

# Define variables
x, y = sp.symbols('x y')
