First, let's start by understanding the problem.

1. **Compartment Diagram**: The compartment has six seats, labeled from 1 to 6.
2. **Broken Seats**: Four of these seats are broken.
3. **Checking Off Seats**: Wilhelm checks off any four seats randomly out of the six.

We need to determine the probability that Wilhelm checks off the exact four seats that are broken. First, we identify all ways to choose 4 seats out of 6. This can be calculated using combinations, denoted as \( \binom{6}{4} \).

\[ \binom{6}{4} = \frac{6!}{4!(6-4)!} = \frac{6!}{4! \cdot 2!} = \frac{6 \times 5}{2 \times 1} = 15 \]

So, there are 15 different ways to choose 4 seats out of 6.

Next, we need to consider the scenario where the four chosen seats are the four specific seats that are broken. There is exactly 1 way to choose the 4 broken seats out of the 4 broken seats. 

Hence, the probability that Wilhelm marks the correct seats is given by the ratio of the number of ways to choose the 4 broken seats to the total number of ways to choose 4 seats out of 6.

\[ \text{Probability} = \frac{1}{15} \]

Let's confirm this using Python to ensure there are no errors in our reasoning. We will consider all possible ways to choose 4 seats out of 6 and count how many of those ways match a predefined set of the 4 broken seats.
```python
import itertools

# List of 6 seats
seats = [1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6]

# Four seats are considered "broken"
broken_seats = [1, 2, 3, 4]  # We assume these are the broken seats (labels may vary but that's irrelevant for combinatorial counting)

# Get all ways to choose 4 seats out of 6
ways_to_choose_4_seats = list(itertools.combinations(seats, 4))

# Count how many of those choices are exactly the broken seats chosen
count_correct_permutations = sum(1 for choice in ways_to_choose_4_seats if set(choice) == set(broken_seats))

# Total number of ways to choose 4 seats out of 6
total_ways = len(ways_to_choose_4_seats)

# Calculate the probability
probability = count_correct_permutations / total_ways
print(probability)
```
Code execution result: 0.06666666666666667

This result is equivalent to \(\frac{1}{15}\) when expressed as a fraction.

So, the probability that Wilhelm marked the correct seats is:

\(\boxed{\frac{1}{15}}\)
