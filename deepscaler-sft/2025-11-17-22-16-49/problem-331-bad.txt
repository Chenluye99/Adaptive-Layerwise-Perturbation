To solve this problem, we need to determine the number of ways to form triangles by choosing three points such that no two of the three points are on the same side of the rectangle. This is because, for three points to form a triangle, no two points should be collinear. Given that the points are chosen from different sides of the rectangle, any combination of one point from \(AB\), one point from \(BC\), and one point from either \(AD\) or \(CD\) would allow us to create a triangle.

However, since the points on \(AD\) and \(CD\) are not chosen directly, we interpret that a triangle will be formed only if we choose points from different sides of the rectangle. Let's define:

- Six points on side \(AB\).
- Seven points on side \(BC\).

For any three points to form a triangle, one must be chosen from \(AB\), one from \(BC\), and the third point can come from either side of the rectangle but since sides \(AD\) and \(CD\) are considered as potential addition when forming a triangle with points chosen from \(AB\) and \(BC\), we consider them as extra logical possibilities (even though in practical form, a third point logically fits on some other side of the rectangle but here, it's redundant as long as we ensure choosing from 2 distinct sides, here chosen as logical distinct pairs).
