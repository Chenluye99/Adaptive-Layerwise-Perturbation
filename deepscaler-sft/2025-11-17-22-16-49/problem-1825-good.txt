To solve this problem, we need to calculate the probability that either two kings or at least one ace occurs when two cards are drawn randomly from a standard deck of 52 cards.

We will use combinatorial methods and probability calculations to solve this problem.

1. **Calculate the total number of ways to select 2 cards from a deck of 52 cards:**
   The total number of ways to choose 2 cards from 52 is given by the combination formula \( \binom{52}{2} \):
   \[
   \binom{52}{2} = \frac{52!}{2!(52-2)!} = \frac{52 \times 51}{2 \times 1} = 1326
   \]

2. **Calculate the number of favorable outcomes:**

   We need to consider the cases where either two kings or at least one ace (or both) occur.

   - **Number of ways to get two kings:**
     There are 4 kings in a deck, so the number of ways to choose 2 kings from 4 is:
     \[
     \binom{4}{2} = \frac{4!}{2!(4-2)!} = \frac{4 \times 3}{2 \times 1} = 6
     \]

   - **Number of ways to get at least one ace:**
     First, calculate the number of ways to get no aces (i.e., 2 cards chosen from the 48 non-ace cards):
     \[
     \binom{48}{2} = \frac{48!}{2!(48-2)!} = \frac{48 \times 47}{2 \times 1} = 1128
     \]
     Then, the number of ways to get at least one ace is the total ways to choose 2 cards from 52 minus the ways to choose 2 cards from the 48 non-ace cards:
     \[
     \text{Number of ways to get at least one ace} = \binom{52}{2} - \binom{48}{2} = 1326 - 1128 = 198
     \]

   - **Number of ways to get two kings and at least one ace:**
     We need to consider the overlap, i.e., the ways to choose 2 cards such that we get both two kings and at least one ace. It is impossible to have two kings and at least one ace at the same time because a deck only has 4 kings, and drawing 2 kings means we exclude all other cards, including aces (unless we draw a king-ace pair but even then it doesn't meet both criteria at once in a single draw set we are considering here).
     
     Therefore, the intersection of these two events is actually the sum of the individual events minus the overcounting, but here it’s simpler, as the event "draw two kings" is disjoint from "draw at least one ace" in this context since they both mean distinct outcomes when picking 2 cards (a king-ace combination doesn’t fit as we are talking about either event happening in the context of selecting 2 cards):

     So, we just need to add the number of ways to get two kings and the number of ways to get at least one ace without double-counting any scenarios which in this case isn’t possible directly, but we may have some scenario overlap which needs filtered out logically.

     So, we get:
     \[
     \text{Number of favorable outcomes} = \text{Number of ways to get two kings} + \text{Number of ways to get at least one ace}
     \]
     We don't have to subtract any overlapping scenarios here because the intersection (getting two kings and at least one ace in just a two-card draw select) isn't relevant here as they're mutually exclusive in valid 2-card draw perspective of fulfilling both conditions in one set selection of two cards.

3. **Calculate the probability:**

   The probability is then given by the ratio of the number of favorable outcomes to the total number of outcomes:
   \[
   P(\text{either two kings or at least one ace}) = \frac{\text{Number of ways to get two kings} + \text{Number of ways to get at least one ace}}{\binom{52}{2}} = \frac{6 + 198}{1326} = \frac{204}{1326}
   \]

   Simplify this fraction:

   We can find the greatest common divisor (GCD) of 204 and 1326 to simplify the fraction accurately. Then, let's use Python to ensure the accuracy and get the simplified result.
```python
import math

# Given values
numerator = 6 + 198
denominator = 1326

# Find the greatest common divisor (GCD) of numerator and denominator
gcd = math.gcd(numerator, denominator)

# Simplify the fraction
simplified_numerator = numerator // gcd
simplified_denominator = denominator // gcd

print(((simplified_numerator, simplified_denominator)))
```
Code execution result: (2, 13)

Thus, the simplified fraction is \(\frac{2}{13}\).

So, the probability that either two kings or at least 1 ace occurs when 2 cards are selected randomly from a standard deck is:

\(\boxed{\frac{2}{13}}\)
