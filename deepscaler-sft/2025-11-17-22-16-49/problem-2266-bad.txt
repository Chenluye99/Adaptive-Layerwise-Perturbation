To solve the problem, we need to use the formula for the sum of an infinite geometric series. The sum \( S \) of an infinite geometric series with the first term \( a \) and common ratio \( r \) (where \( |r| < 1 \)) is given by:

\[ S = \frac{a}{1 - r} \]

In this problem, the sum of the series is 45, and the common ratio is \(-\frac{1}{2}\). Plugging these values into the formula, we have:

\[ 45 = \frac{a}{1 - \left(-\frac{1}{2}\right)} = \frac{a}{1 + \frac{1}{2}} = \frac{a}{\frac{3}{2}} = \frac{a \cdot 2}{3} = \frac{2a}{3} \]

To solve for \( a \), we can set up the equation:

\[ 45 = \frac{2a}{3} \]

Then, solve for \( a \):

\[ 45 \cdot 3 = 2a \]
\[ 135 = 2a \]
\[ a = \frac{135}{2} \]
