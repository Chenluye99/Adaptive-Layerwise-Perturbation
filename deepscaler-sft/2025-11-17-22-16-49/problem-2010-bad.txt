First, let's begin by understanding and visualizing the problem. We have a square, and two points \(P\) and \(Q\) are midpoints of two of its sides.

1. **Define the Square:**
    - Assume the square has side length 2 for simplicity (this choice doesn't affect the final fraction because the answer will be a ratio which is independent of the square's size).
    - The vertices of the square can therefore be taken as \((0, 0)\), \((2, 0)\), \((2, 2)\), and \((0, 2)\).

2. **Find the Coordinates of Points \(P\) and \(Q\):**
    - Point \(P\) is the midpoint of the side from \((0, 0)\) to \((0, 2)\), so its coordinates are \((0, 1)\).
    - Point \(Q\) is the midpoint of the side from \((0, 2)\) to \((2, 2)\), so its coordinates are \((1, 2)\).

3. **Form the Shaded Region:**
    - The shaded region is a triangle with vertices at \((0, 1)\), \((1, 2)\), and \((2, 2)\).

4. **Calculate the Area of the Square:**
    - The area of the square with side length 2 is \(2 \times 2 = 4\).

5. **Calculate the Area of the Triangle:**
    - To find the area of the triangle with vertices \((0, 1)\), \((1, 2)\), and \((2, 2)\), we use the formula for the area of a triangle given by:
      \[
      \text{Area} = \frac{1}{2} \left| x_1(y_2 - y_3) + x_2(y_3 - y_1) + x_3(y_1 - y_2) \right|
      \]
      Substituting the coordinates \((x_1, y_1) = (0, 1)\), \((x_2, y_2) = (1, 2)\), and \((x_3, y_3) = (2, 2)\):
      \[
      \text{Area} = \frac{1}{2} \left| 0(2 - 2) + 1(2 - 1) + 2(1 - 2) \right| = \frac{1}{2} \left| 0 + 1 - 2 \right| = \frac{1}{2} \left| -1 \right| = \frac{1}{2}
      \]

6. **Find the Fraction of the Interior of the Square that is Shaded:**
    - The fraction of the interior of the square that is shaded is the ratio of the area of the triangle to the area of the square:
      \[
      \text{Fraction} = \frac{\frac{1}{2}}{4} = \frac{1}{2} \times \frac{1}{4} = \frac{1}{8}
      \]
