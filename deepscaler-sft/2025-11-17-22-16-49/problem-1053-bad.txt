To solve this problem, let's start by setting up the relationships and using Python to ensure our answer is accurate.

1. **Problem Understanding:**
    - The train is 1 mile long.
    - The train is moving at 60 miles per hour.
    - The tail of the train exits the tunnel exactly 3 minutes after the front of the train enters the tunnel.

2. **Convert Units:**
    - First, convert 3 minutes to hours because the speed of the train is given in miles per hour. 
      \[
      3 \text{ minutes} = \frac{3}{60} \text{ hours} = \frac{1}{20} \text{ hours}
      \]

3. **Distance Covered by the Train in 3 Minutes:**
    - In \(\frac{1}{20}\) hours, the train covers a distance of:
      \[
      \text{Distance} = \text{Speed} \times \text{Time} = 60 \, \text{miles/hour} \times \frac{1}{20} \, \text{hours} = 3 \, \text{miles}
      \]
    - This distance of 3 miles includes both the length of the tunnel and the length of the train (since the tail of the train exits the tunnel after 3 minutes, the total distance the front of the train has traveled minus the length of the train is the length of the tunnel).

4. **Formulate the Equation:**
    - Let the length of the tunnel be \( L \) miles. When the tail of the train exits the tunnel, the front of the train has traveled a distance of \( L + 1 \) miles (since the train is 1 mile long and covers the length of the tunnel plus its own length by the time the tail exits).
    - The distance the front of the train travels in \(\frac{1}{20}\) hours is 3 miles. So, we can set up the following equation:
      \[
      \text{Distance traveled by the front of the train in } \frac{1}{20} \text{ hours} = L + 1
      \]
      Since the distance traveled by the front of the train in \(\frac{1}{20}\) hours is 3 miles:
      \[
      L + 1 = 3
      \]

5. **Solve for \( L \):**
    - Solving the equation for \( L \):
      \[
      L = 3 - 1 = 2
      \]

So, the length of the tunnel is 2 miles. Let's confirm this with Python to ensure the accuracy of our result.
```python
# Given values
train_speed_mph = 60  # speed of the train in miles per hour
time_difference_hours = 3 / 60  # 3 minutes converted to hours

# Calculate the distance traveled by the front of the train in that time
distance_traveled_by_train = train_speed_mph * time_difference_hours

# The distance traveled by the front of the train in that time is equal to the length of the tunnel + the length of the train
# Let L be the length of the tunnel, then we have:
# L + length_of_train = distance_traveled_by_train
# Given that the length of the train is 1 mile, we get:
L = distance_traveled_by_train - 1

print(L)
```
