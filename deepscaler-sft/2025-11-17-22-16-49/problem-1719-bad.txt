First, let's understand the problem. We have a right triangle \( \triangle PQR \) with \( \angle QPR = 30^\circ \). The hypotenuse is 9√3 and we need to find the length of side \( PQ \).

In a right triangle, the sine, cosine, and tangent of an angle relate the lengths of the sides. For a 30° angle, we know from trigonometry that:
\[
\sin(30^\circ) = \frac{1}{2}, \quad \cos(30^\circ) = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}, \quad \tan(30^\circ) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}
\]

Given that \( QR \) is the hypotenuse and its length is \( 9\sqrt{3} \), and that \( \angle QPR = 30^\circ \), we can use the definition of cosine for the 30° angle:
\[
\cos(30^\circ) = \frac{\text{adjacent}}{\text{hypotenuse}} = \frac{PQ}{QR}
\]
Substituting the known values, we get:
\[
\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = \frac{PQ}{9\sqrt{3}}
\]
We can solve for \( PQ \) by cross-multiplying:
\[
PQ = 9\sqrt{3} \cdot \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = \frac{9 \cdot 3}{2} = \frac{27}{2}
\]

Let's confirm this by coding the calculation to ensure accuracy.
```python
import sympy as sp

# Given values
hypotenuse = 9 * sp.sqrt(3)
angle_deg = 30

# Convert angle to radians for trigonometric function
angle_rad = sp.rad(angle_deg)

# Calculate the length of PQ using the cosine function
PQ = hypotenuse * sp.cos(angle_rad)
print(PQ)
```
Code execution result: 27/2

Thus, the length of \( PQ \) is \(\boxed{\frac{27}{2}}\). This confirms our analytical solution.
