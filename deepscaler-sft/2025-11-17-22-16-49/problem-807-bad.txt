To solve this problem, let's start by understanding the geometric properties of the sector of the circle and the circle circumscribed about it.

1. **Understand the Problem:**
    - We have a sector of a circle with radius 6 and central angle \(\theta\).
    - The vertices of this sector are points \(A\) and \(B\) on the circle, with the center of the circle being \(O\).
    - The circumscribed circle (circumcircle) of the sector will pass through points \(A\), \(B\), and \(O\).

2. **Geometric Insight:**
    - Consider the triangle formed by points \(A\), \(B\), and \(O\). This is an isosceles triangle with two sides of length 6 (the radii of the original circle).
    - To find the radius of the circumcircle of triangle \(OAB\), we can use properties of the circumcircle and trigonometry.

3. **Using Properties of Circumscribed Circle:**
    - For any triangle, the radius \(R\) of the circumcircle can be given by the formula:
      \[
      R = \frac{a}{2 \sin A}
      \]
      where \(a\) is a side of the triangle and \(A\) is the opposite angle.
    - For triangle \(OAB\):
      - \(OA = OB = 6\) (both are radii of the original circle).
      - \(\angle AOB = \theta\).
    - The side \(AB\) of triangle \(OAB\) can be calculated using the Law of Cosines:
      \[
      AB = \sqrt{OA^2 + OB^2 - 2 \cdot OA \cdot OB \cdot \cos \theta} = \sqrt{6^2 + 6^2 - 2 \cdot 6 \cdot 6 \cdot \cos \theta} = \sqrt{72 - 72 \cos \theta} = 6 \sqrt{2 (1 - \cos \theta)}
      \]
    - Using the identity \(\sin \frac{\theta}{2} = \sqrt{\frac{1 - \cos \theta}{2}}\), we get:
      \[
      \sin \frac{\theta}{2} = \sqrt{\frac{1 - \cos \theta}{2}}
      \]
      So,
      \[
      2 \sin \frac{\theta}{2} \cos \frac{\theta}{2} = 6 \sqrt{2 (1 - \cos \theta)} = 6 \sqrt{4 \cdot \frac{1 - \cos \theta}{2}} = 12 \sin \frac{\theta}{2}
      \]
    - Then we have:
      \[
      R = \frac{6 \sqrt{2 (1 - \cos \theta)}}{2 \sin \frac{\theta}{2}} = \frac{6 \cdot 2 \sin \frac{\theta}{2}}{2 \sin \frac{\theta}{2}} = 3 \sec \frac{\theta}{2}
      \]

4. **Conclusion:**
    - Therefore, the radius of the circle circumscribed about the sector is \(3 \sec \frac{\theta}{2}\).
