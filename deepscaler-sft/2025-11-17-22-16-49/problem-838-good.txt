To solve the problem, we need to use the property of orthogonality and the dot product. Two vectors are orthogonal if their dot product is zero.

Given:
1. Vectors \(3 \mathbf{p} + \mathbf{q}\) and \(5 \mathbf{p} - 3 \mathbf{q}\) are orthogonal.
2. Vectors \(2 \mathbf{p} + \mathbf{q}\) and \(4 \mathbf{p} - 2 \mathbf{q}\) are orthogonal.

This gives us the following equations based on the dot product condition:

1. \((3 \mathbf{p} + \mathbf{q}) \cdot (5 \mathbf{p} - 3 \mathbf{q}) = 0\)
2. \((2 \mathbf{p} + \mathbf{q}) \cdot (4 \mathbf{p} - 2 \mathbf{q}) = 0\)

Let's denote \(\mathbf{p} \cdot \mathbf{p} = \|\mathbf{p}\|^2 = p^2\), \(\mathbf{q} \cdot \mathbf{q} = \|\mathbf{q}\|^2 = q^2\), and \(\mathbf{p} \cdot \mathbf{q} = \|\mathbf{p}\| \|\mathbf{q}\| \cos \theta = pq \cos \theta\).

First, we compute the dot products for the given equations:

1. \((3 \mathbf{p} + \mathbf{q}) \cdot (5 \mathbf{p} - 3 \mathbf{q}) = 0\)
   \[
   3 \mathbf{p} \cdot 5 \mathbf{p} + 3 \mathbf{p} \cdot (-3 \mathbf{q}) + \mathbf{q} \cdot 5 \mathbf{p} + \mathbf{q} \cdot (-3 \mathbf{q}) = 0
   \]
   \[
   15 (\mathbf{p} \cdot \mathbf{p}) - 9 (\mathbf{p} \cdot \mathbf{q}) + 5 (\mathbf{q} \cdot \mathbf{p}) - 3 (\mathbf{q} \cdot \mathbf{q}) = 0
   \]
   \[
   15 p^2 - 9 pq \cos \theta + 5 pq \cos \theta - 3 q^2 = 0
   \]
   \[
   15 p^2 - 4 pq \cos \theta - 3 q^2 = 0 \quad \text{(Equation 1)}
   \]

2. \((2 \mathbf{p} + \mathbf{q}) \cdot (4 \mathbf{p} - 2 \mathbf{q}) = 0\)
   \[
   2 \mathbf{p} \cdot 4 \mathbf{p} + 2 \mathbf{p} \cdot (-2 \mathbf{q}) + \mathbf{q} \cdot 4 \mathbf{p} + \mathbf{q} \cdot (-2 \mathbf{q}) = 0
   \]
   \[
   8 (\mathbf{p} \cdot \mathbf{p}) - 4 (\mathbf{p} \cdot \mathbf{q}) + 4 (\mathbf{q} \cdot \mathbf{p}) - 2 (\mathbf{q} \cdot \mathbf{q}) = 0
   \]
   \[
   8 p^2 - 4 pq \cos \theta + 4 pq \cos \theta - 2 q^2 = 0
   \]
   \[
   8 p^2 - 2 q^2 = 0 \quad \text{(Equation 2)}
   \]

From Equation 2, we can solve for the ratio of \( p^2 \) to \( q^2 \):

\[
8 p^2 = 2 q^2 \implies 4 p^2 = q^2 \implies q^2 = 4 p^2 \implies q = 2p \quad \text{or} \quad q = -2p
\]

We can substitute \( q = 2p \) (we can also substitute \( q = -2p \), but it will lead to the same cosine value because the cosine of an angle is the same as the cosine of its negative, i.e., \(\cos \theta = \cos (-\theta)\)) into Equation 1:

\[
15 p^2 - 4 p (2p) \cos \theta - 3 (2p)^2 = 0
\]
\[
15 p^2 - 8 p^2 \cos \theta - 12 p^2 = 0
\]
\[
3 p^2 - 8 p^2 \cos \theta = 0
\]
\[
3 - 8 \cos \theta = 0
\]
\[
8 \cos \theta = 3
\]
\[
\cos \theta = \frac{3}{8}
\]

Thus, the value of \(\cos \theta\) is \(\frac{3}{8}\). Let's confirm this by coding it to ensure there are no discrepancies.
```python
from sympy import symbols, Eq, solve

# Define the variables
p, q, cos_theta = symbols('p q cos_theta', real=True, positive=True)

# Define the equations based on dot product conditions
eq1 = Eq(15 * p**2 - 4 * p * q * cos_theta - 3 * q**2, 0)
eq2 = Eq(8 * p**2 - 2 * q**2, 0)

# Solve the system of equations
solution = solve((eq1, eq2), (p, q, cos_theta))

print(solution)
```
