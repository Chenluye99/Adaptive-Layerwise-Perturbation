To get the length of a median in a triangle with sides \(a\), \(b\), and \(c\), we use Apollonius's theorem. For a triangle with sides \(a, b, c\) and a median \(m_a\) corresponding to side \(a\), the formula for the length of the median is:

\[ m_a = \frac{1}{2} \sqrt{2b^2 + 2c^2 - a^2} \]

For our triangle with sides 10, 10, and 12, we will have three medians:

1. Median corresponding to the side of length 10 (let's call it \(m_1\)):
   \[ m_1 = \frac{1}{2} \sqrt{2 \cdot 10^2 + 2 \cdot 12^2 - 10^2} = \frac{1}{2} \sqrt{2 \cdot 100 + 2 \cdot 144 - 100} = \frac{1}{2} \sqrt{200 + 288 - 100} = \frac{1}{2} \sqrt{388} = \frac{1}{2} \sqrt{4 \cdot 97} = \sqrt{97} \]

2. Median corresponding to the side of length 10 (let's call it \(m_2\)):
   \[ m_2 = \frac{1}{2} \sqrt{2 \cdot 10^2 + 2 \cdot 12^2 - 10^2} = \sqrt{97} \]

3. Median corresponding to the side of length 12 (let's call it \(m_3\)):
   \[ m_3 = \frac{1}{2} \sqrt{2 \cdot 10^2 + 2 \cdot 10^2 - 12^2} = \frac{1}{2} \sqrt{2 \cdot 100 + 2 \cdot 100 - 144} = \frac{1}{2} \sqrt{200 + 200 - 144} = \frac{1}{2} \sqrt{256} = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 16 = 8 \]

So, the lengths of the medians are \(\sqrt{97}\), \(\sqrt{97}\), and 8.
