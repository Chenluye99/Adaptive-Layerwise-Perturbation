First, let's establish the general form of the equation of a hyperbola that is centered at the origin and opens either horizontally or vertically.

1. **Equation Forms:**
    - A hyperbola that opens horizontally has the form: \(\frac{x^2}{a^2} - \frac{y^2}{b^2} = 1\).
    - A hyperbola that opens vertically has the form: \(\frac{y^2}{a^2} - \frac{x^2}{b^2} = 1\).

2. **Substitute the Points:**
    - We are given that the hyperbola passes through the points \((-3, 4)\), \((-2, 0)\), and \((t, 2)\).

Let's start by checking if these points can satisfy either of the forms of the hyperbola equations.

### Step-by-Step Solution:

#### Step 1: Check if it's a Horizontal Hyperbola

Substitute \((-3, 4)\) and \((-2, 0)\) into \(\frac{x^2}{a^2} - \frac{y^2}{b^2} = 1\):

1. For \((-3, 4)\):
   \[
   \frac{(-3)^2}{a^2} - \frac{4^2}{b^2} = 1 \implies \frac{9}{a^2} - \frac{16}{b^2} = 1
   \]

2. For \((-2, 0)\):
   \[
   \frac{(-2)^2}{a^2} - \frac{0^2}{b^2} = 1 \implies \frac{4}{a^2} = 1 \implies a^2 = 4
   \]

Substitute \(a^2 = 4\) into the first equation:

\[
\frac{9}{4} - \frac{16}{b^2} = 1 \implies \frac{9}{4} - 1 = \frac{16}{b^2} \implies \frac{5}{4} = \frac{16}{b^2} \implies 5b^2 = 64 \implies b^2 = \frac{64}{5}
   \]

So, if it is a horizontal hyperbola, the equations are \(\frac{x^2}{4} - \frac{y^2}{\frac{64}{5}} = 1\).

Now, substitute \((t, 2)\) into this equation and solve for \(t\):

\[
\frac{t^2}{4} - \frac{2^2}{\frac{64}{5}} = 1 \implies \frac{t^2}{4} - \frac{4}{\frac{64}{5}} = 1 \implies \frac{t^2}{4} - \frac{4 \cdot 5}{64} = 1 \implies \frac{t^2}{4} - \frac{5}{16} = 1 \implies \frac{t^2}{4} = 1 + \frac{5}{16} \implies \frac{t^2}{4} = \frac{16}{16} + \frac{5}{16} \implies \frac{t^2}{4} = \frac{21}{16} \implies t^2 = 4 \cdot \frac{21}{16} \implies t^2 = \frac{84}{16} = \frac{21}{4}
   \]

Let's confirm if there's a possibility that it could be a vertical hyperbola next.

#### Step 2: Check if it's a Vertical Hyperbola

Substitute \((-3, 4)\) and \((-2, 0)\) into \(\frac{y^2}{a^2} - \frac{x^2}{b^2} = 1\):

1. For \((-3, 4)\):
   \[
   \frac{4^2}{a^2} - \frac{(-3)^2}{b^2} = 1 \implies \frac{16}{a^2} - \frac{9}{b^2} = 1
   \]

2. For \((-2, 0)\):
   \[
   \frac{0^2}{a^2} - \frac{(-2)^2}{b^2} = 1 \implies - \frac{4}{b^2} = 1
   \]
   This equation is not possible because \(- \frac{4}{b^2} \) is always negative and cannot be equal to 1. Hence, the hyperbola cannot be a vertical hyperbola.

Since the vertical hyperbola form does not provide a solution and the horizontal hyperbola form gave us a plausible solution, we will conclude that the hyperbola is horizontal with the parameters we found and solve for \( t \) using it.
