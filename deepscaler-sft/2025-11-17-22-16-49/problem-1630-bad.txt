To solve this problem, we need to determine the rotation matrix that transforms \(\begin{pmatrix} 13 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}\) to \(\begin{pmatrix} 5 \\ -12 \end{pmatrix}\). A general rotation matrix centered at the origin is given by:

\[
R = \begin{pmatrix} \cos \theta & -\sin \theta \\ \sin \theta & \cos \theta \end{pmatrix}
\]

Let's call \(\begin{pmatrix} 13 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}\) vector \( \mathbf{v_1} \) and \(\begin{pmatrix} 5 \\ -12 \end{pmatrix}\) vector \( \mathbf{v_2} \). The rotation matrix \( R \) should satisfy:

\[
R \cdot \mathbf{v_1} = \mathbf{v_2}
\]

\[
\begin{pmatrix} \cos \theta & -\sin \theta \\ \sin \theta & \cos \theta \end{pmatrix} \cdot \begin{pmatrix} 13 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 5 \\ -12 \end{pmatrix}
\]

\[
\begin{cases}
\cos \theta \cdot 13 + (-\sin \theta) \cdot 0 = 5 \\
\sin \theta \cdot 13 + \cos \theta \cdot 0 = -12
\end{cases}
\]

Which simplifies to:

\[
\begin{cases}
13 \cos \theta = 5 \\
13 \sin \theta = -12
\end{cases}
\]
