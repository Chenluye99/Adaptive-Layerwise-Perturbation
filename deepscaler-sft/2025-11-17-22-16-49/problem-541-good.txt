The coefficients of three consecutive terms in the expansion of \((x+1)^n\) are \(\binom{n}{k}\), \(\binom{n}{k+1}\), and \(\binom{n}{k+2}\), and these coefficients are in the ratio 1:2:3. Mathematically, this means that:

\[
\frac{\binom{n}{k+1}}{\binom{n}{k}} = 2 \quad \text{and} \quad \frac{\binom{n}{k+2}}{\binom{n}{k+1}} = \frac{3}{2}
\]

First, let's recall that \(\binom{n}{k} = \frac{n!}{k!(n-k)!}\). So, we have:

\[
\frac{\binom{n}{k+1}}{\binom{n}{k}} = \frac{\frac{n!}{(k+1)!(n-k-1)!}}{\frac{n!}{k!(n-k)!}} = \frac{k!(n-k)!}{(k+1)!(n-k-1)!} = \frac{n-k}{k+1} = 2
\]

This simplifies to:

\[
n - k = 2(k + 1) \implies n - k = 2k + 2 \implies n = 3k + 2 \quad \text{(1)}
\]

Similarly, for the second ratio:

\[
\frac{\binom{n}{k+2}}{\binom{n}{k+1}} = \frac{\frac{n!}{(k+2)!(n-k-2)!}}{\frac{n!}{(k+1)!(n-k-1)!}} = \frac{(k+1)!(n-k-1)!}{(k+2)!(n-k-2)!} = \frac{n-k-1}{k+2} = \frac{3}{2}
\]

This simplifies to:

\[
2(n - k - 1) = 3(k + 2) \implies 2n - 2k - 2 = 3k + 6 \implies 2n = 5k + 8 \implies n = \frac{5k + 8}{2} \quad \text{(2)}
\]
