To solve this problem, we will use the Angle Bisector Theorem and the formula for the length of the angle bisector of a triangle.

1. **Understand the Problem:**
    - We have a triangle \(ABC\) with \(AB = 3\), \(AC = 6\), and \(\cos \angle A = \frac{1}{8}\).
    - We need to find the length of the angle bisector \(AD\), where \(D\) is the point on \(BC\) such that \(AD\) bisects \(\angle A\).

2. **Use the Law of Cosines to Find \(BC\):**
    - By the Law of Cosines, we have:
      \[
      BC^2 = AB^2 + AC^2 - 2 \cdot AB \cdot AC \cdot \cos \angle A
      \]
      Substituting the given values:
      \[
      BC^2 = 3^2 + 6^2 - 2 \cdot 3 \cdot 6 \cdot \frac{1}{8} = 9 + 36 - \frac{36}{8} = 45 - \frac{9}{2} = 45 - 4.5 = 40.5 = \frac{81}{2}
      \]
      So,
      \[
      BC = \sqrt{\frac{81}{2}} = \frac{9}{\sqrt{2}} = \frac{9 \sqrt{2}}{2}
      \]

3. **Find the Length of the Angle Bisector Using the Angle Bisector Length Formula:**
    - The length of the angle bisector \(AD\) of angle \(A\) in a triangle with sides \(a = BC\), \(b = AC\), and \(c = AB\) is given by:
      \[
      AD = \frac{2 \cdot AB \cdot AC \cdot \cos \left(\frac{\angle A}{2}\right)}{AB + AC}
      \]
      To get \(\cos \left(\frac{\angle A}{2}\right)\), we use the half-angle formula:
      \[
      \cos \left(\frac{\angle A}{2}\right) = \sqrt{\frac{1 + \cos \angle A}{2}} = \sqrt{\frac{1 + \frac{1}{8}}{2}} = \sqrt{\frac{\frac{9}{8}}{2}} = \sqrt{\frac{9}{16}} = \frac{3}{4}
      \]
      Now substituting \(\cos \left(\frac{\angle A}{2}\right) = \frac{3}{4}\), \(AB = 3\), and \(AC = 6\) into the formula for the length of the angle bisector:
      \[
      AD = \frac{2 \cdot 3 \cdot 6 \cdot \frac{3}{4}}{3 + 6} = \frac{2 \cdot 3 \cdot 6 \cdot \frac{3}{4}}{9} = \frac{2 \cdot 3 \cdot 6 \cdot 3}{4 \cdot 9} = \frac{108}{36} = 3
      \]
