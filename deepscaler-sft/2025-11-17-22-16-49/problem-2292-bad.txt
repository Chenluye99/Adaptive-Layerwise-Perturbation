Given the equations:
\[ 4^a = 5, \]
\[ 5^b = 6, \]
\[ 6^c = 7, \]
\[ 7^d = 8, \]

we can take the natural logarithm (or any logarithm) of both sides of each equation to solve for \( a, b, c, \) and \( d \).

1. From \( 4^a = 5 \), taking the natural logarithm of both sides gives:
\[ a \ln 4 = \ln 5, \]
so,
\[ a = \frac{\ln 5}{\ln 4}. \]

2. From \( 5^b = 6 \), taking the natural logarithm of both sides gives:
\[ b \ln 5 = \ln 6, \]
so,
\[ b = \frac{\ln 6}{\ln 5}. \]

3. From \( 6^c = 7 \), taking the natural logarithm of both sides gives:
\[ c \ln 6 = \ln 7, \]
so,
\[ c = \frac{\ln 7}{\ln 6}. \]

4. From \( 7^d = 8 \), taking the natural logarithm of both sides gives:
\[ d \ln 7 = \ln 8, \]
so,
\[ d = \frac{\ln 8}{\ln 7}. \]

Now, we need to find the product \( a \cdot b \cdot c \cdot d \):
\[ a \cdot b \cdot c \cdot d = \frac{\ln 5}{\ln 4} \cdot \frac{\ln 6}{\ln 5} \cdot \frac{\ln 7}{\ln 6} \cdot \frac{\ln 8}{\ln 7}. \]

Observe that \(\ln 5\) in the numerator of the first fraction cancels with \(\ln 5\) in the denominator of the second fraction, \(\ln 6\) in the numerator of the second fraction cancels with \(\ln 6\) in the denominator of the third fraction, and \(\ln 7\) in the numerator of the third fraction cancels with \(\ln 7\) in the denominator of the fourth fraction. This leaves us with:

\[ \ln 8 = \ln (2^3) = 3 \ln 2, \]

Thus,
\[ \frac{\ln 8}{\ln 4} = \frac{3 \ln 2}{2 \ln 2} = \frac{3}{2}. \]
