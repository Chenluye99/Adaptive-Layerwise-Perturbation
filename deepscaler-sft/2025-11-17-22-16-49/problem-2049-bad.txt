If the hyperbola given by \[\frac{(x-3)^2}{5^2} - \frac{(y-17)^2}{12^2} = 1\] is shifted 4 units to the left and 2 units down, find the coordinates of the new foci.
To solve this problem, we need to start with the standard form of the hyperbola equation and apply the specified transformations.

The original hyperbola is given by:

\[
\frac{(x-3)^2}{5^2} - \frac{(y-17)^2}{12^2} = 1
\]

This is a hyperbola centered at \((3, 17)\) with a horizontal transverse axis. The distance from the center to each focus is given by \( c = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2} \), where \( a = 5 \) and \( b = 12 \). So,

\[
c = \sqrt{5^2 + 12^2} = \sqrt{25 + 144} = \sqrt{169} = 13
\]

This means the foci of the original hyperbola are at \((3 \pm 13, 17)\), i.e., at \((16, 17)\) and \((-10, 17)\).

Now, if we shift this hyperbola 4 units to the left and 2 units down, we need to adjust the coordinates of the center and the foci accordingly.

Shifting 4 units to the left means subtracting 4 from the x-coordinate of the center, so the new center becomes \((3 - 4, 17 - 2) = (-1, 15)\). The distances from the center to the foci (which are 13 units, as calculated earlier) don't change with the shift; only the positions of the foci change according to the new center.

So, the new coordinates of the foci will be:

1. For the focus at \((16, 17)\):
    \[
    (16 - 4, 17 - 2) = (12, 15)
    \]

2. For the focus at \((-10, 17)\):
    \[
    (-10 - 4, 17 - 2) = (-14, 15)
    \]

Thus, the new coordinates of the foci are \((12, 15)\) and \((-14, 15)\).
