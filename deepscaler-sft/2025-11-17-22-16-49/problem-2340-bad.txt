First, let's identify the first three positive composite numbers. Composite numbers are positive integers that have at least one positive divisor other than one or the number itself in other words, a composite number is any positive integer greater than 1 that is not a prime number.

The first few composite numbers are: 4, 6, 8, 9, 10, ...

So, the first three positive composite numbers are 4, 6, and 8.

Next, we need to find the product of these numbers: \(4 \times 6 \times 8\).

Finally, we will determine the units digit of this product. The units digit of a number is the rightmost digit of that number.

Let's perform the calculation step-by-step using Python to ensure accuracy.
```python
# First three positive composite numbers
composite_numbers = [4, 6, 8]

# Calculate the product of these numbers
product = 1
for num in composite_numbers:
    product *= num

# Get the units digit of the product
units_digit = product % 10
print(units_digit)
```
Code execution result: 2

Thus, the units digit of the product of the first three positive composite numbers is \(\boxed{2}\). This is our final answer.
