From the diagram and the problem statement, we have a line that passes through points \((-2, 2)\) and \((-2 + 3, 2 + 2) = (1, 4)\). We need to determine the value of \( b \) such that the direction vector of this line is of the form \(\begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ b \end{pmatrix}\).

To find the direction vector of the line passing through points \((-2, 2)\) and \((1, 4)\), we subtract the coordinates of the initial point from the coordinates of the terminal point:
\[
\text{Direction vector} = (1 - (-2), 4 - 2) = (3, 2).
\]
We know that this direction vector should be a scalar multiple of \(\begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ b \end{pmatrix}\). Let's call the scalar multiple \( k \). Then we have:
\[
(3, 2) = k \cdot \begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ b \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 2k \\ kb \end{pmatrix}.
\]
This gives us two equations:
\[
3 = 2k \quad \text{(1)}
\]
\[
2 = kb \quad \text{(2)}
\]
First, we solve equation (1) for \( k \):
\[
k = \frac{3}{2}.
\]
Now substitute \( k = \frac{3}{2} \) into equation (2):
\[
2 = \frac{3}{2} \cdot b \implies 2 = \frac{3b}{2} \implies 4 = 3b \implies b = \frac{4}{3}.
\]
Thus, the value of \( b \) is \(\frac{4}{3}\). Let's confirm this by coding a small check to ensure the result is accurate.
