First, let's recall the formulas for the lateral surface area and volume of a right circular cylinder. For a right circular cylinder, the formulas are as follows:

1. Lateral Surface Area: \( A = 2 \pi r h \)
2. Volume: \( V = \pi r^2 h \)

We are given:
- The lateral surface area is \( 24 \pi \) square inches.
- The volume is \( 24 \pi \) cubic inches.

So, we can set up the following equations based on the given information:

1. \( 2 \pi r h = 24 \pi \)
2. \( \pi r^2 h = 24 \pi \)

We can simplify both equations by dividing through by \(\pi\):

1. \( 2 r h = 24 \)
2. \( r^2 h = 24 \)

From the first equation, we can solve for \( h \):

\[ 2 r h = 24 \implies r h = 12 \implies h = \frac{12}{r} \]

Next, we substitute this expression for \( h \) into the second equation:

\[ r^2 \left( \frac{12}{r} \right) = 24 \implies 12 r = 24 \implies r = 2 \]

Now that we have \( r = 2 \), we can substitute it back into the equation \( r h = 12 \) to find \( h \):

\[ 2 h = 12 \implies h = 6 \]

So, the radius \( r \) is 2 inches. To ensure that this is accurate, we can substitute \( r = 2 \) and \( h = 6 \) back into the original formulas and verify the results.

Let's verify this using Python to ensure accuracy.
```python
import sympy as sp

# Define the variables
r, h = sp.symbols('r h')

# Define the equations based on the problem statement
eq1 = 2 * sp.pi * r * h - 24 * sp.pi
eq2 = sp.pi * r**2 * h - 24 * sp.pi

# Solve the system of equations
solution = sp.solve((eq1, eq2), (r, h))
print(solution)
```
Code execution result: [(2, 6)]

This confirms that the solution to the system of equations is \( r = 2 \) and \( h = 6 \). Thus, the radius of the right circular cylinder is \(\boxed{2}\) inches. This solution has been verified both through logical steps and by the computational check.
