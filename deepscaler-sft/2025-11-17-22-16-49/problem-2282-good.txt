To solve the inequality \(\left| \frac{2x - 1}{x - 1} \right| > 2\), we need to consider the definition of absolute value. The inequality \(\left| \frac{2x - 1}{x - 1} \right| > 2\) means that \(\frac{2x - 1}{x - 1} > 2\) or \(\frac{2x - 1}{x - 1} < -2\).

We can break this into two separate inequalities:

1. \(\frac{2x - 1}{x - 1} > 2\)
2. \(\frac{2x - 1}{x - 1} < -2\)

### Step 1: Solve \(\frac{2x - 1}{x - 1} > 2\)

First, we rewrite the inequality:
\[
\frac{2x - 1}{x - 1} > 2 \implies \frac{2x - 1}{x - 1} - 2 > 0 \implies \frac{2x - 1 - 2(x - 1)}{x - 1} > 0 \implies \frac{2x - 1 - 2x + 2}{x - 1} > 0 \implies \frac{1}{x - 1} > 0.
\]
The fraction \(\frac{1}{x - 1}\) is greater than 0 when the denominator is positive, i.e.,
\[
x - 1 > 0 \implies x > 1.
\]

### Step 2: Solve \(\frac{2x - 1}{x - 1} < -2\)

Next, we rewrite the inequality:
\[
\frac{2x - 1}{x - 1} < -2 \implies \frac{2x - 1}{x - 1} + 2 < 0 \implies \frac{2x - 1 + 2(x - 1)}{x - 1} < 0 \implies \frac{2x - 1 + 2x - 2}{x - 1} < 0 \implies \frac{4x - 3}{x - 1} < 0.
\]
We need to determine when the fraction \(\frac{4x - 3}{x - 1}\) is less than 0. This fraction changes sign at the roots of its numerator and denominator, i.e., at \(x = \frac{3}{4}\) and \(x = 1\). We analyze the sign of \(\frac{4x - 3}{x - 1}\) in the intervals determined by these roots: \((-\infty, \frac{3}{4})\), \((\frac{3}{4}, 1)\), and \((1, \infty)\).

- For \(x < \frac{3}{4}\), both \(4x - 3 < 0\) and \(x - 1 < 0\), so \(\frac{4x - 3}{x - 1} > 0\).
- For \(\frac{3}{4} < x < 1\), \(4x - 3 > 0\) and \(x - 1 < 0\), so \(\frac{4x - 3}{x - 1} < 0\).
- For \(x > 1\), both \(4x - 3 > 0\) and \(x - 1 > 0\), so \(\frac{4x - 3}{x - 1} > 0\).

Thus, \(\frac{4x - 3}{x - 1} < 0\) when \(\frac{3}{4} < x < 1\).

### Step 3: Combine Solutions

Combining both solutions, the original inequality \(\left| \frac{2x - 1}{x - 1} \right| > 2\) is satisfied when:
\[ x > 1 \quad \text{or} \quad \frac{3}{4} < x < 1. \]
