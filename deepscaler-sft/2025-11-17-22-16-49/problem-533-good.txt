To solve this problem, we need to calculate the probability that the first card drawn is a heart and the second card drawn is a diamond from a standard 52-card deck. Here are the steps to solve it:

1. **Determine the Probability for the First Card:**
    - There are 13 hearts in a deck of 52 cards.
    - The probability that the first card drawn is a heart is $\frac{13}{52} = \frac{1}{4}$.

2. **Determine the Probability for the Second Card Given that the First Card Was a Heart:**
    - After drawing one card, there are 51 cards left in the deck.
    - There are still 13 diamonds in the deck.
    - The probability that the second card drawn is a diamond, given that the first card was a heart, is $\frac{13}{51}$.

3. **Combine the Probabilities:**
    - The probability of both events happening (first card is a heart and second card is a diamond) is the product of these two probabilities:
      \[
      \text{Probability} = \frac{13}{52} \times \frac{13}{51} = \frac{1}{4} \times \frac{13}{51} = \frac{13}{204}
      \]

Let's confirm this by coding it in Python.
```python
from sympy import Rational

# Probability that the first card is a heart
prob_first_heart = Rational(13, 52)

# Probability that the second card is a diamond given that the first card was a heart
prob_second_diamond_given_first_heart = Rational(13, 51)

# Combined probability
combined_prob = prob_first_heart * prob_second_diamond_given_first_heart
print(combined_prob)
```
Code execution result: 13/204

Thus, the probability that the first card drawn is a heart and the second card drawn is a diamond is \(\boxed{\frac{13}{204}}\). This matches our earlier calculation, so we can be confident in this answer.
