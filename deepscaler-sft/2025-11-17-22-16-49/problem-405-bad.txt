To solve this problem, we need to maximize \( n \) such that \(\frac{810 \times 811 \times 812 \times \cdots \times 2010}{810^n}\) is an integer.

First, we observe that the product in the numerator is the product of all integers from 810 to 2010, and we need to determine how many factors of 810 fit into this product without leaving a non-integer quotient. 

The prime factorization of 810 is:
\[ 810 = 2 \times 3^4 \times 5 \]

Hence, we need to count how many factors of 2, 3, and 5 are in the product of integers from 810 to 2010, and then determine the limiting factor for \( n \).

1. **Count the number of factors of 2 in the product from 810 to 2010.**
2. **Count the number of factors of 3 in the product from 810 to 2010.**
3. **Count the number of factors of 5 in the product from 810 to 2010.**

The value of \( n \) will be limited by the prime factor that has the fewest factors in the product from 810 to 2010 when considering the prime factors of 810 (i.e., 2, 3, and 5).

Let's use Python to handle these calculations accurately.
```python
import math

# Initialize counts for factors of 2, 3, and 5 in the product from 810 to 2010
count_of_2 = 0
count_of_3 = 0
count_of_5 = 0

# Count factors of 2, 3, and 5 from 810 to 2010
for num in range(810, 2011):
    temp_num = num
    # Count factors of 2
    while temp_num % 2 == 0:
        count_of_2 += 1
        temp_num //= 2
    
    # Count factors of 3
    while temp_num % 3 == 0:
        count_of_3 += 1
        temp_num //= 3
    
    # Count factors of 5
    while temp_num % 5 == 0:
        count_of_5 += 1
        temp_num //= 5

# Get the minimum number of factors of 2, 3, and 5 necessary based on the prime factorization of 810 (2 * 3^4 * 5)
# We need to consider that each 810 factors as 2^1 * 3^4 * 5^1 so we need at least as many factors of 2, 3, and 5 as are needed to cover the factorization of 810

min_exponent = min(count_of_2, 4 * count_of_3, count_of_5)

print(min_exponent)
```
