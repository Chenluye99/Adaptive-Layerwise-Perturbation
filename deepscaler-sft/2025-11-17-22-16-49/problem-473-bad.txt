To solve the problem, let's start by understanding the geometric configuration.

1. **Problem Understanding:**
    - A \(1 \times 3\) rectangle is inscribed in a semicircle with its longer side (which is 3) on the diameter of the semicircle.
    - Let the radius of the semicircle be \(r\). Then, the diameter of the semicircle is \(2r\).
    - Since the longer side of the rectangle (which is 3) is on the diameter, we have \(2r = 3\), so \(r = \frac{3}{2}\).

2. **Formulating the Problem:**
    - The shorter side of the rectangle (which is 1) will be the vertical distance from the center of the semicircle to the top of the rectangle. This vertical distance is the y-coordinate of the topmost point of the rectangle, which is at a height of 1 from the center of the semicircle (since the center of the semicircle is at the midpoint of the diameter, and the height of the rectangle from the center is half of 1, i.e., 1).
    - This vertical distance (1) forms a right triangle with the radius of the semicircle (which is \(r = \frac{3}{2}\)) and half the length of the rectangle's shorter side as legs. The hypotenuse of this right triangle is the radius of the semicircle. Using the Pythagorean theorem, we have:
      \[
      \left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^2 = 1^2 + x^2
      \]
      where \(x\) is half the length of the shorter side of the rectangle, but since it is not contributing to the y-coordinate directly (it will be the same point's height as that of the shorter side's placement), it simply confirms that the placement fits perfectly for the height consideration which is already given as 1.

3. **Area of the Semicircle:**
    - The area of a full circle with radius \(r = \frac{3}{2}\) is \(\pi \left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^2 = \pi \cdot \frac{9}{4} = \frac{9\pi}{4}\).
    - Since we have a semicircle, its area is half of that of the full circle:
      \[
      \text{Area of the semicircle} = \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{9\pi}{4} = \frac{9\pi}{8}
      \]
