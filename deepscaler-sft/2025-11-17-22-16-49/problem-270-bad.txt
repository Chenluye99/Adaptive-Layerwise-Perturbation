To solve this problem, we need to consider all possible ways of dividing 5 students into 3 groups such that the constraints are satisfied. Let's label the groups as A, B, and C and denote the sizes of these groups as \(|A|, |B|, |C|\), respectively.

1. **Constraints**:
    - Group A must have at least 2 people.
    - Groups B and C must have at least 1 person each.
    - The total number of students is 5, so we must have \(|A| + |B| + |C| = 5\).

2. **Enumerate All Possible Group Sizes**:
    - For Group A, possible sizes are 2, 3, or 4 (since it needs to have at least 2 people, and at most 4 people because the other two groups need at least one person each).
    - For each possible size of Group A, we need to determine the sizes of Groups B and C such that their sizes are at least 1 and they fill up the remaining students after Group A is chosen.

3. **Iteratively Count Valid Distributions**:
    - For each valid combination of sizes for groups A, B, and C, we need to consider that different ways of choosing the students for these groups will lead to different distributions. This involves using combinatorial methods to generate all possible ways to assign the 5 students to these groups and then count how many of these assignments satisfy the size constraints.

Let's use Python to implement this step-by-step and get an accurate count of the valid distributions. We'll iterate over all possible ways to assign 5 students into 3 groups and then check if they meet the conditions (i.e., Group A has at least 2 people, and Groups B and C have at least 1 person each).
```python
import itertools

students = list(range(5))
count = 0

# We need to check all possible ways to split 5 students into 3 non-empty groups
for i in range(1, 5):
    for j in range(1, 5 - i + 1):
        k = 5 - i - j
        if k > 0:
            # Generate all ways to assign the students to the groups based on sizes i, j, and k
            for comb_a in itertools.combinations(students, i):
                remaining_after_a = [s for s in students if s not in comb_a]
                
                for comb_b in itertools.combinations(remaining_after_a, j):
                    comb_c = [s for s in remaining_after_a if s not in comb_b]
                    
                    if len(comb_c) == k:
                        # We have a valid (A, B, C) group assignment of sizes (i, j, k)
                        count += 1
                        
print(count)
```
