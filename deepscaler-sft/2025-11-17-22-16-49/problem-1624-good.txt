To solve the given problem, we need to find the value of \( n \) that satisfies the equation \( 2(n+1)! + 6n! = 3(n+1)! \).

First, let's simplify the equation algebraically. We start by moving all terms involving \((n+1)!\) to one side of the equation:

\[ 2(n+1)! + 6n! = 3(n+1)! \]

Subtract \( 2(n+1)! \) from both sides:

\[ 6n! = (3(n+1)! - 2(n+1)!) \]

This simplifies to:

\[ 6n! = (3 - 2)(n+1)! \]

So,

\[ 6n! = (n+1)! \]

We know that \((n+1)! = (n+1) \cdot n!\), so we can substitute this in:

\[ 6n! = (n+1) \cdot n! \]

By dividing both sides of the equation by \( n! \) (assuming \( n! \neq 0 \), i.e., \( n \geq 0 \)):

\[ 6 = n + 1 \]

Then, solve for \( n \):

\[ n = 6 - 1 \]
\[ n = 5 \]
