Let's denote the side lengths of the rectangle by \(a\) and \(b\). Then:

- The area of the rectangle is \(a \cdot b\).
- The perimeter of the rectangle is \(2(a + b)\).

According to the problem, the area is equal to twice the perimeter, so we can write:
\[ a \cdot b = 2 \cdot 2(a + b) \implies a \cdot b = 4(a + b) \]

Rearrange this equation:
\[ a \cdot b = 4a + 4b \]
\[ a \cdot b - 4a - 4b = 0 \]

To make it factorable, we can add 16 to both sides:
\[ a \cdot b - 4a - 4b + 16 = 16 \]
\[ (a - 4)(b - 4) = 16 \]

(1, 16), (-1, -16), (2, 8), (-2, -8), (4, 4), (-4, -4), (8, 2), (-8, -2), (16, 1), (-16, -1)
\]

\[ a - 4 = x \implies a = x + 4 \]
\[ b - 4 = y \implies b = y + 4 \]

We only consider the pairs that give positive integer side lengths \(a\) and \(b\):
