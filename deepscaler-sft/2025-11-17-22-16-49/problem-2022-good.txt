First, we need to determine the center and radius of the circle given by the equation \(x^2 + y^2 = 6x - 8y + 24\).

To do this, we will rewrite the equation in standard form \((x - h)^2 + (y - k)^2 = r^2\), where \((h, k)\) is the center of the circle and \(r\) is the radius. 

1. Start with the given equation:
\[ x^2 + y^2 = 6x - 8y + 24 \]

2. Move all terms to one side of the equation:
\[ x^2 - 6x + y^2 + 8y = 24 \]

3. Complete the square for both \(x\) and \(y\):

   - For the \(x\) terms: \(x^2 - 6x\), take the coefficient of \(x\), divide by 2, and square it. The coefficient of \(x\) is \(-6\), so \(\left(\frac{-6}{2}\right)^2 = 9\). Add and subtract 9:
     \[ x^2 - 6x + 9 - 9 \]
     This becomes:
     \[ (x - 3)^2 - 9 \]

   - For the \(y\) terms: \(y^2 + 8y\), take the coefficient of \(y\), divide by 2, and square it. The coefficient of \(y\) is 8, so \(\left(\frac{8}{2}\right)^2 = 16\). Add and subtract 16:
     \[ y^2 + 8y + 16 - 16 \]
     This becomes:
     \[ (y + 4)^2 - 16 \]

4. Substitute these back into the equation:
\[ (x - 3)^2 - 9 + (y + 4)^2 - 16 = 24 \]

5. Simplify:
\[ (x - 3)^2 + (y + 4)^2 - 25 = 24 \]
\[ (x - 3)^2 + (y + 4)^2 = 49 \]

Now, the equation is in standard form, \((x - 3)^2 + (y + 4)^2 = 49\), where the center of the circle is \((3, -4)\) and the radius is \(\sqrt{49} = 7\).

Next, we need to find the distance between the center of the circle \((3, -4)\) and the point \((-3, -12)\). We use the distance formula:

\[
d = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2}
\]

Substitute \((x_1, y_1) = (3, -4)\) and \((x_2, y_2) = (-3, -12)\):

\[
d = \sqrt{((-3) - 3)^2 + ((-12) - (-4))^2} = \sqrt{(-6)^2 + (-8)^2} = \sqrt{36 + 64} = \sqrt{100} = 10
\]
