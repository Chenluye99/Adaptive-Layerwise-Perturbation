To solve this problem, we need to follow these steps:

1. Find the greatest common factor (GCF) of 3 and 6.
2. Find the least common multiple (LCM) of 3 and 6.
3. Sum the GCF and the LCM obtained in the previous steps.

### Step-by-Step Solution:

1. **Finding the GCF of 3 and 6:**
   - The factors of 3 are: 1, 3.
   - The factors of 6 are: 1, 2, 3, 6.
   - The greatest common factor of 3 and 6 is 3.

2. **Finding the LCM of 3 and 6:**
   - The multiples of 3 are: 3, 6, 9, 12, ...
   - The multiples of 6 are: 6, 12, 18, ...
   - The least common multiple of 3 and 6 is 6, because 6 is the smallest number that is a multiple of both 3 and 6.

3. **Summing the GCF and LCM:**
   - GCF of 3 and 6 is 3.
   - LCM of 3 and 6 is 6.
   - Sum of GCF and LCM is \(3 + 6 = 9\).

Let's confirm this by using Python to ensure accuracy.
```python
import math

# Step 1: Find the GCF (greatest common factor) of 3 and 6
gcf = math.gcd(3, 6)

# Step 2: Find the LCM (least common multiple) of 3 and 6
lcm = (3 * 6) // gcf  # LCM of two numbers a and b is given by (a * b) // GCD(a, b)

# Step 3: Sum the GCF and LCM
result = gcf + lcm
print(result)
```
Code execution result: 9

Thus, the sum of the greatest common factor of 3 and 6 and the least common multiple of 3 and 6 is \(\boxed{9}\). This confirms our reasoning and calculations are accurate.
