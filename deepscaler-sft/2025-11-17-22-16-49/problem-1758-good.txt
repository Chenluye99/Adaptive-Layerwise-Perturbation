First, let's define the variables that will help us solve the problem:

- Let \( R \) be the set of red cars.
- Let \( W \) be the set of white cars.
- Let \( D_2 \) be the set of 2-door cars.
- Let \( D_4 \) be the set of 4-door cars.

We are given the following information:

1. The total number of cars is 20.
2. 12 of the cars are red.
3. 15 of the cars are 4-door.
4. 4 of the cars are 2-door and white.

We want to find the number of cars that are both 4-door and red.

### Step-by-Step Solution

1. **Set Up Equations:**
    - Let the total number of red cars be \( |R| = 12 \).
    - Let the total number of 4-door cars be \( |D_4| = 15 \).
    - Let the number of 2-door and white cars be \( |D_2 \cap W| = 4 \).
    - The total number of cars is 20, so:
      \[
      |R \cup W| = 20
      \]
    - Since every car is either red or white, we have:
      \[
      |R \cup W| = |R| + |W| - |R \cap W|
      \]
      But we know all cars are either red or white, so \( |R \cap W| = 0 \), meaning there's no car that is both red and white at the same time in this interpretation (which isn't typical but let's solve it considering the constraints).

2. **Deduce Other Values:**
    - The total number of 2-door cars can be found as \( |D_2| = 20 - |D_4| = 20 - 15 = 5 \).
    - The number of white cars can be determined using the information about 2-door and white cars. Let \( |W| \) be the total number of white cars, and let \( |R \cap D_2| \) be the number of 2-door and red cars, and let \( |R \cap D_4| \) be the number of 4-door and red cars, and let \( |W \cap D_2| \) be the number of 2-door and white cars, and let \( |W \cap D_4| \) be the number of 4-door and white cars.

    - We know that the total number of 2-door cars is 5, and 4 of those are white, so the number of 2-door and red cars is:
      \[
      |R \cap D_2| = \text{Total 2-door cars} - \text{2-door and white cars} = 5 - 4 = 1
      \]

    - The number of white cars can be calculated as:
      \[
      |W| = |W \cap D_2| + |W \cap D_4|
      \]
      Since 4 cars are 2-door and white, and the rest of the white cars are 4-door, let's call the number of 4-door and white cars \( |W \cap D_4| \). Therefore:
      \[
      |W| = 4 + |W \cap D_4|
      \]

    - The number of red cars can be calculated as:
      \[
      |R| = |R \cap D_2| + |R \cap D_4|
      \]
      Substituting \( |R \cap D_2| = 1 \):
      \[
      12 = 1 + |R \cap D_4|
      \]
      Therefore:
      \[
      |R \cap D_4| = 11
      \]

    - To get the consistency, the remaining cars that we didn't account for explicitly can be verified, but it might be a little complex without direct equations considering all intersections. Let's implement this using Python for an accurate check.
```python
from sympy import symbols, Eq, solve

# Define variables to represent the unknowns
R_D4, W_D2, W_D4, R_D2 = symbols('R_D4 W_D2 W_D4 R_D2')

# We have these constraints:
# 1. The total number of cars is 20.
eq1 = Eq(R_D2 + R_D4 + W_D2 + W_D4, 20)

# 2. There are 12 red cars in total, so:
eq2 = Eq(R_D2 + R_D4, 12)

# 3. There are 15 four-door cars in total, so:
eq3 = Eq(R_D4 + W_D4, 15)

# 4. There are 4 two-door white cars, so:
eq4 = Eq(W_D2, 4)

# We are solving for R_D4, i.e., the number of 4-door and red cars.
solution = solve((eq1, eq2, eq3, eq4), (R_D4, W_D2, W_D4, R_D2))

print(solution)
```
Code execution result: {R_D2: 1, R_D4: 11, W_D2: 4, W_D4: 4}

Hence, the number of cars that are 4-door and red is \( \boxed{11} \). This is our final answer.
