To solve this problem, we need to find a three-digit positive integer \( x \) such that:

\[ 100x \equiv 1 \pmod{997}. \]

This means that we are looking for the modular inverse of 100 modulo 997. The modular inverse of \( a \) modulo \( m \) is an integer \( x \) such that:

\[ ax \equiv 1 \pmod{m}. \]

In our case, \( a = 100 \) and \( m = 997 \). We can find this inverse using the Extended Euclidean Algorithm, which finds solutions to equations of the form:

\[ a \cdot x + m \cdot y = \gcd(a, m). \]

For our problem, since we are looking for the inverse, we need \(\gcd(100, 997) = 1\), and the equation we need to solve is:

\[ 100 \cdot x + 997 \cdot y = 1. \]

Once we get \( x \), we need to ensure that it is a three-digit number, i.e., \( 100 \leq x \leq 999 \). Let's implement this step-by-step in Python.
```python
import sympy as sp
```
