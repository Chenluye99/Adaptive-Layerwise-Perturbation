We need to find the coefficient of \(a^4b^2\) in the expansion of \(\left(2a - \frac{b}{3}\right)^6\). To solve this problem, we can use the binomial theorem. The binomial theorem states that:

\[
(x + y)^n = \sum_{k=0}^{n} \binom{n}{k} x^{n-k} y^k
\]

For the expression \(\left(2a - \frac{b}{3}\right)^6\), we can identify \(x = 2a\) and \(y = -\frac{b}{3}\), with \(n = 6\). We need the term that contains \(a^4b^2\).

In the expansion, the general term is given by:

\[
\binom{6}{k} (2a)^{6-k} \left(-\frac{b}{3}\right)^k
\]

We want the term where the power of \(a\) is 4 and the power of \(b\) is 2. This means we need:

\[
(2a)^{6-k} \text{ to give } a^4 \quad \text{and} \quad \left(-\frac{b}{3}\right)^k \text{ to give } b^2
\]

This implies that:

1. The exponent of \(a\) must be 4: \(6 - k = 4 \implies k = 2\).
2. The exponent of \(b\) must be 2: \(k = 2\).

Substitute \(k = 2\) into the general term:

\[
\binom{6}{2} (2a)^{6-2} \left(-\frac{b}{3}\right)^2 = \binom{6}{2} (2a)^4 \left(-\frac{b}{3}\right)^2
\]

Now we can compute this step-by-step:

1. Calculate \(\binom{6}{2}\):
\[
\binom{6}{2} = \frac{6!}{2!(6-2)!} = \frac{6!}{2!4!} = \frac{6 \cdot 5}{2 \cdot 1} = 15
\]

2. Calculate \((2a)^4\):
\[
(2a)^4 = 2^4 \cdot a^4 = 16a^4
\]

3. Calculate \(\left(-\frac{b}{3}\right)^2\):
\[
\left(-\frac{b}{3}\right)^2 = \left(\frac{-b}{3}\right)^2 = \frac{b^2}{9}
\]

Putting it all together, we get:

\[
\binom{6}{2} \cdot (2a)^4 \cdot \left(-\frac{b}{3}\right)^2 = 15 \cdot 16a^4 \cdot \frac{b^2}{9} = 15 \cdot 16 \cdot \frac{1}{9} \cdot a^4 b^2 = \frac{15 \cdot 16}{9} a^4 b^2 = \frac{240}{9} a^4 b^2 = \frac{80}{3} a^4 b^2
\]
