To solve this problem, let's start by understanding the projection formula.

The projection of a vector \(\mathbf{v}\) onto a vector \(\mathbf{w}\) is given by:
\[
\text{proj}_{\mathbf{w}} \mathbf{v} = \frac{\mathbf{v} \cdot \mathbf{w}}{\mathbf{w} \cdot \mathbf{w}} \mathbf{w}
\]

However, we are only interested in finding the scalar projection (i.e., the scalar factor that scales \(\mathbf{w}\)), because we already know the direction of \(\mathbf{w}\) from the given projection.

The scalar projection of \(\mathbf{v}\) onto \(\mathbf{w}\) is:
\[
\frac{\mathbf{v} \cdot \mathbf{w}}{\|\mathbf{w}\|}
\]

Given that the projection of \(\begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix}\) onto \(\mathbf{w}\) is \(\begin{pmatrix} -9/10 \\ 3/10 \end{pmatrix}\), let's denote \(\mathbf{w} = \begin{pmatrix} w_1 \\ w_2 \end{pmatrix}\).

We know:
\[
\text{proj}_{\mathbf{w}} \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix} = \frac{\begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix} \cdot \mathbf{w}}{\mathbf{w} \cdot \mathbf{w}} \mathbf{w} = \begin{pmatrix} -9/10 \\ 3/10 \end{pmatrix}
\]

This means that the vector \(\frac{\begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix} \cdot \mathbf{w}}{\mathbf{w} \cdot \mathbf{w}} \mathbf{w} = \begin{pmatrix} -9/10 \\ 3/10 \end{pmatrix}\).

Let's denote the scalar projection (without the vector \(\mathbf{w}\)) as \(k\):
\[
k \mathbf{w} = \begin{pmatrix} -9/10 \\ 3/10 \end{pmatrix}
\]
So,
\[
k \begin{pmatrix} w_1 \\ w_2 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} -9/10 \\ 3/10 \end{pmatrix}
\]
This implies:
\[
k w_1 = -9/10 \quad \text{and} \quad k w_2 = 3/10
\]

From these, we get:
\[
k = \frac{-9/10}{w_1} = \frac{3/10}{w_2}
\]
So,
\[
\frac{-9/10}{w_1} = \frac{3/10}{w_2} \implies -9 w_2 = 3 w_1 \implies w_1 = -3 w_2
\]

This gives us the direction of \(\mathbf{w}\). We don't actually need the entire \(\mathbf{w}\) to solve the problem, just its direction, because the projection of another vector onto \(\mathbf{w}\) will scale the same way.
