We can use the identity for the square of a sum:

\[
(a + b + c)^2 = a^2 + b^2 + c^2 + 2(ab + bc + ca)
\]

Substituting the given values into this identity:

\[
(a + b + c)^2 = 13 + 2 \cdot 6 = 13 + 12 = 25
\]

Taking the square root of both sides, we get:

\[
a + b + c = \sqrt{25} \quad \text{or} \quad a + b + c = -\sqrt{25}
\]

Since \(a, b,\) and \(c\) are nonnegative real numbers, their sum \(a + b + c\) must also be nonnegative. Therefore, we have:

\[
a + b + c = 5
\]
