To solve the problem, we need to determine how the curves \( y = 2 - x^2 \) and \( y = x^2 - 1 \) interact with the x-axis and with each other, and then count the number of distinct regions created by these curves.

### Step-by-Step Solution:

1. **Find the Intersection Points of the Curves:**
   To find the intersection points of the two parabolas \( y = 2 - x^2 \) and \( y = x^2 - 1 \), we set the equations equal to each other:
   \[
   2 - x^2 = x^2 - 1
   \]
   Rearrange to get:
   \[
   2 + 1 = x^2 + x^2 \implies 3 = 2x^2 \implies x^2 = \frac{3}{2} \implies x = \pm \sqrt{\frac{3}{2}} = \pm \frac{\sqrt{6}}{2}
   \]
   Substitute \( x = \pm \frac{\sqrt{6}}{2} \) into either equation to find the corresponding \( y \)-values:
   \[
   y = \left( \frac{\sqrt{6}}{2} \right)^2 - 1 = \frac{6}{4} - 1 = \frac{3}{2} - 1 = \frac{1}{2}
   \]
   So, the intersection points are \(\left( \frac{\sqrt{6}}{2}, \frac{1}{2} \right)\) and \(\left( -\frac{\sqrt{6}}{2}, \frac{1}{2} \right)\).

2. **Find the Intersection Points of Each Curve with the x-axis:**
   - For \( y = 2 - x^2 = 0 \):
     \[
     2 - x^2 = 0 \implies x^2 = 2 \implies x = \pm \sqrt{2}
     \]
     So, the curve \( y = 2 - x^2 \) intersects the x-axis at \((\sqrt{2}, 0)\) and \((- \sqrt{2}, 0)\).

   - For \( y = x^2 - 1 = 0 \):
     \[
     x^2 - 1 = 0 \implies x^2 = 1 \implies x = \pm 1
     \]
     So, the curve \( y = x^2 - 1 \) intersects the x-axis at \((1, 0)\) and \((-1, 0)\).

3. **Visualize and Count the Regions:**
   - The x-axis, \( y = 2 - x^2 \), and \( y = x^2 - 1 \) create different regions in the plane. We can use Python to help visualize and confirm the number of distinct regions.

Let's implement this in Python to ensure the accuracy and get the final answer.
```python
import sympy as sp

# Define the variables and functions
x, y = sp.symbols('x y')
curve1 = 2 - x**2
curve2 = x**2 - 1

# Find the intersection points of the two curves
intersection_points = sp.solve([curve1 - curve2], (x, y))
print(intersection_points)
```
Code execution result: [(-sqrt(6)/2, y), (sqrt(6)/2, y)]

From this, we know that the points of intersection in the y-direction for different x-values are \(\frac{1}{2}\) as determined earlier when we equated the two functions. So, substituting back, the intersection points are \(\left( \frac{\sqrt{6}}{2}, \frac{1}{2} \right)\) and \(\left( -\frac{\sqrt{6}}{2}, \frac{1}{2} \right)\).
