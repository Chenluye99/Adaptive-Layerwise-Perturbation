To solve the problem, let's start by finding two solutions to the equation $\frac{2}{x} + \frac{3}{y} = 0$.

1. **Formulate the Equation:**
    \[
    \frac{2}{x} + \frac{3}{y} = 0
    \]
    Rearrange the equation to solve for $y$ in terms of $x$:
    \[
    \frac{2}{x} = - \frac{3}{y}
    \]
    \[
    2y = -3x
    \]
    \[
    y = - \frac{3}{2} x
    \]

2. **Identify Two Solutions:**
    We can choose any two values for $x$ and find the corresponding $y$ values using the equation $y = - \frac{3}{2} x$.

    - Let's choose \( x_1 = 2 \):
      \[
      y_1 = - \frac{3}{2} \cdot 2 = -3
      \]
      So, one solution is \((x_1, y_1) = (2, -3)\).

    - Let's choose \( x_2 = 4 \):
      \[
      y_2 = - \frac{3}{2} \cdot 4 = -6
      \]
      So, another solution is \((x_2, y_2) = (4, -6)\).

3. **Find the Slope:**
    The slope \( m \) of the line passing through the points \((x_1, y_1)\) and \((x_2, y_2)\) is given by:
    \[
    m = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}
    \]
    Substituting our points \((2, -3)\) and \((4, -6)\):
    \[
    m = \frac{-6 - (-3)}{4 - 2} = \frac{-6 + 3}{2} = \frac{-3}{2} = - \frac{3}{2}
    \]

We can confirm this by coding it to ensure the accuracy of the result.
