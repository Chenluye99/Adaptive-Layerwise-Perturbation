The domain of \( b(x) \) is all real numbers if and only if the denominator is never zero for any real \( x \). In other words, the quadratic equation \(-5x^2 + 2x + k = 0\) should have no real roots.

A quadratic equation \( ax^2 + bx + c = 0 \) has no real roots if its discriminant is less than zero. The discriminant \(\Delta\) of the quadratic equation \(-5x^2 + 2x + k = 0\) is given by:

\[
\Delta = b^2 - 4ac
\]

For our quadratic equation \(-5x^2 + 2x + k = 0\), we have \( a = -5 \), \( b = 2 \), and \( c = k \). Substituting these values into the formula for the discriminant, we get:

\[
\Delta = 2^2 - 4(-5)k = 4 + 20k
\]

For the quadratic equation \(-5x^2 + 2x + k = 0\) to have no real roots, we need:

\[
4 + 20k < 0
\]

Solving this inequality for \( k \):

\[
20k < -4 \implies k < -\frac{4}{20} \implies k < -\frac{1}{5}
\]

Thus, the values of \( k \) that ensure the domain of \( b(x) \) is all real numbers are \( k \in (-\infty, -\frac{1}{5}) \).
