We are given the equation

\[ 1 + 2x + 3x^2 + 4x^3 + \dotsb = 9. \]

This is an infinite series where the \(n\)-th term is \(n \cdot x^{n-1}\). This is known as the derivative of the geometric series sum formula.

The sum of the infinite series \( 1 + 2x + 3x^2 + 4x^3 + \dotsb \) can be found by differentiating the sum of the geometric series \( \frac{1}{1 - x} \) with respect to \( x \).

First, recall that the sum of the infinite geometric series is given by

\[ \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} x^n = \frac{1}{1 - x}, \quad \text{for } |x| < 1. \]

If we differentiate both sides of this equation with respect to \( x \), we get

\[ \frac{d}{dx} \left( \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} x^n \right) = \frac{d}{dx} \left( \frac{1}{1 - x} \right). \]

The left side becomes

\[ \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} n x^{n-1}, \]

and the right side becomes

\[ \frac{d}{dx} \left( \frac{1}{1 - x} \right) = \frac{1}{(1 - x)^2}. \]

So, we have

\[ \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} n x^{n-1} = \frac{1}{(1 - x)^2}. \]

To match the form of our original series \( 1 + 2x + 3x^2 + 4x^3 + \dotsb \), we can substitute \( x \) with \( x \) instead of \( x^{n-1} \), which means we need to multiply both sides by \( x \):

\[ \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} n x^n = x \cdot \frac{1}{(1 - x)^2} = \frac{x}{(1 - x)^2}. \]

We are given that this sum is equal to 9:

\[ \frac{x}{(1 - x)^2} = 9. \]
