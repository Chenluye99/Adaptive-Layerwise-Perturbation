To solve the problem, we need to determine how many numbers from 1 to 150 are perfect powers and then find out how many are not. The probability of selecting a number that is not a perfect power can then be calculated.

### Step-by-step Plan:

1. **Identify Perfect Powers from 1 to 150:**
   - A perfect power is a number that can be expressed as \( x^y \) where \( x \) is an integer and \( y \) is an integer greater than 1.
   - We will iterate through all numbers from 1 to 150 and check if they can be written in the form \( x^y \) for some integers \( x \) and \( y \) with \( y > 1 \).

2. **Check for Perfect Powers:**
   - For each number from 1 to 150, we check if it can be written as \( x^y \) for any integer \( x \) and integer \( y > 1 \).
   - We need to ensure that we check a reasonable range of \( x \) and \( y \) values to cover all possibilities up to 150.

3. **Count the Numbers that are Perfect Powers:**
   - We'll create a list of numbers from 1 to 150 that are perfect powers.

4. **Count the Numbers that are Not Perfect Powers:**
   - Subtract the count of perfect powers from 150 to get the count of numbers that are not perfect powers.

5. **Compute the Probability:**
   - The probability that a randomly selected number from 1 to 150 is not a perfect power is given by the ratio of the count of numbers that are not perfect powers to the total count of numbers (which is 150).

6. **Express the Answer as a Fraction:**
   - We need to express this probability as a common fraction and ensure it is in its simplest form.

Let's implement this step-by-step process in Python:
```python
import math
from fractions import Fraction

# Step 1: Identify perfect powers from 1 to 150
perfect_powers = []

for num in range(1, 151):
    for x in range(1, int(math.sqrt(150)) + 1):
        for y in range(2, 15):
            if x ** y == num:
                perfect_powers.append(num)
                break

# Step 2: Remove duplicates and get the unique perfect powers
perfect_powers = list(set(perfect_powers))

# Step 3: Count the numbers that are not perfect powers
count_not_perfect_powers = 150 - len(perfect_powers)

# Step 4: Compute the probability
probability = Fraction(count_not_perfect_powers, 150)

print(probability)
```
Code execution result: 133/150

Thus, the probability that a randomly selected number from 1 to 150 is not a perfect power is \(\boxed{\frac{133}{150}}\). This is the final answer.
