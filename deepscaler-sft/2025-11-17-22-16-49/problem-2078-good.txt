To solve this problem, let's start with some properties and geometric insights about the triangle and its medians:

1. **Properties of Medians in a Triangle:**
    - Medians of a triangle intersect at the centroid, which divides each median into a ratio of 2:1. That means, if $DP = 18$, then the segment from $D$ to the centroid (let's call it $G$) is \(\frac{2}{3} \times 18 = 12\) and the segment from the centroid $G$ to $P$ is \(\frac{1}{3} \times 18 = 6\).
    - Similarly, if $EQ = 24$, then the segment from $E$ to the centroid $G$ is \(\frac{2}{3} \times 24 = 16\) and the segment from the centroid $G$ to $Q$ is \(\frac{1}{3} \times 24 = 8\).

2. **Applying the Perpendicular Condition:**
    - Since the medians \( \overline{DP} \) and \( \overline{EQ} \) are perpendicular, if we place the triangle in a coordinate system for simplicity, we can place the centroid \(G\) at the origin \((0, 0)\), and place the points \(D\) and \(E\) such that the coordinates of \(D\) and \(E\) along with the right angle condition lead to an easy computation.
    - Place \(D\) at \((12, 0)\) and \(E\) at \((0, 16)\). This way, both segments \(DP\) and \(EQ\) will reach to points that maintain our conditions above, and they will be perpendicular because we're using the right coordinates.

3. **Find Coordinates of \(F\):**
    - To find the coordinates of \(F\), let's place it such that \(P\) and \(Q\) are midpoints of \(EF\) and \(DF\) respectively, and then calculate \(F\).

4. **Formulate and Compute \(DF\):**
    - The midpoint \(P\) of \(EF\) will be \(\left( \frac{E_x + F_x}{2}, \frac{E_y + F_y}{2} \right) = \left( \frac{0 + F_x}{2}, \frac{16 + F_y}{2} \right) = \left( \frac{F_x}{2}, \frac{16 + F_y}{2} \right)\).
    - Similarly, the midpoint \(Q\) of \(DF\) will be \(\left( \frac{D_x + F_x}{2}, \frac{D_y + F_y}{2} \right) = \left( \frac{12 + F_x}{2}, \frac{0 + F_y}{2} \right) = \left( \frac{12 + F_x}{2}, \frac{F_y}{2} \right)\).
    - Given that \(DP = 18\) and \(EQ = 24\), and remembering that these lengths will stem from Pythagorean distances from the midpoints to vertices \(D\) and \(E\) respectively factoring in the distances and using Pythagoras in context, we form systems that solve for \(F_x, F_y\).

Let's now implement this step-by-step calculation and solve for \(DF\) using Python for exact results.
```python
import sympy as sp

# Define variables
Fx, Fy = sp.symbols('Fx Fy')

# Coordinates of D and E based on the earlier placement
D = (12, 0)
E = (0, 16)

# Midpoints P and Q are:
P = ((E[0] + Fx) / 2, (E[1] + Fy) / 2)
Q = ((D[0] + Fx) / 2, (D[1] + Fy) / 2)

# We know the distances |DP| = 18 and |EQ| = 24
dist_DP = sp.sqrt((P[0] - D[0])**2 + (P[1] - D[1])**2) - 18
dist_EQ = sp.sqrt((Q[0] - E[0])**2 + (Q[1] - E[1])**2) - 24

# Solve the system of equations
solution = sp.solve([dist_DP, dist_EQ], (Fx, Fy))
```
