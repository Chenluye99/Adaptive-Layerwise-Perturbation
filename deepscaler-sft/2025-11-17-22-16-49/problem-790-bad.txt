To solve this problem, we need to determine the number of ways to choose 2 people from a committee of 6, where the order of selection does not matter. This is a combination problem, and the number of ways to choose 2 people from 6 is given by the binomial coefficient "6 choose 2," which is denoted as \(\binom{6}{2}\).

The formula for the binomial coefficient \(\binom{n}{k}\) is:

\[
\binom{n}{k} = \frac{n!}{k!(n-k)!}
\]

For our problem, \( n = 6 \) and \( k = 2 \), so we need to compute:

\[
\binom{6}{2} = \frac{6!}{2!(6-2)!} = \frac{6!}{2! \cdot 4!} = \frac{6 \times 5}{2 \times 1} = 15
\]

Let's confirm this by implementing it in Python.
```python
import math

# Calculate the binomial coefficient (6 choose 2)
n = 6
k = 2
num_combinations = math.comb(n, k)
print(num_combinations)
```
Code execution result: 15

Thus, the number of different two-person sub-committees that can be selected from a committee of six people is \(\boxed{15}\). This confirms our reasoning and calculation were accurate.
