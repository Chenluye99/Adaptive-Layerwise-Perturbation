First, let's start by setting up the equation and solving it step by step.

The given equation is:

\[
\sqrt{Q^3} = 16 \sqrt[8]{16}
\]

### Step-by-Step Solution:

1. **Simplify the Right Side of the Equation:**

    - First, express \( \sqrt[8]{16} \) in a more manageable form.
    - We know that \( 16 = 2^4 \), so:
      \[
      \sqrt[8]{16} = \sqrt[8]{2^4} = 2^{4/8} = 2^{1/2} = \sqrt{2}
      \]
    - Substitute this back into the original equation:
      \[
      \sqrt{Q^3} = 16 \cdot \sqrt{2}
      \]

2. **Simplify Further:**

    - We know that \( 16 = 2^4 \), so:
      \[
      16 \cdot \sqrt{2} = 2^4 \cdot \sqrt{2} = 2^4 \cdot 2^{1/2} = 2^{4 + 1/2} = 2^{9/2}
      \]
    - So, our equation now is:
      \[
      \sqrt{Q^3} = 2^{9/2}
      \]

3. **Simplify the Left Side of the Equation:**

    - We know that \( \sqrt{Q^3} = (Q^3)^{1/2} = Q^{3/2} \), so we have:
      \[
      Q^{3/2} = 2^{9/2}
      \]

4. **Solve for \( Q \):**

    - To solve for \( Q \), we need to raise both sides of the equation to the power of \( \frac{2}{3} \) (the reciprocal of \( \frac{3}{2} \)):
      \[
      \left( Q^{3/2} \right)^{2/3} = \left( 2^{9/2} \right)^{2/3}
      \]
    - Simplifying both sides, we get:
      \[
      Q = 2^{(9/2) \cdot (2/3)} = 2^{9/3} = 2^3 = 8
      \]
