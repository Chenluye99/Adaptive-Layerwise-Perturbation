To solve the problem of raising the matrix
\[
A = \begin{pmatrix} 3 & -4 \\ 1 & -1 \end{pmatrix}
\]
to the 2016th power, we can make use of diagonalization if the matrix is diagonalizable. If a matrix \( A \) is diagonalizable, then \( A = PDP^{-1} \), where \( D \) is a diagonal matrix containing the eigenvalues of \( A \), and \( P \) is a matrix composed of the corresponding eigenvectors of \( A \). 

Once we have \( A = PDP^{-1} \), raising \( A \) to the 2016th power becomes much simpler because:
\[
A^{2016} = (PDP^{-1})^{2016} = PD^{2016}P^{-1}
\]
Here, \( D^{2016} \) is easy to compute because raising a diagonal matrix to a power simply involves raising each of its diagonal elements to that power.

Let's proceed step-by-step to solve this problem:

1. **Find the Eigenvalues and Eigenvectors of the Matrix:**
   To determine if the matrix is diagonalizable, we first need to find its eigenvalues by solving the characteristic equation \(\det(A - \lambda I) = 0\).

   The characteristic equation for \( A \) is:
   \[
   \det \begin{pmatrix} 3 - \lambda & -4 \\ 1 & -1 - \lambda \end{pmatrix} = (3 - \lambda)(-1 - \lambda) - (-4)(1) = 0
   \]
   \[
   (3 - \lambda)(-1 - \lambda) + 4 = 0
   \]
   \[
   -3 - 3\lambda + \lambda + \lambda^2 + 4 = 0
   \]
   \[
   \lambda^2 - 2\lambda + 1 = 0
   \]
   \[
   (\lambda - 1)^2 = 0
   \]
   So, the eigenvalue is \(\lambda = 1\) with algebraic multiplicity 2.

2. **Find the Eigenvectors Corresponding to the Eigenvalue \(\lambda = 1\):**
   We solve \((A - I)\mathbf{v} = 0\) to find the eigenvectors:
   \[
   \begin{pmatrix} 3 - 1 & -4 \\ 1 & -1 - 1 \end{pmatrix} \mathbf{v} = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & -4 \\ 1 & -2 \end{pmatrix} \mathbf{v} = 0
   \]
   This gives us the system of equations:
   \[
   2v_1 - 4v_2 = 0 \implies v_1 = 2v_2
   \]
   So, the eigenvector corresponding to \(\lambda = 1\) can be written as:
   \[
   \mathbf{v}_1 = \begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}
   \]
   However, we need a second linearly independent eigenvector. Since \((\lambda - 1)^2 = 0\) indicates that \(\lambda = 1\) has algebraic multiplicity 2 but might not have 2 linearly independent eigenvectors, we check if the matrix is defective (i.e., does not have 2 linearly independent eigenvectors), or if it can still be put into Jordan canonical form. 

   We find that the matrix \( A \) is not diagonalizable because it does not have 2 linearly independent eigenvectors. Instead, it can be put in Jordan canonical form. The Jordan form for a matrix with a repeated eigenvalue but only one linearly independent eigenvector is a Jordan block.

3. **Find the Jordan Canonical Form:**
   If the matrix is not diagonalizable, it can be written in Jordan canonical form. For \(\lambda = 1\), the Jordan form \( J \) of \( A \) will be:
   \[
   J = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}
   \]
   and there exists a matrix \( P \) such that:
   \[
   A = PJP^{-1}
   \]
   To find \( P \) and \( P^{-1} \), we need a generalized eigenvector in addition to the eigenvector.

   We already have one eigenvector \(\mathbf{v}_1 = \begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}\). We solve \((A - I)\mathbf{w} = \mathbf{v}_1\) to find a generalized eigenvector \(\mathbf{w}\):
   \[
   \begin{pmatrix} 2 & -4 \\ 1 & -2 \end{pmatrix} \mathbf{w} = \begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}
   \]
   Let \(\mathbf{w} = \begin{pmatrix} w_1 \\ w_2 \end{pmatrix}\), then we have:
   \[
   \begin{cases}
   2w_1 - 4w_2 = 2 \\
   w_1 - 2w_2 = 1
   \end{cases}
   \]
   Solving the second equation, we get \( w_1 = 2w_2 + 1 \). Substitute into the first equation:
   \[
   2(2w_2 + 1) - 4w_2 = 2 \implies 4w_2 + 2 - 4w_2 = 2 \implies 2 = 2
   \]
   This is always true, so it means the system is consistent. We can choose \( w_2 = 0 \), then \( w_1 = 1 \). So, one generalized eigenvector is:
   \[
   \mathbf{w} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}
   \]

   Therefore, we can form the matrix \( P \) as:
   \[
   P = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}
   \]
   and the Jordan form \( J \) is:
   \[
   J = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}
   \]
   The inverse of \( P \) is:
   \[
   P^{-1} = \frac{1}{2 \cdot 0 - 1 \cdot 1} \begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ -1 & 2 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & -2 \end{pmatrix}
   \]

4. **Compute \( A^{2016} \):**
   Now we need to compute \( J^{2016} \):
   \[
   J = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}
   \]
   Raising \( J \) to the 2016th power, we get:
   \[
   J^{2016} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}^{2016} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2016 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}
   \]
   Therefore,
   \[
   A^{2016} = P J^{2016} P^{-1} = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2016 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & -2 \end{pmatrix}
   \]
   First, compute \( J^{2016} P^{-1} \):
   \[
   \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2016 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & -2 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \cdot 0 + 2016 \cdot 1 & 1 \cdot 1 + 2016 \cdot (-2) \\ 0 \cdot 0 + 1 \cdot 1 & 0 \cdot 1 + 1 \cdot (-2) \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 2016 & 1 - 4032 \\ 1 & -2 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 2016 & -4031 \\ 1 & -2 \end{pmatrix}
   \]
   Then, compute \( P \cdot \begin{pmatrix} 2016 & -4031 \\ 1 & -2 \end{pmatrix} \):
   \[
   \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} 2016 & -4031 \\ 1 & -2 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 2 \cdot 2016 + 1 \cdot 1 & 2 \cdot (-4031) + 1 \cdot (-2) \\ 1 \cdot 2016 + 0 \cdot 1 & 1 \cdot (-4031) + 0 \cdot (-2) \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 4032 + 1 & -8062 - 2 \\ 2016 & -4031 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 4033 & -8064 \\ 2016 & -4031 \end{pmatrix}
   \]
