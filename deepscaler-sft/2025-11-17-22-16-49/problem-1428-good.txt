To solve the problem, let's start by approaching it step-by-step:

1. **Understand the Problem:**
    - We need to find the number of ways to seat 6 people at a round table such that John and Sam are seated next to each other.
    - Two seatings are considered the same if one can be obtained by rotating the other, so it's a matter of counting unique circular permutations.

2. **Formulate a Solution:**
    - First, we treat John and Sam as a single "block" or "unit". This "unit" can be arranged internally in 2 ways (John-Sam or Sam-John).
    - So, essentially, we are now arranging 5 units (this "block" of John and Sam, and the other 4 people) around a round table.

3. **Counting Circular Permutations:**
    - The number of ways to arrange 5 units around a round table is \((5-1)!\) (i.e., 4!), because in a circular permutation of \(n\) objects, we fix one position and only arrange the others, which gives us \((n-1)!\) ways.
    - Then, there are 2 ways to arrange John and Sam within their "block".

4. **Combine the Steps:**
    - So, the total number of ways is \(2 \times 4!\).

Let's calculate this step-by-step using Python to ensure accuracy.
```python
import sympy as sp

# Calculate 4! (since we are arranging 5 items in a circle, it's (5-1)! = 4!)
factorial_4 = sp.factorial(4)

# There are 2 ways to arrange John and Sam within their "block"
ways_to_arrange_john_sam = 2

# The total number of ways is 2 * 4!
total_ways = ways_to_arrange_john_sam * factorial_4
print(total_ways)
```

Thus, the number of ways to seat 6 people at a round table such that John and Sam are seated next to each other is \(\boxed{48}\). This is our final answer.
