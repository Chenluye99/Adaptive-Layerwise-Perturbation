To solve the problem, let's start by understanding what we need to do:

1. **Define the Problem:**
    - A year on Mars has 697 days.
    - A week on Mars is 12 days long.
    - Year 0 begins on the first day of the week.

2. **Determine the Day of the Week for Year 1:**
    - To find out what day of the week year 1 begins, we need to determine the remainder when 697 is divided by 12. This is because the week on Mars repeats every 12 days, and the remainder of the division by 12 will indicate how far the year has "shifted" in terms of the week from the starting day of year 0.
    - Mathematically, we get \( 697 \mod 12 \).

3. **Find the Next Year That Begins on the First Day of the Week:**
    - We start from year 0 and check each subsequent year until we find one that begins on the first day of the week.
    - If the remainder from the previous step is \( r \), then year 1 will start on the \((r + 1) \mod 12\)-th day of the week. For it to begin on the first day of the week, this remainder must be such that it makes the start of year 1 fall on the first day, meaning we need to continue checking beyond year 1 until we get back to the first day of the week.

Let's perform this step-by-step using Python to ensure accuracy.
```python
# Step 1: Determine how many days in a Martian year in terms of weeks and days
days_in_year = 697
days_in_week = 12

# Find the remainder when 697 is divided by 12
remainder = days_in_year % days_in_week

# Initialize the day of the week for year 0 as 0 (index for first day of the week)
current_day_of_week = 0

# Find the next year that starts on the first day of the week (day 0)
year = 1
while True:
    # Move to the next year
    year += 1
    current_day_of_week = (current_day_of_week + days_in_year % days_in_week) % days_in_week
    if current_day_of_week == 0:
        break

print(year)
```
