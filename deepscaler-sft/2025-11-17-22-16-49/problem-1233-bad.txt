First, let's begin by analyzing the equation \(x^2 + my^2 = 4\). This equation represents a conic section. Depending on the value of \(m\):

- If \(m > 0\) and \(m \neq 1\), it is an ellipse.
- If \(m = 1\), it is a circle, but we are told \(m\) is not equal to 1.
- If \(m < 0\), it is a hyperbola.

We are given that this conic section (which must be an ellipse because the foci lie on the circle of radius 4) has foci that both lie on the circle \(x^2 + y^2 = 16\). For the equation \(x^2 + my^2 = 4\):

1. Rewrite it in standard form:
   \[
   \frac{x^2}{4} + \frac{my^2}{4} = 1 \implies \frac{x^2}{4} + \frac{y^2}{\frac{4}{m}} = 1
   \]
   This is the standard form of an ellipse \(\frac{x^2}{a^2} + \frac{y^2}{b^2} = 1\) where \(a^2 = 4\) and \(b^2 = \frac{4}{m}\). Without loss of generality, assume \(a > b\), so \(a^2 = 4\) and \(b^2 = \frac{4}{m}\).

2. For an ellipse, the distance of the foci from the center is given by \(c\) where \(c^2 = a^2 - b^2\). Substituting \(a^2 = 4\) and \(b^2 = \frac{4}{m}\), we get:
   \[
   c^2 = 4 - \frac{4}{m}
   \]
   So,
   \[
   c = \sqrt{4 - \frac{4}{m}} = 2 \sqrt{1 - \frac{1}{m}}
   \]
   The foci of the ellipse are at \((\pm c, 0)\), and we are given that these foci lie on the circle \(x^2 + y^2 = 16\). Therefore, the coordinates of the foci \((\pm c, 0)\) must satisfy \(x^2 + y^2 = 16\). Since the foci are on the x-axis, we substitute \(y = 0\) and get:
   \[
   c^2 = 16 \implies \left(2 \sqrt{1 - \frac{1}{m}}\right)^2 = 16 \implies 4 \left(1 - \frac{1}{m}\right) = 16 \implies 1 - \frac{1}{m} = 4 \implies - \frac{1}{m} = 3 \implies \frac{1}{m} = -3 \implies m = -\frac{1}{3}
   \]
   Or,
   \[
   1 - \frac{1}{m} = 4 \implies - \frac{1}{m} = 3 \implies \frac{1}{m} = -3 \implies m = -\frac{1}{3}
   \]
   is not correct because we need to check both positive and negative possibilities.

   Let's check another possibility: maybe our assumption was that \(a^2 = 4\) and \(b^2 = \frac{4}{m}\) where \( \frac{4}{m} > 4 \) which means \( 0 < m < 1 \) because then \( b^2 > a^2 \) and we will get a different form of the ellipse.

3. If \( \frac{4}{m} > 4 \), then \( 0 < m < 1 \), and we get \( b^2 = \frac{4}{m} \) and \( a^2 = 4 \). So,
   \[
   c^2 = \frac{4}{m} - 4 \implies c = \sqrt{\frac{4}{m} - 4}
   \]
   And this \(c\) must also satisfy \( c^2 = 16 \):
   \[
   \frac{4}{m} - 4 = 16 \implies \frac{4}{m} = 20 \implies 4 = 20m \implies m = \frac{1}{5}
   \]
