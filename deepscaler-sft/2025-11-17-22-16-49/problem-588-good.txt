### Part 1: Find the Intervals of Monotonicity for \( f(x) \)

The function is given by:
\[ f(x) = \frac{1}{2}x^2 - 2 \ln x + a. \]
To find the intervals of monotonicity, we need to find the first derivative of \( f(x) \) and determine where it is positive or negative.

1. Compute the first derivative of \( f(x) \):
\[ f'(x) = \frac{d}{dx}\left(\frac{1}{2}x^2 - 2 \ln x + a\right) = x - \frac{2}{x}. \]

2. Set the first derivative to zero to find critical points:
\[ x - \frac{2}{x} = 0 \implies x^2 = 2 \implies x = \sqrt{2} \quad (\text{we only consider positive } x \text{ since } \ln x \text{ is defined for } x > 0). \]

3. Determine the intervals of monotonicity by analyzing the sign of \( f'(x) \):
- For \( 0 < x < \sqrt{2} \), \( f'(x) < 0 \) (so \( f(x) \) is decreasing).
- For \( x > \sqrt{2} \), \( f'(x) > 0 \) (so \( f(x) \) is increasing).

Thus, the function \( f(x) \) is decreasing on \( (0, \sqrt{2}) \) and increasing on \( (\sqrt{2}, \infty) \).

### Part 2: Find the Value of \( t \) When \( |MN| \) Reaches Its Minimum Value

Given \( a = 0 \), so \( f(x) = \frac{1}{2}x^2 - 2 \ln x \) and \( g(x) = -x^2 + 3x - 4 \).

When the line \( x = t \) intersects the graphs of \( f(x) \) and \( g(x) \), the points of intersection are:
\[ M = (t, f(t)) = \left(t, \frac{1}{2}t^2 - 2 \ln t\right), \]
\[ N = (t, g(t)) = \left(t, -t^2 + 3t - 4\right). \]

So, the vertical distance between the points \( M \) and \( N \) is:
\[ |MN| = \left| \frac{1}{2}t^2 - 2 \ln t - (-t^2 + 3t - 4) \right| = \left| \frac{1}{2}t^2 - 2 \ln t + t^2 - 3t + 4 \right| = \left| \frac{3}{2}t^2 - 3t - 2 \ln t + 4 \right|. \]

To minimize \( |MN| \), we need to find the minimum of the expression \( h(t) = \frac{3}{2}t^2 - 3t - 2 \ln t + 4 \) (since the absolute value function achieves its minimum at the same points as the function inside the absolute value achieves its minimum, as long as it is minimized at a zero of the derivative or boundary points, etc.)

\[ h'(t) = \frac{d}{dt} \left( \frac{3}{2}t^2 - 3t - 2 \ln t + 4 \right) = 3t - 3 - \frac{2}{t}. \]

2. Set the first derivative equal to zero to find critical points:
\[ 3t - 3 - \frac{2}{t} = 0 \implies 3t^2 - 3t - 2 = 0. \]

\[ t = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a} = \frac{3 \pm \sqrt{9 + 24}}{6} = \frac{3 \pm \sqrt{33}}{6}. \]

Since \( t > 0 \), we take the positive root:
\[ t = \frac{3 + \sqrt{33}}{6}. \]
