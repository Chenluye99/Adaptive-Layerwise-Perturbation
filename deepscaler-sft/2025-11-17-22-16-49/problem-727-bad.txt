To solve the problem, let's begin by identifying the key properties of the ellipse given its endpoints of the major axis and a point it passes through.

1. **Identify the Center and Length of the Major Axis:**
    - The endpoints of the major axis are \((-11, 4)\) and \((9, 4)\). Since these points share the same \(y\)-coordinate, the major axis is horizontal, and the center of the ellipse is the midpoint of these endpoints.
    - The midpoint (center of the ellipse) \((h, k)\) is calculated as:
      \[
      h = \frac{-11 + 9}{2} = \frac{-2}{2} = -1, \quad k = \frac{4 + 4}{2} = \frac{8}{2} = 4
      \]
      So, the center of the ellipse is \((-1, 4)\).
    - The length of the semi-major axis \(a\) is half the distance between \((-11, 4)\) and \((9, 4)\):
      \[
      2a = \text{distance between } (-11, 4) \text{ and } (9, 4) = 9 - (-11) = 20 \implies a = 10
      \]

2. **Equation of the Ellipse:**
    - The standard form of the equation of an ellipse with a horizontal major axis and center at \((-1, 4)\) is:
      \[
      \frac{(x + 1)^2}{a^2} + \frac{(y - 4)^2}{b^2} = 1
      \]
      where \(a = 10\), so \(a^2 = 100\). We need to find \(b\).

3. **Substitute the Point (7, 7) into the Equation:**
    - The ellipse passes through the point \((7, 7)\), so substituting \(x = 7\) and \(y = 7\) into the equation, we get:
      \[
      \frac{(7 + 1)^2}{100} + \frac{(7 - 4)^2}{b^2} = 1 \implies \frac{8^2}{100} + \frac{3^2}{b^2} = 1 \implies \frac{64}{100} + \frac{9}{b^2} = 1 \implies \frac{16}{25} + \frac{9}{b^2} = 1
      \]
    - Rearrange to solve for \(b^2\):
      \[
      \frac{9}{b^2} = 1 - \frac{16}{25} = \frac{25}{25} - \frac{16}{25} = \frac{9}{25} \implies \frac{9}{b^2} = \frac{9}{25} \implies b^2 = 25 \implies b = 5
      \]

4. **Find the Area of the Ellipse:**
    - The area \(A\) of an ellipse is given by the formula:
      \[
      A = \pi \cdot a \cdot b
      \]
    - Substituting \(a = 10\) and \(b = 5\):
      \[
      A = \pi \cdot 10 \cdot 5 = 50 \pi
      \]
