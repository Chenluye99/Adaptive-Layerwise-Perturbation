First, let's understand the powers of the imaginary unit \(i\):

The powers of \(i\) repeat every 4 cycles as follows:
\[
\begin{align*}
i^1 &= i, \\
i^2 &= -1, \\
i^3 &= -i, \\
i^4 &= 1, \\
i^5 &= i^1 = i, \\
i^6 &= i^2 = -1, \\
i^7 &= i^3 = -i, \\
i^8 &= i^4 = 1, \\
\text{and so on.}
\end{align*}
\]

In general, for any integer \(n\), \(i^n\) can be determined by finding the remainder when \(n\) is divided by 4, because the pattern repeats every 4 powers:

\[
i^n = i^{n \mod 4}.
\]

Now, let's determine \(i^5\), \(i^{-25}\), and \(i^{45}\) using this pattern.

1. For \(i^5\):
\[
5 \mod 4 = 1 \implies i^5 = i^1 = i.
\]

2. For \(i^{-25}\):
First, note that \(-25 \mod 4 = -25 + 25 = 0 \implies -25 \equiv 0 \pmod{4} \implies i^{-25} = i^0 = 1.\)

3. For \(i^{45}\):
\[
45 \mod 4 = 1 \implies i^{45} = i^1 = i.
\]

So, we have:
\[
i^5 = i, \quad i^{-25} = 1, \quad i^{45} = i.
\]

Now, substitute these values into the original expression:
\[
i^5 + i^{-25} + i^{45} = i + 1 + i = 1 + 2i.
\]
