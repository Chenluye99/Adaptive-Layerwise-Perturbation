First, let's define the problem clearly. Amy has 3 identical chocolate chip cookies (let's call them \( C \)) and 4 identical sugar cookies (let's call them \( S \)). That means she has 7 cookies in total to eat in different orders.

We need to find out how many different orders Amy can eat the cookies such that either she eats a chocolate chip cookie first, or she eats a chocolate chip cookie last, or both.

1. **Count All Possible Orders:**
   The total number of ways to arrange 3 identical chocolate chip cookies and 4 identical sugar cookies is given by the multinomial coefficient:
   \[
   \frac{7!}{3!4!}
   \]
   This represents the total number of ways to arrange 7 items where 3 of them are identical and 4 are identical.

2. **Generate All Possible Orders:**
   We need to generate all distinct permutations of the sequence 'CCCCSSS'. For example, if the sequence starts or ends with a 'C', those permutations should be counted as valid.

3. **Filter Orders Based on Conditions:**
   We have to filter these permutations to get the ones where either the first cookie eaten is a chocolate chip cookie, or the last cookie eaten is a chocolate chip cookie, or both. We will use Python to generate all unique permutations of 'CCCCSSS' and then check each permutation against the conditions mentioned.

4. **Count the Valid Orders:**
   For each of these permutations, we check if the first or last element is a 'C', and count how many such permutations meet the criteria.

Let's implement this step-by-step in Python to get the accurate result.
```python
import itertools

# Generate all distinct permutations of the sequence 'CCCCSSS'
cookies = 'CCCCSSS'
permutations = set()

for p in set(itertools.permutations(cookies)):
    permutations.add(''.join(p))

# Count how many permutations meet the conditions: either the first or the last cookie eaten is a chocolate chip cookie, or both
count = 0

for perm in permutations:
    if perm[0] == 'C' or perm[-1] == 'C':
        count += 1

print(count)
```
Code execution result: 30

Thus, the number of different orders in which Amy can eat the cookies such that either she eats a chocolate chip cookie first, she eats a chocolate chip cookie last, or both, is \(\boxed{30}\). This is our final answer.
