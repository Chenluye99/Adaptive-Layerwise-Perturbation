First, let's outline the problem and then proceed to solve it with Python.

We are given a triangle \( \triangle ABC \) with 15 distinct points on it: the 3 vertices \( A, B, C \), 3 points on side \(\overline{AB}\), 4 points on side \(\overline{BC}\), and 5 points on side \(\overline{CA}\).

The total number of ways to choose 3 points from these 15 points to form a triangle is given by the combination formula \(\binom{15}{3}\). However, not every selection of 3 points will form a triangle with positive area. Three collinear points will not form a triangle with positive area.

In the problem, the only sets of points that could possibly be collinear are the sets of points on \(\overline{AB}\), \(\overline{BC}\), and \(\overline{CA}\).

1. There are \(\binom{3}{3} = 1\) ways to choose 3 points that are all on \(\overline{AB}\) (which are collinear and thus do not form a triangle with positive area).
2. There are \(\binom{4}{3} = 4\) ways to choose 3 points that are all on \(\overline{BC}\) (which are collinear and thus do not form a triangle with positive area).
3. There are \(\binom{5}{3} = 10\) ways to choose 3 points that are all on \(\overline{CA}\) (which are collinear and thus do not form a triangle with positive area).

So, the total number of ways to choose 3 points that are collinear and thus do not form a triangle with positive area is \(1 + 4 + 10 = 15\).

The total number of ways to choose any 3 points from 15 points is \(\binom{15}{3} = \frac{15 \cdot 14 \cdot 13}{3 \cdot 2 \cdot 1} = 455\).

Therefore, the number of ways to choose 3 points that do form a triangle with positive area is \(455 - 15 = 440\).

Let's confirm this with Python code to ensure accuracy.
```python
import itertools

# List all 15 points (we can label them for clarity, but they are just indices here)
points = list(range(15))

# Generate all possible ways to choose 3 points from the 15 points
combinations_of_3 = list(itertools.combinations(points, 3))

# Function to check if three points form a triangle with positive area (i.e., they are not collinear)
def form_triangle_with_positive_area(p1, p2, p3):
    # Let's map points back to their original locations in order to check for collinearity:
    # The first 3 points are A, B, C.
    # Next 3 points are on AB, points 3 to 5 are on BC, points 6 to 9 are on CA.
    
    # Map each point to its corresponding location (dummy representation for simplicity in this context)
    map_points = {
        0: "A", 1: "B", 2: "C",
        3: "AB1", 4: "AB2", 5: "AB3",
        6: "BC1", 7: "BC2", 8: "BC3", 9: "BC4",
        10: "CA1", 11: "CA2", 12: "CA3", 13: "CA4", 14: "CA5"
    }
    
    # Determine the locations of the points chosen
    loc1, loc2, loc3 = map_points[p1], map_points[p2], map_points[p3]
    
    # Only check for collinearity if points are on the same side of the triangle (AB, BC, CA)
    if (loc1 in ["AB1", "AB2", "AB3"] and loc2 in ["AB1", "AB2", "AB3"] and loc3 in ["AB1", "AB2", "AB3"]):
        return False  # These are collinear on AB
    elif (loc1 in ["BC1", "BC2", "BC3", "BC4"] and loc2 in ["BC1", "BC2", "BC3", "BC4"] and loc3 in ["BC1", "BC2", "BC3", "BC4"]):
        return False  # These are collinear on BC
    elif (loc1 in ["CA1", "CA2", "CA3", "CA4", "CA5"] and loc2 in ["CA1", "CA2", "CA3", "CA4", "CA5"] and loc3 in ["CA1", "CA2", "CA3", "CA4", "CA5"]):
        return False  # These are collinear on CA
    
    return True  # If we haven't returned False, then they must form a triangle with positive area

# Count how many of the combinations form a triangle with positive area
positive_area_triangle_count = sum(1 for combo in combinations_of_3 if form_triangle_with_positive_area(*combo))

print(positive_area_triangle_count)
```
