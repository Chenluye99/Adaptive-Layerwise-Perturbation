To solve the problem of finding the constant term in the expansion of \(\left( \frac{3}{2} x^2 - \frac{1}{3x} \right)^6\), we can use the binomial theorem. The binomial theorem states that:

\[
(a + b)^n = \sum_{k=0}^{n} \binom{n}{k} a^{n-k} b^k
\]

For our specific problem, \( a = \frac{3}{2} x^2 \), \( b = -\frac{1}{3x} \), and \( n = 6 \). So, the general term in the expansion is given by:

\[
T_k = \binom{6}{k} \left( \frac{3}{2} x^2 \right)^{6-k} \left( -\frac{1}{3x} \right)^k
\]

Let's write out the general term explicitly:

\[
T_k = \binom{6}{k} \left( \frac{3}{2} \right)^{6-k} (x^2)^{6-k} \left( -\frac{1}{3} \right)^k (x)^{-k}
\]

\[
T_k = \binom{6}{k} \left( \frac{3}{2} \right)^{6-k} (-1)^k \left( \frac{1}{3} \right)^k x^{2(6-k) - k}
\]

\[
T_k = \binom{6}{k} \left( \frac{3}{2} \right)^{6-k} (-1)^k \left( \frac{1}{3} \right)^k x^{12 - 3k}
\]

For the term to be constant, the exponent of \(x\) must be zero, i.e.,

\[
12 - 3k = 0
\]

Solving for \(k\):

\[
12 = 3k \implies k = 4
\]

Substituting \( k = 4 \) into the general term, we get:

T_4 = \binom{6}{4} \left( \frac{3}{2} \right)^{6-4} (-1)^4 \left( \frac{1}{3} \right)^4 x^{12 - 3 \cdot 4}
