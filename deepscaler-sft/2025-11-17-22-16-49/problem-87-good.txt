First, let's follow the problem step-by-step to get the final price after applying all the successive discounts.

1. **Initial Price**: The original price of the laptop is $1200.
2. **First Discount (15% off)**:
   - The first discount is 15%, so the price after the first discount is \(1200 \times (1 - 0.15) = 1200 \times 0.85 = 1020\).
3. **Second Coupon (10% off the discounted price)**:
   - The second coupon gives an additional 10% off the already discounted price, so the price after the second coupon is \(1020 \times (1 - 0.10) = 1020 \times 0.90 = 918\).
4. **Third Coupon (5% off the subsequent price)**:
   - The third coupon gives an additional 5% off the already discounted price again, so the price after the third coupon is \(918 \times (1 - 0.05) = 918 \times 0.95 = 872.10\).

Now, we want to find out what a single discount \(d\) would be that would reduce the original price of $1200 to $872.10. Mathematically, this means finding \(d\) in the equation:

\[ 1200 \times (1 - d) = 872.10. \]

Rearrange this equation to solve for \(d\):

\[ 1 - d = \frac{872.10}{1200}, \]
\[ d = 1 - \frac{872.10}{1200}. \]

Let's compute this step-by-step using Python to ensure accuracy.
```python
# Initial price of the laptop
initial_price = 1200

# Apply the successive discounts step-by-step as described:
price_after_first_discount = initial_price * (1 - 0.15)
price_after_second_discount = price_after_first_discount * (1 - 0.10)
final_price_after_all_discounts = price_after_second_discount * (1 - 0.05)

# Now find the single equivalent discount d that gives the same final price
d = 1 - (final_price_after_all_discounts / initial_price)
print(d * 100)  # To get the discount percentage
```
