We start with the vector identity for the double cross product:

\[
(\mathbf{a} \times \mathbf{b}) \times \mathbf{c} = (\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{c}) \mathbf{b} - (\mathbf{b} \cdot \mathbf{c}) \mathbf{a}.
\]

We are given that

\[
(\mathbf{a} \times \mathbf{b}) \times \mathbf{c} = \frac{1}{3} \|\mathbf{b}\| \|\mathbf{c}\| \mathbf{a}.
\]

By substituting the identity into the equation, we get:

\[
(\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{c}) \mathbf{b} - (\mathbf{b} \cdot \mathbf{c}) \mathbf{a} = \frac{1}{3} \|\mathbf{b}\| \|\mathbf{c}\| \mathbf{a}.
\]

Since \(\mathbf{a}\), \(\mathbf{b}\), and \(\mathbf{c}\) are linearly independent (no two of them are parallel, and they are nonzero), the coefficients of \(\mathbf{b}\) and \(\mathbf{a}\) on both sides of the equation must match separately. The coefficient of \(\mathbf{b}\) on the left side is \((\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{c})\) and on the right side it is 0, so we get:

\[
(\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{c}) = 0.
\]

This means that \(\mathbf{a}\) and \(\mathbf{c}\) are orthogonal, so \(\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{c} = 0\). The coefficient of \(\mathbf{a}\) on the left side is \(-( \mathbf{b} \cdot \mathbf{c} )\) and on the right side it is \(\frac{1}{3} \|\mathbf{b}\| \|\mathbf{c}\|\), so we get:

\[
- (\mathbf{b} \cdot \mathbf{c}) = \frac{1}{3} \|\mathbf{b}\| \|\mathbf{c}\|.
\]

We know that \(\mathbf{b} \cdot \mathbf{c} = \|\mathbf{b}\| \|\mathbf{c}\| \cos \theta\), where \(\theta\) is the angle between \(\mathbf{b}\) and \(\mathbf{c}\). Substituting this in, we get:

\[
- (\|\mathbf{b}\| \|\mathbf{c}\| \cos \theta) = \frac{1}{3} \|\mathbf{b}\| \|\mathbf{c}\|.
\]

We can cancel \(\|\mathbf{b}\| \|\mathbf{c}\|\) from both sides (since \(\mathbf{b}\) and \(\mathbf{c}\) are nonzero):

\[
- \cos \theta = \frac{1}{3}.
\]

From this, we get:

\[
\cos \theta = - \frac{1}{3}.
\]

To find \(\sin \theta\), we use the Pythagorean identity:

\[
\sin^2 \theta + \cos^2 \theta = 1.
\]

Substitute \(\cos \theta = - \frac{1}{3}\) into the identity:

\[
\sin^2 \theta + \left( - \frac{1}{3} \right)^2 = 1 \implies \sin^2 \theta + \frac{1}{9} = 1 \implies \sin^2 \theta = 1 - \frac{1}{9} = \frac{8}{9}.
\]

Taking the square root of both sides, we get:

\[
\sin \theta = \pm \sqrt{\frac{8}{9}} = \pm \frac{2 \sqrt{2}}{3}.
\]

Since \(\theta\) is an angle between two vectors, \(\sin \theta\) is non-negative. Thus, we have:

\[
\sin \theta = \frac{2 \sqrt{2}}{3}.
\]
