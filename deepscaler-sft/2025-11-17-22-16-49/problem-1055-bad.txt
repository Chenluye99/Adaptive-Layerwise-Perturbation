First, let's define the relationship for the energy stored between any two point charges. The energy \( E \) stored by a pair of point charges \( q_1 \) and \( q_2 \) which are separated by a distance \( r \) is given by:

\[ E \propto \frac{q_1 q_2}{r} \]

Since the charges are identical, say \( q \), and the energy stored by each pair of charges in the equilateral triangle configuration is the same, we can present the formula as:

\[ E_{\text{pair}} \propto \frac{q \cdot q}{r} = \frac{q^2}{r} \]

If there are three charges at the vertices of an equilateral triangle, there are three pairs of charges, and the total energy stored in this configuration is the sum of the energies stored by each pair of charges.

Let's denote the side length of the equilateral triangle as \( a \). Then, the energy stored in the equilateral triangle configuration (with three pairs of charges) is:

\[ E_{\text{total, initial}} = 3 \cdot E_{\text{pair}} = 3 \cdot \frac{q^2}{a} \]

We know that the total energy stored in the initial configuration is 15 Joules, so:

\[ 3 \cdot \frac{q^2}{a} = 15 \]
\[ \frac{q^2}{a} = 5 \]

Next, we need to find out how much energy is stored when one of these charges is moved to the midpoint of the opposite side. When one of the charges is moved to the midpoint of the opposite side, we now have two pairs of charges with distance \( a \) apart and one pair of charges with distance \( \frac{a}{2} \) apart (since the distance from a vertex to the midpoint of the opposite side in an equilateral triangle is \(\frac{a}{2}\)).

The energy stored by the two pairs of charges at a distance of \( a \) apart is:

\[ 2 \cdot \frac{q^2}{a} \]

The energy stored by the pair of charges at a distance of \( \frac{a}{2} \) apart is:

\[ \frac{q^2}{\frac{a}{2}} = \frac{q^2 \cdot 2}{a} = 2 \cdot \frac{q^2}{a} \]

So, the total energy stored in this new configuration is:

\[ E_{\text{total, new}} = 2 \cdot \frac{q^2}{a} + 2 \cdot \frac{q^2}{a} = 2 \cdot \frac{q^2}{a} + 2 \cdot \frac{q^2}{a} = 4 \cdot \frac{q^2}{a} \]

Since we know that \(\frac{q^2}{a} = 5\):

\[ E_{\text{total, new}} = 4 \cdot 5 = 20 \text{ Joules} \]

The additional energy stored in the new configuration compared to the initial configuration is:

\[ E_{\text{additional}} = E_{\text{total, new}} - E_{\text{total, initial}} = 20 - 15 = 5 \text{ Joules} \]

Let's confirm this with Python code:
