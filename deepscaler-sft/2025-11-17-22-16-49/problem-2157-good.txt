Let's denote the expression \(\sqrt[3]{x + \sqrt[3]{x + \sqrt[3]{x + \dotsb}}} = y\) and the expression \(\sqrt[3]{x \sqrt[3]{x \sqrt[3]{x \dotsm}}} = z\).

From the first expression, we get:
\[ y = \sqrt[3]{x + y}. \]
Cubing both sides, we obtain:
\[ y^3 = x + y. \]
Rearrange this equation to:
\[ y^3 - y = x. \quad \text{(1)} \]

From the second expression, we get:
\[ z = \sqrt[3]{x z}. \]
Cubing both sides, we obtain:
\[ z^3 = x z. \]
For the non-trivial solution where \(z \neq 0\), we can divide both sides by \(z\):
\[ z^2 = x. \quad \text{(2)} \]

Now, we set the expressions for \(x\) from the two equations equal to each other because the left-hand sides of the original equations are equal:
\[ y = z. \]
Substitute \( y = z \) into equations (1) and (2):
\[ y^3 - y = y^2. \]
Rearrange this equation to:
\[ y^3 - y^2 - y = 0. \]
Factor out \( y \):
\[ y(y^2 - y - 1) = 0. \]
This gives us three potential solutions:
\[ y = 0, \quad y = \frac{1 + \sqrt{5}}{2}, \quad y = \frac{1 - \sqrt{5}}{2}. \]

Since we are looking for the positive solution, we discard \( y = 0 \) and \( y = \frac{1 - \sqrt{5}}{2} \) (as it is negative), and we are left with:
\[ y = \frac{1 + \sqrt{5}}{2}. \]

Now substitute \( y = \frac{1 + \sqrt{5}}{2} \) back into equation (2) to solve for \( x \):
\[ \left( \frac{1 + \sqrt{5}}{2} \right)^2 = x. \]
