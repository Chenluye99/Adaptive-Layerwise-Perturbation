First, let's start with the expression \( g^4 + 12g^2 + 9 \) and try to express it in the form \( c(g^2 + p)^2 + q \).

1. Expand the form \( c(g^2 + p)^2 + q \):
\[ c(g^2 + p)^2 + q = c(g^4 + 2g^2p + p^2) + q = cg^4 + 2cg^2p + cp^2 + q. \]

2. We want this to be equal to \( g^4 + 12g^2 + 9 \). By comparing the coefficients of corresponding terms, we get:
   - The coefficient of \( g^4 \): \( c = 1 \).
   - The coefficient of \( g^2 \): \( 2cp = 12 \).
   - The constant term: \( cp^2 + q = 9 \).

Given \( c = 1 \), we substitute it into the second equation:
\[ 2 \cdot 1 \cdot p = 12 \implies 2p = 12 \implies p = 6. \]

Now, substitute \( c = 1 \) and \( p = 6 \) into the third equation:
\[ 1 \cdot 6^2 + q = 9 \implies 36 + q = 9 \implies q = 9 - 36 \implies q = -27. \]

So, the expression \( g^4 + 12g^2 + 9 \) can be written as \( 1(g^2 + 6)^2 - 27 \), meaning that \( c = 1 \), \( p = 6 \), and \( q = -27 \).

Thus, the value of \( q \) is \(-27\). Let's confirm this by coding the solution to ensure the accuracy.
```python
import sympy as sp

# Define the variable
g = sp.symbols('g')

# Original expression
expr = g**4 + 12*g**2 + 9

# Try to express it in the form c*(g^2 + p)^2 + q
# We assume the form is c*(g^2 + p)^2 + q and solve for c, p, q
pattern = sp.expand(sp.symbols('c') * (sp.symbols('g')**2 + sp.symbols('p'))**2 + sp.symbols('q'))

# We match this with g**4 + 12*g**2 + 9 and solve for c, p, q
coefficients = sp.solve([sp.Eq(pattern.coeff(g, i), expr.coeff(g, i)) for i in range(5)], (sp.symbols('c'), sp.symbols('p'), sp.symbols('q')))

print(coefficients)
```
