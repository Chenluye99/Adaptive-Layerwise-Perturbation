First, let's define the roots of the quadratic equation. If $\sqrt{3} - 2$ is a root of the quadratic equation and the quadratic equation has rational coefficients, then its other root must be the conjugate of $\sqrt{3} - 2$, which is $-\sqrt{3} - 2$. This is because the sum and product of the roots of a quadratic equation with rational coefficients must also be rational.

Let's denote the roots of the quadratic equation by $r_1 = \sqrt{3} - 2$ and $r_2 = -\sqrt{3} - 2$. The quadratic equation with these roots can be written as:
\[
(x - r_1)(x - r_2) = 0
\]
Substituting the roots:
\[
(x - (\sqrt{3} - 2))(x - (-\sqrt{3} - 2)) = 0 \implies (x - \sqrt{3} + 2)(x + \sqrt{3} + 2) = 0
\]
Now, we expand this product:
\[
(x - \sqrt{3} + 2)(x + \sqrt{3} + 2) = [(x + 2) - \sqrt{3}][(x + 2) + \sqrt{3}]
\]
This is a difference of squares:
\[
[(x + 2) - \sqrt{3}][(x + 2) + \sqrt{3}] = (x + 2)^2 - (\sqrt{3})^2 = (x + 2)^2 - 3
\]
Now, we expand $(x + 2)^2 - 3$:
\[
(x + 2)^2 - 3 = x^2 + 4x + 4 - 3 = x^2 + 4x + 1
\]
So, the quadratic equation is $x^2 + 4x + 1$.
