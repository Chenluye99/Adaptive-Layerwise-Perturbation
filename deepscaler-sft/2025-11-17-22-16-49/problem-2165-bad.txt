To solve this problem, we need to determine the number of ways to form a committee by choosing one 7th-grader from four available 7th-graders and three 8th-graders from six available 8th-graders.

1. **Choose 1 7th-grader from 4 7th-graders:**
   - The number of ways to choose 1 7th-grader from 4 is given by the combination formula \( \binom{4}{1} \).
   - Mathematically, this is equal to 4.

2. **Choose 3 8th-graders from 6 8th-graders:**
   - The number of ways to choose 3 8th-graders from 6 is given by the combination formula \( \binom{6}{3} \).
   - Mathematically, this is equal to \(\frac{6!}{3!(6-3)!} = \frac{6!}{3!3!} = \frac{6 \times 5 \times 4}{3 \times 2 \times 1} = 20 \).

3. **Combine the Choices:**
   - To get the total number of ways to form the committee, multiply the number of ways to choose the 7th-grader by the number of ways to choose the 8th-graders.
   - Therefore, the total number of ways is \( 4 \times 20 = 80 \).

Let's confirm this with Python code to ensure accuracy.
```python
import math

# Calculate combinations
ways_to_choose_1_7th_grader = math.comb(4, 1)
ways_to_choose_3_8th_graders = math.comb(6, 3)

# Total ways to form the committee
total_ways = ways_to_choose_1_7th_grader * ways_to_choose_3_8th_graders
print(total_ways)
```
Code execution result: 80

Thus, the number of ways to fill the committee by choosing one 7th-grader from four and three 8th-graders from six is \(\boxed{80}\). This confirms our solution is accurate.
