To solve the problem of factoring the expression \(36 - 4x^2\) completely, let's follow these steps:

1. **Understand the Expression:**
    - The expression is \(36 - 4x^2\).

2. **Factor Out the Greatest Common Factor (GCF):**
    - Both 36 and \(4x^2\) have a common factor of 4. Factoring out 4, we get:
      \[
      36 - 4x^2 = 4(9 - x^2)
      \]

3. **Factor Further:**
    - The expression \(9 - x^2\) is a difference of squares. The difference of squares formula states that \(a^2 - b^2 = (a - b)(a + b)\). In this case, \(9 = 3^2\) and \(x^2 = (x)^2\), so:
      \[
      9 - x^2 = (3)^2 - (x)^2 = (3 - x)(3 + x)
      \]
    - Substituting this back into our factored expression, we get:
      \[
      4(9 - x^2) = 4(3 - x)(3 + x)
      \]

So, the complete factorization of \(36 - 4x^2\) is \(4(3 - x)(3 + x)\).

Let's confirm this by using Python to ensure our result is accurate.
```python
import sympy as sp

# Define the variable
x = sp.symbols('x')

# Define the expression
expression = 36 - 4 * x**2

# Factor the expression
factored_expression = sp.factor(expression)
print(factored_expression)
```
Code execution result: -4*(x - 3)*(x + 3)

The result from the Python code is \(-4 \cdot (x - 3) \cdot (x + 3)\). This is essentially the same factorization we obtained, but with a negative sign difference, which means it's another valid form of the factorization because constants can be factored out and moved around.

We know that \((x - 3) = -(3 - x)\) and \((x + 3) = (3 + x)\), so \(-4 \cdot (x - 3) \cdot (x + 3) = -4 \cdot [-(3 - x)] \cdot (3 + x) = 4 \cdot (3 - x) \cdot (3 + x)\).

Hence, both \(\boxed{4(3 - x)(3 + x)}\) and \(-4(x - 3)(x + 3)\) are valid factorizations, but the most conventional and positive factorization is \(\boxed{4(3 - x)(3 + x)}\). This is the answer we'll stick to, as it reflects a standard approach to presenting factored forms of expressions.
