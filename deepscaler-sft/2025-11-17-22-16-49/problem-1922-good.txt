To solve this problem, we need to use the properties of a regular tetrahedron. For a regular tetrahedron, if the side length of each equilateral triangular face is \(a\), then the height (or altitude) of the tetrahedron, denoted as \(h\), can be given by:

\[ h = \frac{\sqrt{6}}{3} a \]

We are given that the height of the tetrahedron is 20 inches, so we can set up the equation:

\[ 20 = \frac{\sqrt{6}}{3} a \]

We can solve this equation for \(a\):

1. Multiply both sides by 3 to get rid of the denominator:

\[ 60 = \sqrt{6} \cdot a \]

2. Isolate \(a\) by dividing both sides by \(\sqrt{6}\):

\[ a = \frac{60}{\sqrt{6}} \]

3. To rationalize the denominator, multiply the numerator and the denominator by \(\sqrt{6}\):

\[ a = \frac{60 \cdot \sqrt{6}}{\sqrt{6} \cdot \sqrt{6}} = \frac{60 \cdot \sqrt{6}}{6} = 10 \cdot \sqrt{6} \]

Thus, the length of each edge of the tetrahedron is \(10 \sqrt{6}\).
