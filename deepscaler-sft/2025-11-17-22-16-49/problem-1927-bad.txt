First, let's start with finding the prime factorization of 2002. 

We start by checking if 2002 is divisible by the smallest prime numbers:

- 2002 is even, so it is divisible by 2: \( 2002 \div 2 = 1001 \)
- Next, we factorize 1001. We check for divisibility by the next smallest primes:
  - 1001 is not divisible by 2 (it's odd).
  - The sum of the digits of 1001 is 1 + 0 + 0 + 1 = 2, which is not divisible by 3, so 1001 is not divisible by 3.
  - 1001 does not end in 0 or 5, so it is not divisible by 5.
  - To check for divisibility by 7, we perform the division: \( 1001 \div 7 \approx 143 \) and \( 1001 \mod 7 = 0 \), so 1001 is divisible by 7: \( 1001 \div 7 = 143 \)
- Now, we factorize 143:
  - 143 is not divisible by 2, 3, or 5.
  - To check for divisibility by 7, we perform the division: \( 143 \div 7 \approx 20.4286 \) (not an integer), so it is not divisible by 7.
  - To check for divisibility by 11, we perform the division: \( 143 \div 11 = 13 \) and \( 143 \mod 11 = 0 \), so 143 is divisible by 11: \( 143 \div 11 = 13 \)
- 13 is a prime number.

Thus, the prime factorization of 2002 is:
\[ 2002 = 2 \times 7 \times 11 \times 13 \]

To find the number of positive divisors of 2002, we use the formula for finding the number of divisors based on the prime factorization. If the prime factorization of a number \( n \) is \( p_1^{e_1} \times p_2^{e_2} \times \cdots \times p_k^{e_k} \), then the number of positive divisors of \( n \) is given by:
\[ (e_1 + 1)(e_2 + 1) \cdots (e_k + 1) \]

For \( 2002 = 2^1 \times 7^1 \times 11^1 \times 13^1 \), the exponents are all 1. Therefore, the number of positive divisors is:
\[ (1 + 1)(1 + 1)(1 + 1)(1 + 1) = 2 \times 2 \times 2 \times 2 = 16 \]
