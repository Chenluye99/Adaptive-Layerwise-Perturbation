To solve the problem, we need to use some properties of the circumcenter and dot products in vector geometry.

1. **Understanding the Problem:**
    - We have a triangle \(ABC\) with \(AB = 3\) and \(AC = 5\).
    - The circumcenter \(O\) of triangle \(ABC\) is the center of the circle that passes through all three vertices of the triangle.
    - We need to find \(\overrightarrow{OA} \cdot \overrightarrow{BC}\).

2. **Mathematical Background:**
    - Let's place the triangle in the coordinate system for convenience.
    - Suppose \(A = (x_A, y_A)\), \(B = (x_B, y_B)\), and \(C = (x_C, y_C)\).
    - The circumcenter \(O\) of the triangle is the intersection of the perpendicular bisectors of the sides of the triangle.
    - The vector \(\overrightarrow{OA} = (x_A - x_O, y_A - y_O)\) and \(\overrightarrow{BC} = (x_C - x_B, y_C - y_B)\).
    - To find \(\overrightarrow{OA} \cdot \overrightarrow{BC}\), we need to get the coordinates of \(O\).

3. **Using Properties of Circumcenter:**
    - One useful property is that for any point \(P\) in the plane of triangle \(ABC\) and the circumcenter \(O\), the following relation holds for dot products with \(\overrightarrow{BC}\):
      \[
      \overrightarrow{OA} \cdot \overrightarrow{BC} = \overrightarrow{OB} \cdot \overrightarrow{BC} = \overrightarrow{OC} \cdot \overrightarrow{BC}
      \]
      because \(O\) is equidistant from \(A, B,\) and \(C\), and because \(\overrightarrow{OA}, \overrightarrow{OB}, \overrightarrow{OC}\) are all perpendicular to the opposite sides when projected onto \(\overrightarrow{BC}\) in the context of circumcenter properties.

    - However, a more direct approach for solving this problem is using vector properties and identities involving the circumcenter and the dot product. A useful identity is that:
      \[
      \overrightarrow{OA} \cdot \overrightarrow{BC} = \frac{1}{2} [ ( \overrightarrow{OB} \cdot \overrightarrow{BC} ) + ( \overrightarrow{OC} \cdot \overrightarrow{BC} ) - ( \overrightarrow{OB} \cdot \overrightarrow{BC} ) ]
      \]

    - We utilize another property where for any point \(P\) and a set of points \(A, B, C\) on a circle with circumcenter \(O\), we get the identity:
      \[
      \overrightarrow{OA} \cdot \overrightarrow{BC} = \frac{1}{2} ( AB^2 - AC^2 )
      \]
      This stems from the formula for the circumcenter and properties of dot products in that context.

4. **Applying the Identity:**
    - Substituting the lengths \(AB = 3\) and \(AC = 5\) into the formula, we get:
      \[
      \overrightarrow{OA} \cdot \overrightarrow{BC} = \frac{1}{2} ( AB^2 - AC^2 ) = \frac{1}{2} ( 3^2 - 5^2 ) = \frac{1}{2} ( 9 - 25 ) = \frac{1}{2} ( -16 ) = -8
      \]
