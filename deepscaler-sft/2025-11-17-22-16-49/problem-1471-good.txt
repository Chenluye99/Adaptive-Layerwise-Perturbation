1. **Focus and Directrices of Parabolas:**
    - Both parabolas share the same focus at \((3, -28)\).
    - One parabola has its directrix as the \(x\)-axis (i.e., \(y = 0\)).
    - The other parabola has its directrix as the \(y\)-axis (i.e., \(x = 0\)).

2. **Formulating the Equations of Parabolas:**
    - For the parabola with directrix \(y = 0 \) (the \(x\)-axis), the definition of a parabola states that any point \((x, y)\) on the parabola is equidistant from the focus and the directrix. That is:
      \[
      \sqrt{(x - 3)^2 + (y + 28)^2} = |y|
      \]
      Squaring both sides gives:
      \[
      (x - 3)^2 + (y + 28)^2 = y^2
      \]
      Expanding and simplifying:
      \[
      (x - 3)^2 + y^2 + 56y + 784 = y^2 \implies (x - 3)^2 + 56y + 784 = 0 \implies (x - 3)^2 = -56y - 784
      \]
      \[
      (x - 3)^2 = -56(y + 14)
      \]
      This is the equation of the first parabola.

    - For the parabola with directrix \(x = 0 \) (the \(y\)-axis), any point \((x, y)\) on the parabola is equidistant from the focus and the directrix. That is:
      \[
      \sqrt{(x - 3)^2 + (y + 28)^2} = |x|

      \[

3. **Finding the Points of Intersection (Common Chord):**

      \[
      (x - 3)^2 = -56(y + 14)
      \]
      \[
      y^2 + 56y - 6x + 793 = 0
      \]

Let's solve this system of equations using Python to get the points of intersection. Then, we'll compute the slope of the line passing through these points.
