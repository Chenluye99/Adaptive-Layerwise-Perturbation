To solve this problem, we need to find the volume of the cube and then subtract the volume of the cylindrical section that is removed.

1. **Volume of the Cube:**
   The cube has a side length of 4 feet. The volume \( V_{\text{cube}} \) of a cube is given by:
   \[
   V_{\text{cube}} = \text{side length}^3 = 4^3 = 64 \text{ cubic feet}
   \]

2. **Volume of the Cylinder:**
   The cylindrical section that is removed from the cube has a radius of 2 feet and a height of 4 feet (same as the side length of the cube). The volume \( V_{\text{cylinder}} \) of a cylinder is given by:
   \[
   V_{\text{cylinder}} = \pi \cdot \text{radius}^2 \cdot \text{height} = \pi \cdot 2^2 \cdot 4 = \pi \cdot 4 \cdot 4 = 16\pi \text{ cubic feet}
   \]

3. **Remaining Volume:**
   The remaining volume of the cube after removing the cylindrical section is:
   \[
   V_{\text{remaining}} = V_{\text{cube}} - V_{\text{cylinder}} = 64 - 16\pi
   \]

Let's confirm this step-by-step calculation using Python to ensure accuracy.
```python
import sympy as sp

# Side length of the cube
side_length = 4

# Calculate the volume of the cube
volume_cube = side_length ** 3

# Radius and height of the cylinder
radius = 2
height = 4

# Calculate the volume of the cylinder
volume_cylinder = sp.pi * radius ** 2 * height

# Calculate the remaining volume
volume_remaining = volume_cube - volume_cylinder
print(volume_remaining)
```
Code execution result: 64 - 16*pi

Thus, the total remaining volume of the cube after the cylindrical section is removed is \(\boxed{64 - 16\pi}\) cubic feet. This matches our analytical solution.
