First, let's find all two-digit prime numbers and then determine if they are palindromic primes. A palindromic number reads the same forwards and backwards, so a two-digit palindromic number has the form \( \text{ab} \) where \( \text{a} = \text{b} \), meaning it is of the form \( \text{aa} \) (i.e., 11, 22, 33, 44, etc.). However, in the context of two-digit numbers, the only possible palindromic numbers are 11, 22, 33, 44, and 55 but only 11 meets the criteria of being a prime number in this context because 22, 33, 44, and 55 are not prime. So, 11 is the only two-digit number that is a prime and reads the same forwards and backwards. However, we are asked to find all palindromic primes less than 50 and ensure the checking process is thorough for accuracy. 

We will:

1. Generate all two-digit prime numbers.
2. Check if the reverse of each of these primes is also a prime number.
3. Ensure that all such primes are less than 50.
4. Sum up all such palindromic primes that meet the conditions.

Let's write Python code to ensure accuracy.
```python
import sympy

# Step 1: Generate all two-digit prime numbers
two_digit_primes = [num for num in range(10, 50) if sympy.isprime(num)]

# Step 2 and 3: Check if the reverse of each of these primes is also a prime number, and ensure the number is a two-digit number and less than 50
palindromic_primes = []

for prime in two_digit_primes:
    reversed_num = int(str(prime)[::-1])
    if sympy.isprime(reversed_num) and 10 <= reversed_num < 50:
        palindromic_primes.append(prime)

# Step 4: Sum up all such palindromic primes that meet the conditions
sum_of_palindromic_primes = sum(palindromic_primes)
print(sum_of_palindromic_primes)
```
Code execution result: 55

Thus, the sum of all palindromic primes less than 50 is \(\boxed{55}\). This result is obtained by ensuring that the prime numbers meet the conditions of being a palindrome and also being prime when their digits are reversed, all while being less than 50.
