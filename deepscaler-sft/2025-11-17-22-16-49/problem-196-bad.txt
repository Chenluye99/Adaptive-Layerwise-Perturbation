If a number \( n \) has the prime factorization \( n = p_1^{e_1} \cdot p_2^{e_2} \cdot \ldots \cdot p_k^{e_k} \), then the number of positive factors of \( n \) is given by \((e_1 + 1) \cdot (e_2 + 1) \cdot \ldots \cdot (e_k + 1)\).

We need to find the smallest \( n \) such that \((e_1 + 1) \cdot (e_2 + 1) \cdot \ldots \cdot (e_k + 1) = 12\). The number 12 can be factored into the following products:

1. \( 12 = 12 \) which corresponds to \( n = p_1^{11} \)
2. \( 12 = 6 \cdot 2 \) which corresponds to \( n = p_1^5 \cdot p_2^1 \)
3. \( 12 = 4 \cdot 3 \) which corresponds to \( n = p_1^3 \cdot p_2^2 \)
4. \( 12 = 3 \cdot 2 \cdot 2 \) which corresponds to \( n = p_1^2 \cdot p_2^1 \cdot p_3^1 \)

We will check each of these forms to find the smallest \( n \) by iterating through possible values of \( p_1, p_2, p_3, \ldots \) (starting with the smallest prime numbers, i.e., 2, 3, 5, etc.) and compute the corresponding \( n \).

import sympy

def count_factors(n):
    """Return the number of positive factors of n."""
    return len(sympy.divisors(n))

# We will check numbers sequentially and count their factors until we find the smallest number with exactly 12 factors.
n = 1
while True:
    if count_factors(n) == 12:
