First, we start with the formula for the volume of a right circular cone:

\[ V = \frac{1}{3} \pi r^2 h \]

where:
- \( V \) is the volume of the cone,
- \( r \) is the radius of the base of the cone,
- \( h \) is the height of the cone.

We are given:
- The volume \( V = 12 \pi \) cubic centimeters,
- The height \( h = 4 \) cm.

We substitute these values into the volume formula to find \( r \):

\[ 12 \pi = \frac{1}{3} \pi r^2 \cdot 4 \]

First, we can cancel \(\pi\) from both sides of the equation:

\[ 12 = \frac{1}{3} \cdot 4 \cdot r^2 \]

This simplifies to:

\[ 12 = \frac{4}{3} r^2 \]

Next, we solve for \( r^2 \) by multiplying both sides of the equation by 3:

\[ 12 \cdot 3 = 4 \cdot r^2 \]

\[ 36 = 4 \cdot r^2 \]

Then, we divide both sides by 4:

\[ r^2 = \frac{36}{4} = 9 \]

Taking the square root of both sides, we get:

\[ r = \sqrt{9} = 3 \]

Now that we have the radius \( r = 3 \) cm, we need to find the circumference of the base of the cone. The formula for the circumference \( C \) of a circle is:

\[ C = 2 \pi r \]

Substituting \( r = 3 \) into the formula, we get:

\[ C = 2 \pi \cdot 3 = 6 \pi \]

So, the circumference of the base of the cone is \( 6 \pi \) cm. Let's confirm this with Python to ensure accuracy.
```python
import sympy as sp

# Given values
V = 12 * sp.pi  # Volume of the cone
h = 4           # Height of the cone

# Define the symbol for the radius
r = sp.symbols('r', positive=True)

# Volume formula of the cone
volume_formula = (1/3) * sp.pi * r**2 * h

# Set the equation equal to the given volume and solve for r
equation = sp.Eq(volume_formula, V)
radius_solution = sp.solve(equation, r)[0]

# Calculate the circumference of the base of the cone
circumference = 2 * sp.pi * radius_solution
print(circumference)
```
Code execution result: 6.0*pi

Thus, the circumference of the base of the cone is \(\boxed{6 \pi}\) centimeters. This confirms that our solution is accurate.
