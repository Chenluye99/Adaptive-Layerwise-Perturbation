To solve this problem, we need to determine the area of the shaded region in the diagram.

1. **Understand the Problem:**
    - We have a larger circle with a radius of 6.
    - There are two smaller circles, each with a radius of 1 (because the smaller circles touch each other at the center of the larger circle and also touch the larger circle, implying that the diameter of each smaller circle is the radius of the larger circle, so each smaller circle has a radius of 3/3 = 1).
    - The shaded region is the area of the larger circle minus the areas of the two smaller circles.

2. **Calculate the Areas:**
    - The area of the larger circle is given by the formula \( \text{Area} = \pi \times \text{radius}^2 \). For the larger circle with a radius of 6, the area is \( \pi \times 6^2 = 36\pi \).
    - The area of one smaller circle is given by the formula \( \pi \times \text{radius}^2 \). For each smaller circle with a radius of 1, the area is \( \pi \times 1^2 = \pi \). Since there are two smaller circles, their combined area is \( 2 \times \pi = 2 \pi \).
    - The area of the shaded region is the area of the larger circle minus the combined area of the two smaller circles: \( 36\pi - 2\pi = 34\pi \).

3. **Write the Python Code to Confirm:**
```python
import sympy as sp

# Define the radii
R_large = 6
R_small = 1

# Calculate the areas
area_large_circle = sp.pi * R_large**2
area_small_circle = sp.pi * R_small**2

# There are two smaller circles, so we multiply the area of one smaller circle by 2
total_area_small_circles = 2 * area_small_circle

# Calculate the shaded area
area_shaded = area_large_circle - total_area_small_circles
print(area_shaded)
```
Code execution result: 34*pi

Thus, the area of the shaded region is \(\boxed{34 \pi}\). This is our final answer.
