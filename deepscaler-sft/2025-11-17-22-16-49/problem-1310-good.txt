To solve the problem, let's start by understanding the constraints and rules given:

1. **Car Length and Speed Relationship:**
    - Each car is 4 meters long.
    - For each car traveling at speed \( v \) km/h, the distance between the front of one car and the back of the car ahead is \(\left\lceil \frac{v}{15} \right\rceil \times 4\) meters, where \(\left\lceil \frac{v}{15} \right\rceil\) is the ceiling function that returns the smallest integer greater than or equal to \(\frac{v}{15}\).

2. **Distance Calculation:**
    - If a car is traveling at speed \( v \) km/h, the distance from the back of one car to the front of the next car is \(\left\lceil \frac{v}{15} \right\rceil \times 4\) meters. This includes the length of the preceding car, so the space from the front of one car to the front of the next car is \(\left\lceil \frac{v}{15} \right\rceil \times 4 + 4\) meters.

3. **Number of Cars Passing in One Hour:**
    - In one hour, a car traveling at speed \( v \) km/h will cover a distance of \( v \) km.
    - To determine how many cars can pass a certain point in one hour, we need to figure out how many car lengths (including the space in front) fit into the distance covered by a car traveling at speed \( v \) in one hour.

4. **Maximizing the Number of Cars:**
    - To maximize the number of cars passing a certain point in one hour, we should consider the speed that allows us to fit the most cars within the 1-hour interval.
    - Let's denote the number of cars that can pass the photoelectric eye in one hour as \( M \). For each car traveling at speed \( v \) km/h, the distance covered is \( v \) km, and the distance from the front of one car to the front of the next car is \(\left\lceil \frac{v}{15} \right\rceil \times 4 + 4 \) meters, or equivalently, \(\left\lceil \frac{v}{15} \right\rceil \times 4 + 4 = \frac{\left\lceil \frac{v}{15} \right\rceil \times 4}{1000} \) kilometers.

    - So, the number of cars that can pass a point in one hour is given by:
      \[
      M = \frac{v}{\frac{\left\lceil \frac{v}{15} \right\rceil \times 4}{1000} }
      \]
      Simplifying this, we get:
      \[
      M = \frac{v \cdot 1000}{ \left\lceil \frac{v}{15} \right\rceil \times 4}
      \]
      Or
      \[
      M = \frac{250 v}{ \left\lceil \frac{v}{15} \right\rceil }
      \]

    - To find the maximum \( M \), we need to find an optimal speed \( v \) that maximizes this expression. We can do this by iterating over different speeds and computing \( M \) for each speed.

Let's implement this in Python and find the value of \( M \) by iterating over a reasonable range of speeds and then find the quotient when the maximum \( M \) is divided by 10.
```python
import math

max_cars = 0

# We will check a reasonable range of speeds, for example, from 1 to 1000 km/h
for v in range(1, 1001):
    ceil_value = math.ceil(v / 15)
    distance_between_fronts = ceil_value * 4
    cars_passing_per_hour = v / distance_between_fronts * 1000
    
    if cars_passing_per_hour > max_cars:
        max_cars = int(cars_passing_per_hour)

print(max_cars)
```
