1. **Start with the given equation:**
   \[
   x^2 + 1 = 7x
   \]
   Rearrange this equation to standard quadratic form:
   \[
   x^2 - 7x + 1 = 0
   \]

2. **Find the solutions for \( x \) using the quadratic formula:**
   The quadratic formula is given by:
   \[
   x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}
   \]
   For the equation \( x^2 - 7x + 1 = 0 \), the coefficients are \( a = 1 \), \( b = -7 \), and \( c = 1 \). Plugging these into the quadratic formula, we get:
   \[
   x = \frac{-(-7) \pm \sqrt{(-7)^2 - 4 \cdot 1 \cdot 1}}{2 \cdot 1} = \frac{7 \pm \sqrt{49 - 4}}{2} = \frac{7 \pm \sqrt{45}}{2} = \frac{7 \pm 3\sqrt{5}}{2}
   \]
   So, the solutions for \( x \) are:
   \[
   x_1 = \frac{7 + 3\sqrt{5}}{2}, \quad x_2 = \frac{7 - 3\sqrt{5}}{2}
   \]

3. **Find the reciprocal of each solution:**
   For \( x_1 = \frac{7 + 3\sqrt{5}}{2} \), its reciprocal is:
   \[
   \frac{1}{x_1} = \frac{2}{7 + 3\sqrt{5}}
   \]
   For \( x_2 = \frac{7 - 3\sqrt{5}}{2} \), its reciprocal is:
   \[
   \frac{1}{x_2} = \frac{2}{7 - 3\sqrt{5}}
   \]

4. **Simplify the reciprocals:**
   To simplify, we rationalize the denominators by multiplying the numerator and the denominator by the conjugate of the denominator.

   For \( \frac{1}{x_1} = \frac{2}{7 + 3\sqrt{5}} \):
   \[
   \frac{2}{7 + 3\sqrt{5}} \cdot \frac{7 - 3\sqrt{5}}{7 - 3\sqrt{5}} = \frac{2 (7 - 3\sqrt{5})}{(7)^2 - (3\sqrt{5})^2} = \frac{2 (7 - 3\sqrt{5})}{49 - 45} = \frac{2 (7 - 3\sqrt{5})}{4} = \frac{7 - 3\sqrt{5}}{2}
   \]

   For \( \frac{1}{x_2} = \frac{2}{7 - 3\sqrt{5}} \):
   \[
   \frac{2}{7 - 3\sqrt{5}} \cdot \frac{7 + 3\sqrt{5}}{7 + 3\sqrt{5}} = \frac{2 (7 + 3\sqrt{5})}{(7)^2 - (3\sqrt{5})^2} = \frac{2 (7 + 3\sqrt{5})}{49 - 45} = \frac{2 (7 + 3\sqrt{5})}{4} = \frac{7 + 3\sqrt{5}}{2}
   \]

   We see that:
   \[
   \frac{1}{x_1} = \frac{7 - 3\sqrt{5}}{2} = x_2 \quad \text{and} \quad \frac{1}{x_2} = \frac{7 + 3\sqrt{5}}{2} = x_1
   \]

5. **Find the sum of \( x \) and its reciprocal:**
   For either \( x_1 \) or \( x_2 \), the sum of \( x \) and its reciprocal is:
   \[
   x + \frac{1}{x}
   \]
   We can use either \( x_1 \) or \( x_2 \) (as their reciprocals will be the other root), so let’s take \( x_1 = \frac{7 + 3 \sqrt{5}}{2} \) and its reciprocal \( \frac{7 - 3 \sqrt{5}}{2} \):

   Therefore, we have:
   \[
   x + \frac{1}{x} = \frac{7 + 3 \sqrt{5}}{2} + \frac{7 - 3 \sqrt{5}}{2} = \frac{(7 + 3 \sqrt{5}) + (7 - 3 \sqrt{5})}{2} = \frac{14}{2} = 7
   \]
