To solve the problem, we need to find how many positive integers less than 200 are divisible by 2, 3, and 5. A number that is divisible by 2, 3, and 5 must be divisible by their least common multiple (LCM). 

First, let's determine the LCM of 2, 3, and 5:

- The prime factorization of 2 is \(2\).
- The prime factorization of 3 is \(3\).
- The prime factorization of 5 is \(5\).

Since 2, 3, and 5 are all prime numbers, their LCM is simply their product:
\[ \text{LCM}(2, 3, 5) = 2 \times 3 \times 5 = 30 \]

So, we need to find how many numbers less than 200 are divisible by 30. To do this, we'll iterate through the numbers from 1 to 199 and check how many of them are divisible by 30.

Let's write some Python code to get the accurate count.
```python
# Initialize a counter
count = 0

# Check each number from 1 to 199 to see if it is divisible by 30
for num in range(1, 200):
    if num % 30 == 0:
        count += 1

print(count)
```
Code execution result: 6

Thus, the number of positive integers less than 200 that are divisible by 2, 3, and 5 is \(\boxed{6}\). This is our final answer.
