To solve this problem, we need to find the equation of the line that is tangent to both parabolas $y^2 = 4x$ and $x^2 = -32y$. 

1. **Formulate the Tangent Conditions:**
    - For the parabola $y^2 = 4x$, the general form of the tangent line is given by $y = mx + \frac{1}{m}$, where $m$ is the slope of the tangent line. This is derived from the fact that for a parabola of the form $y^2 = 4ax$, the tangent line at any point has this form.
    - For the parabola $x^2 = -32y$, we can use the general form for the tangent line to a parabola $x^2 = 4ay$, which is $y = \frac{x^2}{4a} - a$. For $x^2 = -32y$, we have $4a = -32$, so $a = -8$, and the tangent line equation takes the form $y = mx - \frac{m^2}{32}$.

2. **Equate the Tangent Forms:**
    - For the line to be tangent to both parabolas, its equation must match the above forms for both parabolas. So, we set $y = mx + \frac{1}{m}$ and $y = mx - \frac{m^2}{32}$ equal to each other, because they both represent the same line. This implies:
      \[
      \frac{1}{m} = -\frac{m^2}{32}
      \]

3. **Solve for \( m \):**
    - Rearrange and solve the equation \(\frac{1}{m} = -\frac{m^2}{32}\):
      \[
      1 = -\frac{m^3}{32} \implies m^3 = -32 \implies m = -2
      \]

4. **Find the Equation of the Tangent Line:**
    - Substitute \( m = -2 \) back into either of the tangent line forms. We'll use $y = mx + \frac{1}{m}$:
      \[
      y = -2x + \frac{1}{-2} \implies y = -2x - \frac{1}{2}
      \]

So, the equation of the line that is tangent to both parabolas is \( y = -2x - \frac{1}{2} \).
