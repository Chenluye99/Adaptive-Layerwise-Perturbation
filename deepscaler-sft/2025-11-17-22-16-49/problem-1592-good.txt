To solve for the focus of the parabola given by the equation \( y = -3x^2 - 6x \), we need to follow these steps:

1. **Rewrite the Equation in Standard Form:**
   The standard form of a parabola that opens up or down is \( (x - h)^2 = 4p(y - k) \), where \((h, k)\) is the vertex and \(p\) is the distance from the vertex to the focus.

   First, we need to rewrite the given equation \( y = -3x^2 - 6x \) in vertex form \( y = a(x - h)^2 + k \).

2. **Complete the Square:**
   To rewrite the equation, we complete the square for the quadratic expression \( -3x^2 - 6x \).
   
   Factor out \(-3\) from the quadratic and linear terms:
   \[
   y = -3(x^2 + 2x)
   \]
   To complete the square inside the parentheses, take the coefficient of \(x\), which is 2, divide it by 2 to get 1, and then square it to get 1. Add and subtract 1 inside the parentheses:
   \[
   y = -3(x^2 + 2x + 1 - 1) = -3((x + 1)^2 - 1) = -3(x + 1)^2 + 3
   \]
   So, the equation in vertex form is:
   \[
   y = -3(x + 1)^2 + 3
   \]
   Here, the vertex \((h, k)\) is \((-1, 3)\).

3. **Find the Focus:**
   For a parabola in the form \( y = a(x - h)^2 + k \), the distance \( p \) from the vertex to the focus is given by \( p = \frac{1}{4|a|} \).
   In our equation, \( a = -3 \), so:
   \[
   p = \frac{1}{4 \cdot | -3 |} = \frac{1}{12}
   \]
   Since the parabola opens downward (because \( a = -3 \) is negative), the focus will be located \( \frac{1}{12} \) units below the vertex. So, the focus has coordinates \((-1, 3 - \frac{1}{12}) = (-1, \frac{36}{12} - \frac{1}{12}) = (-1, \frac{35}{12}) \).
