To solve this problem, we need to use some properties of a triangle and trigonometry.

1. **Identify the Triangle Properties:**
    - The triangle has angles of 30 and 60 degrees, so the third angle is 90 degrees, making it a 30-60-90 triangle.
    - The altitude to the hypotenuse of a 30-60-90 triangle divides it into two smaller 30-60-90 triangles that are similar to the original triangle.
    - The altitude to the hypotenuse in a right triangle is given by the formula \( \text{altitude} = \frac{a \cdot b}{c} \), where \(a\) and \(b\) are the legs of the triangle, and \(c\) is the hypotenuse.

2. **Formulating the Triangle:**
    - In a 30-60-90 triangle, the sides are in the ratio \(1 : \sqrt{3} : 2\). We can denote the sides of the original triangle as \(a, a \sqrt{3}, \text{and } 2a \), where \(2a\) is the hypotenuse.
    - The altitude to the hypotenuse of this triangle is 3 units. For a right triangle, the altitude to the hypotenuse can also be given by the formula \( \text{altitude} = \frac{a \cdot b}{c} \), but for a 30-60-90 triangle, it can be simplified in another way. However, it is also known that in any right triangle, the altitude to the hypotenuse creates two smaller right triangles that are similar to the original triangle and to each other.

3. **Find Relationships:**
    - For a 30-60-90 right triangle, if we drop the altitude to the hypotenuse, it will bisect the 30-degree and 60-degree angles and will create two smaller 30-60-90 triangles. The length of this altitude can be derived as \(\frac{a \cdot b}{c}\) but in the context of a 30-60-90 triangle, it is also related to the properties of the triangle. Specifically, for a 30-60-90 triangle, if the shorter leg is \(a\), then the hypotenuse is \(2a\) and the longer leg is \(a \sqrt{3}\). The altitude from the right angle to the hypotenuse divides the hypotenuse into segments of lengths proportional to the legs.

4. **Use the Given Altitude:**
    - For a 30-60-90 triangle, if the altitude to the hypotenuse is 3, it is known that this altitude for such a triangle can be related to the shorter leg of the smaller 30-60-90 triangle created by dropping the altitude.
    - For a 30-60-90 triangle, if the shorter leg is \(a\), the hypotenuse is \(2a\), and the altitude to the hypotenuse will be \( \frac{a \cdot a \sqrt{3}}{2a} = \frac{a \sqrt{3}}{2} \), but this formulation isn't directly what we use when the altitude is given and needs another approach for verification.

    However, if we consider a right triangle formed by dropping the altitude to the hypotenuse in a more direct way for a 30-60-90 triangle, the relationship simplifies as follows:

    - In a 30-60-90 triangle, if we drop the altitude to the hypotenuse, it forms two smaller right triangles, and the altitude itself creates a relationship in terms of the sides of the original triangle that simplifies the setup.
    
    - Given the altitude to the hypotenuse is 3, we know that if we position it correctly, using properties of 30-60-90 triangles that fit standard proportions, the setup aligns that the altitude of 3 implies a connection that matches a specific proportional setup of side lengths leading to \(a = 2 \sqrt{3}\), for instance.

5. **Setup and Find Side Lengths:**
    - A more straightforward approach considers that the length derived fits into known proportional setups related to geometric properties of 30-60-90 triangles, where specific lengths satisfy \( \tan(30^\circ) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}} \) and \( \tan(60^\circ) = \sqrt{3} \), particularly marking out key configurations allowing for direct relations like segment divisions forming other related triangles helping aligning proper lengths setup verifying those configurations and outputs which tend to give lengths fit configurations fitting longer leg= \(a \sqrt{3}\), shorter leg = \(a\), and hypotenuse = \(2a\).

To resolve this problem, we can follow these steps:

1. **Identify the triangle and properties:**
   - A 30-60-90 triangle has sides in the ratio \(1 : \sqrt{3} : 2\). Suppose the shorter leg of the 30-60-90 triangle is \(a\), then the longer leg is \(a \sqrt{3}\), and the hypotenuse is \(2a\).
   
2. **Find the Altitude Relationship:**
   - The altitude to the hypotenuse of a right triangle can also be calculated as \(\frac{a \cdot b}{c}\) where \(a\) and \(b\) are the legs of the triangle and \(c\) is the hypotenuse. For a 30-60-90 triangle with sides \(a, a \sqrt{3}, 2a\), this altitude to the hypotenuse would be given as a length of 3 units as mentioned in the problem.

3. **Establish equations based on altitude relationship:**
   - In a right triangle, the altitude to the hypotenuse can also be given by the product of the lengths of the segments created on the hypotenuse divided by the length of the hypotenuse.
