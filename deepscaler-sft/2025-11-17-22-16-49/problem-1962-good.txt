First, let's start by solving the equation \(-4x^2 + 7x + 2 = 0\) to find the roots. The roots of this quadratic equation will help us determine the intervals to test for the inequality \(-4x^2 + 7x + 2 < 0\).

We can use the quadratic formula \(x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}\), where \(a = -4\), \(b = 7\), and \(c = 2\).

Substitute these values into the quadratic formula:

\[
x = \frac{-7 \pm \sqrt{7^2 - 4(-4)(2)}}{2(-4)} = \frac{-7 \pm \sqrt{49 + 32}}{-8} = \frac{-7 \pm \sqrt{81}}{-8} = \frac{-7 \pm 9}{-8}
\]

This will give us two solutions:

\[
x = \frac{-7 + 9}{-8} = \frac{2}{-8} = -\frac{1}{4}
\]
\[
x = \frac{-7 - 9}{-8} = \frac{-16}{-8} = 2
\]

So, the roots of the equation \(-4x^2 + 7x + 2 = 0\) are \(x = -\frac{1}{4}\) and \(x = 2\). These roots divide the number line into three intervals: \((-\infty, -\frac{1}{4})\), \((- \frac{1}{4}, 2)\), and \((2, \infty)\).

Therefore, the expression will be negative outside the interval between the roots \(-\frac{1}{4}\) and \(2\).
