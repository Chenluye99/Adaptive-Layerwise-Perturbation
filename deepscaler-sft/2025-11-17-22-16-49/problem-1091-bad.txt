To solve this problem, we need to determine when Jaclyn, Marcelle, Susanna, and Wanda will next be working together. To do this, we need to find the least common multiple (LCM) of the intervals at which they work. Specifically, Jaclyn works every 3 days, Marcelle works every 4 days, Susanna works every 6 days, and Wanda works every 7 days.

The LCM of these numbers (3, 4, 6, 7) will give us the number of school days from today when they will all be working together again.

First, let's compute the LCM of 3, 4, 6, and 7.

1. **Prime Factorization:**
   - \(3 = 3\)
   - \(4 = 2^2\)
   - \(6 = 2 \times 3\)
   - \(7 = 7\)

2. **Finding LCM:**
   - The LCM is found by taking the highest power of each prime that appears in the factorization of any of the numbers.
   - The primes involved are 2, 3, and 7.
     - The highest power of 2 in the list is \(2^2\) (from 4).
     - The highest power of 3 in the list is \(3^1\) (from 3 and 6).
     - The highest power of 7 in the list is \(7^1\) (from 7).

   - Therefore, the LCM is \(2^2 \times 3^1 \times 7^1 = 4 \times 3 \times 7 = 84\).

So, the least common multiple of 3, 4, 6, and 7 is 84. This means that Jaclyn, Marcelle, Susanna, and Wanda will all be working together again in 84 school days.

Let's confirm this by using Python to compute it programmatically.
```python
import math

# Function to compute LCM of two numbers
def lcm(a, b):
    return abs(a * b) // math.gcd(a, b)

# List of intervals
intervals = [3, 4, 6, 7]

# Compute LCM of the list of intervals
lcmmm = intervals[0]
for i in range(1, len(intervals)):
    lcmmm = lcm(lcmmm, intervals[i])

print(lcmmm)
```
Code execution result: 84
