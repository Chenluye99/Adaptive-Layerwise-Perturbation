To solve this problem, let's start by understanding the mathematical properties of projection and reflection matrices.

1. **Projection Matrix (P):**
   The projection matrix \(\mathbf{P}\) that projects any vector \(\mathbf{x}\) onto a vector \(\mathbf{v}\) is given by:
   \[
   \mathbf{P} = \frac{\mathbf{v} \mathbf{v}^T}{\mathbf{v}^T \mathbf{v}}
   \]
   Here, \(\mathbf{v} \mathbf{v}^T\) is the outer product of \(\mathbf{v}\) with itself, and \(\mathbf{v}^T \mathbf{v}\) is the dot product of \(\mathbf{v}\) with itself (which is a scalar).

2. **Reflection Matrix (R):**
   The reflection matrix \(\mathbf{R}\) that reflects any vector \(\mathbf{x}\) over the vector \(\mathbf{v}\) is given by:
   \[
   \mathbf{R} = 2 \mathbf{P} - \mathbf{I}
   \]
   Here, \(\mathbf{I}\) is the identity matrix.

By substituting the expression for \(\mathbf{P}\) into the expression for \(\mathbf{R}\), we get:
\[
\mathbf{R} = 2 \left( \frac{\mathbf{v} \mathbf{v}^T}{\mathbf{v}^T \mathbf{v}} \right) - \mathbf{I}
\]
This can be rewritten as:
\[
\mathbf{R} = \frac{2 \mathbf{v} \mathbf{v}^T}{\mathbf{v}^T \mathbf{v}} - \mathbf{I}
\]
We want to express \(\mathbf{R}\) in the form \(\mathbf{R} = a \mathbf{P} + b \mathbf{I}\). By comparing this with our expression for \(\mathbf{R}\), we see:
\[
\mathbf{R} = 2 \mathbf{P} - \mathbf{I}
\]
It is clear that:
\[
a = 2 \quad \text{and} \quad b = -1
\]

Thus, the ordered pair \((a, b)\) is \((2, -1)\).
