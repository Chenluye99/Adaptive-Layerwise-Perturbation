First, let's translate the given conditions into mathematical equations that we can solve using Python.

1. **First Condition:**
   - A point \((x, y)\) is a distance of 12 units from the \(x\)-axis. The distance from a point \((x, y)\) to the \(x\)-axis is the absolute value of its \(y\)-coordinate. This gives us:
     \[
     |y| = 12 \implies y = 12 \quad \text{or} \quad y = -12
     \]

2. **Second Condition:**
   - The point \((x, y)\) is a distance of 10 units from the point \((1, 6)\). The distance between \((x, y)\) and \((1, 6)\) is given by:
     \[
     \sqrt{(x - 1)^2 + (y - 6)^2} = 10
     \]
     Squaring both sides, we get:
     \[
     (x - 1)^2 + (y - 6)^2 = 100
     \]

3. **Third Condition:**
   - The point \((x, y)\) is a distance \(n\) from the origin. The distance from \((x, y)\) to the origin is given by:
     \[
     \sqrt{x^2 + y^2} = n
     \]
     Squaring both sides, we get:
     \[
     x^2 + y^2 = n^2
     \]

We now have a system of equations to solve:

1. \( y = 12 \) or \( y = -12 \)
2. \((x - 1)^2 + (y - 6)^2 = 100\)
3. \( x^2 + y^2 = n^2 \)

We'll solve this step-by-step by plugging in the possible values of \( y \) and solving for \( x \) and subsequently \( n \).

Let's start with \( y = 12 \):

\[
(x - 1)^2 + (12 - 6)^2 = 100 \implies (x - 1)^2 + 6^2 = 100 \implies (x - 1)^2 + 36 = 100 \implies (x - 1)^2 = 64 \implies x - 1 = 8 \text{ or } x - 1 = -8
\]

So, we get:

\[
x - 1 = 8 \implies x = 9
\]
\[
x - 1 = -8 \implies x = -7
\]

Since we are given that \( x > 1 \), we can discard \( x = -7 \) and keep \( x = 9 \).

Now, let's check if \( y = -12 \) gives any valid solutions:

\[
(x - 1)^2 + (-12 - 6)^2 = 100 \implies (x - 1)^2 + (-18)^2 = 100 \implies (x - 1)^2 + 324 = 100 \implies (x - 1)^2 = -224
\]

Since \((x - 1)^2 = -224\) is not possible (as a square of a real number cannot be negative), there are no valid solutions for \( y = -12 \).

So, the only valid solution from the above is \( x = 9 \) and \( y = 12 \). Now, we substitute these values into the equation \( x^2 + y^2 = n^2 \) to find \( n \):

\[
n^2 = 9^2 + 12^2 = 81 + 144 = 225 \implies n = \sqrt{225} = 15
\]

import sympy as sp

# Define variables

eq1 = (x - 1)**2 + (y - 6)**2 - 100
eq2_pos = y - 12
eq2_neg = y + 12

# Solve for (x, y) with y = 12
solution_pos = sp.solve([eq1, eq2_pos], (x, y))
# Solve for (x, y) with y = -12
solution_neg = sp.solve([eq1, eq2_neg], (x, y))

# Filter solutions where x > 1
valid_solutions = []
for sol in solution_pos + solution_neg:
    if sol[0] > 1:
        valid_solutions.append(sol)

# Calculate n for valid (x, y) solutions
n_values = []

    x_val, y_val = sol

print(n_values)
