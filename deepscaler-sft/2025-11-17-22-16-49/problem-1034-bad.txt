To solve the problem, we need to rationalize the denominator of \(\frac{3}{2 \sqrt[3]{5}}\) and express it in the form \(\frac{A \sqrt[3]{B}}{C}\).

1. **Understand the Problem:**
    - We have the expression \(\frac{3}{2 \sqrt[3]{5}}\).
    - To rationalize the denominator, we need to get rid of the cube root in the denominator.

2. **Rationalize the Denominator:**
    - We can do this by multiplying both the numerator and the denominator by \(\sqrt[3]{5^2}\) (which is \(\sqrt[3]{25}\)), because \(\sqrt[3]{5} \cdot \sqrt[3]{25} = \sqrt[3]{5^3} = 5\).
    - So, we get:
      \[
      \frac{3}{2 \sqrt[3]{5}} \cdot \frac{\sqrt[3]{25}}{\sqrt[3]{25}} = \frac{3 \sqrt[3]{25}}{2 \cdot 5} = \frac{3 \sqrt[3]{25}}{10}
      \]
    - This gives us the expression in the form \(\frac{A \sqrt[3]{B}}{C}\), where \(A = 3\), \(B = 25\), and \(C = 10\).

3. **Check Conditions:**
    - We need to ensure that \(B = 25\) is not divisible by the cube of any prime. The prime factorization of 25 is \(5^2\), and since 25 is not divisible by \(5^3 = 125\), it meets the condition.

4. **Find \(A + B + C\):**
    - With \(A = 3\), \(B = 25\), and \(C = 10\), we can compute \(A + B + C = 3 + 25 + 10 = 38\).
