To solve this problem, let's simulate the movements of both the snail and the person step-by-step using Python.

1. **Initial Setup:**
    - Initially, the snail is 1 meter (100 cm) away from the starting pole.
    - The snail moves away from the pole at a speed of \(1 \text{ cm/s}\).

2. **Movement Simulation:**
    - For each round \( n \):
        - The person will start a round at the beginning of round \( n+1 \).
        - During round \( n \), first, the person moves directly toward the snail at a speed of \( n+1 \text{ cm/s} \).
        - Let's say it takes time \( t_1 \) for the person to reach the snail. The distance between the person and the snail decreases at a rate of \( (n+1) - 1 = n \text{ cm/s} \) while the snail is moving at 1 cm/s away. Therefore, the relative speed at which the person approaches the snail is \( n - 1 \text{ cm/s} \).
        - So, the time \( t_1 \) it takes for the person to reach the snail is given by:
          \[
          t_1 = \frac{\text{distance between person and snail at start of round } n}{n}
          \]
          Initially, this distance is the distance covered by the snail since the start.
        - Once the person reaches the snail, they immediately turn around and move back to the starting pole at the same speed \( n+1 \text{ cm/s} \). Let's say it takes time \( t_2 \) for the person to reach the pole again. The distance covered by the snail in the time it takes the person to move back is \( 1 \cdot t_2 = t_2 \text{ cm} \).
        - The person then moves back to the starting point at \( n+1 \text{ cm/s} \), so the time it takes for the person to reach the starting pole is:
          \[
          t_2 = \frac{\text{the distance traveled by snail during } t_1 + t_2}{n+1}
          \]
        - When the person reaches the pole, they immediately start the next round.

3. **Iterative Calculation:**
    - We need to repeat this process for each round, incrementing the round number and updating the distance between the person and the snail until we reach round 100.

Let's implement this step-by-step simulation in Python to determine the distance of the snail from the pole at the start of round 100.
```python
import sympy as sp

# Initialize variables
distance_snail_from_pole = 100  # Initial distance of the snail from the pole in cm
current_round = 1

while current_round < 100:
    # Move towards the snail at speed (current_round + 1) until we catch up with the snail
    # The snail is moving away from the pole at 1 cm/s, so its position after time t is distance_snail_from_pole + t
    # We are moving towards the snail at (current_round + 1) cm/s, so we need to solve for t such that we meet the snail
    
    # Let's denote the time taken to catch up as t1. During this time, the snail travels an additional t1 cm.
    # The distance between person and snail will reduce at a rate of (current_round + 1 - 1) = current_round cm/s.
    t1 = distance_snail_from_pole / current_round
    
    # After meeting, we move back to the pole at the same speed, and while the person is moving back, the snail continues to move away.
    # The distance covered by the snail while the person is moving back to the pole is 1 * t2
    # The person's speed back is the same as current_round + 1, so the distance the person covers while moving back in time t2 is (current_round + 1) * t2.
    # After time t2, the distance between the person and the pole is 0, which means the person reached back to the starting pole, and it's time for the next round.
    t2 = distance_snail_from_pole / (current_round + 1)
    
    # After reaching the starting pole, the snail would have moved an additional t2 cm further.
    distance_snail_from_pole += t2

    # Move to the next round
    current_round += 1

print(distance_snail_from_pole)
```
