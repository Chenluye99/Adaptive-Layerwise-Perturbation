To solve the problem, let's follow these steps:

1. **Define the Problem:**
    - We start with 100 numbers, let's call them \(a_1, a_2, \ldots, a_{100}\).

2. **First Increase:**
    - Each of the 100 numbers is increased by 1 the first time. So, the new numbers become \(a_1+1, a_2+1, \ldots, a_{100}+1\).
    - The sum of the squares of the new numbers after the first increase is:
      \[
      S_1 = (a_1 + 1)^2 + (a_2 + 1)^2 + \ldots + (a_{100} + 1)^2
      \]
    - We know that the sum of the squares of the numbers \(S_1\) is not the same as \(S_0\), but it will become the same after the second increase.

3. **Second Increase:**
    - Each of the numbers is increased by 1 again, so the new numbers become \(a_1 + 2, a_2 + 2, \ldots, a_{100} + 2\).
    - The sum of the squares of these new numbers after the second increase is:
      \[
      S_2 = (a_1 + 2)^2 + (a_2 + 2)^2 + \ldots + (a_{100} + 2)^2
      \]

\[

\[
S_0 = \sum_{i=1}^{100} a_i^2
\]

\[
S_1 = \sum_{i=1}^{100} a_i^2 + 2 \sum_{i=1}^{100} a_i + 100
\]

After the second increase, the sum of the squares is:

\[
S_2 = \sum_{i=1}^{100} a_i^2 + 4 \sum_{i=1}^{100} a_i + 400
\]

We are given that \(S_1 \neq S_0\) but \(S_2 = S_0 + \Delta\), where \(\Delta\) is the change in the sum of the squares that we need to find.

So, the difference between \(S_2\) and \(S_0\) is:

\[
\Delta = S_2 - S_0 = \left( \sum_{i=1}^{100} a_i^2 + 4 \sum_{i=1}^{100} a_i + 400 \right) - \sum_{i=1}^{100} a_i^2 = 4 \sum_{i=1}^{100} a_i + 400
