### Part (I): Find angle \( A \)

We are given that in triangle \( ABC \), the sides opposite to angles \( A \), \( B \), and \( C \) are \( a \), \( b \), and \( c \) respectively, and that the relationship \( c = a \cos B + b \sin A \) holds. We will use the Law of Cosines and the Law of Sines to solve for angle \( A \).

1. **Use the Law of Cosines and Law of Sines:**
    - Law of Cosines: \(\cos B = \frac{a^2 + c^2 - b^2}{2ac}\)
    - Law of Sines: \(\frac{a}{\sin A} = \frac{b}{\sin B} = \frac{c}{\sin C} = 2R\), so \( b = 2R \sin B \) and \( c = 2R \sin C \).

2. **Substitute into the Given Equation:**
    - Substitute \(\cos B = \frac{a^2 + c^2 - b^2}{2ac}\) and express \( c = a \cos B + b \sin A \):
      \[
      c = a \cdot \frac{a^2 + c^2 - b^2}{2ac} + b \sin A
      \]
    - Multiply through by \( 2ac \):
      \[
      2c^2 = (a^2 + c^2 - b^2) + 2ac \cdot b \sin A
      \]
    - Rearrange to isolate the trigonometric term:
      \[
      2c^2 - a^2 - c^2 + b^2 = 2ac \cdot b \sin A \implies c^2 + b^2 - a^2 = 2ac \cdot b \sin A
      \]
    - Use the Law of Cosines again on the left side, \( \cos A = \frac{b^2 + c^2 - a^2}{2bc} \), so:
      \[
      c^2 + b^2 - a^2 = 2ac \cdot b \sin A \implies 2bc \cos A = 2ac \cdot b \sin A \implies c \cos A = c \sin A \implies \cos A = \sin A
      \]
    - This implies:
      \[
      \tan A = 1 \implies A = \frac{\pi}{4} = 45^\circ
      \]

### Part (II): Find the Maximum Area of \(\triangle ABC\) when \(a = 2\)

1. **Express the Area of \(\triangle ABC\):**
    - The area of a triangle is given by \(\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2}bc \sin A\). Since \( A = 45^\circ \), we have \(\sin 45^\circ = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\), so:
      \[
      \text{Area} = \frac{1}{2} bc \cdot \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{4} bc
      \]
    - We also use the Law of Cosines for \( a = 2 \):
      \[
      a^2 = b^2 + c^2 - 2bc \cos A \implies 4 = b^2 + c^2 - 2bc \cdot \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} \implies 4 = b^2 + c^2 - \sqrt{2} bc
      \]

2. **Maximize the Area:**
    - To maximize \(\text{Area} = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{4} bc\), we need to maximize the product \( bc \) under the constraint \( 4 = b^2 + c^2 - \sqrt{2} bc \).
    - We can solve this using SymPy by setting up the constraint and maximize the product \( bc \).

Let's implement this step-by-step in Python:

```python
import sympy as sp

# Define variables
b, c = sp.symbols('b c', positive=True)

# Define the constraint: 4 = b^2 + c^2 - sqrt(2)*bc
constraint = b**2 + c**2 - sp.sqrt(2)*b*c - 4

# Use Lagrange multipliers to maximize bc under this constraint
λ = sp.symbols('λ')
L = b * c - λ * (b**2 + c**2 - sp.sqrt(2)*b*c - 4)

# Take partial derivatives of L with respect to b, c, and λ, and set them to 0
partial_b = sp.diff(L, b)
partial_c = sp.diff(L, c)
partial_λ = sp.diff(L, λ)

# Solve the system of equations formed by setting the partial derivatives to 0
solutions = sp.solve([partial_b, partial_c, partial_λ], (b, c, λ))

# Find the maximum area by evaluating the area expression for each solution
max_area = 0
for sol in solutions:
    b_val, c_val, _ = sol
    area = (sp.sqrt(2) / 4) * b_val * c_val
    if area > max_area:
        max_area = area
```
