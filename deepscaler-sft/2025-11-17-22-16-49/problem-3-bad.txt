1. **Understand the Problem:**
    - We have two infinite geometric series.
    - The first geometric series has a first term \( a_1 = 15 \) and a second term \( a_2 = 5 \).
    - The common ratio \( r_1 \) of the first series can be calculated as:
      \[
      r_1 = \frac{a_2}{a_1} = \frac{5}{15} = \frac{1}{3}
      \]
    - The sum \( S_1 \) of an infinite geometric series is given by \( S = \frac{a}{1 - r} \) where \( |r| < 1 \). So, the sum of the first series is:
      \[
      S_1 = \frac{15}{1 - \frac{1}{3}} = \frac{15}{\frac{2}{3}} = 15 \cdot \frac{3}{2} = \frac{45}{2}
      \]
    - The second geometric series has the same first term \( a_1 = 15 \) and a second term \( a_2 = 5 + n \). The common ratio \( r_2 \) of the second series is:
      \[
      r_2 = \frac{a_2}{a_1} = \frac{5 + n}{15} = \frac{5 + n}{15}
      \]
    - The sum \( S_2 \) of the second series is:
      \[
      S_2 = \frac{15}{1 - r_2} = \frac{15}{1 - \frac{5 + n}{15}} = \frac{15}{\frac{15 - (5 + n)}{15}} = \frac{15 \cdot 15}{15 - 5 - n} = \frac{225}{10 - n}
      \]
    - According to the problem, the sum of the second series is three times the sum of the first series:
      \[
      S_2 = 3 \cdot S_1
      \]
      Substituting the values of \( S_1 \) and \( S_2 \):
      \[
      \frac{225}{10 - n} = 3 \cdot \frac{45}{2}
      \]
      Simplify the right-hand side:
      \[
      3 \cdot \frac{45}{2} = \frac{135}{2}
      \]
      So we have:
      \[
      \frac{225}{10 - n} = \frac{135}{2}
      \]

2. **Set Up the Equation and Solve for \( n \):**
    - Cross-multiply to get rid of the fractions:
      \[
      225 \cdot 2 = 135 \cdot (10 - n)
      \]
      \[
      450 = 1350 - 135n
      \]
    - Rearrange to isolate \( n \):
      \[
      450 - 1350 = -135n

      \[

      \[
      900 = 135n
      \]
      \[
      n = \frac{900}{135} = \frac{900 \div 45}{135 \div 45} = \frac{20}{3}
