To solve the problem, let's define the cost of a muffin as \( m \) and the cost of a banana as \( b \).

From the problem, we have the following information:

1. Susie pays for 4 muffins and 3 bananas:
   \[
   \text{Cost for Susie} = 4m + 3b
   \]

2. Calvin spends twice as much as Susie for 2 muffins and 16 bananas:
   \[
   \text{Cost for Calvin} = 2 \cdot (4m + 3b) = 2 \cdot (2m + 16b) 
   \]

   Simplifying, we get:
   \[
   2 \cdot (4m + 3b) = 2 \cdot (2m + 16b)
   \]

   Which means:
   \[
   8m + 6b = 4m + 32b
   \]

3. Equate and solve for \( m \) and \( b \):
   \[
   8m + 6b = 4m + 32b
   \]
   \[
   8m - 4m = 32b - 6b
   \]
   \[
   4m = 26b
   \]
   \[
   2m = 13b
   \]
   \[
   m = \frac{13}{2} b
   \]

Hence, a muffin is \(\frac{13}{2}\) times as expensive as a banana. Let's verify this by coding the solution to ensure it is accurate.
```python
from sympy import symbols, Eq, solve

# Define the symbols
m, b = symbols('m b')

# Set up the equation based on the problem description

# Solve for m in terms of b

print(solution)
```
