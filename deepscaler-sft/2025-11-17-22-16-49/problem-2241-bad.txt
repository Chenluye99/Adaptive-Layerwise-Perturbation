To solve the problem, we need to find the area of the annulus, which is the region between the two concentric circles with radii \( b \) and \( c \) where \( b > c \). The area of the annulus is given by the difference in the areas of the two circles:

\[
\text{Area of the annulus} = \pi b^2 - \pi c^2 = \pi (b^2 - c^2)
\]

We need to express this area in terms of at most one of the variables \( a, b, c, d, e \). To do this, we need to relate the variables \( a, b, c, d, e \) to each other.

1. **Identify Relationships:**
    - \( XZ = a \) is the length of the tangent from \( X \) to the smaller circle, tangent to it at \( Z \).
    - \( YZ = d \) is the vertical distance from \( Y \) to \( Z \).
    - \( XY = e \) is the distance between points \( X \) and \( Y \).

2. **Geometric Relationships:**
    - Point \( O \) is the center of both circles.
    - Point \( X \) is on the larger circle, so its coordinates in a coordinate system with \( O \) at the origin could be \( (x_1, y_1) \) such that its distance from \( O \) is \( b \).
    - Point \( Z \) is on the smaller circle, so its coordinates could be \( (0, z_1) \) such that its distance from \( O \) is \( c \).
    - Point \( Y \) is on the larger circle and contains \( Z \), so its coordinates could be \( (0, y_2) \) such that its distance from \( O \) is \( b \).

3. **Using Geometry of Tangent:**
    - For a tangent from a point outside a circle, the length of the tangent segment (from \( X \) to the point of tangency \( Z \)) can be calculated using right triangle properties. Since \( \overline{OX} \) is a radius of the larger circle and \( \overline{XZ} \) is tangent to the smaller circle at \( Z \), \( \overline{OX} \perp \overline{OZ} \).

    - The length of \( XZ \) can be given by the formula for the length of a tangent to a circle from an external point:

    \[
    a = XZ = \sqrt{OX^2 - OZ^2} = \sqrt{b^2 - c^2}
    \]

4. **Other Variables:**
    - The distance \( YZ = d \) is the vertical distance between \( Y \) and \( Z \). Since both \( Y \) and \( Z \) have the same x-coordinate (0), this is just the difference in their y-coordinates: \( d = |b - c| \).
    - \( XY = e \) is the distance between points \( X \) and \( Y \). Given the coordinates, \( X = (x_1, y_1) \) and \( Y = (0, y_2) \), the distance \( e = XY \) is given by:

    \[
    e = \sqrt{(x_1 - 0)^2 + (y_1 - y_2)^2} = \sqrt{x_1^2 + (y_1 - y_2)^2}
    \]

However, to express the area of the annulus in terms of at most one variable, it's evident that \( a = \sqrt{b^2 - c^2} \). Therefore, we can substitute \( b^2 - c^2 \) with \( a^2 \).

So the area of the annulus can be written as:

\[
\pi (b^2 - c^2) = \pi a^2
\]

Hence, the area of the annulus is \(\boxed{\pi a^2}\). This expresses the area of the annulus solely in terms of the variable \( a \).
