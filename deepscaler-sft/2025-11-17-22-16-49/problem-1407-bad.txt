To solve this problem, let's start by understanding the properties of a regular hexagon.

1. **Properties of a Regular Hexagon:**
    - A regular hexagon can be divided into 6 equilateral triangles.
    - Each side of the hexagon is of the same length, say \( a \).

2. **Diagonals of a Regular Hexagon:**
    - The shorter length of a diagonal in a regular hexagon spans two sides of the hexagon (i.e., it connects every second vertex).
    - The longer length of a diagonal in a regular hexagon spans three sides of the hexagon (i.e., it connects every third vertex).

3. **Lengths of the Diagonals:**
    - The shorter length of the diagonal is equal to the side of the hexagon, i.e., \( a \).
    - The longer length of the diagonal is the same as the distance across two of these equilateral triangles, which is \( 2 \cdot a \cdot \cos(30^\circ) = 2 \cdot a \cdot \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = a \cdot \sqrt{3} \).

Thus, the two possible lengths of the diagonals of a regular hexagon are \( a \) and \( a \cdot \sqrt{3} \).

4. **Finding the Ratio:**
    - The ratio of the shorter length to the longer length is given by:
      \[
      \frac{a}{a \cdot \sqrt{3}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}
      \]
    - To rationalize the denominator, we multiply the numerator and the denominator by \( \sqrt{3} \):
      \[
      \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}} \cdot \frac{\sqrt{3}}{\sqrt{3}} = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{3}
      \]

Thus, the ratio of the shorter length to the longer length of the diagonals of a regular hexagon is \(\frac{\sqrt{3}}{3}\).
