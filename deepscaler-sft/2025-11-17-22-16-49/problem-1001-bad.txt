\[
\sum_{k=0}^n \log_2 \left( 1 + \frac{1}{2^{2^k}} \right) \ge 1 + \log_2 \frac{2014}{2015}.
\]

Let's break this down step-by-step:

1. **Evaluate the Right Hand Side (RHS):**
   \[
   1 + \log_2 \frac{2014}{2015} = \log_2 2 + \log_2 \frac{2014}{2015} = \log_2 \left( 2 \cdot \frac{2014}{2015} \right) = \log_2 \frac{4028}{2015}.
   \]

2. **Set Up the Left Hand Side (LHS):**
   We need to compute the sum on the left hand side:
   \[
   \sum_{k=0}^n \log_2 \left( 1 + \frac{1}{2^{2^k}} \right).
   \]
   Using properties of logarithms, we can combine the sum of logarithms into a single logarithm:
   \[
   \sum_{k=0}^n \log_2 \left( 1 + \frac{1}{2^{2^k}} \right) = \log_2 \left( \prod_{k=0}^n \left( 1 + \frac{1}{2^{2^k}} \right) \right).
   \]

3. **Iterative Calculation:**
   We'll iterate through values of \( n \) starting from 1, compute the product inside the logarithm, and check if it is greater than or equal to \(\frac{4028}{2015}\).

import math

# Calculate the right-hand side of the inequality
