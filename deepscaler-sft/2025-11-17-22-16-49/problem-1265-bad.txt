We will start by using trigonometric identities and properties of a triangle. For a triangle with sides \(a, b, c\) and opposite angles \(\alpha, \beta, \gamma\), we know:

\[
\cot \alpha = \frac{\cos \alpha}{\sin \alpha}, \quad \cot \beta = \frac{\cos \beta}{\sin \beta}, \quad \cot \gamma = \frac{\cos \gamma}{\sin \gamma}
\]

We also know that in any triangle, the sum of the angles is \(\alpha + \beta + \gamma = \pi\), and the Law of Cosines and Law of Sines are applicable:

\[
\cos \gamma = \frac{a^2 + b^2 - c^2}{2ab}, \quad \cos \alpha = \frac{b^2 + c^2 - a^2}{2bc}, \quad \cos \beta = \frac{a^2 + c^2 - b^2}{2ac}
\]

\[
\sin \alpha = \frac{a}{2R}, \quad \sin \beta = \frac{b}{2R}, \quad \sin \gamma = \frac{c}{2R}
\]

where \(R\) is the circumradius of the triangle, but we don't need to use it explicitly here due to cancellations in ratios.

First, let's express \(\cot \alpha, \cot \beta, \cot \gamma\) in terms of the sides of the triangle. Then we can substitute these into the expression \(\frac{\cot \gamma}{\cot \alpha + \cot \beta}\):

\[
\cot \alpha = \frac{\frac{b^2 + c^2 - a^2}{2bc}}{\frac{a}{2R}}, \quad \cot \beta = \frac{\frac{a^2 + c^2 - b^2}{2ac}}{\frac{b}{2R}}, \quad \cot \gamma = \frac{\frac{a^2 + b^2 - c^2}{2ab}}{\frac{c}{2R}}
\]

We can simplify this as:

\[
\cot \alpha = \frac{b^2 + c^2 - a^2}{2bc} \cdot \frac{2R}{a}, \quad \cot \beta = \frac{a^2 + c^2 - b^2}{2ac} \cdot \frac{2R}{b}, \quad \cot \gamma = \frac{a^2 + b^2 - c^2}{2ab} \cdot \frac{2R}{c}
\]

Since \(\sin \alpha = \frac{a}{2R}\), \(\sin \beta = \frac{b}{2R}\), and \(\sin \gamma = \frac{c}{2R}\), we recognize that the \(2R\) terms will cancel out when forming ratios, so we simplify it straightforwardly. \]

\[
\frac{\cot \gamma}{\cot \alpha + \cot \beta} = \frac{\frac{a^2 + b^2 - c^2}{2ab} \cdot \frac{2R}{c}}{\frac{b^2 + c^2 - a^2}{2bc a} + \frac{a^2 + c^2 - b^2}{2ac b}}
\]

As mentioned, the factor of \(\frac{2R}{c}\) can be canceled in the numerator, simplifying it further.
