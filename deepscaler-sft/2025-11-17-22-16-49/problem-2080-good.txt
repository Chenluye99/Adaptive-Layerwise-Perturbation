1. **Set Up the Problem:**
    - A unit circle has a radius of 1, so its equation is \(x^2 + y^2 = 1\).
    - Consider a rectangle inscribed in this unit circle. The vertices of the rectangle will have coordinates \((x, y)\), \((-x, y)\), \((-x, -y)\), and \((x, -y)\).
    - The length and width of the rectangle will be \(2x\) and \(2y\) respectively (since we take the full extent from \(-x\) to \(x\) and from \(-y\) to \(y\)).
    - The area \(A\) of the rectangle will be given by:
      \[
      A = \text{length} \times \text{width} = 2x \cdot 2y = 4xy
      \]
    - Since \((x, y)\) is on the unit circle, it must satisfy the equation \(x^2 + y^2 = 1\).

2. **Formulate the Function to Maximize:**
    - We need to maximize \(A = 4xy\) subject to the constraint \(x^2 + y^2 = 1\).
    - We can use the method of substitution or Lagrange multipliers, but here we will substitute the constraint into the expression for \(A\).

3. **Use Substitution:**
    - From the constraint, express \(y\) in terms of \(x\):
      \[
      y = \sqrt{1 - x^2}
      \]
    - Substitute this into the area formula:
      \[
      A = 4x \cdot \sqrt{1 - x^2}
      \]
    - Now, we need to find the critical points by taking the derivative of \(A\) with respect to \(x\) and setting it to zero:
      \[
      A(x) = 4x \sqrt{1 - x^2}
      \]
    - Differentiate \(A(x)\) using the product rule:
      \[
      A'(x) = 4 \left( \sqrt{1 - x^2} + x \cdot \frac{-x}{\sqrt{1 - x^2}} \right) = 4 \left( \sqrt{1 - x^2} - \frac{x^2}{\sqrt{1 - x^2}} \right) = 4 \cdot \frac{(1 - x^2) - x^2}{\sqrt{1 - x^2}} = 4 \cdot \frac{1 - 2x^2}{\sqrt{1 - x^2}}
      \]
    - Set the derivative equal to zero to find the critical points:
      \[
      4 \cdot \frac{1 - 2x^2}{\sqrt{1 - x^2}} = 0 \implies 1 - 2x^2 = 0 \implies 2x^2 = 1 \implies x^2 = \frac{1}{2} \implies x = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}
      \]
    - Substitute \(x = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\) back into the equation \(x^2 + y^2 = 1\) to find \(y\):
      \[
      \left( \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} \right)^2 + y^2 = 1 \implies \frac{1}{2} + y^2 = 1 \implies y^2 = \frac{1}{2} \implies y = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}

      \[
      A = 4 \cdot \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} \cdot \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} = 4 \cdot \frac{2}{4} = 4 \cdot \frac{1}{2} = 1 \cdot 2 = 2
