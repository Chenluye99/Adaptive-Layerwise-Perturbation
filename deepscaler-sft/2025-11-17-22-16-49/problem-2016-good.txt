First, let's start by understanding the problem and how we can mathematically derive a solution.

1. **Problem Restatement**:
    - We have a 3-digit integer \( m \).
    - The least common multiple (\(\text{lcm}\)) of \( 8m \) and \( 10^{10} \) is equal to \( 4 \cdot \text{lcm}(m, 10^{10}) \).
    - We need to find the value of \( m \).

2. **Mathematical Background**:
    - The prime factorization of \( 10^{10} \) is \( 2^{10} \cdot 5^{10} \).
    - The prime factorization of 8 is \( 2^3 \), so the prime factorization of \( 8m \) will be the same as \( m \) but with the additional factors of 2 up to 3 more 2's in its factorization.
    - To solve this, we can use Sympy to help us iterate through all 3-digit numbers and check which of them satisfies the equation \(\text{lcm}[8m, 10^{10}] = 4 \cdot \text{lcm}[m, 10^{10}]\).

Let's implement this step-by-step in Python using Sympy.

```python
import sympy as sp

# Initialize a list to store potential values of m
potential_m = []

# Iterate through all 3-digit numbers to check the condition
for m in range(100, 1000):
    lcm_8m_10_10 = sp.lcm(8 * m, 10**10)
    lcm_m_10_10 = sp.lcm(m, 10**10)
    
    if lcm_8m_10_10 == 4 * lcm_m_10_10:
        potential_m.append(m)

# Print the potential value of m
print(potential_m)
```
