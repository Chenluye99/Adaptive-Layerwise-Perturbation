To solve this problem, we need to determine how many different convex quadrilaterals can be formed from 10 distinct points on the circumference of a circle. Given that the points lie on a circle, any set of 4 points chosen from these 10 points will form a convex quadrilateral because all such points will be on the circumference of the circle and no points will be inside or outside each other in such a way that they don't form a convex shape.

This problem translates to finding the number of ways to choose 4 points out of 10, which is a combination problem. The number of ways to choose 4 points from 10 is given by the combination formula:

\[ \binom{10}{4} = \frac{10!}{4!(10-4)!} = \frac{10!}{4! \cdot 6!} = \frac{10 \times 9 \times 8 \times 7}{4 \times 3 \times 2 \times 1} = 210 \]

Let's confirm this by using Python to calculate the combination.
```python
import math

# Calculate the combination of 10 choose 4
num_points = 10
num_points_to_choose = 4

num_quadrilaterals = math.comb(num_points, num_points_to_choose)
print(num_quadrilaterals)
```
Code execution result: 210

Thus, the number of different convex quadrilaterals that can be formed by selecting 4 out of 10 distinct points on the circumference of a circle is \(\boxed{210}\). This confirms our reasoning and calculation are correct.
