First, let's establish the positions of the car and the center of the storm as functions of time.

1. **Position of the Car:**
   - The car travels due east at a speed of \(\frac{2}{3}\) mile per minute.
   - So, after \(t\) minutes, the car will be at position \((\frac{2}{3} t, 0)\).

2. **Position of the Storm:**
   - The storm moves southeast at a speed of \(\frac{1}{2} \sqrt{2}\) mile per minute, which means it moves \(\frac{1}{2}\) mile south and \(\frac{1}{2}\) mile east each minute.
   - So, after \(t\) minutes, the center of the storm will be at position \((\frac{1}{2} t, 110 - \frac{1}{2} t)\).

3. **Distance Between the Car and the Storm's Center:**
   - The distance \(d(t)\) between the car and the center of the storm at any time \(t\) is given by the formula for the distance between two points:
     \[
     d(t) = \sqrt{ \left( \frac{2}{3} t - \frac{1}{2} t \right)^2 + \left( 0 - (110 - \frac{1}{2} t) \right)^2 } = \sqrt{ \left( \frac{1}{6} t \right)^2 + \left( \frac{1}{2} t - 110 \right)^2 }
     \]
   - The car is inside the storm when the distance \(d(t)\) is less than or equal to 51 miles (the radius of the storm):
     \[
     \sqrt{ \left( \frac{1}{6} t \right)^2 + \left( \frac{1}{2} t - 110 \right)^2 } \leq 51
     \]
   - Square both sides of the inequality:
     \[
     \left( \frac{1}{6} t \right)^2 + \left( \frac{1}{2} t - 110 \right)^2 \leq 51^2
     \]
     \[
     \frac{1}{36} t^2 + \left( \frac{1}{2} t - 110 \right)^2 \leq 2601
     \]
   - Let's define the function and solve this inequality to find the times \( t_1 \) and \( t_2 \) when the car enters and leaves the storm circle, respectively.

We can use Python to solve this inequality and find the appropriate values for \( t_1 \) and \( t_2 \).
```python
import sympy as sp

# Define the variable

# Define the inequality

solutions = sp.solve(eq, t)
```
