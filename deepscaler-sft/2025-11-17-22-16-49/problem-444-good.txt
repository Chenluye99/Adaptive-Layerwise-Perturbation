### Part 1: Find the Polar Equation of Circle \( C \)

1. **Understand the Problem:**
    - The center of the circle in polar coordinates is given as \( C \left(2, -\frac{\pi}{6}\right) \).
    - The radius of the circle is 2.
    - The general form of the equation of a circle in Cartesian coordinates with center \((x_0, y_0)\) and radius \(r\) is \((x - x_0)^2 + (y - y_0)^2 = r^2\).

2. **Convert the Center to Cartesian Coordinates:**
    - The polar coordinates \( \left(2, -\frac{\pi}{6}\right) \) convert to Cartesian coordinates as follows:
      \[
      x_0 = 2 \cos \left(-\frac{\pi}{6}\right) = 2 \cdot \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = \sqrt{3}
      \]
      \[
      y_0 = 2 \sin \left(-\frac{\pi}{6}\right) = 2 \cdot \left(-\frac{1}{2}\right) = -1
      \]
    - Therefore, the center of the circle in Cartesian coordinates is \((\sqrt{3}, -1)\).
    - The equation of the circle in Cartesian coordinates is then:
      \[
      (x - \sqrt{3})^2 + (y + 1)^2 = 4
      \]

3. **Convert the Equation to Polar Coordinates:**
    - Using the transformations \( x = \rho \cos \theta \) and \( y = \rho \sin \theta \), substitute these into the Cartesian equation:
      \[
      (\rho \cos \theta - \sqrt{3})^2 + (\rho \sin \theta + 1)^2 = 4
      \]
    - Expanding and simplifying this equation will give us the polar equation of the circle.

### Part 2: Find the Length of the Chord Cut by the Line \( \theta = -\frac{5\pi}{12} \)

1. **Understand the Problem:**
    - The line \( \theta = -\frac{5\pi}{12} \) in Cartesian coordinates is a line passing through the origin with a slope of \( \tan \left(-\frac{5\pi}{12}\right) \).
    - To find the points of intersection of this line with the circle, substitute \( \theta = -\frac{5\pi}{12} \) into the polar equation of the circle and solve for \( \rho \).

2. **Find the Points of Intersection:**
    - Substitute \( \theta = -\frac{5\pi}{12} \) into the polar equation of the circle and solve for \( \rho \):
      \[
      \left( \rho \cos \left( -\frac{5\pi}{12} \right) - \sqrt{3} \right)^2 + \left( \rho \sin \left( -\frac{5\pi}{12} \right) + 1 \right)^2 = 4
      \]
    - The solutions for \( \rho \) will give us the points of intersection. The length of the chord will be the distance between these two intersection points.

Let's implement this step-by-step in Python using SymPy:

```python
import sympy as sp

# Define variables
rho, theta = sp.symbols('rho theta')

# Part 1: Find the Polar Equation of Circle C
# Convert center (2, -pi/6) to Cartesian coordinates
x0 = 2 * sp.cos(-sp.pi / 6)
y0 = 2 * sp.sin(-sp.pi / 6)
# The equation of the circle in Cartesian coordinates is (x - sqrt(3))^2 + (y + 1)^2 = 4
x, y = sp.symbols('x y')
circle_eq = (x - x0)**2 + (y - y0)**2 - 4
# Convert this equation to polar coordinates
polar_eq = circle_eq.subs({x: rho * sp.cos(theta), y: rho * sp.sin(theta)})
polar_eq = sp.simplify(polar_eq)

# Part 2: Find the Length of the Chord Cut by the Line θ = -5π/12
# Substitute θ = -5π/12 into the polar equation of the circle and solve for ρ
theta_line = -5 * sp.pi / 12
circle_polar_sub = polar_eq.subs(theta, theta_line)
rho_solutions = sp.solve(circle_polar_sub, rho)

# Get the two solutions for ρ
rho1, rho2 = rho_solutions

# Find the length of the chord, which is the distance between the two points of intersection
length_of_chord = abs(rho1 - rho2)

print(f"Polar Equation of Circle C: {polar_eq}")
print(f"Length of the Chord Cut by the Line θ = -5π/12: {length_of_chord}")
```
