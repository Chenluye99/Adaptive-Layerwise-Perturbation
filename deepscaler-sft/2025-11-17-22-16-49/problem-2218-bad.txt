First, we need to find the area of the triangle \(ABC\) using Heron's formula. Heron's formula states that the area of a triangle whose sides have lengths \(a\), \(b\), and \(c\) is:

\[
\text{Area} = \sqrt{s(s - a)(s - b)(s - c)}
\]

where \(s\) is the semi-perimeter of the triangle:

\[
s = \frac{a + b + c}{2}
\]

For triangle \(ABC\) with \(AB = 9\), \(BC = 10\), and \(AC = 11\):

\[
s = \frac{9 + 10 + 11}{2} = 15
\]

Using Heron's formula:

\[
\text{Area}_{ABC} = \sqrt{15(15 - 9)(15 - 10)(15 - 11)} = \sqrt{15 \cdot 6 \cdot 5 \cdot 4} = \sqrt{15 \cdot 6 \cdot 5 \cdot 4} = \sqrt{1800} = 30 \sqrt{2}
\]

Next, we need to find the area of triangle \(ADE\). Notice that triangles \(ADE\) and \(ABC\) are similar because \(\angle ADE = \angle ABC\) and \(\angle AED = \angle ACB\) (since \(D\) and \(E\) lie on \(AB\) and \(AC\) respectively, and \( \triangle ADE \sim \triangle ABC \)).

The ratio of the corresponding sides of triangles \(ADE\) and \(ABC\) is given by:

\[
\frac{AD}{AB} = \frac{4}{9} \quad \text{and} \quad \frac{AE}{AC} = \frac{7}{11}
\]

Since the triangles are similar, the ratio of the areas of two similar triangles is the square of the ratio of their corresponding sides. So, the ratio of the area of \(\triangle ADE\) to the area of \(\triangle ABC\) is:

\[
\frac{\text{Area}_{ADE}}{\text{Area}_{ABC}} = \left( \frac{AD}{AB} \right) \cdot \left( \frac{AE}{AC} \right) = \frac{4}{9} \cdot \frac{7}{11} = \frac{4 \cdot 7}{9 \cdot 11} = \frac{28}{99}
\]

Thus, the area of \(\triangle ADE\) is:

\[
\text{Area}_{ADE} = \text{Area}_{ABC} \cdot \frac{28}{99} = 30 \sqrt{2} \cdot \frac{28}{99}
\]
