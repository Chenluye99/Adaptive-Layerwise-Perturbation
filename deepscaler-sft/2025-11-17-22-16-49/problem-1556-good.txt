Given that one of the roots of the polynomial equation \(x^3 + ax^2 + bx + 31 = 0\) is \(-1 - 4\sqrt{2}\) and that the coefficients \(a\) and \(b\) are rational, we can use properties of polynomials and conjugates.

If a polynomial with rational coefficients has a root that is irrational, then its conjugate is also a root. So, if \(-1 - 4\sqrt{2}\) is a root, then its conjugate \(-1 + 4\sqrt{2}\) must also be a root.

Let's call the roots of the polynomial \(r_1 = -1 - 4\sqrt{2}\), \(r_2 = -1 + 4\sqrt{2}\), and \(r_3\) (which is the third root, and it must be rational because the coefficients of the polynomial are rational).

By Vieta's formulas, for a cubic equation of the form \(x^3 + ax^2 + bx + c = 0\) with roots \(r_1, r_2, r_3\):
1. \(r_1 + r_2 + r_3 = -a\)
2. \(r_1 r_2 + r_2 r_3 + r_3 r_1 = b\)
3. \(r_1 r_2 r_3 = -31\)

First, let's compute \(r_1 r_2\):

\[
r_1 r_2 = (-1 - 4\sqrt{2})(-1 + 4\sqrt{2}) = (-1)^2 - (4\sqrt{2})^2 = 1 - 16 \cdot 2 = 1 - 32 = -31
\]

Now, if we let the third root be \(r_3\), then from the product of the roots, we have:

\[
r_1 r_2 r_3 = (-31) = -31 \implies (-31) r_3 = -31 \implies r_3 = 1
\]

So, the roots of the polynomial are \(-1 - 4\sqrt{2}\), \(-1 + 4\sqrt{2}\), and \(1\). Using Vieta's formula for the sum of the roots:

\[
r_1 + r_2 + r_3 = (-1 - 4\sqrt{2}) + (-1 + 4\sqrt{2}) + 1 = -1 - 1 + 1 = -1
\]

From Vieta's formulas, we know that:

\[
r_1 + r_2 + r_3 = -a \implies -1 = -a \implies a = 1
\]

Thus, the value of \(a\) is \(1\). Let's confirm this by coding the steps to ensure accuracy.
