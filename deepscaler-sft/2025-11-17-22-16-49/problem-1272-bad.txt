1. **Properties of a Regular Octahedron:**
    - A regular octahedron has 8 equilateral triangular faces, 6 vertices, and 12 edges.
    - Let's assume that the side length of the regular octahedron is $a$.

2. **Properties of the Cube Whose Vertices are the Centers of the Faces of the Octahedron:**
    - The centers of the faces of a regular octahedron form a cube. To find the relationship between the side length of this cube and the side length of the original octahedron, we can use geometric properties and coordinate geometry.
    - Place the regular octahedron in a coordinate system such that its vertices are at $(\pm a/\sqrt{2}, 0, 0)$, $(0, \pm a/\sqrt{2}, 0)$, and $(0, 0, \pm a/\sqrt{2})$. The centers of the faces of this octahedron would form a cube.
    - By considering the geometry, we find that the side length of the cube (let's call it $s$) is half the length of the space diagonal of a face of the octahedron. Each face of the octahedron is an equilateral triangle, and the distance between the centers of two adjacent faces of the octahedron is half of the distance between the midpoints of the corresponding opposite edges of the octahedron.
    - The midpoints of the opposite edges of the octahedron are at a distance of $a$ (the length of an edge of the original octahedron), so the length between the centers of faces (which gives the side length of the cube) is \( s = \frac{a \sqrt{2}}{2} = \frac{a}{\sqrt{2}} \).

3. **Volume Calculations:**
    - The volume of a regular octahedron with side length $a$ is given by:
      \[
      V_{\text{octahedron}} = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{3} a^3
      \]
    - The volume of a cube with side length $s = \frac{a}{\sqrt{2}}$ is:
      \[
      V_{\text{cube}} = \left( \frac{a}{\sqrt{2}} \right)^3 = \frac{a^3}{2 \sqrt{2}} = \frac{a^3 \sqrt{2}}{4}

      \[
      \frac{V_{\text{octahedron}}}{V_{\text{cube}}} = \frac{\frac{\sqrt{2}}{3} a^3}{\frac{a^3 \sqrt{2}}{4}} = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{3} \cdot \frac{4}{\sqrt{2}} = \frac{4}{3}
      \]
