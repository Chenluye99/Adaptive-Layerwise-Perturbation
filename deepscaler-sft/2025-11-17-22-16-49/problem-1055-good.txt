To solve this problem, let's start by setting up the relationship given in the problem statement. The energy \(E\) stored by a pair of point charges \(q_1\) and \(q_2\) separated by a distance \(d\) is given by:

\[ E \propto \frac{q_1 q_2}{d} \]

For three identical point charges \(q\) placed at the vertices of an equilateral triangle, each side of the triangle has length \(a\). The energy stored by each pair of charges is:

\[ E_{ij} = k \frac{q \cdot q}{a} = k \frac{q^2}{a} \]

where \(k\) is the proportionality constant. Since there are three pairs of charges in the equilateral triangle, the total energy stored in this configuration is:

\[ E_{\text{total, initial}} = 3 \cdot k \frac{q^2}{a} = 15 \, \text{Joules} \]

From this equation, we can find the product \( k \frac{q^2}{a} \):

\[ k \frac{q^2}{a} = \frac{15}{3} = 5 \]

Now, let's consider the new configuration where one of the charges is moved to the midpoint of the opposite side. The new distance between the charge at the midpoint and the other two charges will be \( \frac{a}{2} \). 

The energy stored by the pair consisting of the charge at the midpoint and each of the other two charges will be:

\[ E_{\text{mid, i}} = k \frac{q \cdot q}{\frac{a}{2}} = k \frac{q^2}{\frac{a}{2}} = 2 k \frac{q^2}{a} \]

The energy stored by the pair of charges that remain at the vertices of the original triangle is still:

\[ E_{\text{ij}} = k \frac{q^2}{a} \]

Since there are two pairs of charges at distance \( \frac{a}{2} \) and one pair at distance \( a \), the new total energy stored is:

\[ E_{\text{total, new}} = 2 \cdot 2 k \frac{q^2}{a} + 1 \cdot k \frac{q^2}{a} = 4 k \frac{q^2}{a} + k \frac{q^2}{a} = 5 k \frac{q^2}{a} \]

Substituting \( k \frac{q^2}{a} = 5 \):

\[ E_{\text{total, new}} = 5 \cdot 5 = 25 \, \text{Joules} \]

The additional energy stored in the new configuration compared to the initial configuration is:

\[ \Delta E = E_{\text{total, new}} - E_{\text{total, initial}} = 25 - 15 = 10 \, \text{Joules} \]

Let's confirm this with Python to ensure the accuracy of the result.
```python
# Given total energy for initial configuration of three identical point charges in an equilateral triangle
E_total_initial = 15  # Joules

# From the energy equation for initial configuration: 3 * (k * (q^2 / a)) = 15, we get:
# k * (q^2 / a) = 5 Joules

# New configuration: one charge is moved to the midpoint of the opposite side of the equilateral triangle.
# Energy calculations for the new configuration:
# - Two pairs of charges have a distance of a/2 and one pair has a distance of a.
# - Energy for each pair at a/2 distance is 2 * (k * (q^2 / a))
# - Energy for the pair at distance a is k * (q^2 / a)

# So, the new total energy will be:
E_total_new = 2 * 2 * (5) + 1 * 5

# Compute the additional energy stored
delta_E = E_total_new - E_total_initial
print(delta_E)
```
