To solve this problem, we need to use conditional probability. Let's define the events:

- \( A \): The event that at least half of the rolls are 20. Since Steven rolls five dice, "at least half" means that at least 3 of the dice show 20.
- \( B \): The event that all three of the remaining concealed dice show 20.

We want to find the probability \( P(B | A) \), the probability that all three remaining concealed dice show 20, given that at least half of the dice show 20.

By Bayes' theorem, the probability \( P(B | A) \) is given by:
\[ P(B | A) = \frac{P(A | B) P(B)}{P(A)} \]

First, let's define \( P(B) \), the probability that all three of the remaining dice show 20, if we do not have any information about Steven's result. This is straightforward:
\[ P(B) = \left( \frac{1}{20} \right)^3 = \frac{1}{8000} \]

However, we need \( P(A) \), the probability that at least half of the dice show 20. We also need \( P(A | B) \), the probability that at least half of the dice show 20 given that all three of the remaining dice show 20. 

If we know that all three of the remaining dice show 20, then at least half of the dice show 20 if and only if at least one of the first two dice also show 20. So,
\[ P(A | B) = P(\text{at least one of the first two dice shows 20}) \]
The probability that a single die does not show 20 is \(\frac{19}{20}\), so the probability that neither of the first two dice shows 20 is \(\left( \frac{19}{20} \right)^2 = \frac{361}{400}\). So, the probability that at least one of the first two dice shows 20 is:
\[ P(A | B) = 1 - \frac{361}{400} = \frac{39}{400} \]

Now, let's define \( P(A) \), the probability that at least half of the dice show 20. We need to consider all possible ways to get 3, 4, or 5 of the 5 dice to show 20, and the probability for each of these scenarios.

We can calculate this by summing up the probabilities of getting 3, 4, or 5 successes in 5 Bernoulli trials where the probability of success (dice showing 20) is \(\frac{1}{20}\). Each of these can be calculated using the binomial distribution formula:
\[ P(\text{exactly } k \text{ successes in 5 trials}) = \binom{5}{k} \left( \frac{1}{20} \right)^k \left( \frac{19}{20} \right)^{5 - k} \]

So,
\[ P(A) = \sum_{k=3}^{5} \binom{5}{k} \left( \frac{1}{20} \right)^k \left( \frac{19}{20} \right)^{5 - k} \]

Let's compute this value step by step using Python.
```python
import sympy as sp

# Calculate P(A) - the probability that at least 3 out of 5 dice show 20
prob_at_least_3_show_20 = sum(sp.binomial(5, k) * (1/20)**k * (19/20)**(5 - k) for k in range(3, 6))

# We already calculated P(A | B) and P(B)
P_B = (1 / 20) ** 3
P_A_given_B = 39 / 400

# Now use Bayes' theorem to get P(B | A)
P_B_given_A = (P_A_given_B * P_B) / prob_at_least_3_show_20
print(P_B_given_A)
```
