First, we need to rewrite the given circle equation \(x^2 + y^2 = 4x + 12y - 39\) in standard form. The standard form of a circle's equation is \((x - h)^2 + (y - k)^2 = r^2\), where \((h, k)\) is the center and \(r\) is the radius.

To do this, we will complete the square for both \(x\) and \(y\) terms in the given equation.

Starting with the equation:

\[x^2 + y^2 = 4x + 12y - 39\]

1. Move the right side's constant term to the left side:

\[x^2 - 4x + y^2 - 12y = -39\]

2. Complete the square for the \(x\) terms:
   - Take the coefficient of \(x\), which is \(-4\), divide it by 2 to get \(-2\), and then square it to get \(4\).
   - Complete the square for the \(y\) terms:
     - Take the coefficient of \(y\), which is \(-12\), divide it by 2 to get \(-6\), and then square it to get \(36\).

So, we add and subtract these squares on the left side:

\[x^2 - 4x + 4 - 4 + y^2 - 12y + 36 - 36 = -39\]

This simplifies to:

\[(x - 2)^2 - 4 + (y - 6)^2 - 36 = -39\]

Combining the constants on the left side:

\[(x - 2)^2 + (y - 6)^2 - 40 = -39\]

Move \(-40\) to the right side:

\[(x - 2)^2 + (y - 6)^2 = 1\]
