To find the area of the parallelogram formed by the vectors \(\mathbf{v}\) and \(\mathbf{w}\), we can use the cross product method. For two-dimensional vectors, the magnitude of the cross product can be computed as the absolute value of the determinant of the matrix formed by placing the vectors as rows or columns.

Given:
\[
\mathbf{v} = \begin{pmatrix} -7 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}, \quad \mathbf{w} = \begin{pmatrix} 5 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix}
\]

The area \( A \) of the parallelogram formed by \(\mathbf{v}\) and \(\mathbf{w}\) is given by:
\[
A = \left| \det \begin{pmatrix} -7 & 1 \\ 5 & 2 \end{pmatrix} \right|
\]

The determinant of the matrix \(\begin{pmatrix} -7 & 1 \\ 5 & 2 \end{pmatrix}\) is calculated as follows:
\[
\det \begin{pmatrix} -7 & 1 \\ 5 & 2 \end{pmatrix} = (-7) \cdot 2 - 1 \cdot 5 = -14 - 5 = -19
\]

The area is the absolute value of this determinant:
\[
A = |-19| = 19
\]

Let's confirm this by implementing it in Python.
```python
import numpy as np

# Define the vectors
v = np.array([-7, 1])
w = np.array([5, 2])

# Compute the determinant of the matrix formed by v and w as its rows or columns
matrix = np.array([v, w])
det = np.linalg.det(matrix)

# The absolute value of the determinant gives the area of the parallelogram
area = abs(det)
print(area)
```

Due to floating-point precision, the result is extremely close to 19, so we can confidently state that the area of the parallelogram is:

\(\boxed{19}\)
