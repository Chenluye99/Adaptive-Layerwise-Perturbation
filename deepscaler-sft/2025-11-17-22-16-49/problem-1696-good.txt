- The radius of the semicircle with diameter $AB$ is $r_1$.
- The radius of the semicircle with diameter $BC$ is $r_2$.
- The radius of the semicircle with diameter $CD$ is $r_3$.

Given the ratio, let's denote $r_1 = k$, $r_2 = 2k$, and $r_3 = 3k$ for some constant $k$. Therefore, the radius of the largest semicircle with diameter $AD$ will be $r_{\text{AD}} = k + 2k + 3k = 6k$.

Next, we will compute the areas of the semicircles and then determine the areas of interest (the shaded and unshaded areas).

1. Calculate the area of the largest semicircle with diameter $AD$:
   \[
   \text{Area}_{\text{AD}} = \frac{1}{2} \pi (6k)^2 = \frac{1}{2} \pi (36k^2) = 18 \pi k^2
   \]

2. Calculate the areas of the smaller semicircles with diameters $AB, BC, CD$:
   \[
   \text{Area}_{AB} = \frac{1}{2} \pi (k)^2 = \frac{1}{2} \pi k^2
   \]
   \[
   \text{Area}_{BC} = \frac{1}{2} \pi (2k)^2 = \frac{1}{2} \pi (4k^2) = 2 \pi k^2
   \]
   \[
   \text{Area}_{CD} = \frac{1}{2} \pi (3k)^2 = \frac{1}{2} \pi (9k^2) = \frac{9}{2} \pi k^2
   \]

3. The total area of the unshaded regions (the areas of the smaller semicircles):
   \[
   \text{Area}_{\text{unshaded}} = \frac{1}{2} \pi k^2 + 2 \pi k^2 + \frac{9}{2} \pi k^2 = \frac{1}{2} \pi k^2 + \frac{4}{2} \pi k^2 + \frac{9}{2} \pi k^2 = \frac{14}{2} \pi k^2 = 7 \pi k^2
   \]

4. The shaded area is the area of the largest semicircle minus the area of the unshaded regions:
   \[
   \text{Area}_{\text{shaded}} = \text{Area}_{\text{AD}} - \text{Area}_{\text{unshaded}} = 18 \pi k^2 - 7 \pi k^2 = 11 \pi k^2
   \]

5. Now, we find the ratio of the shaded area to the unshaded area:
   \[
   \text{Ratio} = \frac{\text{Area}_{\text{shaded}}}{\text{Area}_{\text{unshaded}}} = \frac{11 \pi k^2}{7 \pi k^2} = \frac{11}{7}
