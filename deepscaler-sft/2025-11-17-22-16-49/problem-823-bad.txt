To solve this problem, we need to determine the probability that Ella's 10th roll is her last roll, meaning that her 10th roll is the same as her 9th roll, and none of the first 9 rolls have any consecutive duplicates.

### Step-by-Step Reasoning:

1. **Conditions for the 10th Roll to be Her Last Roll:**
    - The 9th and 10th rolls must be the same.
    - None of the pairs of consecutive rolls among the first 9 rolls should be the same.

2. **Formulating the Problem:**
    - Let's denote the outcome of the \(n\)-th roll by \(d_n\).
    - For the 10th roll to be her last roll, the following conditions must hold:
        - \(d_9 = d_{10}\)
        - For \(1 \leq i \leq 8\), \(d_i \neq d_{i+1}\)

3. **Generating All Possible Outcomes:**
    - Each roll of a standard six-sided die has 6 possible outcomes (1 through 6).
    - We need to enumerate all sequences of 10 rolls and then check how many of these sequences meet the conditions above.

4. **Counting Valid Sequences:**
    - We need to iterate through all possible sequences of 10 rolls, and for each sequence, we need to check:
      - Whether the 9th and 10th rolls are the same.
      - Whether there are no consecutive duplicates in the first 9 rolls.
    - If both conditions are met, we count that sequence as valid.

5. **Calculating the Probability:**
    - The probability is the number of valid sequences divided by the total number of possible sequences of 10 rolls. Since each roll is independent and there are 6 possible outcomes for each roll, there are \(6^{10}\) possible sequences of 10 rolls.
    - The probability is then given by \(\frac{\text{Number of valid sequences}}{6^{10}}\).

Let's implement this step-by-step reasoning in Python to get the accurate result.
```python
import itertools

# Total possible outcomes for 10 rolls of a six-sided die
total_outcomes = 6 ** 10

# Count valid sequences where the 10th roll is the last roll (i.e., 9th and 10th rolls are the same and no consecutive duplicates in the first 9 rolls)
count_valid_sequences = 0

for seq in itertools.product(range(1, 7), repeat=10):
    if seq[8] == seq[9]:  # 9th and 10th rolls must be the same
        has_consecutive_duplicates = any(seq[i] == seq[i + 1] for i in range(8))
        if not has_consecutive_duplicates:  # No consecutive duplicates in the first 9 rolls
            count_valid_sequences += 1

# Calculate the probability
probability = count_valid_sequences / total_outcomes

# Return the probability to the nearest thousandth
probability_rounded = round(probability, 3)
print(probability_rounded)
```
