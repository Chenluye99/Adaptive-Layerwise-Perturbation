\[ N = 10000A + 1000B + 100,C + 10B + A \]

For a number to be divisible by 11, the difference between the sum of the digits in the odd positions and the sum of the digits in the even positions should be a multiple of 11 (including 0).

The positions of the digits in the number \( AB,CBA \) are as follows:
- 1st digit: \( A \)
- 2nd digit: \( B \)
- 3rd digit: \( , \) (separator, not a digit)
- 4th digit: \( C \)
- 5th digit: \( B \)
- 6th digit: \( A \)

So, we will consider the digits in positions 1, 3, 5 (odd positions) and 2, 4 (even positions):

- Sum of the digits in the odd positions: \( A + C + A = 2A + C \)
- Sum of the digits in the even positions: \( B + B = 2B \)

The difference between these sums should be a multiple of 11:

\[ (2A + C) - 2B = k \cdot 11 \]

where \( k \) is an integer. We need to check different values of \( A, B, \) and \( C \) to find the greatest possible value of \( N \) that meets all these conditions, and ensures that \( A, B, \) and \( C \) are distinct.
