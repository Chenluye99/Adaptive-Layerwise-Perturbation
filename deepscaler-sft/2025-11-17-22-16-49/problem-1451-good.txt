First, we need to solve for the integers \( x \) and \( y \) in the equation \( 2^x \cdot 3^y = 1296 \). Once we find the appropriate values of \( x \) and \( y \), we can calculate \( x + y \).

To start, let's factorize 1296 to express it in terms of its prime factors.

1. We can begin by dividing 1296 by 2 repeatedly until it is no longer divisible by 2, then by 3 repeatedly until it is no longer divisible by 3. This will give us the exponents for the prime factors 2 and 3 respectively.

Let's do the prime factorization of 1296 step-by-step:

\[
1296 \div 2 = 648 \\
648 \div 2 = 324 \\
324 \div 2 = 162 \\
162 \div 2 = 81 \\
81 \div 3 = 27 \\
27 \div 3 = 9 \\
9 \div 3 = 3 \\
3 \div 3 = 1
\]

From the above division, we get:

\[
1296 = 2^4 \cdot 3^4
\]

So, by comparing, we get that:

\[
2^x \cdot 3^y = 2^4 \cdot 3^4
\]

This implies:

\[
x = 4 \quad \text{and} \quad y = 4
\]

Now, we need to verify this by coding to ensure that no other combinations of \( x \) and \( y \) could produce 1296, and then compute \( x + y \).
```python
# We will iterate over possible values of x and y to find the correct pair that satisfies 2^x * 3^y = 1296, and then return x + y.

solution = None

for x in range(1, 21):  # We limit the range reasonably because higher values of x and y will make 2^x * 3^y much larger than 1296
    for y in range(1, 21):
        if 2**x * 3**y == 1296:
            solution = (x, y)
            break
    if solution:
        break

print((solution, sum(solution)))
```
