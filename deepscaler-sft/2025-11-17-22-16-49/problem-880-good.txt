To solve this problem, let's start by understanding the constraints and breaking it down.

1. **Finding the Form of \( n \):**
    - We know that \( n \) is an odd integer with exactly 11 positive divisors.
    - The number of divisors of a number \( n \) with prime factorization \( n = p_1^{e_1} p_2^{e_2} \cdots p_k^{e_k} \) is given by \((e_1 + 1)(e_2 + 1) \cdots (e_k + 1)\).
    - Since 11 is a prime number, the only way to write 11 as a product of integers greater than 1 is \( 11 = 11 \times 1 \). This implies that \( n \) must be of the form \( p^{10} \), where \( p \) is an odd prime (because \( n \) is an odd integer).

2. **Form of \( 8n^3 \):**
    - We know \( n = p^{10} \) for some odd prime \( p \).
    - Therefore, \( n^3 = (p^{10})^3 = p^{30} \).
    - Consequently, \( 8n^3 = 8 \cdot p^{30} = 2^3 \cdot p^{30} \).

3. **Finding the Number of Divisors of \( 8n^3 \):**
    - To find the number of positive divisors of \( 8n^3 = 2^3 \cdot p^{30} \), we use the formula for the number of divisors again.
    - The number of divisors of \( 2^3 \cdot p^{30} \) is given by \((3 + 1)(30 + 1) = 4 \cdot 31 = 124 \).

Let's confirm and ensure this solution by coding it in Python to ensure no errors in our reasoning.
```python
import sympy

# Step 1: Identify the form of n
# We know n is an odd integer with exactly 11 divisors, and 11 = 11 * 1, so n must be of the form p^10 where p is an odd prime.

# Let's choose a few odd primes and check if they meet the criteria, but in this case, choosing any odd prime (say 3) will suffice because the form p^10 is unique in getting exactly 11 divisors.

p = 3  # an example odd prime
n = p ** 10

# Step 2: Construct 8 * n^3
expression = 8 * (n ** 3)

# Step 3: Find the number of positive divisors of 8 * n^3
num_divisors = sympy.divisor_count(expression)

print(num_divisors)
```
