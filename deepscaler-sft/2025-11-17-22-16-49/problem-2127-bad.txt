To solve this problem, we need to determine how many positive integers from 1 to 10,000 are divisible by 2, 5, or 10. We can use the principle of Inclusion-Exclusion to solve this problem.

1. **Count Numbers Divisible by 2:**
   - A number is divisible by 2 if it is even.
   - The count of numbers divisible by 2 up to 10,000 is \(\left\lfloor \frac{10000}{2} \right\rfloor\).

2. **Count Numbers Divisible by 5:**
   - A number is divisible by 5 if it ends with 0 or 5.
   - The count of numbers divisible by 5 up to 10,000 is \(\left\lfloor \frac{10000}{5} \right\rfloor\).

3. **Count Numbers Divisible by 10:**
   - A number is divisible by 10 if it ends with 0.
   - The count of numbers divisible by 10 up to 10,000 is \(\left\lfloor \frac{10000}{10} \right\rfloor\).

4. **Apply the Principle of Inclusion-Exclusion:**
   - We need to account for double-counting of numbers that are divisible by both 2 and 5 (i.e., divisible by 10), and correct for this by subtracting the count of numbers divisible by 10 once (because they are counted twice, once in the numbers divisible by 2 and once in the numbers divisible by 5).
   - The count of numbers divisible by 10 is already considered in both 2 and 5, so we don't need to consider it again directly in the addition step but need to ensure we don't overcount.

Let's implement this step-by-step calculation in Python to get the accurate result.
```python
# Calculate the counts
count_2 = 10000 // 2
count_5 = 10000 // 5
count_10 = 10000 // 10

# Use the principle of inclusion-exclusion
count_result = count_2 + count_5 + count_10 - count_10

print(count_result)
```
Code execution result: 7000

Thus, the number of positive integers less than or equal to 10,000 that are divisible by 2, 5, or 10 is \(\boxed{7000}\). This is our final answer.
