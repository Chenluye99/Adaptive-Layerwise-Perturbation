To solve the problem, we need to find the smallest integer that satisfies the inequality \((x-5)(x+5) < 0\).

1. **Understand the Inequality:**
    - The expression \((x-5)(x+5)\) is a product of two factors.
    - The product of \((x-5)\) and \((x+5)\) is negative when one of the factors is positive and the other is negative.
    - This means we need to find the interval where one of \((x-5)\) or \((x+5)\) is positive and the other is negative.

2. **Find the Critical Points:**
    - The critical points where \((x-5)(x+5) = 0\) are \(x = 5\) and \(x = -5\).
    - These points divide the number line into three intervals: \((-\infty, -5)\), \((-5, 5)\), and \((5, \infty)\).

3. **Test the Intervals:**
    - For \((-\infty, -5)\): Both \((x-5)\) and \((x+5)\) are negative, so their product is positive.
    - For \((-5, 5)\): \((x-5)\) is negative and \((x+5)\) is positive, so their product is negative.
    - For \((5, \infty)\): Both \((x-5)\) and \((x+5)\) are positive, so their product is positive.
    - Therefore, the inequality \((x-5)(x+5) < 0\) is satisfied in the interval \((-5, 5)\).

4. **Find the Smallest Integer in the Interval \((-5, 5)\):**
    - The integers in the interval \((-5, 5)\) are \(-4, -3, -2, -1, 0, 1, 2, 3, 4\).
    - The smallest integer in this range is \(-4\).

Let's confirm this by writing Python code to verify the solution.
```python
import sympy as sp

# Define the variable and the inequality
x = sp.symbols('x')
inequality = (x - 5) * (x + 5) < 0

# Solve the inequality
solution = sp.solve_univariate_inequality(inequality, x, relational=False)

# Find the smallest integer that satisfies the solution
smallest_integer = min(sp.solveset(inequality, x, domain=sp.S.Integers))

print(smallest_integer)
```
