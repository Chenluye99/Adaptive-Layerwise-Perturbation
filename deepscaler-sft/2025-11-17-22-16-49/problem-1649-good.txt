The given equation is:

\[
-\frac{2}{3}(x - 5) = \frac{3}{2}(x + 1)
\]

1. **Eliminate the fractions by cross-multiplying or finding a common denominator:**
   To get rid of the fractions, we can multiply both sides of the equation by the least common multiple (LCM) of 3 and 2, which is 6:
   \[
   6 \cdot \left(-\frac{2}{3}(x - 5)\right) = 6 \cdot \left(\frac{3}{2}(x + 1)\right)
   \]

   Simplifying both sides gives:
   \[
   -4(x - 5) = 9(x + 1)
   \]

2. **Distribute the constants on both sides:**
   Distribute the -4 on the left side and the 9 on the right side:
   \[
   -4x + 20 = 9x + 9
   \]

3. **Combine like terms:**
   To isolate \(x\), we first get all the \(x\) terms on one side and the constants on the other side. Let's move \(-4x\) to the right side and 20 to the left side:
   \[
   20 - 9 = 9x + 4x \implies 11 = 13x
   \]

4. **Solve for \(x\):**
   Divide both sides by 13:
   \[
   x = \frac{11}{13}
   \]

