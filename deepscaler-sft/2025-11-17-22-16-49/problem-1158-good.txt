To solve this problem, we need to consider how the fractions of the square that are shaded change with each iteration of dividing the center square into smaller squares and then shading those smaller squares.

1. **Initial Setup**:
    - Start with a square that is divided into nine smaller squares of equal area.
    - We shade four of the corner squares in the first iteration.
    - Each of these four corner squares has an area that is \(\frac{1}{9}\) of the original square. So, in the first iteration, the total shaded area as a fraction of the original square is:
      \[
      4 \times \frac{1}{9} = \frac{4}{9}
      \]

2. **Subsequent Iterations**:
    - In the next iteration, the center square (which is one of the smaller squares from the first iteration) is again divided into nine even smaller squares. Each of these smaller squares has an area of \(\frac{1}{9}\) of the area of the center square from the previous iteration, which in turn is \(\frac{1}{9}\) of the original square. Hence, each of these even smaller squares has an area of \(\frac{1}{9^2} = \frac{1}{81}\) of the original square.
    - From this second iteration, we shade four more squares in the same way as before (one in each corner), so we have a similar fraction being shaded in this smaller set of squares. The total shaded area from these smaller squares is:
      \[
      4 \times \frac{1}{81} = \frac{4}{81}
      \]
    - In the third iteration, each of the four smaller center squares (from the previous iteration) is divided into nine even smaller squares and each of these smaller squares has an area of \(\frac{1}{9^3} = \frac{1}{729}\) of the original square. The shaded area from these squares is:
      \[
      4 \times \frac{4}{9^3} = 4 \times \frac{4}{729} = \frac{16}{729}
      \]
    - We can observe that in each subsequent iteration, the shaded part follows a geometric series pattern where the common ratio is \(\frac{1}{9}\).

3. **Formulating a Geometric Series**:
    - Let's denote the total shaded area as \(S\). Then, we have:
      \[
      S = \frac{4}{9} + \frac{4}{81} + \frac{16}{729} + \cdots
      \]
    - This is an infinite geometric series with the first term \(a = \frac{4}{9}\) and the common ratio \(r = \frac{1}{9}\):
      \[
      S = \frac{\frac{4}{9}}{1 - \frac{1}{9}} = \frac{\frac{4}{9}}{\frac{8}{9}} = \frac{4}{8} = \frac{1}{2}
      \]

Let's confirm this with Python by summing a large number of terms of this series and checking that it converges to \(\frac{1}{2}\).
