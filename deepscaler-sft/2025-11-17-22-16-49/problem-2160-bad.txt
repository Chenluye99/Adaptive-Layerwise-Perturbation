First, we start by solving the equation \( |y| = 2(|y| - 1) \).

1. Let \( |y| = x \). Then, the equation becomes:
   \[
   x = 2(x - 1)
   \]
2. Simplify and solve for \( x \):
   \[
   x = 2x - 2 \implies x - 2x = -2 \implies -x = -2 \implies x = 2
   \]
   So, \( |y| = 2 \).

3. The equation \( |y| = 2 \) means that \( y = 2 \) or \( y = -2 \). Therefore, the solutions to the original equation are \( y = 2 \) and \( y = -2 \).

4. The product of the solutions \( y = 2 \) and \( y = -2 \) is:
   \[
   2 \cdot (-2) = -4
   \]

We can verify this solution by implementing it in Python to ensure accuracy.
```python
# Define the solutions
solutions = [2, -2]

# Calculate the product of the solutions
product_of_solutions = solutions[0] * solutions[1]
print(product_of_solutions)
```
Code execution result: -4
