To solve the problem, we need to identify the center of the hyperbola given by the equation:

\[
\frac{(2y - 2)^2}{5^2} - \frac{(3x - 4)^2}{4^2} = 1
\]

A standard form of a hyperbola's equation is:

\[
\frac{(Y - K)^2}{a^2} - \frac{(X - H)^2}{b^2} = 1
\]

where \((H, K)\) is the center of the hyperbola. By comparing this standard form with our given equation, we can identify the center.

First, let's rewrite the given equation in a more recognizable form by making appropriate substitutions:

\[
\frac{(2y - 2)^2}{25} - \frac{(3x - 4)^2}{16} = 1
\]

We can set \(2y - 2 = 2(Y - 1)\) and \(3x - 4 = 3(X - \frac{4}{3})\). Comparing with the standard form, it becomes evident that the equation corresponds to:

\[
\frac{(2(Y - 1))^2}{25} - \frac{(3(X - \frac{4}{3}))^2}{16} = 1
\]

This can be simplified to:

\[
\frac{4(Y - 1)^2}{25} - \frac{9(X - \frac{4}{3})^2}{16} = 1
\]

However, it is more straightforward to recognize that the center of the hyperbola \(\frac{(2y - 2)^2}{25} - \frac{(3x - 4)^2}{16} = 1\) is the point where the expressions inside the parentheses are zero, i.e., where \(2y - 2 = 0\) and \(3x - 4 = 0\).

1. Set \(2y - 2 = 0 \):
   \[
   2y - 2 = 0 \implies 2y = 2 \implies y = 1
   \]

2. Set \(3x - 4 = 0 \):
   \[
   3x - 4 = 0 \implies 3x = 4 \implies x = \frac{4}{3}
   \]

So, the center of the hyperbola is at \(\left( \frac{4}{3}, 1 \right)\).
