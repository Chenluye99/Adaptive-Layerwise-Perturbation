To solve this problem, we need to find the determinant of the given matrix and then determine its maximum value as the angle \(\theta\) varies.

The matrix is:
\[
\begin{vmatrix}
1 & 1 & 1 \\
1 & 1 + \sin \theta & 1 \\
1 + \cos \theta & 1 & 1
\end{vmatrix}
\]

The determinant of a 3x3 matrix \(\begin{vmatrix} a & b & c \\ d & e & f \\ g & h & i \end{vmatrix}\) is given by:
\[
a(ei - fh) - b(di - fg) + c(dh - eg)
\]

Applying this formula to our matrix, we get:
\[
\text{Det} = 1 \cdot \left( (1 + \sin \theta) \cdot 1 - 1 \cdot 1 \right) - 1 \cdot \left( 1 \cdot 1 - 1 \cdot (1 + \cos \theta) \right) + 1 \cdot \left( 1 \cdot 1 - (1 + \sin \theta) \cdot (1 + \cos \theta) \right)
\]

Let's break this down step-by-step and compute it using Python to ensure accuracy. We will then find the maximum value of this determinant as \(\theta\) ranges over all angles (i.e., \( \theta \in [0, 2\pi) \)).
```python
import numpy as np
import scipy.optimize as opt

# Define the determinant function as a function of theta
def determinant(theta):
    matrix = np.array([
        [1, 1, 1],
        [1, 1 + np.sin(theta), 1],
        [1 + np.cos(theta), 1, 1]
    ])
    return np.linalg.det(matrix)

# Use optimization to find the maximum value of the determinant over the range of theta from 0 to 2*pi
result = opt.minimize_scalar(lambda theta: -determinant(theta), bounds=(0, 2 * np.pi), method='bounded')

# Get the maximum value of the determinant
max_determinant = -result.fun
print(max_determinant)
```
