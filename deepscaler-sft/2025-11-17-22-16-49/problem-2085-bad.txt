To solve this problem, we will use properties related to angle bisectors and the angle bisector theorem. The diagram indicates that \( CX \) is the angle bisector of \( \angle ACB \), and by the Angle Bisector Theorem:

\[
\frac{AX}{BX} = \frac{AC}{BC}
\]

From the diagram, we have:
- \( AC = 21 \)
- \( BC = 28 \)

We also know that \( X \) divides \( AB \) in the ratio of the lengths of \( AC \) and \( BC \). We can denote the lengths \( AX \) and \( BX \) as follows:

\[
AX = d \quad \text{and} \quad BX = 24 - d
\]

According to the Angle Bisector Theorem:

\[
\frac{AX}{BX} = \frac{AC}{BC} = \frac{21}{28} = \frac{3}{4}
\]

So we can write:

\[
\frac{d}{24 - d} = \frac{3}{4}
\]

Cross-multiply to get:

\[
4d = 3(24 - d)
\]

Solve for \( d \):

\[
4d = 72 - 3d
\]

Combine like terms:

\[
4d + 3d = 72
\]

\[
7d = 72
\]

So,

\[
d = \frac{72}{7}
\]
