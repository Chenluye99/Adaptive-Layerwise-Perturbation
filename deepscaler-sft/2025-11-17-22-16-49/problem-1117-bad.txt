To solve the problem, we will use the Remainder Theorem. The Remainder Theorem states that the remainder of the division of a polynomial \(f(x)\) by \(x - c\) is \(f(c)\).

Given the polynomial \( f(x) = ax^3 - 6x^2 + bx - 5 \):

1. When \( f(x) \) is divided by \( x - 1 \), the remainder is \(-5\). By the Remainder Theorem, this means:
   \[
   f(1) = -5
   \]
   Substituting \( x = 1 \) into \( f(x) \):
   \[
   f(1) = a(1)^3 - 6(1)^2 + b(1) - 5 = a - 6 + b - 5 = a + b - 11 = -5 \implies a + b - 11 = -5 \implies a + b = 6 \quad \text{(Equation 1)}
   \]

2. When \( f(x) \) is divided by \( x + 2 \), the remainder is \(-53\). By the Remainder Theorem, this means:
   \[
   f(-2) = -53
   \]
   Substituting \( x = -2 \) into \( f(x) \):
   \[
   f(-2) = a(-2)^3 - 6(-2)^2 + b(-2) - 5 = -8a - 6 \cdot 4 - 2b - 5 = -8a - 24 - 2b - 5 = -8a - 2b - 29 = -53 \implies -8a - 2b - 29 = -53 \implies -8a - 2b = -24 \implies 8a + 2b = 24 \implies 4a + b = 12 \quad \text{(Equation 2)}
   \]

Now, we have a system of linear equations:
\[
\begin{cases}
a + b = 6 \\
4a + b = 12
\end{cases}
\]

We can solve this system of equations to find the values of \(a\) and \(b\). We will subtract Equation 1 from Equation 2:
\[
(4a + b) - (a + b) = 12 - 6 \implies 3a = 6 \implies a = 2
\]
Substituting \( a = 2 \) back into Equation 1:
\[
2 + b = 6 \implies b = 4
\]

Thus, the ordered pair \((a, b)\) is \((2, 4)\). Let's verify this solution by substituting \(a\) and \(b\) back into the original conditions to ensure the results are accurate. We'll use Python to confirm our solution.
