1. **Understand the Problem:**
    - We have a geometric progression \( b_1, b_2, \ldots, b_{3000} \).
    - Let the first term of the geometric progression be \( a \) and the common ratio be \( r \). Therefore, the \( n \)-th term of the geometric progression is given by \( b_n = ar^{n-1} \).
    - The sum \( S \) of all 3000 terms of this geometric progression is given by:
      \[
      S = a \frac{1 - r^{3000}}{1 - r} \quad \text{(for } r \neq 1\text{)}
      \]
    - If every term with an index that is a multiple of 3 (i.e., \( b_3, b_6, \ldots, b_{3000} \)) is increased by 50 times, the sum \( S \) increases by 10 times. Our task is to determine how \( S \) will change if every term in an even position (i.e., \( b_2, b_4, \ldots, b_{3000} \)) is increased by 2 times.

2. **Formulate the Problem with Mathematical Expressions:**
    - First, we express what happens when every term with an index that is a multiple of 3 is increased by 50 times. These terms are \( b_3, b_6, \ldots, b_{3000} \). This means the term \( b_{3k} \) is replaced with \( 50 b_{3k} \). Thus, the new sum \( S' \) becomes:
      \[
      S' = \sum_{n=1}^{3000} b_n' = \sum_{n=1, n \not\equiv 0 \pmod{3}}^{3000} b_n + \sum_{k=1}^{1000} 50 b_{3k}
      \]
      where \( b_{3k} = ar^{3k-1} \).
    - We know this new sum \( S' \) is 10 times the original sum \( S \):
      \[
      S' = 10 S
      \]
    - Substitute the expressions for \( S \) and \( S' \):
      \[
      S' = S + \sum_{k=1}^{1000} 50 ar^{3k-1} - \sum_{k=1}^{1000} ar^{3k-1}
      \]
      \[
      10 S = S + 49 \sum_{k=1}^{1000} ar^{3k-1}
      \]
      \[
      9 S = 49 \sum_{k=1}^{1000} ar^{3k-1}
      \]
      \[
      \sum_{k=1}^{1000} ar^{3k-1} = \frac{9 S}{49}
      \]

3. **Find How \( S \) Changes When Every Term in an Even Position Is Increased by 2 Times:**

    - The new sum \( S'' \) becomes:
      \[
      S'' = \sum_{n=1}^{3000} b_n'' = \sum_{n=1, n \text{ even}}^{3000} 2 b_n + \sum_{n=1, n \text{ odd}}^{3000} b_n
      \]
      where \( b_{2m} = ar^{2m-1} \) and \( b_{2m+1} = ar^{2m} \).
    - Let's define the sum of all odd-positioned terms and all even-positioned terms of the original geometric progression:
      \[
      S_{\text{odd}} = \sum_{m=0}^{1499} ar^{2m}

      \[

      \]
      So, the original sum \( S \) can be written as:
      \[
      S = S_{\text{odd}} + S_{\text{even}}
      \]
    - The new sum \( S'' \) will be:
      \[
      S'' = S_{\text{odd}} + 2 S_{\text{even}}
      \]
    - We need to express \( S_{\text{odd}} \) and \( S_{\text{even}} \) in a useful way. Note that:
      \[
      S_{\text{odd}} = a \frac{1 - (r^2)^{1500}}{1 - r^2}
      \]
      \[
      S_{\text{even}} = r \frac{1 - (r^2)^{1500}}{1 - r^2}
      \]
    - Substitute these into the expression for \( S'' \):
      \[
      S'' = a \frac{1 - (r^2)^{1500}}{1 - r^2} + 2 \cdot r \frac{1 - (r^2)^{1500}}{1 - r^2}
      \]
      \[
      S'' = \frac{1 - (r^2)^{1500}}{1 - r^2} \left( a + 2 r \right)
      \]
    - We need to compare this to the original sum \( S \):
      \[
      S = \frac{1 - (r^2)^{1500}}{1 - r^2} \cdot a

    - To find how \( S'' \) relates to \( S \), we can divide \( S'' \) by \( S \):
      \[

      \]
    - This means that:
      \[
      S'' = S \cdot \frac{a + 2 r}{a}
      \]
