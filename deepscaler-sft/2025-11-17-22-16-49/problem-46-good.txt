2. To get \( QT \), we can use the Pythagorean theorem since \( QU \), \( UT \), and \( QT \) form a right triangle. The relationship is given by:
   \[
   QT = \sqrt{QU^2 + UT^2}
   \]
   Substituting the given values:
   \[
   QT = \sqrt{(9 \sqrt{33})^2 + 40^2} = \sqrt{81 \cdot 33 + 1600} = \sqrt{2673 + 1600} = \sqrt{4273}
   \]

3. Now, we need to calculate \( QT^2 \):
   \[
   QT^2 = 4273
   \]

4. We then need to find the remainder when 4273 is divided by 100. This is simply the last two digits of 4273 because the remainder when a number is divided by 100 is the number formed by its last two digits. Therefore:
   \[
   4273 \mod 100 = 73
   \]

Let's confirm this by implementing it in Python to ensure the accuracy of our result.
```python
import math

# Given lengths
QU = 9 * math.sqrt(33)
UT = 40

# Calculate QT using the Pythagorean theorem
QT = math.sqrt(QU**2 + UT**2)

# Calculate QT^2
QT_squared = QT ** 2
```
