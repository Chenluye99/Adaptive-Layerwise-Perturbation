To solve the inequality \(\frac{1}{5}|9 + 2a| < 1\), let's follow these steps:

1. **Isolate the Absolute Value Expression:**
   Multiply both sides of the inequality by 5 to get:
   \[
   |9 + 2a| < 5
   \]

2. **Interpret the Absolute Value Inequality:**
   The inequality \(|9 + 2a| < 5\) means that the expression \(9 + 2a\) is between \(-5\) and \(5\). So, we can write:
   \[
   -5 < 9 + 2a < 5
   \]

3. **Solve the Compound Inequality:**
   To isolate \(a\), subtract 9 from all parts of the inequality:
   \[
   -5 - 9 < 2a < 5 - 9 \implies -14 < 2a < -4
   \]
   Next, divide all parts of the inequality by 2:
   \[
   -7 < a < -2
   \]

4. **Express the Answer in Interval Notation:**
   The solution in interval notation is \((-7, -2)\).
