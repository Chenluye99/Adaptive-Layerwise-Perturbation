To solve this problem, we need to consider the symmetries of the regular five pointed star, particularly the rotations and reflections that make some arrangements equivalent. The dihedral group \(D_5\) is the group of symmetries of a regular pentagon (and, by extension, of a regular five pointed star), which consists of 10 elements (5 rotations and 5 reflections).

### Step-by-Step Solution

1. **Count All Possible Arrangements Without Considering Symmetry:**
    - There are 10 distinct sea shells and 10 positions (5 outward and 5 inward points) to place these shells. The total number of ways to place these shells without considering reflections and rotations is \(10!\) (10 factorial).

2. **Account for Rotations and Reflections:**
    - The symmetric group of the 10 points (the dihedral group \(D_5\)) has 10 elements, meaning there are 10 different ways to rotate and reflect the star.
    - According to Burnside's Lemma, the number of distinct ways to place the shells, considering these 10 symmetries, is given by averaging the number of arrangements that remain invariant (i.e., look the same) under each of these 10 symmetries.

3. **Using Burnside's Lemma:**
    - **Identity (1 element):** All \(10!\) arrangements are invariant under the identity transformation.
    - **Rotations (4 elements, rotations by 72°, 144°, 216°, 288°):** For an arrangement to remain invariant under a rotation, the patterns of the shells at corresponding positions before and after the rotation must be the same. For a 5-point star, the only way to achieve this is if the shells are repeated every 5 positions (i.e., shells in position 1 must be in position 2 after a 72° rotation, position 2 must be in position 3, and so on, until position 5 returns to position 1). However, there are no ways to place 10 different shells such that the pattern repeats every 5 positions in a non-trivial way (i.e., different shells in corresponding points after rotation), so no arrangements remain invariant under these rotations.
    - **Reflections (5 elements, 5 axes of symmetry):** For an arrangement to remain invariant under a reflection, the shells must be mirrored across the axis of reflection. Each reflection swaps pairs of points symmetrically across the axis of reflection and fixes another point. For the arrangement to remain invariant, the shells in corresponding points around the axis of reflection must be the same when considering the mirrored positions and the fixed point. To preserve the condition that all 10 shells are different, there is only one way for each reflection to have an invariant arrangement, and that is by no change at all (but since we have different shells, this means no arrangement can remain invariant under a non-trivial reflection without repeating or having some shells in non-different positions, which is not possible with distinct shells). Thus, any distinct arrangement will not remain the same under these reflections either, except if we consider just the one that by chance aligns with these symmetries trivially (which does not exist with distinct shells).

    - Therefore, the only arrangements that remain invariant under the symmetries are those that coincide with the identity transformation (i.e., without rotating or reflecting, every different arrangement is changed by some rotation or reflection).

Given that rotations and reflections of an arrangement are considered equivalent, the only distinct arrangement we need to consider is the one counted by \(10!\) but then adjusted for the 10 symmetries of \(D_5\).

By Burnside's Lemma, the formula is given by:
\[
\text{Number of distinct arrangements} = \frac{1}{|G|} \sum_{g \in G} \text{Fix}(g)
\]
where \(|G| = 10\) (the order of \(D_5\)), and \(\text{Fix}(g)\) is the number of arrangements that are unchanged by the symmetry \(g\).

So, we get:
\[
\text{Number of distinct arrangements} = \frac{1}{10} \left( 1 \cdot 10! + 4 \cdot 0 + 5 \cdot 0 \right) = \frac{10!}{10} = \frac{3628800}{10} = 362880
\]
