- \( B = (0, 0) \)

# Define the variables
AD, BD, CD = 1, 4, sp.symbols('CD', positive=True)

# Using the power of a point theorem for point D on AC with respect to circle with diameter BC:
# For any point P on a line segment, the power of the point with respect to a circle is given by:
  - Power of point D = \( AD \cdot DC = BD \cdot DE \)
    - Where E is the second intersection of the secant line (which passes through B and D) with the circle.

# Considering that D lies on AC and BC is the diameter of the circle, point E will coincide with point C because D and C are intersection points of the circle and line AC, and DE = EC because E is the same point as C (as D is a different point on AC other than C).

# Therefore, the equation based on the property of the power of a point for D becomes:
  - \( AD \cdot DC = BD \cdot CD \)
# Substituting the known values into the equation:
  - \( 1 \cdot CD = 4 \cdot CD \)
  
This equation setup isn't leading to a straightforward solution because it appears to be a tautology. Let's correct this by reexamining using properties of right triangles and similar triangles.

Considering triangles \( \triangle ABD \) and \( \triangle DBC \) where \( \angle ADB = \angle CDB = 90^\circ \):

- Since \( \angle ADB = \angle CDB = 90^\circ \), and both triangles share \( \angle D \), triangles \( \triangle ABD \) and \( \triangle DBC \) are similar by AA (Angle-Angle) similarity criterion.
- This implies: \(\frac{AD}{BD} = \frac{BD}{CD}\).

So, substituting the known values:
  - \(\frac{1}{4} = \frac{4}{CD} \)

Cross-multiply to solve for \( CD \):
  - \( 1 \cdot CD = 4 \cdot 4 \)
  - \( CD = 16 \)
