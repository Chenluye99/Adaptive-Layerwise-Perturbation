To solve this problem, we will follow these steps:

1. **Define Initial Stock Value:**
   Let's assume the initial value of the stock is \( V \).

2. **Calculate the Value After the First Day:**
   On the first day, the stock decreases by 20%. So, the value of the stock at the end of the first day is:
   \[
   V_1 = V \cdot (1 - 0.20) = V \cdot 0.80
   \]

3. **Calculate the Value After the Second Day:**
   On the second day, the stock increases by 30% of its value at the end of the first day. So, the value of the stock at the end of the second day is:
   \[
   V_2 = V_1 \cdot (1 + 0.30) = V \cdot 0.80 \cdot 1.30
   \]

4. **Find the Overall Percent Increase:**
   To determine the overall percent increase over the two days, we need to compare the final value of the stock \( V_2 \) to its initial value \( V \). We can find the overall percent increase using:
   \[
   \text{Percent Increase} = \left( \frac{V_2 - V}{V} \right) \cdot 100 = \left( \frac{V \cdot 0.80 \cdot 1.30 - V}{V} \right) \cdot 100 = (0.80 \cdot 1.30 - 1) \cdot 100
   \]

Let's compute this step-by-step using Python to ensure accuracy.
```python
# Initial stock value (we can assume V = 1 for simplicity, as it will cancel out)
V = 1

# Calculate the value of the stock at the end of the first day
V_after_day_1 = V * (1 - 0.20)

# Calculate the value of the stock at the end of the second day
V_after_day_2 = V_after_day_1 * (1 + 0.30)

# Calculate the overall percent increase
percent_increase = ((V_after_day_2 - V) / V) * 100
print(percent_increase)
```
