To solve the problem of finding the constant term in the expansion of \(\left(\sqrt{x} + \frac{5}{x}\right)^9\), we will use the binomial theorem.

The binomial theorem states that:
\[
(a + b)^n = \sum_{k=0}^{n} \binom{n}{k} a^{n-k} b^k
\]
For \(\left(\sqrt{x} + \frac{5}{x}\right)^9\), we have \(a = \sqrt{x}\), \(b = \frac{5}{x}\), and \(n = 9\). So, the general term in the expansion is given by:
\[
T_k = \binom{9}{k} (\sqrt{x})^{9-k} \left(\frac{5}{x}\right)^k = \binom{9}{k} (\sqrt{x})^{9-k} \frac{5^k}{x^k} = \binom{9}{k} 5^k x^{\frac{9-k}{2} - k}
\]
We need to find the term where the exponent of \(x\) is zero (i.e., the constant term). Therefore, we set the exponent of \(x\) to zero:
\[
\frac{9 - k}{2} - k = 0
\]
Let's solve this equation for \(k\):
\[
\frac{9 - k}{2} = k \implies 9 - k = 2k \implies 9 = 3k \implies k = 3
\]
So, the constant term corresponds to \(k = 3\). Substituting \(k = 3\) into the general term, we get:
\[
T_3 = \binom{9}{3} 5^3 x^{\frac{9 - 3}{2} - 3} = \binom{9}{3} 5^3 x^{\frac{6}{2} - 3} = \binom{9}{3} 5^3 x^{3 - 3} = \binom{9}{3} 5^3 x^0 = \binom{9}{3} 5^3
\]
Now, we need to compute \(\binom{9}{3}\) and \(5^3\):
\[
\binom{9}{3} = \frac{9!}{3!(9-3)!} = \frac{9!}{3!6!} = \frac{9 \cdot 8 \cdot 7}{3 \cdot 2 \cdot 1} = 84
\]
\[
5^3 = 125
\]
Thus, the constant term is:
\[
T_3 = 84 \cdot 125
\]
