To solve this problem, we need to use the formula for the projection of vector \(\mathbf{v}\) onto vector \(\mathbf{w}\):

\[
\text{proj}_{\mathbf{w}} \mathbf{v} = \frac{\mathbf{v} \cdot \mathbf{w}}{\mathbf{w} \cdot \mathbf{w}} \mathbf{w}
\]

We are given:
\[
\mathbf{v} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ y \end{pmatrix}, \quad \mathbf{w} = \begin{pmatrix} 9 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix}, \quad \text{proj}_{\mathbf{w}} \mathbf{v} = \begin{pmatrix} -6 \\ -2 \end{pmatrix}
\]

First, let's compute the dot products:

1. Compute \(\mathbf{v} \cdot \mathbf{w}\):
\[
\mathbf{v} \cdot \mathbf{w} = 1 \cdot 9 + y \cdot 3 = 9 + 3y
\]

2. Compute \(\mathbf{w} \cdot \mathbf{w}\):
\[
\mathbf{w} \cdot \mathbf{w} = 9 \cdot 9 + 3 \cdot 3 = 81 + 9 = 90
\]

So, the projection \(\text{proj}_{\mathbf{w}} \mathbf{v}\) can be written as:
\[
\text{proj}_{\mathbf{w}} \mathbf{v} = \frac{9 + 3y}{90} \begin{pmatrix} 9 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} \frac{9 + 3y}{10} \cdot 9 \\ \frac{9 + 3y}{10} \cdot 3 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} \frac{81 + 27y}{10} \\ \frac{27 + 9y}{10} \end{pmatrix}
\]

We are given that:
\[
\text{proj}_{\mathbf{w}} \mathbf{v} = \begin{pmatrix} -6 \\ -2 \end{pmatrix}
\]

So, we can set up the following equations by equating the corresponding components:
\[
\frac{81 + 27y}{10} = -6 \quad \text{(1)}
\]
\[
\frac{27 + 9y}{10} = -2 \quad \text{(2)}
\]

Let's solve these equations step-by-step. We'll start with equation (1):

\[
\frac{81 + 27y}{10} = -6
\]

Multiply both sides by 10:
\[
81 + 27y = -60
\]

Rearrange to isolate \( y \):
\[
27y = -60 - 81
\]
\[
27y = -141
\]
\[
y = \frac{-141}{27} = \frac{-47}{9}
\]

To ensure that this value of \( y \) is consistent, we should substitute it into equation (2) and check if it holds true:

\[
\frac{27 + 9 \left( \frac{-47}{9} \right)}{10} = -2
\]

Simplify inside the numerator:
\[
27 + 9 \left( \frac{-47}{9} \right) = 27 - 47 = -20
\]

So, we have:
\[
\frac{-20}{10} = -2
\]

This is true, so our solution is consistent. We can box the final answer:

\[
y = \frac{-47}{9}
