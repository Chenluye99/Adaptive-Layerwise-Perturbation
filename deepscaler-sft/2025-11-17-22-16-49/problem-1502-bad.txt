First, let's outline what the problem is asking us to solve. We have a polynomial \( P(x) \). When \( P(x) \) is divided by \( x-13 \), the remainder is 19. When \( P(x) \) is divided by \( x+17 \), the remainder is -11.

We are asked to find the remainder when \( P(x) \) is divided by \((x-13)(x+17)\).

When dividing \( P(x) \) by \((x-13)(x+17)\), the remainder will be a polynomial of degree less than 2 (since \((x-13)(x+17)\) is a polynomial of degree 2). So, let's assume the remainder is of the form \( ax + b \). This means we can write:
\[ P(x) = (x-13)(x+17)q(x) + ax + b \]
for some polynomial \( q(x) \).

Given the information in the problem, we can plug in \( x = 13 \) and \( x = -17 \) into the equation above:

1. When \( x = 13 \):
\[ P(13) = a \cdot 13 + b = 19. \]
2. When \( x = -17 \):
\[ P(-17) = a \cdot (-17) + b = -11. \]

This gives us the following system of linear equations:
\[
\begin{cases}
13a + b = 19, \\
-17a + b = -11.
\end{cases}
\]

We can solve this system of equations to find \( a \) and \( b \). Let's subtract the second equation from the first:
\[
(13a + b) - (-17a + b) = 19 - (-11) \implies 13a + b + 17a - b = 30 \implies 30a = 30 \implies a = 1.
\]

Substitute \( a = 1 \) back into the first equation:
\[
13 \cdot 1 + b = 19 \implies 13 + b = 19 \implies b = 6.
\]

So, the remainder when \( P(x) \) is divided by \((x-13)(x+17)\) is \( x + 6 \). Let's confirm this solution using Python to ensure the result is accurate.
