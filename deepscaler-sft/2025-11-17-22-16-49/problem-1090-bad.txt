To determine the values of \( k \) for which the system of equations has a non-trivial solution, we need to check when the system has a solution other than the trivial solution \((x, y, z) = (0, 0, 0)\). A system of linear equations has a non-trivial solution if and only if its coefficient matrix is singular, i.e., its determinant is zero.

The system of equations is:
\[
\begin{align*}
x + ky - z &= 0, \\
kx - y - z &= 0, \\
x + y - kz &= 0
\end{align*}
\]

We can represent this system as an augmented matrix and find its determinant:
\[
\begin{pmatrix}
1 & k & -1 & 0 \\
k & -1 & -1 & 0 \\
1 & 1 & -k & 0
\end{pmatrix}
\]

The coefficient matrix of the system is:
\[
A = \begin{pmatrix}
1 & k & -1 \\
k & -1 & -1 \\
1 & 1 & -k
\end{pmatrix}
\]

We need to find the determinant of \( A \) and set it to 0:
\[
\text{det}(A) = \begin{vmatrix}
1 & k & -1 \\
k & -1 & -1 \\
1 & 1 & -k
\end{vmatrix} = 0
\]

The determinant of a 3x3 matrix is calculated as follows:
\[
\text{det}(A) = 1 \cdot \begin{vmatrix} -1 & -1 \\ 1 & -k \end{vmatrix} - k \cdot \begin{vmatrix} k & -1 \\ 1 & -k \end{vmatrix} + (-1) \cdot \begin{vmatrix} k & -1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{vmatrix}
\]

Let's compute these 2x2 determinants:
\[
\begin{vmatrix} -1 & -1 \\ 1 & -k \end{vmatrix} = (-1)(-k) - (-1)(1) = k + 1
\]
\[
\begin{vmatrix} k & -1 \\ 1 & -k \end{vmatrix} = (k)(-k) - (-1)(1) = -k^2 + 1
\]
\[
\begin{vmatrix} k & -1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{vmatrix} = (k)(1) - (-1)(1) = k + 1
\]

Substituting these back into the determinant formula, we get:
\[
\text{det}(A) = 1 \cdot (k + 1) - k \cdot (-k^2 + 1) - 1 \cdot (k + 1) = k + 1 + k^3 - k - k - 1 = k^3 - k
\]

So, we need to solve:
\[
k^3 - k = 0
\]

Factoring out \( k \):
\[
k(k^2 - 1) = 0 \implies k(k - 1)(k + 1) = 0
\]

Thus, the solutions are:
\[
k = 0, \, k = 1, \, k = -1
\]

Let's verify this by coding the solution in Python to ensure the accuracy.
```python
import sympy as sp

# Define the variables
k = sp.symbols('k')

# Define the coefficient matrix
matrix = sp.Matrix([
    [1, k, -1],
    [k, -1, -1],
    [1, 1, -k]
])

# Calculate the determinant of the matrix
det = matrix.det()

# Solve for k when the determinant is 0
solution = sp.solve(det, k)
print(solution)
```
Code execution result: [-1, 0, 1]

Thus, the values of \( k \) for which the system has a non-trivial solution are \(\boxed{-1, 0, 1}\). This confirms our previous analytical solution.
