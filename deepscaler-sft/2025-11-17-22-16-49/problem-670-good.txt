1. **Understand the Problem:**
    - We have two circles, one with a radius of 5 inches and the other with a radius of 2 inches.
    - The circles are tangent at point \( P \).
    - We have two bugs, one starting to crawl along the larger circle at a speed of \( 3\pi \) inches per minute, and the other starting to crawl along the smaller circle at a speed of \( 2.5\pi \) inches per minute.
    - We need to find out when they will next meet at point \( P \).

2. **Formulate the Problem Mathematically:**
    - The distance each bug travels around its respective circle before returning to point \( P \) can be calculated using the circumference of the circles.
    - The circumference of the larger circle with radius 5 inches is:
      \[
      C_{\text{large}} = 2 \pi \times 5 = 10 \pi \text{ inches}
      \]
    - The circumference of the smaller circle with radius 2 inches is:
      \[
      C_{\text{small}} = 2 \pi \times 2 = 4 \pi \text{ inches}
      \]
    - The time it takes for the first bug to complete one full revolution (and thus return to point \( P \)) is:
      \[
      T_{\text{large}} = \frac{10 \pi}{3 \pi} = \frac{10}{3} \text{ minutes}
      \]
    - The time it takes for the second bug to complete one full revolution (and thus return to point \( P \)) is:
      \[
      T_{\text{small}} = \frac{4 \pi}{2.5 \pi} = \frac{4}{2.5} = \frac{4 \times 2}{2.5 \times 2} = \frac{8}{5} \text{ minutes}
      \]

3. **Find the Least Common Multiple (LCM):**
    - To find when both bugs will be back at point \( P \) simultaneously, we need to determine the least common multiple (LCM) of \(\frac{10}{3}\) and \(\frac{8}{5}\).
    - To get a common frame for comparison, convert these fractions to a common denominator or find their LCM by converting them to the same unit over integers.
    - Find the LCM of \(\frac{10}{3}\) and \(\frac{8}{5}\):
      \[
      T_{\text{large}} = \frac{10}{3}, \quad T_{\text{small}} = \frac{8}{5}
      \]
      To compare these easily, we get the LCM considering their denominators and numerators in their whole number equivalent cycles:
      - LCM of \(\frac{10}{3}\): Find when \( \frac{10}{3} \cdot k = \frac{8}{5} \cdot m \) for integers \( k \) and \( m \).

4. **Computational Solution:**
    - To ensure accuracy, use Python to solve this by finding the smallest positive time at which both bugs meet back at point \( P \).

Let's implement this step-by-step in Python to determine the correct answer.
```python
import math

# Circumferences of the circles
circumference_large = 2 * math.pi * 5
circumference_small = 2 * math.pi * 2

# Speeds of the bugs
speed_bug_large = 3 * math.pi
speed_bug_small = 2.5 * math.pi

# Time taken for each bug to complete one full revolution and return to point P
time_for_large_bug = circumference_large / speed_bug_large
time_for_small_bug = circumference_small / speed_bug_small

# We need to find the smallest time after which both bugs are at point P simultaneously
# This is the least common multiple (LCM) of the times it takes each bug to return to point P

def lcm(x, y):
    return abs(x * y) // math.gcd(int(x), int(y))

# Convert the times to fractions and get their LCM
time_for_large_bug_fraction = (10, 3)
time_for_small_bug_fraction = (8, 5)

# Find the LCM of these fractions considering their times as periods
lcm_value = lcm(time_for_large_bug_fraction[0] * time_for_small_bug_fraction[1],
                time_for_large_bug_fraction[1] * time_for_small_bug_fraction[0]) / \
            (time_for_large_bug_fraction[1] * time_for_small_bug_fraction[1])

print(lcm_value)
```

Thus, the bugs will meet again at point \( P \) after \(\boxed{40}\) minutes. This is the final answer.
