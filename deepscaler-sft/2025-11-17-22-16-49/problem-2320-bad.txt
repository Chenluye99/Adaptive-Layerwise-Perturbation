First, let's define the problem more clearly. We have a rectangular field that measures 24 meters by 52 meters, and we want to partition it into congruent square test plots. Let's assume that each square plot has a side length of \( s \) meters. 

For the field to be partitioned into squares, both 24 and 52 must be divisible by \( s \). Hence, \( s \) must be a common divisor of 24 and 52. 

First, we find the common divisors of 24 and 52. The divisors of 24 are \( 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 24 \), and the divisors of 52 are \( 1, 2, 4, 13, 26, 52 \). Therefore, the common divisors of 24 and 52 are \( 1, 2, 4 \). So, the possible side lengths for the squares are 1, 2, and 4 meters.

Next, for each possible side length of the square plots, we determine how many such squares can fit into the field and how much internal fencing will be needed. The internal fencing would include horizontal and vertical fences that partition the field into smaller squares.

For a square plot of side length \( s \):
- The field will need to be partitioned into \(\frac{24}{s}\) squares along the 24-meter side and \(\frac{52}{s}\) squares along the 52-meter side.
- The total number of square test plots will be \(\frac{24}{s} \times \frac{52}{s}\).

The internal fencing required will consist of additional fences placed inside the field to create the smaller squares. To determine this, consider that:
- To create \(\frac{24}{s}\) squares along the 24-meter side, we need \(\frac{24}{s} - 1\) vertical fences, and each of these fences will be 52 meters long.
- To create \(\frac{52}{s}\) squares along the 52-meter side, we need \(\frac{52}{s} - 1\) horizontal fences, and each of these fences will be 24 meters long.

Thus, the total length of the internal fencing required will be:
\[ \text{Total internal fencing} = \left( \frac{24}{s} - 1 \right) \times 52 + \left( \frac{52}{s} - 1 \right) \times 24 \]

We need this total length to be less than or equal to 1994 meters. We will calculate this for \( s = 4, 2, 1 \) (since 4 is the largest of the common divisors), and find which of these gives the maximum number of square plots while using no more than 1994 meters of fencing.

Let's implement this step-by-step in Python to get the accurate result.
```python
max_squares = 0
best_s = 0

# Check for each possible side length s (4, 2, 1)
for s in [4, 2, 1]:
    num_squares_along_24 = 24 // s
    num_squares_along_52 = 52 // s
    total_squares = num_squares_along_24 * num_squares_along_52
    
    # Calculate the required internal fencing
    internal_fencing = (num_squares_along_24 - 1) * 52 + (num_squares_along_52 - 1) * 24
    
    # Check if this internal fencing is within the limit of 1994 meters
    if internal_fencing <= 1994:
        if total_squares > max_squares:
            max_squares = total_squares
            best_s = s

print((max_squares, best_s))
```
