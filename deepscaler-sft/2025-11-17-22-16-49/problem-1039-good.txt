To solve the inequality \(\frac{x}{x + 3} \ge 0\), we need to determine the values of \(x\) for which the expression \(\frac{x}{x + 3}\) is non-negative (i.e., greater than or equal to zero).

1. **Find the critical points:**
    - The expression \(\frac{x}{x + 3}\) is zero when the numerator is zero, i.e., \(x = 0\).
    - The expression \(\frac{x}{x + 3}\) is undefined when the denominator is zero, i.e., \(x = -3\).

2. **Determine the sign of \(\frac{x}{x + 3}\) in the intervals defined by these critical points:**
    - The critical points divide the real number line into three intervals: \((-\infty, -3)\), \((-3, 0)\), and \((0, \infty)\). We also need to consider the points \(-3\) and \(0\) themselves.

3. **Test the sign of \(\frac{x}{x + 3}\) in each interval:**
    - For \((-\infty, -3)\), choose a test point, say \(x = -4\):
      \[
      \frac{-4}{-4 + 3} = \frac{-4}{-1} = 4 \quad \text{(which is greater than 0)}
      \]
    - For \((-3, 0)\), choose a test point, say \(x = -1\):
      \[
      \frac{-1}{-1 + 3} = \frac{-1}{2} = -\frac{1}{2} \quad \text{(which is less than 0)}
      \]
    - For \((0, \infty)\), choose a test point, say \(x = 1\):
      \[
      \frac{1}{1 + 3} = \frac{1}{4} \quad \text{(which is greater than 0)}
      \]

4. **Consider the critical points themselves:**
    - At \(x = 0\):
      \[
      \frac{0}{0 + 3} = \frac{0}{3} = 0 \quad \text{(which is equal to 0 and satisfies the inequality)}
      \]
    - At \(x = -3\):
      \[
      \frac{-3}{-3 + 3} = \frac{-3}{0} \quad \text{(which is undefined and does not satisfy the inequality)}
      \]

From this analysis, we see that \(\frac{x}{x + 3} \ge 0\) when:
- \(x \in (-\infty, -3) \cup [0, \infty)\).

Thus, the solution in interval notation is \((- \infty, -3) \cup [0, \infty)\).
