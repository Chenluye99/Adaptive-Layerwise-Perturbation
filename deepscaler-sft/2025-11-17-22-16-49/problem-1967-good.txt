First, let's start by setting up the problem and solving it step-by-step.

1. **Understand the Problem:**
    - We have a bag with 10 red marbles and 6 blue marbles, making a total of 16 marbles.
    - We need to select 3 marbles at random and without replacement.
    - We want to find the probability that exactly 1 marble is red and 2 marbles are blue.

2. **Formulate the Solution:**
    - The number of ways to choose 1 red marble out of 10 is given by \(\binom{10}{1}\).
    - The number of ways to choose 2 blue marbles out of 6 is given by \(\binom{6}{2}\).
    - The total number of ways to choose 3 marbles out of 16 is given by \(\binom{16}{3}\).

    The probability is then calculated by:
    \[
    P(\text{1 red, 2 blue}) = \frac{\binom{10}{1} \cdot \binom{6}{2}}{\binom{16}{3}}
    \]

3. **Calculate the Binomial Coefficients:**
    - \(\binom{10}{1} = 10\)
    - \(\binom{6}{2} = \frac{6!}{2!(6-2)!} = \frac{6 \cdot 5}{2 \cdot 1} = 15\)
    - \(\binom{16}{3} = \frac{16!}{3!(16-3)!} = \frac{16 \cdot 15 \cdot 14}{3 \cdot 2 \cdot 1} = 560\)

4. **Substitute and Compute the Probability:**
    \[
    P(\text{1 red, 2 blue}) = \frac{10 \cdot 15}{560} = \frac{150}{560} = \frac{15}{56}
    \]
