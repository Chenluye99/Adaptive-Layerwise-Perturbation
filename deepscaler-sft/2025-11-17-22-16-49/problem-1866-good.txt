The formula for the volume \( V \) of a tetrahedron when the areas of two faces, the common edge, and the angle between the faces are known is:
\[ V = \frac{abc}{6} \cdot \frac{\sin \theta}{1} = \frac{1}{6} \cdot a \cdot b \cdot c \cdot \sin \theta \]
where \( a, b, c \) are the lengths of the edges of the tetrahedron forming the two faces, and \( \theta \) is the angle between those faces.

However, it is more convenient to use the following formula that directly involves the areas of the faces and the angle between them:
\[ V = \frac{1}{6} \cdot BC \cdot h_{ABC} \cdot h_{BCD} \cdot \sin \theta \]

Where:
- \( BC \) is the common edge of faces \( ABC \) and \( BCD \).
- \( h_{ABC} \) and \( h_{BCD} \) are the heights of faces \( ABC \) and \( BCD \) corresponding to edge \( BC \).

First, we need to compute the heights \( h_{ABC} \) and \( h_{BCD} \). The area of a triangle can be given by the formula:
\[ \text{Area} = \frac{1}{2} \cdot \text{base} \cdot \text{height} \]

For face \( ABC \):
\[ 120 = \frac{1}{2} \cdot BC \cdot h_{ABC} \implies 120 = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 10 \cdot h_{ABC} \implies 120 = 5 \cdot h_{ABC} \implies h_{ABC} = \frac{120}{5} = 24 \]

\[ 80 = \frac{1}{2} \cdot BC \cdot h_{BCD} \implies 80 = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 10 \cdot h_{BCD} \implies 80 = 5 \cdot h_{BCD} \implies h_{BCD} = \frac{80}{5} = 16 \]

\[ V = \frac{1}{6} \cdot BC \cdot h_{ABC} \cdot h_{BCD} \cdot \sin(30^\circ) = \frac{1}{6} \cdot 10 \cdot 24 \cdot 16 \cdot \frac{1}{2} \]
