To solve this problem, we need to consider both the principles of conservation of momentum and kinematics.

1. **Initial Set-up:**
    - The firecracker is thrown vertically upward with an initial velocity of 20 m/s.
    - Three seconds after it is thrown, it explodes.
    - At \( t = 3 \) seconds, the velocity of the firecracker before the explosion is given by the equation of motion \( v_y = v_0 - g t \), where \( v_0 = 20 \) m/s and \( g = 10 \) m/s².
    - Substituting the values, we get:
      \[
      v_y = 20 - 10 \cdot 3 = -10 \text{ m/s}
      \]
      So, the speed of the firecracker 3 seconds after it is thrown is 10 m/s, but it is directed downwards (hence, the negative sign).

2. **Conservation of Momentum:**
    - Let's denote the mass of the smaller fragment as \( m \) and the mass of the larger fragment as \( 2m \) (since their mass ratio is 1:2).
    - Just before the explosion, the momentum of the firecracker in the vertical direction is \( m_{\text{total}} \cdot v_y = 3m \cdot (-10) = -30m \).
    - In the horizontal direction, the momentum of the firecracker is 0 because it is thrown vertically.
    - After the explosion, the smaller fragment flies horizontally with a speed of 16 m/s. Since it is flying horizontally, its vertical component of velocity is 0.
    - Let's denote the velocity of the smaller fragment just after the explosion as \( \mathbf{v}_{\text{small}} = 16 \hat{i} + 0 \hat{j} \).
    - Let the velocity of the larger fragment just after the explosion be \( \mathbf{v}_{\text{large}} = v_{\text{large},x} \hat{i} + v_{\text{large},y} \hat{j} \).

3. **Horizontal and Vertical Momentum Conservation:**
    - **Horizontal momentum conservation:**
      \[
      m \cdot 16 + 2m \cdot v_{\text{large},x} = 0 \implies 16 + 2 v_{\text{large},x} = 0 \implies 2 v_{\text{large},x} = -16 \implies v_{\text{large},x} = -8
      \]
    - **Vertical momentum conservation:**
      \[
      m \cdot 0 + 2m \cdot v_{\text{large},y} = -30m \implies 2 v_{\text{large},y} = -30 \implies v_{\text{large},y} = -15
      \]

4. **Find the Magnitude of the Speed of the Larger Fragment:**
    - The speed of the larger fragment is given by the magnitude of its velocity vector:
      \[
      v_{\text{large}} = \sqrt{v_{\text{large},x}^2 + v_{\text{large},y}^2} = \sqrt{(-8)^2 + (-15)^2} = \sqrt{64 + 225} = \sqrt{289} = 17
      \]
