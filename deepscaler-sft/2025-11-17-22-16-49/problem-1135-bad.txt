1. **Understand the Equation:**
    - The equation \( r = \cos \theta \) is in polar coordinates, where \( r \) is the radius (or distance from the origin) and \( \theta \) is the angle.
    - To trace a complete circle, we need to explore the range of \( \theta \) such that \( r \) traces out all points on the circle exactly once and then repeats itself.

2. **Conversion to Cartesian Coordinates:**
    - In Cartesian coordinates, \( r = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2} \) and \( \cos \theta = \frac{x}{\sqrt{x^2 + y^2}} \).
    - Substituting \( r = \cos \theta \) into these equations, we get:
      \[
      \sqrt{x^2 + y^2} = \frac{x}{\sqrt{x^2 + y^2}}
      \]
    - Squaring both sides, we obtain:
      \[
      x^2 + y^2 = \frac{x^2}{x^2 + y^2}
      \]
    - Multiplying through by \( x^2 + y^2 \) gives:
      \[
      (x^2 + y^2)^2 = x^2
      \]
    - Expanding and rearranging terms, we get a circle centered at \((\frac{1}{2}, 0)\) with radius \(\frac{1}{2}\):
      \[
      x^2 + y^2 = \cos^2 \theta
      \]
      \[
      x = r \cos \theta = \cos^2 \theta
      \]
      \[
      y = r \sin \theta = \cos \theta \sin \theta
      \]

3. **Determine the Range of \(\theta\):**
    - To trace out the full circle, \(\theta\) needs to be over an interval that maps \( r = \cos \theta \) back to its starting point or to cover the full periodicity. 
    - Notice that \(\cos \theta\) is periodic with a period of \(2\pi\), but we need to determine the smallest \( t \) such that the entire circle is traced out.
