Let's begin by understanding the geometric properties of a regular hexagon. A regular hexagon can be divided into 6 equilateral triangles, making all its sides of equal length.

For a regular hexagon with side length \( s \):

1. **Diagonals of a Regular Hexagon:**
    - **Longer Diagonals (Passing Through Center):** These diagonals pass through the center of the hexagon and connect two vertices that are three steps apart (i.e., skipping one vertex). The length of these diagonals is equal to twice the side length of the hexagon, so it is \( 2s \).
    - **Shorter Diagonals (Not Passing Through Center):** These diagonals connect two vertices that are two steps apart (i.e., skipping two vertices in between). The length of these diagonals is equal to the length of the sides of the equilateral triangles that make up the hexagon, but extended to connect these specific pairs of vertices. By properties of regular hexagons, the length of these diagonals is \( s \sqrt{3} \) (this can be derived using geometry or trigonometry).

2. **Find the Ratio:**
    - We want the ratio of the shorter length of the diagonals to the longer length of the diagonals. So, we need the ratio of \( s \sqrt{3} \) to \( 2s \).

By simplifying this ratio:

\[
\text{Ratio} = \frac{s \sqrt{3}}{2s} = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}
\]

Let's confirm this with a bit of code to ensure the result is accurate. We can implement this reasoning through simple symbolic computation to verify.
```python
import sympy as sp

# Define the side length of the hexagon (it can be any value, as it will cancel out in the ratio)
s = sp.symbols('s', positive=True)

# Lengths of the diagonals
shorter_diagonal = s * sp.sqrt(3)
longer_diagonal = 2 * s

# Calculate the ratio of the shorter diagonal length to the longer diagonal length
ratio = shorter_diagonal / longer_diagonal
print(ratio.simplify())
```
Code execution result: sqrt(3)/2

Thus, the ratio of the shorter length to the longer length of the diagonals of a regular hexagon is \(\boxed{\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}}\). This confirms our previous geometric reasoning and calculation.
