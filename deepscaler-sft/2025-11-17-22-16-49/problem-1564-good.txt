To solve this problem, we need to determine the number of ways to get from point \( C \) to point \( D \) on the grid, moving only down or to the right.

From observing the grid:

- Point \( C \) is at \((0, 6)\).
- Point \( D \) is at \((4, 0)\).

To get from \( C \) to \( D \), we need to make a total of 4 steps to the right and 6 steps down, in any order.

This is a combinatorial problem where we need to find the number of distinct sequences of 4 right (R) moves and 6 down (D) moves. This is given by the binomial coefficient \(\binom{10}{4}\) (or equivalently \(\binom{10}{6}\)), which is the number of ways to choose 4 positions out of 10 for the right moves (the remaining 6 will automatically be down moves).

Mathematically, this is represented as:
\[
\binom{10}{4} = \frac{10!}{4!(10-4)!} = \frac{10!}{4!6!}
\]

Let's compute this using Python to ensure accuracy.
```python
import math

# Calculate the binomial coefficient C(10, 4)
num_paths = math.comb(10, 4)
print(num_paths)
```
