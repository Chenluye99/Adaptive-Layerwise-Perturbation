1. **Understand the Problem:**
    - We have a dihedral angle, and two spheres are inscribed in this dihedral angle such that they touch each other.
    - Let the radius of the smaller sphere be \(r_1\) and the radius of the larger sphere be \(r_2 = 1.5 r_1\).
    - The centers of the two spheres and the point of tangency of the spheres form a straight line. So, the distance between the centers of the two spheres is \(r_1 + r_2 = r_1 + 1.5 r_1 = 2.5 r_1\).
    - The line connecting the centers of the spheres forms an angle of \(30^\circ\) with the edge of the dihedral angle. Let the dihedral angle have an angle of \(\theta\). When we consider the geometry in 3D, the angle between the line connecting the centers of the spheres and the edge of the dihedral angle can be related to the angle \(\theta\) of the dihedral angle.

    - The cosine of the angle between the line connecting the centers of the spheres and the edge of the dihedral angle is \(\cos(30^\circ) = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\).

Let's go ahead and implement this reasoning in a more accurate computational manner, using Python. We need to solve the trigonometric relationship involving the dihedral angle and the cosine of half that angle relating it to the given geometric constraints.
```python
import sympy as sp

# Define the variables
r = sp.symbols('r', positive=True)
theta = sp.symbols('theta', positive=True)

# The radius of the smaller sphere is r, and the radius of the larger sphere is 1.5 * r.
# The distance between the centers of the spheres is 2.5 * r.
# The cosine of the angle between the line connecting the centers of the spheres and the edge of the dihedral angle is cos(30°) = sqrt(3) / 2.
cos_30 = sp.sqrt(3) / 2

# We know that the angle between the line connecting the centers of the spheres and the edge of the dihedral angle is 30 degrees, and we consider the relation to the dihedral angle.
# The relationship can be described as involving the cosine of half the dihedral angle, so we set up the equation:
# cos(theta / 2) = cos_30, i.e., cos(theta / 2) = sqrt(3) / 2.
cos_theta_over_2 = sp.cos(theta / 2)
eq = sp.Eq(cos_theta_over_2, cos_30)

# Solve for theta
solution = sp.solve(eq, theta)
print(solution)
```
Code execution result: [pi/3, 11*pi/3]

It's clear that \(\frac{\pi}{3}\) is the correct solution because \(\frac{11\pi}{3}\) is not a valid angle for a dihedral angle since it would be greater than \(2\pi\). 

So, the valid solution is \(\theta = \frac{\pi}{3}\). The cosine of this angle is:

\[
\cos \left( \frac{\pi}{3} \right) = \frac{1}{2}
\]

Let's confirm by rounding \(\frac{1}{2}\) to two decimal places if necessary, but it is already in its simplest and most accurate form without any need for rounding. So, the final answer is:
