To solve this problem, we want to maximize the expression

\[ \left|x_{1}-1\right|+\left|x_{2}-2\right|+\ldots+\left|x_{63}-63\right|. \]

This can be achieved by permuting the numbers 1 to 63 in such a way that the values inside the absolute functions are as far from their respective indices as possible. The maximum value for each term \( |x_i - i| \) will be maximized if \( x_i \) is chosen to be the smallest or largest number that isn't already assigned to previous terms in ways that minimize the values of other terms.

1. **Initialize the Numbers and Indices:**
   - We need to consider the numbers from 1 to 63 and assign them to \( x_1, x_2, \ldots, x_{63} \) in such a way that maximizes the sum of absolute differences.

2. **Construct a Permutation:**
   - One effective way to ensure maximum values for each term is to assign the smallest numbers to the indices with the highest offsets and the largest numbers to the indices with the lowest offsets. For example, assign 1 to 63, 2 to 62, 3 to 61, and so on, as this will spread out the differences.

3. **Calculate the Sum of Absolute Differences:**
   - Using the constructed permutation, calculate the value of the expression above.

# Create a list of numbers from 1 to 63
