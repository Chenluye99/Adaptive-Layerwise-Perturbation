First, let's start by understanding the geometric relationship between the larger square and the smaller square in the problem.

1. **Find the side length of the larger square:**
    - The area of the larger square is given as 60.
    - If the side length of the larger square is \( s \), then the area of the larger square is \( s^2 = 60 \). So, \( s = \sqrt{60} = 2 \sqrt{15} \).

2. **Locate Points \( A, B, C, \) and \( D \):**
    - Points \( A, B, C, \) and \( D \) are midpoints of the sides of the larger square. If we place the larger square in the coordinate system such that its vertices are at \((\pm s/2, \pm s/2)\), then:
      - \( A \) is the midpoint of one side, so its coordinates can be something like \((s/2, 0)\).
      - \( B \) is the midpoint of another side, so its coordinates can be something like \((0, s/2)\).
      - \( C \) is the midpoint of another side, so its coordinates can be something like \((-s/2, 0)\).
      - \( D \) is the midpoint of the fourth side, so its coordinates can be something like \((0, -s/2)\).

3. **Form the Smaller Square:**
    - The smaller square is formed by connecting the points \( A, B, C, \) and \( D \).
    - Notice that the vertices of the smaller square are the midpoints of the sides of the larger square, and the side length of the smaller square is the distance between, for example, \( A \) and \( B \).

4. **Find the Side Length of the Smaller Square:**
    - Let's assume the larger square is aligned with the coordinate axes and has vertices at \((\pm s/2, \pm s/2)\).
    - Then, the coordinates for points \( A, B, C, D \) would be:
      - \( A = (s/2, 0) \)
      - \( B = (0, s/2) \)
      - \( C = (-s/2, 0) \)
      - \( D = (0, -s/2) \)
    - The side length of the smaller square is the distance between, for example, points \( A \) and \( B \):
      - The distance between \( A = (s/2, 0) \) and \( B = (0, s/2) \) is given by the distance formula:
        \[
        \text{distance} = \sqrt{\left( \frac{s}{2} - 0 \right)^2 + \left( 0 - \frac{s}{2} \right)^2} = \sqrt{\left( \frac{s}{2} \right)^2 + \left( \frac{s}{2} \right)^2} = \sqrt{2 \left( \frac{s}{2} \right)^2} = \frac{s}{\sqrt{2}} = \frac{s \sqrt{2}}{2}
        \]
    - So, the side length of the smaller square is \( \frac{s \sqrt{2}}{2} \).

5. **Find the Area of the Smaller Square:**
    - If the side length of the smaller square is \( \frac{s \sqrt{2}}{2} \), then the area of the smaller square is:
      \[
      \left( \frac{s \sqrt{2}}{2} \right)^2 = \frac{s^2 \cdot 2}{4} = \frac{2 s^2}{4} = \frac{s^2}{2}
      \]
    - Since \( s^2 = 60 \) (the area of the larger square), we substitute \( s^2 \) with 60:
      \[
      \text{Area of the smaller square} = \frac{60}{2} = 30
      \]
