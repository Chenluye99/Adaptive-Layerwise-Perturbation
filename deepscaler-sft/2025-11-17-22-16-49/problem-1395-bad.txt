Given that the polynomial equation \(x^3 + bx + c = 0\) has rational coefficients, the complex roots (if any) or irrational roots (like \(5 - \sqrt{2}\)) must occur in conjugate pairs. So, if \(5 - \sqrt{2}\) is a root, then its conjugate \(5 + \sqrt{2}\) must also be a root.

Let the integer root be \( r \). By the factor theorem, if \( r \) is a root of the polynomial, then \((x - r)\) is a factor of the polynomial. Similarly, since \( 5 - \sqrt{2} \) and \( 5 + \sqrt{2} \) are roots, \((x - (5 - \sqrt{2}))(x - (5 + \sqrt{2}))\) is also a factor of the polynomial.

\[
(x - (5 - \sqrt{2}))(x - (5 + \sqrt{2})) = (x - 5 + \sqrt{2})(x - 5 - \sqrt{2}) = (x - 5)^2 - (\sqrt{2})^2 = (x - 5)^2 - 2 = x^2 - 10x + 25 - 2 = x^2 - 10x + 23
\]

So, the polynomial can be written as:

\[
(x - r)(x^2 - 10x + 23) = x^3 - (r + 10)x^2 + (23r + 10r)x - 23r
\]

\[
-(r + 10) = 0 \implies r + 10 = 0 \implies r = -10
\]

\[
(x - (-10))(x^2 - 10x + 23) = (x + 10)(x^2 - 10x + 23)
