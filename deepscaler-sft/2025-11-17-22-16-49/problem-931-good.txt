To solve the problem of finding the coefficient of \(x^2\) in the expansion of \(\left(x + \frac{1}{x}\right)^6\), we can use the binomial theorem.

The binomial theorem states that:
\[
(a + b)^n = \sum_{k=0}^{n} \binom{n}{k} a^{n-k} b^k
\]
In this case, \(a = x\), \(b = \frac{1}{x}\), and \(n = 6\). So, the expansion of \(\left(x + \frac{1}{x}\right)^6\) is:
\[
\sum_{k=0}^{6} \binom{6}{k} x^{6-k} \left(\frac{1}{x}\right)^k = \sum_{k=0}^{6} \binom{6}{k} x^{6-k} x^{-k} = \sum_{k=0}^{6} \binom{6}{k} x^{6-2k}
\]
We need to find the term where the exponent of \(x\) is 2. That is, we need to solve for \(k\) in the equation:
\[
6 - 2k = 2
\]
Solving for \(k\):
\[
6 - 2k = 2 \implies 4 = 2k \implies k = 2
\]
So, the term corresponding to \(k = 2\) is:
\[
\binom{6}{2} x^{6 - 2 \cdot 2} = \binom{6}{2} x^2
\]
The coefficient of \(x^2\) is \(\binom{6}{2}\). We can compute \(\binom{6}{2}\) as follows:
\[
\binom{6}{2} = \frac{6!}{2!(6-2)!} = \frac{6!}{2! \cdot 4!} = \frac{6 \cdot 5}{2 \cdot 1} = 15
\]
Thus, the coefficient of \(x^2\) in the expansion of \(\left(x + \frac{1}{x}\right)^6\) is 15. Let's verify this using Python to ensure accuracy.
