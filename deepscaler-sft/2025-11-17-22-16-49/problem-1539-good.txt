To solve the problem of finding the greatest common divisor (GCD) of 957 and 1537, we can use the Euclidean algorithm. This algorithm is efficient and involves a series of division steps in which the divisor in each step becomes the dividend in the next step, and the remainder becomes the new divisor. The process continues until the remainder is zero. The divisor at that step will be the GCD.

The steps for the Euclidean algorithm can be summarized as follows:

1. Divide 1537 by 957 to get a quotient and a remainder.
2. Replace 1537 with 957 and 957 with the remainder from the previous step.
3. Repeat the process until the remainder is 0. The non-zero remainder just before this point will be the GCD.

Let's implement this in Python to get the accurate result.
```python
def gcd(a, b):
    while b:
        a, b = b, a % b
    return a

# Find GCD of 957 and 1537
gcd_result = gcd(957, 1537)
print(gcd_result)
```
Code execution result: 29

Thus, the greatest common divisor (GCD) of 957 and 1537 is \(\boxed{29}\). This is our final answer.
