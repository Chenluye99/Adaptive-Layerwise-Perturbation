1. **Problem Understanding:**
    - The cook starts with 10 red peppers and 5 green peppers, so there are a total of 15 peppers.
    - We need to find the probability that the cook selects at least 4 green peppers when he randomly selects 6 peppers from these 15 peppers.

2. **Formulating Solutions Mathematically:**
    - To have at least 4 green peppers in the 6 selected peppers, the possible number of green peppers in the selection could be 4, 5, or 6.
    - We need to calculate the combinations for each of these cases (i.e., selecting 4, 5, or 6 green peppers while selecting the remaining peppers as red peppers).

3. **Using Combinatorial Methods:**
    - The number of ways to choose 6 peppers out of 15 is given by \(\binom{15}{6}\).
    - The number of ways to choose 4 green peppers out of 5 and 2 red peppers out of 10 is given by \(\binom{5}{4} \cdot \binom{10}{2}\).
    - The number of ways to choose 5 green peppers out of 5 and 1 red pepper out of 10 is given by \(\binom{5}{5} \cdot \binom{10}{1}\).
    - The number of ways to choose 6 green peppers out of 5 and 0 red peppers out of 10 is given by \(\binom{5}{6} \cdot \binom{10}{0}\), but this is 0 because it's impossible to choose 6 green peppers when there are only 5 green peppers available.

4. **Combining the Above Cases:**
    - We only need to consider the cases where the number of green peppers selected is 4, 5, or exactly 6 (which we know is 0 because it's impossible to choose 6 green peppers when there are only 5 green peppers available). So, we only need to consider up to 5 green peppers.

So, we need to compute:
\[ P(\text{at least 4 green peppers}) = \frac{\binom{5}{4} \cdot \binom{10}{2} + \binom{5}{5} \cdot \binom{10}{1}}{\binom{15}{6}}. \]
