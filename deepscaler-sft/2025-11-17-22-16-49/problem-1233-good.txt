Let's start by analyzing the given problem.

We need to determine all values of $m$ such that the conic section described by the equation $x^2 + my^2 = 4$ has its foci on the circle $x^2 + y^2 = 16$.

1. **Rewrite the Conic Section Equation:**
   Rewrite the equation $x^2 + my^2 = 4$ in a more standard form by dividing both sides by 4:
   \[
   \frac{x^2}{4} + \frac{my^2}{4} = 1 \implies \frac{x^2}{4} + \frac{y^2}{\frac{4}{m}} = 1
   \]
   This represents an ellipse if $m > 0$ and a hyperbola if $m < 0$.
   
   For it to be a conic section with two foci, we consider both cases but focus on the ellipse case because hyperbolas do not have real foci unless $m > 0$.

2. **Properties of Ellipses:**
   - For an ellipse of the form $\frac{x^2}{a^2} + \frac{y^2}{b^2} = 1$, the foci are located at $(\pm c, 0)$ where $c = \sqrt{|a^2 - b^2|}$.
   - Here, we have $a^2 = 4$ and $b^2 = \frac{4}{m}$, so $a = 2$ and $b = \frac{2}{\sqrt{m}}$ (provided $m > 0$).

   The positions of the foci for this ellipse are given by $c = \sqrt{|4 - \frac{4}{m}|} = 2 \sqrt{|1 - \frac{1}{m}|}$. The foci of the ellipse are at $(\pm 2 \sqrt{|1 - \frac{1}{m}|}, 0)$.

3. **Condition for Foci to Lie on Circle $x^2 + y^2 = 16$:**
   - For the foci of the ellipse to lie on the circle $x^2 + y^2 = 16$, the foci $(\pm 2 \sqrt{|1 - \frac{1}{m}|}, 0)$ must satisfy the equation of the circle.
   - Substituting the foci into the circle equation, we get:
     \[
     (2 \sqrt{|1 - \frac{1}{m}|})^2 + 0^2 = 16 \implies 4 \cdot |1 - \frac{1}{m}| = 16 \implies |1 - \frac{1}{m}| = 4
     \]
   - This absolute value equation results in two cases:
     \[
     1 - \frac{1}{m} = 4 \quad \text{or} \quad 1 - \frac{1}{m} = -4
     \]
     Let's solve these equations for $m$.

4. **Solve for $m$:**
   - For the first case: 
     \[
     1 - \frac{1}{m} = 4 \implies -\frac{1}{m} = 3 \implies \frac{1}{m} = -3 \implies m = -\frac{1}{3}
     \]
   - For the second case:
     \[
     1 - \frac{1}{m} = -4 \implies -\frac{1}{m} = -5 \implies \frac{1}{m} = 5 \implies m = \frac{1}{5}
     \]

Both solutions, $m = -\frac{1}{3}$ and $m = \frac{1}{5}$, need to be checked to ensure they provide valid foci that lie on the circle $x^2 + y^2 = 16$.
