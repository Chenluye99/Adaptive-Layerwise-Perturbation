First, let's establish the geometric sequence.

The first term \(a_1\) of the geometric sequence is \(\frac{3}{4}\), and the second term \(a_2\) is 15. In a geometric sequence, each term is given by \(a_n = a_1 \cdot r^{n-1}\), where \(r\) is the common ratio.

Given:
\[ a_1 = \frac{3}{4} \]
\[ a_2 = 15 \]

We can find the common ratio \(r\) by using the relationship:
\[ a_2 = a_1 \cdot r \]
\[ 15 = \frac{3}{4} \cdot r \]
\[ r = \frac{15}{\frac{3}{4}} = 15 \cdot \frac{4}{3} = 20 \]

So, the \(n\)-th term of the geometric sequence can be written as:
\[ a_n = \frac{3}{4} \cdot 20^{n-1} \]

We need to find the smallest \(n\) such that the \(n\)-th term of the sequence is divisible by one million (i.e., \(10^6 = 2^6 \cdot 5^6\)). Therefore, we need:
\[ \frac{3}{4} \cdot 20^{n-1} \equiv 0 \pmod{10^6} \]

Since \(\frac{3}{4}\) is a constant and does not have factors of 2 or 5 except for the \(\frac{3}{4} = \frac{3}{2^2}\) part, we really need \(20^{n-1}\) to make up for the factors that \(\frac{3}{4}\) lacks for divisibility by \(10^6\). Noting that \(20 = 2^2 \cdot 5\):

\[ 20^{n-1} = (2^2 \cdot 5)^{n-1} = 2^{2(n-1)} \cdot 5^{n-1} \]

For \(\frac{3}{4} \cdot 20^{n-1}\) to be divisible by \(10^6 = 2^6 \cdot 5^6\), \(20^{n-1}\) must provide the missing factors of 2 and 5. Specifically:

1. \(2^{2(n-1)}\) must contribute at least \(2^6\) because \(\frac{3}{4} = \frac{3}{2^2}\) only provides 2 factors of 2. So, we need:
   \[ 2(n-1) \geq 6 \implies n-1 \geq 3 \implies n \geq 4 \]

2. \(5^{n-1}\) must contribute at least \(5^6\) because there is no factor of 5 in \(\frac{3}{4}\):
   \[ n-1 \geq 6 \implies n \geq 7 \]

Hence, \(n\) must be at least 7 to ensure that both the factor of 2 and the factor of 5 requirements are met. We will verify this by coding the solution to make sure.
```python
import sympy as sp

# Initialize the first term and common ratio
a1 = 3 / 4
r = 20

# We will iterate through the terms until we find the smallest n such that a_n is divisible by 1,000,000
n = 1
while True:
    an = a1 * (r ** (n - 1))
    if an % 1_000_000 == 0:
        break
    n += 1

print(n)
```
Code execution result: 7

Thus, the smallest \( n \) for which the \( n \)-th term of the sequence is divisible by one million is \(\boxed{7}\). This confirms our reasoning is correct.
