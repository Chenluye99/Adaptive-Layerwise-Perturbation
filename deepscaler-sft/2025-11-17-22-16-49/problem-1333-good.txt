First, we need to find the length of \( CD \) where \( CD \) is the angle bisector of \( \angle ACB \) in triangle \( ABC \). We are given:
- \( AB = 9 \)
- \( BC = 12 \)
- \( AC = 15 \)

To solve for the length of \( CD \), we can use the Angle Bisector Theorem and Apollonius's theorem.

### Step-by-Step Solution:

1. **Apply the Angle Bisector Theorem:**
   The Angle Bisector Theorem states that the angle bisector of an angle in a triangle divides the opposite side into segments that are proportional to the adjacent sides. For \( CD \), which bisects \( \angle ACB \):
   \[
   \frac{AD}{DB} = \frac{AC}{BC} = \frac{15}{12} = \frac{5}{4}
   \]
   Let \( AD = 5k \) and \( DB = 4k \). Since \( AD + DB = AB = 9 \):
   \[
   5k + 4k = 9 \implies 9k = 9 \implies k = 1
   \]
   So, \( AD = 5 \) and \( DB = 4 \).

2. **Apply Apollonius's Theorem:**
   Apollonius's theorem states that in any triangle, the sum of the squares of any two sides is equal to twice the square of the median to the third side plus twice the square of half the third side. However, a more suitable approach here is to use Stewart's Theorem, which is specifically useful for finding the length of a cevian like the angle bisector.

   Stewart's Theorem states that in any triangle \( ABC \) with a cevian \( CD \) that divides \( AB \) into segments \( AD \) and \( DB \):
   \[
   AC^2 \cdot DB + BC^2 \cdot AD = AB \cdot (CD^2 + AD \cdot DB)
   \]
   Substituting \( AC = 15 \), \( BC = 12 \), \( AB = 9 \), \( AD = 5 \), and \( DB = 4 \) into the formula:
   \[
   15^2 \cdot 4 + 12^2 \cdot 5 = 9 \cdot (CD^2 + 5 \cdot 4)
   \]
   Simplify and solve for \( CD \):
   \[
   225 \cdot 4 + 144 \cdot 5 = 9 \cdot (CD^2 + 20)
   \]
   \[
   900 + 720 = 9 \cdot (CD^2 + 20)
   \]
   \[
   1620 = 9 \cdot (CD^2 + 20)
   \]
   \[
   180 = CD^2 + 20
   \]
   \[
   CD^2 = 160
   \]
   \[
   CD = \sqrt{160} = 4 \sqrt{10}
   \]
