Let's start by recalling some properties of an ellipse. For an ellipse with a vertical semi-major axis (since the center and the focus have the same x-coordinate but different y-coordinates, this suggests that the major axis is vertical), the standard form of the equation is:

\[
\frac{(x - h)^2}{b^2} + \frac{(y - k)^2}{a^2} = 1
\]

where \((h, k)\) is the center of the ellipse, \(a\) is the length of the semi-major axis, and \(b\) is the length of the semi-minor axis.

From the problem statement, we have:
- The center of the ellipse is at \((-3, 1)\), so \(h = -3\) and \(k = 1\).
- One endpoint of the semi-major axis is at \((-3, 3)\). Since the center is \((-3, 1)\), the length of the semi-major axis, \(a\), is the distance from \((-3, 1)\) to \((-3, 3)\), which is \(3 - 1 = 2\). So, \(a = 2\).
- One focus is at \((-3, 0)\). The distance from the center \((-3, 1)\) to the focus \((-3, 0)\) is \(|0 - 1| = 1\). This distance is denoted as \(c\), so \(c = 1\).

For an ellipse, the relationship between \(a\), \(b\), and \(c\) is given by:

\[
c^2 = a^2 - b^2
\]

Substituting the known values of \(a\) and \(c\) into the equation:

\[
1^2 = 2^2 - b^2 \implies 1 = 4 - b^2 \implies b^2 = 3 \implies b = \sqrt{3}
\]

So, the length of the semi-minor axis is \(b = \sqrt{3}\). Let's confirm this by coding it.
```python
import sympy as sp

# Given values
h, k = -3, 1
a = 2
c = 1

# Calculate b using the equation c^2 = a^2 - b^2
b = sp.solve(c**2 - (a**2 - sp.symbols('b')**2), sp.symbols('b'))[1]  # We take the positive root

print(b)
```
Code execution result: sqrt(3)

Thus, the length of the semi-minor axis of the ellipse is \(\boxed{\sqrt{3}}\). This confirms our solution is correct.
