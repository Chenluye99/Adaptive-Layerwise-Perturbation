To solve the problem, let's begin by understanding the geometry of the rhombus and the region \( R \).

1. **Properties of the Rhombus:**
    - A rhombus has four equal sides. In this case, each side is of length 3.
    - The angle at vertex \( B \) is 110°. Since the opposite angles in a rhombus are equal, \(\angle D = 110^\circ\), and because the sum of the angles in a rhombus is 360°, \(\angle A = \angle C = 180^\circ - 110^\circ = 70^\circ\).

2. **Region \( R \):**
    - Region \( R \) consists of all points inside the rhombus that are closer to vertex \( B \) than to any of the other three vertices \( A, C, \) or \( D \). This region is bounded by the perpendicular bisectors of the segments connecting \( B \) to the other vertices.
    - Each of these perpendicular bisectors will intersect with the adjacent sides of the rhombus. The region \( R \) will therefore be a quadrilateral or a combination of smaller geometric shapes within the rhombus, but importantly, it will be symmetric with respect to the angle bisector of \(\angle B\).

3. **Geometric Construction and Area Calculation:**
    - To get the area of region \( R \), it helps to recognize that region \( R \) is essentially a smaller region bounded by a perpendicular height from point \( B \) to the line that divides the angle \(\angle B\) in half and the sides of the rhombus.
    - The angle bisector of \(\angle B \) will bisect 110° into two 55° angles. Region \( R \) will contain one of these smaller triangles along with some trapezoidal or triangular parts depending on the exact description. For simplicity, we know that the area will be a fraction of the total area of the rhombus based on the central angle.

Now, let's calculate the area of the rhombus first using the formula for the area of a rhombus given its side length \( a \) and the sine of one of its angles:

\[
\text{Area}_{\text{rhombus}} = a^2 \cdot \sin(\theta)
\]

Here, \( a = 3 \) and \(\theta = 110^\circ \), so:

\[
\text{Area}_{\text{rhombus}} = 3^2 \cdot \sin(110^\circ) = 9 \cdot \sin(110^\circ)
\]

We know that \(\sin(110^\circ) = \sin(180^\circ - 70^\circ) = \sin(70^\circ)\). The value of \(\sin(70^\circ)\) is approximately 0.9397.

Hence,

\[
\text{Area}_{\text{rhombus}} \approx 9 \cdot 0.9397 \approx 8.4573
\]

Region \( R \) takes up exactly one of the four regions formed by the perpendicular bisectors of the segments from \( B \) to \( A \), \( C \), and \( D \), each subtending 110° from \( B \). The sector of 110° from the full 360° circle, where it's closer to \( B \), represents \(\frac{110}{360} = \frac{11}{36} \) of the total area if considered in a full circle context but here we need to consider the intersection on the plane and the geometry of the rhombus.
