To solve this problem, we need to understand the polar equation \( r = \cos \theta \).

1. **Understand the Polar Equation:**
    - In polar coordinates, \( r \) is the radius (distance from the origin) and \( \theta \) is the angle.
    - The equation \( r = \cos \theta \) describes a circle. To understand its behavior, let's consider how \( r \) changes with \( \theta \):
      - When \( \theta = 0 \), \( r = \cos(0) = 1 \).
      - As \( \theta \) increases from 0 to \(\frac{\pi}{2}\), \( \cos \theta \) decreases from 1 to 0.
      - When \( \theta = \frac{\pi}{2} \), \( r = \cos \left( \frac{\pi}{2} \right) = 0 \).
      - As \( \theta \) increases from \(\frac{\pi}{2}\) to \(\pi\), \( \cos \theta \) becomes negative, so \( r = \cos \theta \) is also negative, meaning the radius is in the opposite direction from the origin. Specifically, for \(\frac{\pi}{2} < \theta < \pi\), \( r = \cos \theta \) will trace a circle in the opposite direction.
      - When \( \theta = \pi \), \( r = \cos(\pi) = -1 \).
      - As \( \theta \) increases from \(\pi\) to \(\frac{3\pi}{2}\), \( \cos \theta \) increases from -1 to 0.
      - When \( \theta = \frac{3\pi}{2} \), \( r = \cos \left( \frac{3\pi}{2} \right) = 0 \).
      - As \( \theta \) increases from \(\frac{3\pi}{2}\) to \(2\pi\), \( \cos \theta \) increases from 0 to 1, so \( r = \cos \theta \) will trace the circle in the positive direction again.

2. **Form a Complete Circle:**
    - To trace the entire circle, we need to consider the full range of values for \( r \) and how it corresponds to the angle \( \theta \).
    - Notice that:
        - For \( 0 \leq \theta \leq \frac{\pi}{2} \), \( r = \cos \theta \) traces the upper half of the circle.
        - For \( \frac{\pi}{2} \leq \theta \leq \frac{3\pi}{2} \), \( r = \cos \theta \) traces the lower half of the circle and the left half of the circle.
        - For \( \frac{3\pi}{2} \leq \theta \leq 2\pi \), \( r = \cos \theta \) traces the right half of the circle.

    - Therefore, to trace the complete circle, we need to go through a full cycle of the cosine function, which is \( 2\pi \). However, because of the way cosine values repeat and how the circle is traced, we can determine that the smallest value of \( t \) that will trace out the entire circle is \( \pi \), as it effectively covers a full circle considering the periodic nature of the cosine function and how it affects \( r \).

3. **Conclusion:**
    - The smallest value of \( t \) such that the graph of \( r = \cos \theta \) for \( 0 \leq \theta \leq t \) forms the entire circle is \( \pi \).

```python
import numpy as np
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

# Define the polar equation r = cos(theta)
theta = np.linspace(0, np.pi, 1000)
r = np.cos(theta)

# Convert to Cartesian coordinates
x = r * np.cos(theta)
y = r * np.sin(theta)

# Plot the graph
plt.figure(figsize=(6, 6))
plt.plot(x, y)
plt.title('Graph of $r = \cos \\theta$ for $0 \\leq \\theta \\leq \\pi$')
plt.xlabel('x')
plt.ylabel('y')
plt.grid(True)
plt.axhline(0, color='black',linewidth=0.5)
plt.axvline(0, color='black',linewidth=0.5)
plt.gca().set_aspect('equal', adjustable='box')
plt.show()
```
