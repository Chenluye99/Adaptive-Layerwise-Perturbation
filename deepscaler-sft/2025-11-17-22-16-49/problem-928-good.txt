To solve the problem, we need to find the number of ways to distribute 4 scoops among 3 basic flavors (chocolate, vanilla, and strawberry). Each distribution corresponds to a different flavor when the scoops are blended.

This is a combinatorial problem that can be solved using the "stars and bars" theorem from combinatorics. The "stars and bars" theorem states that the number of ways to distribute \(n\) indistinguishable objects (scoops) into \(k\) distinguishable bins (flavors) is given by:

\[
\binom{n + k - 1}{k - 1}
\]

In our case, \(n = 4\) (the scoops) and \(k = 3\) (the flavors: chocolate, vanilla, strawberry). So, we need to compute:

\[
\binom{4 + 3 - 1}{3 - 1} = \binom{6}{2}
\]

The formula for the binomial coefficient \(\binom{n}{r}\) is:

\[
\binom{n}{r} = \frac{n!}{r!(n - r)!}
\]

So,

\[
\binom{6}{2} = \frac{6!}{2!(6 - 2)!} = \frac{6!}{2! \cdot 4!} = \frac{6 \times 5}{2 \times 1} = 15
\]
