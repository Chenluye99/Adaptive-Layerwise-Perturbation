To find the range of the function \( j(x) = 2x^2 + 1 \) defined on the domain \([-1, 2]\), we need to determine the minimum and maximum values of \( j(x) \) over this interval.

1. **Evaluate \( j(x) \) at the endpoints of the domain:**
   - When \( x = -1 \):
     \[
     j(-1) = 2(-1)^2 + 1 = 2 \cdot 1 + 1 = 3
     \]
   - When \( x = 2 \):
     \[
     j(2) = 2(2)^2 + 1 = 2 \cdot 4 + 1 = 9
     \]

2. **Find the critical points of \( j(x) \) in the interval \([-1, 2]\):**
   - To find critical points, we take the derivative of \( j(x) \) and set it to zero:
     \[
     j'(x) = \frac{d}{dx}(2x^2 + 1) = 4x
     \]
     Set \( j'(x) = 0 \):
     \[
     4x = 0 \implies x = 0
     \]
   - Evaluate \( j(x) \) at the critical point \( x = 0 \):
     \[
     j(0) = 2(0)^2 + 1 = 1
     \]

3. **Determine the range:**
   - The minimum value of \( j(x) \) on \([-1, 2]\) is the smallest of \( j(-1) \), \( j(2) \), and \( j(0) \):
     \[
     \min(3, 9, 1) = 1
     \]
   - The maximum value of \( j(x) \) on \([-1, 2]\) is the largest of \( j(-1) \), \( j(2) \), and \( j(0) \):
     \[
     \max(3, 9, 1) = 9
     \]
   - Therefore, the range of \( j(x) \) is all values from 1 to 9, inclusive, so the range is the interval \([1, 9]\).

```python
    return 2 * x ** 2 + 1

```
Code execution result: (1.0, 9.0)
