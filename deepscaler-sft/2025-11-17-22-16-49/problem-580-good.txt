To solve this problem, we need to use some graph theory concepts. We can represent the cities as vertices and the roads connecting the cities as edges. The problem states that between any two cities, there is a unique path that does not pass through any city twice. This implies that the graph is a tree. In a tree with $n$ vertices, there are exactly $n-1$ edges.

Given that there are 2010 cities, our graph is a tree with 2010 vertices, so it has 2009 edges. We want to find the maximum number of vertices that have a degree of at least 3 (i.e., at least 3 roads running out of them), and the rest of the vertices either have a degree of 1 or 2.

1. **Graph Theory Properties of Trees:**
    - In any tree, the sum of the degrees of all vertices is equal to twice the number of edges. So, for a tree with 2010 vertices and 2009 edges, the sum of the degrees of all vertices is \(2 \times 2009 = 4018\).
    - Let's say there are \(k\) vertices with degree at least 3, and the rest (i.e., 2010 - \(k\)) vertices have a degree of either 1 or 2.

2. **Formulate an Equation:**
    - Each of the \(k\) vertices has a degree of at least 3, so their contribution to the sum of the degrees is at least \(3k\).
    - The remaining \(2010 - k\) vertices each have a degree of either 1 or 2, so their contribution to the sum of the degrees is either \(1 \cdot (2010 - k)\) or \(2 \cdot m + 1 \cdot (2010 - k - m)\) where \(0 \leq m \leq 2010 - k\) and \(m\) is the number of vertices with degree 2 and \(2010 - k - m\) is the number of vertices with degree 1.

    - To maximize \(k\), we want to minimize the degrees of the remaining \(2010 - k\) vertices while ensuring that they can connect correctly to maintain the tree structure. The most efficient way to do this is to have as many of these remaining vertices as possible with degree 1, because degree 1 vertices can help support the vertices with higher degrees without adding too much to the overall degree count.

    - If we assume the remaining \(2010 - k\) vertices all have degree 1, then their total contribution to the degree sum would be \(1 \cdot (2010 - k)\). This gives us the equation:
      \[
      3k + 1 \cdot (2010 - k) \leq 4018
      \]
      Simplifying, we get:
      \[
      3k + 2010 - k \leq 4018 \implies 2k + 2010 \leq 4018 \implies 2k \leq 2008 \implies k \leq 1004

      So, \(k \leq 1004\).
