First, let's outline the problem. We need to find the probability that George flips exactly 2 tails in 7 flips of an unfair coin where the probability of heads is $\frac{1}{4}$ and the probability of tails is $\frac{3}{4}$.

When George flips the coin 7 times, getting exactly 2 tails means getting 5 heads. The probability of getting exactly 2 tails (and thus 5 heads) can be computed using the binomial probability formula:

\[ P(X = k) = \binom{n}{k} p^k (1-p)^{n-k} \]

where:
- \( n \) is the number of trials (7 coin flips),
- \( k \) is the number of successful outcomes we want (2 tails),
- \( p \) is the probability of a successful outcome (probability of tails, which is $\frac{3}{4}$),
- \(\binom{n}{k} \) is the binomial coefficient, which is the number of ways to choose \( k \) successes (2 tails) out of \( n \) trials (7 flips), given by \(\binom{7}{2}\).

So, substituting in our values:

\[ P(\text{exactly 2 tails}) = \binom{7}{2} \left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^2 \left(\frac{1}{4}\right)^{7-2} = \binom{7}{2} \left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^2 \left(\frac{1}{4}\right)^5 \]

First, we compute the binomial coefficient \(\binom{7}{2}\):

\[ \binom{7}{2} = \frac{7!}{2!(7-2)!} = \frac{7!}{2!5!} = \frac{7 \times 6}{2 \times 1} = 21 \]

Then we substitute this into our probability formula:

\[ P(\text{exactly 2 tails}) = 21 \cdot \left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^2 \cdot \left(\frac{1}{4}\right)^5 = 21 \cdot \frac{9}{16} \cdot \frac{1}{1024} = 21 \cdot \frac{9}{16384} = \frac{189}{16384} \]
