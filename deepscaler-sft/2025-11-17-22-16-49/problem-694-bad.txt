First, let's recall some properties of a parallelogram:

1. Opposite angles in a parallelogram are equal.
2. Consecutive angles in a parallelogram are supplementary, i.e., they add up to 180 degrees.

Let's denote the measure of angle \( Q \) as \( x \). Then, the measure of angle \( P \) would be \( 5x \).

Since \( P \) and \( Q \) are consecutive angles in the parallelogram, we have:
\[ P + Q = 180^\circ \]
Substituting the expressions for \( P \) and \( Q \):
\[ 5x + x = 180^\circ \]
\[ 6x = 180^\circ \]
\[ x = 30^\circ \]

So, the measure of angle \( Q \) is \( 30^\circ \), and the measure of angle \( P \) is \( 5 \times 30^\circ = 150^\circ \).

Because opposite angles in a parallelogram are equal, angle \( R \) and angle \( S \) will have the same measures as angle \( P \) and angle \( Q \) respectively. Therefore, angle \( R \) will have the same measure as angle \( Q \) and angle \( S \) will have the same measure as angle \( P \).

To summarize, we have:
- Angle \( P = 150^\circ \)
- Angle \( Q = 30^\circ \)
- Angle \( R = 30^\circ \)
- Angle \( S = 150^\circ \)
