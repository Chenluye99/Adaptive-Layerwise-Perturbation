First, let's analyze the problem. For a polynomial with rational coefficients, if a number of the form \( n + \sqrt{n+1} \) is a root, its conjugate \( n - \sqrt{n+1} \) must also be a root. This is because any polynomial with rational coefficients and a root that is an irrational number must also have the conjugate of that root as a root to ensure the coefficients remain rational.

The given numbers are of the form \( n + \sqrt{n+1} \) for \( n \) from 1 to 1000. For each of these numbers, its conjugate \( n - \sqrt{n+1} \) must also be a root of the polynomial. This means for each \( n + \sqrt{n+1} \), there is also \( n - \sqrt{n+1} \) as a root. 

So, for each \( n \) from 1 to 1000, we have two roots: \( n + \sqrt{n+1} \) and \( n - \sqrt{n+1} \). This gives us a total of \( 2 \times 1000 = 2000 \) roots. Therefore, the smallest possible degree of such a polynomial is 2000.

Let's verify and wrap this conclusion in Python to ensure the accuracy.
```python
# We need to confirm our reasoning programmatically, although this problem is more of a theoretical understanding one rather than a computational problem.

# Given numbers
roots = [n + (n + 1)**0.5 for n in range(1, 1001)]

# For each root of the form n + sqrt(n + 1), its conjugate n - sqrt(n + 1) must also be a root.
conjugate_roots = [n - (n + 1)**0.5 for n in range(1, 1001)]

# The total unique roots considering both the original and conjugate roots will be:
total_roots = roots + conjugate_roots

# The degree of the polynomial is equal to the number of distinct roots it needs to have as solutions.
# Since for each n from 1 to 1000, we get two distinct roots, the total number of roots (and hence the degree of the polynomial) is 2 * 1000 = 2000.
smallest_possible_degree = 2 * 1000
print(smallest_possible_degree)
```
