To solve this problem, we need to use the fact that the volume of the steel used to create the smaller balls is equal to the volume of the larger ball. The volume of a sphere is given by the formula:

\[ V = \frac{4}{3} \pi r^3 \]

First, we calculate the total volume of the eight smaller balls. Each smaller ball has a radius of 1 inch, so its volume is:

\[ V_{\text{small}} = \frac{4}{3} \pi (1)^3 = \frac{4}{3} \pi \]

Since there are eight of these smaller balls, the total volume of steel used is:

\[ V_{\text{total}} = 8 \times \frac{4}{3} \pi = \frac{32}{3} \pi \]

This total volume of steel is used to create one larger ball. Let the radius of the larger ball be \( r_{\text{large}} \). The volume of the larger ball is:

\[ V_{\text{large}} = \frac{4}{3} \pi (r_{\text{large}})^3 \]

Since the volume of the steel used to create the larger ball is the same as the total volume of the eight smaller balls, we can set these two volumes equal to each other:

\[ \frac{4}{3} \pi (r_{\text{large}})^3 = \frac{32}{3} \pi \]

We can cancel \(\frac{4}{3} \pi\) from both sides of the equation, leaving:

\[ (r_{\text{large}})^3 = 8 \]

Taking the cube root of both sides, we get:

\[ r_{\text{large}} = \sqrt[3]{8} = 2 \]

So, the radius of the larger ball is 2 inches. Let's confirm this with Python to ensure the accuracy of our solution.
```python
import sympy as sp

# Define the radius of the smaller balls and calculate their total volume
radius_small = 1
volume_small = (4/3) * sp.pi * radius_small**3
total_volume = 8 * volume_small

# Define the radius of the larger ball as a symbol and set up the equation
radius_large = sp.symbols('radius_large')
volume_large = (4/3) * sp.pi * radius_large**3

# Set the volumes equal to each other and solve for the radius of the larger ball
equation = sp.Eq(volume_large, total_volume)
solution = sp.solve(equation, radius_large)

print(solution)
```
