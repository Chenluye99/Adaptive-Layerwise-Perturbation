1. **Understand the Problem:**
    - We have a parabola defined by the equation \(y = x^2\).
    - There is a circle that is tangent to this parabola at two points. This means that at the points of tangency, the circle and the parabola touch each other but do not cross.
    - We need to find how much higher the center of the circle is than the points of tangency.

2. **Set Up the Equations:**
    - Let's assume the circle has a center at \((0, b)\) and a radius \(r\). So, the equation of the circle is \(x^2 + (y - b)^2 = r^2\).
    - At the points of tangency, both the parabola and the circle have the same \(y\) coordinate and the same slope (first derivative). So, let the points of tangency be \((x_0, x_0^2)\). At these points, the equations of the circle and the parabola should be equal, and their first derivatives should also be equal.

3. **Find the Points of Tangency:**
    - Substitute \(y = x_0^2\) into the circle's equation: 
      \[
      x_0^2 + (x_0^2 - b)^2 = r^2.
      \]
    - The first derivative of the parabola \(y = x^2\) is \(y' = 2x\). The first derivative of the circle is obtained by implicit differentiation:
      \[
      2x + 2(y - b) \cdot y' = 0 \implies y' = -\frac{x}{y - b}.
      \]
    - At the points of tangency, the slopes must be equal:
      \[
      2x_0 = -\frac{x_0}{x_0^2 - b} \implies 2x_0 = -\frac{x_0}{x_0^2 - b}.
      \]
    - By canceling \(x_0\) from both sides (assuming \(x_0 \neq 0\)):
      \[
      2 = -\frac{1}{x_0^2 - b} \implies 2(x_0^2 - b) = -1 \implies 2x_0^2 - 2b = -1 \implies 2x_0^2 = 2b - 1 \implies b = x_0^2 + \frac{1}{2}.
      \]
    - Now substitute \(b = x_0^2 + \frac{1}{2}\) into the equation \(x_0^2 + (x_0^2 - b)^2 = r^2\):
      \[
      x_0^2 + \left(x_0^2 - \left(x_0^2 + \frac{1}{2}\right)\right)^2 = r^2 \implies x_0^2 + \left(-\frac{1}{2}\right)^2 = r^2 \implies x_0^2 + \frac{1}{4} = r^2.
      \]
    - The y-coordinate of the points of tangency is \(x_0^2\), and the y-coordinate of the center of the circle is \(b = x_0^2 + \frac{1}{2}\). So, the difference in height between the center of the circle and the points of tangency is:
      \[
      \text{Height Difference} = b - x_0^2 = \left(x_0^2 + \frac{1}{2}\right) - x_0^2 = \frac{1}{2}.
      \]

4. **Conclusion:**
    - The center of the circle is \(\frac{1}{2}\) units higher than the points of tangency.
