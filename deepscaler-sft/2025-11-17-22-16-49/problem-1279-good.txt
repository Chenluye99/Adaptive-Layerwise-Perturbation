To solve this problem, we need to consider the properties of a right triangle and the Pythagorean theorem. The Pythagorean theorem states that for a right triangle with sides \(a\), \(b\), and hypotenuse \(c\), the following equation holds:

\[ a^2 + b^2 = c^2 \]

Given two sides of the right triangle are 5 and 12 units, there are two possible scenarios regarding these sides:

1. One of these sides (either 5 or 12) is the hypotenuse.
2. Neither of these sides is the hypotenuse, so both 5 and 12 are the legs of the triangle, and the third side will be the hypotenuse.

Let's denote the third side as \(x\). We need to determine which of these scenarios produces the smallest possible length for \(x\).

### Scenario 1: Determine if either 5 or 12 could be the hypotenuse

1. **Case 1.1: 12 is the hypotenuse**

   If 12 is the hypotenuse, then:
   \[
   5^2 + x^2 = 12^2 \implies 25 + x^2 = 144 \implies x^2 = 119 \implies x = \sqrt{119}
   \]

2. **Case 1.2: 5 is the hypotenuse**

   This is not a valid scenario because the hypotenuse of a right triangle must be the longest side, but here 5 is smaller than 12. So we can discard this case.

### Scenario 2: Neither 5 nor 12 is the hypotenuse; both 5 and 12 are the legs

If both 5 and 12 are legs of the triangle, then the length of the hypotenuse, denoted as \(x\), would be:
   \[
   x = \sqrt{5^2 + 12^2} = \sqrt{25 + 144} = \sqrt{169} = 13
   \]

Now, we have two possible lengths for the third side: \(\sqrt{119}\) and 13. We need to determine which of these is smaller. We can compare these values by computing \(\sqrt{119}\).

Let's use Python to get the precise value of \(\sqrt{119}\) and compare it with 13.
```python
import math

# Calculate the value of sqrt(119)
sqrt_119 = math.sqrt(119)

print((sqrt_119, 13))
```
Code execution result: (10.908712114635714, 13)

As we can see, \(\sqrt{119} \approx 10.91\) and 13. Therefore, \(\sqrt{119}\) is smaller than 13. 

Thus, the least possible length of the third side is \(\sqrt{119}\).
