To solve this problem, we need to consider all possible ways to choose three points from the set of dots such that they form a triangle.

1. **Initial Setup:**
    - Triangle \(ABC\) has 3 vertices.
    - 2 additional dots are placed on side \(AB\).
    - 3 additional dots are placed on side \(BC\).
    - 7 additional dots are placed on side \(CA\).

2. **Total Number of Dots:**
    - Total number of dots on side \(AB\) including endpoints \(A\) and \(B\) is \(2 + 2 = 4\).
    - Total number of dots on side \(BC\) including endpoints \(B\) and \(C\) is \(3 + 2 = 5\).
    - Total number of dots on side \(CA\) including endpoints \(C\) and \(A\) is \(7 + 2 = 9\).

3. **Counting Possible Triangles:**
    - To form a triangle, we need to choose 3 points such that they are not collinear (i.e., they do not all lie on the same side of the original triangle \(ABC\)). Any set of 3 points chosen from the 14 points (3 original vertices + 2 + 3 + 7 additional dots) will form a triangle unless the 3 points chosen all lie on one of the sides of the triangle \(ABC\).

4. **Exclusion of Collinear Points:**
    - Dots on side \(AB\) will not form a triangle with any dots on side \(BC\) or side \(CA\) except by themselves or with a point on \(AB\), and so on.
    - We need to subtract the ways to choose 3 collinear points (which is just choosing 3 points from the same side of the original triangle).

Let's count it step by step. We start with the total ways to choose 3 points from 14 points, and then subtract the ways where all 3 points chosen are from the same side of the original triangle \(ABC\).

The total ways to choose 3 points from 14 points is given by:
\[ \binom{14}{3} \]

The ways to choose 3 points from the 4 dots on side \(AB\) is given by:
\[ \binom{4}{3} \]

The ways to choose 3 points from the 5 dots on side \(BC\) is given by:
\[ \binom{5}{3} \]

The ways to choose 3 points from the 9 dots on side \(CA\) is given by:
\[ \binom{9}{3} \]

Then our solution will be:
\[ \text{Total ways} - (\text{Ways to choose 3 points from side } AB + \text{Ways to choose 3 points from side } BC + \text{Ways to choose 3 points from side } CA) \]

Let's compute it using Python for accuracy.
```python
import math

# Calculate binomial coefficients
total_ways = math.comb(14, 3)
ways_from_AB = math.comb(4, 3)
ways_from_BC = math.comb(5, 3)
ways_from_CA = math.comb(9, 3)

# Calculate the final answer
result = total_ways - (ways_from_AB + ways_from_BC + ways_from_CA)
print(result)
```
Code execution result: 266

Thus, the number of triangles that can be formed with vertices at these dots is \(\boxed{266}\). This is the final answer.
