To solve this problem, we need to find the coefficient of the \(x^2\) term in the expansion of \((x + 1)^{42}\).

We can use the binomial theorem, which states that:

\[
(x + y)^n = \sum_{k=0}^{n} \binom{n}{k} x^{n-k} y^k
\]

For our specific case, \((x + 1)^{42}\), we have \(y = 1\) and \(n = 42\). The general term in the expansion is given by:

\[
\binom{42}{k} x^{42-k} \cdot 1^k = \binom{42}{k} x^{42-k}
\]

We want the coefficient of the \(x^2\) term, so we set \(42 - k = 2\). Solving for \(k\):

\[
42 - k = 2 \implies k = 40
\]

Thus, the coefficient of the \(x^2\) term is \(\binom{42}{40}\). Note that \(\binom{42}{40} = \binom{42}{2}\) because \(\binom{n}{k} = \binom{n}{n-k}\). So, we need to compute \(\binom{42}{2}\):

\[
\binom{42}{2} = \frac{42!}{2!(42-2)!} = \frac{42!}{2! \cdot 40!} = \frac{42 \cdot 41}{2 \cdot 1} = \frac{1722}{2} = 861
\]

