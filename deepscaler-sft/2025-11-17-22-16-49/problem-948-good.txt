### Step-by-Step Solution:

1. **Find Intersection Point:**
   To find the intersection of the lines \( tx - 2y - 3t = 0 \) and \( x - 2ty + 3 = 0 \), we solve these equations simultaneously.

   We can use substitution or elimination methods to find the intersection point. Let's use the substitution method here.

   From the second equation, express \( x \) in terms of \( y \) and \( t \):
   \[
   x = 2ty - 3
   \]
   Substitute this expression for \( x \) into the first equation:
   \[
   t (2ty - 3) - 2y - 3t = 0
   \]
   Simplify and solve for \( y \):
   \[
   2t^2 y - 3t - 2y - 3t = 0 \implies 2t^2 y - 2y - 6t = 0 \implies 2y (t^2 - 1) = 6t \implies y (t^2 - 1) = 3t
   \]
   So, we get:
   \[
   y = \frac{3t}{t^2 - 1}
   \]
   Substitute this value of \( y \) back into the equation \( x = 2ty - 3 \) to get \( x \):
   \[
   x = 2t \left(\frac{3t}{t^2 - 1}\right) - 3 = \frac{6t^2}{t^2 - 1} - 3 = \frac{6t^2 - 3(t^2 - 1)}{t^2 - 1} = \frac{6t^2 - 3t^2 + 3}{t^2 - 1} = \frac{3t^2 + 3}{t^2 - 1} = \frac{3(t^2 + 1)}{t^2 - 1}
   \]
   Therefore, the coordinates of the intersection point are:
   \[
   \left( \frac{3(t^2 + 1)}{t^2 - 1}, \frac{3t}{t^2 - 1} \right)
   \]

   \[

   \]
   We can solve for \( t \) in terms of \( y \) first:
   \[
   y = \frac{3t}{t^2 - 1} \implies y (t^2 - 1) = 3t \implies y t^2 - 3t - y = 0
   \]
   This is a quadratic equation in \( t \). To solve for \( t \), we use the quadratic formula \( t = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a} \), where \( a = y \), \( b = -3 \), and \( c = -y \):
   \[
   t = \frac{3 \pm \sqrt{9 + 4y^2}}{2y}
   \]
   Substitute this back into the expression for \( x \):
   \[
   x = \frac{3 \left( \left( \frac{3 \pm \sqrt{9 + 4y^2}}{2y} \right)^2 + 1 \right)}{\left( \frac{3 \pm \sqrt{9 + 4y^2}}{2y} \right)^2 - 1}

   This looks complicated, but we can instead look for a simpler relationship by eliminating \( t \) algebraically. We know that:
