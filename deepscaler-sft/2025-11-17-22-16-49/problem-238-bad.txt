First, let's solve the equation \(3^{2x} + 9 = 10 \cdot 3^x\).

We can use a substitution to simplify this equation. Let \( y = 3^x \). Then, \( 3^{2x} = (3^x)^2 = y^2 \). Substituting these into the original equation gives:

\[ y^2 + 9 = 10y \]

Rearrange this equation to form a standard quadratic equation:

\[ y^2 - 10y + 9 = 0 \]

We can solve this quadratic equation using the quadratic formula, but it's also straightforward to factorize it:

\[ y^2 - 10y + 9 = (y - 1)(y - 9) = 0 \]

So, the solutions for \( y \) are:

\[ y = 1 \quad \text{or} \quad y = 9 \]

Recall that \( y = 3^x \), so we substitute back to get two possible equations for \( x \):

1. \( 3^x = 1 \)
2. \( 3^x = 9 \)

We can solve these equations for \( x \):

1. \( 3^x = 1 \) implies \( x = 0 \) because \( 3^0 = 1 \).
2. \( 3^x = 9 \) implies \( x = 2 \) because \( 3^2 = 9 \).

So, the solutions for \( x \) are \( x = 0 \) and \( x = 2 \). We need to find the value of \( x^2 + 1 \) for each of these solutions:

1. If \( x = 0 \):
   \[
   x^2 + 1 = 0^2 + 1 = 1
   \]
2. If \( x = 2 \):
   \[
   x^2 + 1 = 2^2 + 1 = 4 + 1 = 5
   \]

To ensure the correctness, let's verify both solutions by plugging them back into the original equation and then return the appropriate result for \( x^2 + 1 \). We'll use Python to ensure the calculations are accurate.
```python
import sympy as sp

# Define the variable
x = sp.symbols('x')

# Define the equation 3^(2x) + 9 = 10 * 3^x
equation = 3**(2*x) + 9 - 10 * 3**x

# Solve the equation for x
solutions = sp.solve(equation, x)

# Calculate x^2 + 1 for each solution
results = [sol**2 + 1 for sol in solutions]
print(results)
```
