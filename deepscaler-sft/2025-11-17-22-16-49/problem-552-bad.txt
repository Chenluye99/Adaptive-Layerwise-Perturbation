To solve the problem, let's start by finding the inclination angle of the line given by the equation \(x - y + 1 = 0\).

### Step-by-Step Solution:

1. **Find the Slope of the Line:**
    - The equation of the line is given as \(x - y + 1 = 0\).
    - We can rearrange this equation to the slope-intercept form, \(y = mx + c\), where \(m\) is the slope of the line.
    - Rearrange \(x - y + 1 = 0\):
      \[
      -y = -x - 1 \implies y = x + 1
      \]
      So, the slope \(m\) of the line is 1.

2. **Find the Inclination Angle:**
    - The slope \(m\) of a line is related to its inclination angle \(\theta\) by the equation \(m = \tan(\theta)\).
    - Therefore, for our line, we have:
      \[
      \tan(\theta) = 1
      \]
    - The inclination angle \(\theta\) is the angle that the line makes with the positive x-axis, and it is given by:
      \[
      \theta = \arctan(1)
      \]
    - We know that \(\arctan(1) = \frac{\pi}{4}\).

3. **Conclusion:**
    - The inclination angle of the line \(x - y + 1 = 0\) is \(\frac{\pi}{4}\).

Let's confirm this by implementing it in Python.
```python
import sympy as sp

# Define the line equation
x, y = sp.symbols('x y')
line_eq = x - y + 1

# Get the slope of the line by converting it to the form y = mx + c
line_eq_slope = sp.solve(line_eq, y)[0]
slope = sp.diff(line_eq_slope, x)  # Differentiate to get the slope (since it's already in the form y = mx + c, this is just the coefficient of x)

# Calculate the inclination angle
theta = sp.atan(slope)
print(theta)
```
Code execution result: pi/4

Thus, the inclination angle of the line \(x - y + 1 = 0\) is \(\boxed{\frac{\pi}{4}}\). This confirms our earlier solution.
