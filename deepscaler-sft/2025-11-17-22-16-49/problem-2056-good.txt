### Step-by-Step Solution:

1. **Visualize the Problem:**
    - Consider a circle with radius \( r \).
    - A chord of the circle is perpendicular to a radius at the midpoint of the radius, meaning the perpendicular distance from the center of the circle to the chord is equal to \( \frac{r}{2} \).

2. **Find the Length of the Chord:**
    - Using the Pythagorean theorem in the right triangle formed by the radius, half of the chord, and the distance from the center to the chord, if we let the length of the chord be \( 2x \), then:
      \[
      x^2 + \left( \frac{r}{2} \right)^2 = r^2 \implies x^2 + \frac{r^2}{4} = r^2 \implies x^2 = \frac{3r^2}{4} \implies x = \frac{r \sqrt{3}}{2}
      \]
      So, the length of the chord is:
      \[
      2x = r \sqrt{3}
      \]

      \[
      \cos \left( \frac{\theta}{2} \right) = \frac{\frac{r}{2}}{r} = \frac{1}{2} \implies \frac{\theta}{2} = \frac{\pi}{3} \implies \theta = \frac{2\pi}{3}
      \]
    - So, the smaller segment is the area of the sector of angle \( \frac{2\pi}{3} \) minus the area of the triangle formed by the two radii and the chord.

5. **Calculate the Area of Smaller and Larger Segments:**
