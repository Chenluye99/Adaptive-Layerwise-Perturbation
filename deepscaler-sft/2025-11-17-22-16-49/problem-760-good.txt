To solve this problem, we need to consider the constraints of a BINGO card and how numbers are placed within it. 

1. **Set Up of a BINGO Card:**
    - A BINGO card is a 5x5 grid.
    - The middle square is designated as "WILD" and is skipped when placing numbers.
    - The numbers in each column come from specific ranges as described:
      - First column: 5 numbers from 1 to 15
      - Second column: 5 numbers from 16 to 30
      - Third column (middle square is WILD): 4 numbers from 31 to 45
      - Fourth column: 5 numbers from 46 to 60
      - Fifth column: 5 numbers from 61 to 75

2. **Diagonal from Top Left to Bottom Right:**
    - The diagonal going from the top left to the bottom right of the BINGO card will comprise of one square from the first column, one square from the second column, the WILD square from the third column, one square from the fourth column, and one square from the fifth column.
    - Therefore, the numbers on this diagonal will be from different ranges, specifically, one from 1 to 15, one from 16 to 30, one from 31 to 45 (the WILD square is skipped, so it does not contribute to this count), one from 46 to 60, and one from 61 to 75.

3. **Determine the Number of Distinct Possibilities:**
    - We need to calculate the number of ways we can choose and arrange 5 distinct numbers such that one is from 1 to 15, one is from 16 to 30, one is from 46 to 60, and one is from 61 to 75, and the last slot in this diagonal sequence will be filled with any number from the overlapping range of 31 to 45, but it's skipped in this context.

To solve this systematically, we can enumerate through all possible selections of the numbers from the respective ranges and then count the number of distinct combinations for the diagonal positions.

Let's implement this step-by-step using Python to ensure accuracy.
```python
import itertools

# Create ranges for each column based on problem constraints
range_1_to_15 = list(range(1, 16))
range_16_to_30 = list(range(16, 31))
range_31_to_45 = list(range(31, 46))
range_46_to_60 = list(range(46, 61))
range_61_to_75 = list(range(61, 76))

# Find all possible ways to choose one number from each specified range for our diagonal positions
diagonal_values = []

for i1 in range_1_to_15:
    for i2 in range_16_to_30:
        for i4 in range_46_to_60:
            for i5 in range_61_to_75:
                diagonal_values.append((i1, i2, None, i4, i5))

# Remove duplicates and keep only unique tuples
distinct_diagonal_combinations = set(diagonal_values)

# Count the number of distinct possibilities
num_distinct_possibilities = len(distinct_diagonal_combinations)
print(num_distinct_possibilities)
```
