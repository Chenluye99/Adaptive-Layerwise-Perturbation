To solve this problem, we need to determine how many fans received all three items (a free hot dog, a free cup of soda, and a free bag of popcorn). 

To get all three items, a fan must be the 75th, 30th, and 50th fan in the sequence for some multiple of their respective intervals. So, a fan must be at the least common multiple (LCM) position of 75, 30, and 50 multiples of their respective positions.

First, let's find the LCM of 75, 30, and 50. The LCM of a set of integers is the smallest positive integer that is divisible by each of them. Once we have the LCM, we can determine how many such fans exist in a stadium that holds 4000 fans.

### Step-by-Step Solution:

1. **Find the LCM of 75, 30, and 50:**
    - To find the LCM, it is useful to start by finding the prime factorizations of the numbers:
        - \(75 = 3 \times 5^2\)
        - \(30 = 2 \times 3 \times 5\)
        - \(50 = 2 \times 5^2\)
    - The LCM is found by taking the highest power of each prime that appears in the factorizations:
        - The highest power of 2 is \(2^1\)
        - The highest power of 3 is \(3^1\)
        - The highest power of 5 is \(5^2\)
    - Therefore, the LCM is:
      \[
      \text{LCM} = 2^1 \times 3^1 \times 5^2 = 2 \times 3 \times 25 = 150
      \]

2. **Determine How Many Fans Get All Three Items:**
    - A fan who gets all three items will be every 150th fan (since 150 is the LCM of 75, 30, and 50).
    - We need to find how many multiples of 150 are there in the first 4000 fans.

This can be done by computing:
\[
\left\lfloor \frac{4000}{150} \right\rfloor
\]

Let's compute this step-by-step using Python to make sure accuracy.
```python
import math

# Find the LCM of 75, 30, and 50
def lcm(x, y):
    return abs(x * y) // math.gcd(x, y)

lcm_75_30 = lcm(75, 30)
lcm_75_30_50 = lcm(lcm_75_30, 50)

# Number of fans in the stadium
num_fans = 4000

# Find how many fans get all three items
count_fans_with_all_three = num_fans // lcm_75_30_50
print(count_fans_with_all_three)
```
