First, let's start by considering the properties of isosceles triangle \( PQR \). In an isosceles triangle, two of the angles are equal. We are given that the measure of angle \( R \) is \( 40^\circ \), so the possible measures for angles \( P \) and \( Q \) depend on which angles are equal.

### Step-by-Step Solution

1. **Case 1: Angles \( P \) and \( Q \) are equal.**
   - Let the measure of angles \( P \) and \( Q \) be \( a \).
   - The measure of angle \( R \) is \( 40^\circ \).
   - Since the sum of the angles in a triangle is \( 180^\circ \), we have:
     \[
     a + a + 40^\circ = 180^\circ \implies 2a + 40^\circ = 180^\circ \implies 2a = 140^\circ \implies a = 70^\circ.
     \]
   - So, in this case, the measure of angle \( P \) is \( 70^\circ \).

2. **Case 2: Angles \( P \) and \( R \) are equal.**
   - Let the measure of angles \( P \) and \( R \) be \( 40^\circ \).
   - The measure of angle \( Q \) will then be \( b \).
   - Again, the sum of the angles in the triangle is \( 180^\circ \), so:
     \[
     40^\circ + 40^\circ + b = 180^\circ \implies 80^\circ + b = 180^\circ \implies b = 100^\circ.
     \]
   - So, in this case, the measure of angle \( P \) is \( 40^\circ \).

3. **Case 3: Angles \( Q \) and \( R \) are equal.**
   - Let the measure of angles \( Q \) and \( R \) be \( 40^\circ \).
   - The measure of angle \( P \) will then be \( c \).
   - Again, the sum of the angles in the triangle is \( 180^\circ \), so:
     \[
     c + 40^\circ + 40^\circ = 180^\circ \implies c + 80^\circ = 180^\circ \implies c = 100^\circ.
     \]
   - So, in this case, the measure of angle \( P \) is \( 100^\circ \).
