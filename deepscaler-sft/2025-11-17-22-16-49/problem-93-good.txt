To solve this problem, we need to calculate the probability that four out of the six cards drawn from a standard 52-card deck have the same value.

### Step-by-Step Solution:

1. **Count the Total Number of Ways to Draw 6 Cards from a 52-Card Deck:**
   The total number of ways to choose 6 cards from a 52-card deck is given by the combination formula \( \binom{52}{6} \).

2. **Count the Number of Favorable Outcomes:**
   To have four cards of the same value, we need to choose a rank out of the 13 available ranks, and then choose 4 out of the 4 suits for that rank (which can be done in exactly 1 way because there are exactly 4 suits for each rank). Then, we need to choose 2 more cards from the remaining 48 cards (since we excluded the cards with the chosen rank), ensuring that these 2 cards do not form another group of four of the same value. 

   - **Choose 1 Rank out of 13:** There are \( \binom{13}{1} = 13 \) ways to choose the rank.
   - **Choose 4 Cards from the 4 Cards of That Rank:** There is only 1 way to choose 4 cards from the 4 available cards of that rank (since there are exactly 4 cards of each rank, i.e., 4 suits).
   - **Choose 2 Cards from Remaining 48 Cards:** We need to choose 2 cards from the remaining 48 cards (since we have already chosen 4 cards from one rank, there are 52 - 4 = 48 remaining cards). The number of ways to choose 2 cards from 48 cards is given by \( \binom{48}{2} \).
   - **Ensure No Other Rank Forms a Group of 4:** We need to ensure that the 2 remaining cards do not form another group of 4 cards of the same rank. For this, we need to subtract scenarios where these 2 cards form another group of 4 (but since we only choose 2 cards, there is no way to form another group of 4 with only 2 cards, this condition is inherently satisfied if both cards come from different ranks or different sets of 4 cards from different ranks that don't make another group of 4).

Thus, the number of favorable outcomes is \( 13 \times \binom{48}{2} \).

3. **Calculate the Probability:**
   The probability is given by the ratio of the number of favorable outcomes to the total number of outcomes:
   \[
   P(\text{four of the same value}) = \frac{13 \times \binom{48}{2}}{\binom{52}{6}}
   \]
