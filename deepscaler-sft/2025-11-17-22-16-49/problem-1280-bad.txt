To solve this problem, we need to determine the probability that a randomly selected point \( P \) from a square with vertices at \((\pm 2, \pm 2)\) is within a distance of 1 unit from the origin.

1. **Define the Square Region:**
    - The square has side length 4 (from -2 to 2 on both the x and y axes), so its area is \(4 \times 4 = 16\).

2. **Define the Circular Region:**
    - The region within one unit of the origin is a circle with radius 1 and center at the origin (0, 0). The area of this circle is \(\pi \cdot 1^2 = \pi\).

3. **Calculate the Probability:**
    - The probability that a randomly selected point from the square is within the circle is the ratio of the area of the circle to the area of the square. Therefore, the probability is \(\frac{\pi}{16}\).

Let's confirm this with Python to ensure accuracy.
```python
import sympy as sp

# Define the area of the square with side length 4
area_square = 4 * 4

# Define the area of the circle with radius 1
radius = 1
area_circle = sp.pi * radius**2

# Calculate the probability
probability = area_circle / area_square
print(probability)
```
Code execution result: pi/16

Thus, the probability that a randomly selected point \( P \) from the square region with vertices at \((\pm 2, \pm 2)\) is within one unit of the origin is \(\boxed{\frac{\pi}{16}}\). This confirms our reasoning and calculations are accurate.
