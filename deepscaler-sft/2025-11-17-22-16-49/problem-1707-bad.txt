To solve this problem, we need to determine the values of \( b \) for which the quadratic equation \( x^2 + bx + 9 \) has two non-real roots. A quadratic equation of the form \( ax^2 + bx + c = 0 \) has non-real roots if its discriminant is less than 0. The discriminant (\(\Delta\)) of the quadratic equation \( x^2 + bx + 9 \) is given by:

\[
\Delta = b^2 - 4 \cdot 1 \cdot 9 = b^2 - 36
\]

For the quadratic equation to have non-real roots, the discriminant must be less than 0:

\[
b^2 - 36 < 0
\]

This inequality can be rewritten as:

\[
b^2 < 36
\]

Taking the square root of both sides, we get:

\[
-6 < b < 6
\]

Thus, the possible values of \( b \) that make the quadratic equation \( x^2 + bx + 9 \) have two non-real roots are in the interval:

\[
(-6, 6)
\]

Let's confirm this by implementing it in Python to ensure accuracy.
```python
import sympy as sp

# Define the variable and the quadratic equation
b = sp.symbols('b', real=True)
discriminant = b**2 - 36

# Solve the inequality b^2 - 36 < 0
solution = sp.solve_univariate_inequality(discriminant < 0, b, relational=False)
print(solution)
```
Code execution result: Interval.open(-6, 6)

Thus, the solution obtained from solving the inequality is confirmed to be:

\[
\boxed{(-6, 6)}
\]

This is the final answer. The possible values of \( b \) that make the quadratic equation \( x^2 + bx + 9 \) have two non-real roots are all the real numbers in the interval \((-6, 6)\).
