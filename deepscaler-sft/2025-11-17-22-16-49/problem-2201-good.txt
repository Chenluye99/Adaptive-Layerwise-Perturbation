To solve this problem, we need to calculate the probability that George flips exactly 2 tails (and thus exactly 5 heads) in 7 coin flips, given that the probability of tails is \(\frac{3}{4}\) and the probability of heads is \(\frac{1}{4}\).

The probability of getting exactly 2 tails in 7 coin flips can be calculated using the binomial probability formula:

\[ P(X = k) = \binom{n}{k} \cdot p^k \cdot (1 - p)^{n - k} \]

where:
- \( n \) is the number of trials (7 coin flips),
- \( k \) is the number of successful outcomes we want (2 tails),
- \( p \) is the probability of success for a single trial (probability of tails, \(\frac{3}{4}\)),
- \(\binom{n}{k}\) is the binomial coefficient, which is given by \(\frac{n!}{k!(n - k)!}\).

Plugging in the values:

\[ P(X = 2) = \binom{7}{2} \cdot \left( \frac{3}{4} \right)^2 \cdot \left( \frac{1}{4} \right)^{7 - 2} = \binom{7}{2} \cdot \left( \frac{3}{4} \right)^2 \cdot \left( \frac{1}{4} \right)^5 \]

First, we compute the binomial coefficient \(\binom{7}{2}\):

\[ \binom{7}{2} = \frac{7!}{2!(7-2)!} = \frac{7!}{2!5!} = \frac{7 \cdot 6}{2 \cdot 1} = 21 \]

Then we substitute this into our probability formula:

\[ P(X = 2) = 21 \cdot \left( \frac{3}{4} \right)^2 \cdot \left( \frac{1}{4} \right)^5 = 21 \cdot \frac{3^2}{4^2} \cdot \frac{1^5}{4^5} = 21 \cdot \frac{9}{16} \cdot \frac{1}{1024} = 21 \cdot \frac{9}{16384} = \frac{189}{16384} \]

# Number of trials (coin flips)

# Number of tails we want
