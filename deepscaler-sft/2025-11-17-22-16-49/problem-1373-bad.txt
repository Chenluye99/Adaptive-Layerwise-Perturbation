To solve this problem, we need to determine the prime factors of the product \(87 \cdot 89 \cdot 91 \cdot 93\) and then find out the distinct prime numbers that appear in the factorization.

Let's start by finding the prime factors of each number individually:

1. **Factorize 87:**
   \[
   87 = 3 \cdot 29
   \]

2. **Factorize 89:**
   89 is a prime number, so its only prime factor is 89.

3. **Factorize 91:**
   \[
   91 = 7 \cdot 13
   \]

4. **Factorize 93:**
   \[
   93 = 3 \cdot 31
   \]

Now, we can combine all these prime factors from the factorizations of 87, 89, 91, and 93:

\[
87 \cdot 89 \cdot 91 \cdot 93 = (3 \cdot 29) \cdot 89 \cdot (7 \cdot 13) \cdot (3 \cdot 31)
\]

So, the prime factors of the product are the unique prime numbers that appear in this factorization. We need to list all these prime numbers and then determine the distinct ones. The prime factors from the above factorizations are: 3, 29, 89, 7, 13, and 31. 

Next, we can use Python to ensure accuracy and get the distinct prime factors.
```python
import sympy

# List the numbers
numbers = [87, 89, 91, 93]

# Compute the product of the numbers
product = 1
for num in numbers:
    product *= num

# Get the distinct prime factors of the product
prime_factors = sympy.factorint(product).keys()

# Return the distinct prime factors and their count
distinct_prime_factors = list(prime_factors)
num_distinct_primes = len(distinct_prime_factors)

print((num_distinct_primes, distinct_prime_factors))
```
Code execution result: (6, [3, 7, 13, 29, 31, 89])

The distinct prime factors of \(87 \cdot 89 \cdot 91 \cdot 93\) are \([3, 7, 13, 29, 31, 89]\), and there are 6 of them.

Thus, the number of different primes in the prime factorization of \(87 \cdot 89 \cdot 91 \cdot 93\) is \(\boxed{6}\). This is our final answer.
