To solve this problem, we need to use the relationships between the roots of quadratic equations and their coefficients.

1. **Relationship Between Roots and Coefficients of Quadratic Equations:**

    - For the quadratic equation \(x^2 + px + m = 0\), let the roots be \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\). So, by Vieta's formulas, we have:
      \[
      \alpha + \beta = -p \quad \text{(1)}
      \]
      \[
      \alpha \beta = m \quad \text{(2)}
      \]

    - For the quadratic equation \(x^2 + mx + n = 0\), the roots are twice those of the first equation, so the roots are \(2\alpha\) and \(2\beta\). By Vieta's formulas again, we have:
      \[
      2\alpha + 2\beta = -m \quad \text{(3)}
      \]
      \[
      (2\alpha)(2\beta) = n \quad \text{(4)}
      \]

2. **Formulate the Equations:**

    - From equation (3), we can write:
      \[
      2(\alpha + \beta) = -m \implies \alpha + \beta = -\frac{m}{2} \quad \text{(5)}
      \]
    - From equation (4), we can write:
      \[
      4 \cdot \alpha \cdot \beta = n \implies 4m = n \quad \text{(6)}
      \]

3. **Equate Equations (1) and (5):**

    - From equation (1), we have \(\alpha + \beta = -p\).
    - From equation (5), we have \(\alpha + \beta = -\frac{m}{2}\).

    - By equating these two, we get:
      \[
      -p = -\frac{m}{2} \implies p = \frac{m}{2} \quad \text{(7)}
      \]

4. **Find the Ratio \( \frac{n}{p} \):**

    - From equation (6), we have \( n = 4m \).
    - From equation (7), we have \( p = \frac{m}{2} \).
    - Substitute these into \(\frac{n}{p}\):
      \[
      \frac{n}{p} = \frac{4m}{\frac{m}{2}} = \frac{4m \cdot 2}{m} = 8
      \]
