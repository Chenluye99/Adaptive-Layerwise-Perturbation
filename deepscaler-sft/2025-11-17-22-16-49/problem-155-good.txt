First, let's solve the quadratic equation $x^2 - 2x - 3 = 0$ to find the roots $a_1$ and $a_4$.

We can solve the quadratic equation using the quadratic formula:
\[ x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a} \]
where \(a = 1\), \(b = -2\), and \(c = -3\).

Plugging in these values, we get:
\[ x = \frac{-(-2) \pm \sqrt{(-2)^2 - 4 \cdot 1 \cdot (-3)}}{2 \cdot 1} = \frac{2 \pm \sqrt{4 + 12}}{2} = \frac{2 \pm \sqrt{16}}{2} = \frac{2 \pm 4}{2}. \]

This gives us two solutions:
\[ x = \frac{2 + 4}{2} = 3, \]
\[ x = \frac{2 - 4}{2} = -1. \]

So, the roots are $3$ and $-1$. Therefore, $a_1$ and $a_4$ are $3$ and $-1$ (in some order).

In a geometric sequence, the general form of terms is given by $a_n = a_1 \cdot r^{n-1}$, where $r$ is the common ratio. So, we have:
\[ a_4 = a_1 \cdot r^3. \]

This means that either:
1. $a_1 = 3$ and $a_4 = -1$, or
2. $a_1 = -1$ and $a_4 = 3$.

Let's consider both cases:

### Case 1: $a_1 = 3$ and $a_4 = -1$
We substitute $a_4 = a_1 \cdot r^3$:
\[ -1 = 3 \cdot r^3 \implies r^3 = -\frac{1}{3}. \]

### Case 2: $a_1 = -1$ and $a_4 = 3$
We substitute $a_4 = a_1 \cdot r^3$:
\[ 3 = -1 \cdot r^3 \implies r^3 = -3. \]

\[ a_3 = a_1 \cdot r^2. \]

So, the product of the first and third terms is:
\[ a_1 \cdot a_3 = a_1 \cdot (a_1 \cdot r^2) = a_1^2 \cdot r^2. \]
