To solve this, we need to use the properties of orthogonal vectors and projection.

1. **Projection Formula:**
   The projection of vector \(\mathbf{v}\) onto \(\mathbf{a}\) is given by:
   \[
   \operatorname{proj}_{\mathbf{a}} \mathbf{v} = \frac{\mathbf{v} \cdot \mathbf{a}}{\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{a}} \mathbf{a}
   \]
   We know that:
   \[
   \operatorname{proj}_{\mathbf{a}} \begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ -3 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} -\frac{3}{5} \\ -\frac{6}{5} \end{pmatrix}
   \]
   Let \(\mathbf{a} = \begin{pmatrix} a_1 \\ a_2 \end{pmatrix}\). Then, the projection formula becomes:
   \[
   \operatorname{proj}_{\mathbf{a}} \begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ -3 \end{pmatrix} = \frac{\begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ -3 \end{pmatrix} \cdot \begin{pmatrix} a_1 \\ a_2 \end{pmatrix}}{\begin{pmatrix} a_1 \\ a_2 \end{pmatrix} \cdot \begin{pmatrix} a_1 \\ a_2 \end{pmatrix}} \begin{pmatrix} a_1 \\ a_2 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} -\frac{3}{5} \\ -\frac{6}{5} \end{pmatrix}
   \]
   This means:
   \[
   \frac{3a_1 - 3a_2}{a_1^2 + a_2^2} \begin{pmatrix} a_1 \\ a_2 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} -\frac{3}{5} \\ -\frac{6}{5} \end{pmatrix}
   \]
   From this, we get two equations:
   \[
   \frac{3a_1 - 3a_2}{a_1^2 + a_2^2} a_1 = -\frac{3}{5}
   \]
   \[
   \frac{3a_1 - 3a_2}{a_1^2 + a_2^2} a_2 = -\frac{6}{5}
   \]

2. **Finding \(\mathbf{a}\):**
   We can divide the second equation by the first to eliminate the fraction and solve for the ratio of \(a_1\) and \(a_2\):
   \[
   \frac{\frac{3a_1 - 3a_2}{a_1^2 + a_2^2} a_2}{\frac{3a_1 - 3a_2}{a_1^2 + a_2^2} a_1} = \frac{-\frac{6}{5}}{-\frac{3}{5}} \implies \frac{a_2}{a_1} = 2 \implies a_2 = 2a_1
   \]
   Substitute \(a_2 = 2a_1\) into the first equation:
   \[
   \frac{3a_1 - 3(2a_1)}{a_1^2 + (2a_1)^2} a_1 = -\frac{3}{5} \implies \frac{3a_1 - 6a_1}{a_1^2 + 4a_1^2} a_1 = -\frac{3}{5} \implies \frac{-3a_1}{5a_1^2} a_1 = -\frac{3}{5} \implies \frac{-3a_1^2}{5a_1^2} = -\frac{3}{5}
   \]
   This is true, so it confirms our ratio \(a_2 = 2a_1\). We can choose \(a_1 = 1\) and \(a_2 = 2\) so that \(\mathbf{a} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix}\) (we can scale it later if necessary, but here it's enough to determine the direction).

3. **Orthogonal Vector \(\mathbf{b}\):**
   Since \(\mathbf{a}\) and \(\mathbf{b}\) are orthogonal, \(\mathbf{b}\) must be orthogonal to \(\mathbf{a}\). So, \(\mathbf{b} = \begin{pmatrix} b_1 \\ b_2 \end{pmatrix}\) such that:
   \[
   1 \cdot b_1 + 2 \cdot b_2 = 0 \implies b_1 = -2b_2
   \]
   We can choose \(b_2 = 1\) and then \(b_1 = -2\), so \(\mathbf{b} = \begin{pmatrix} -2 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}\).

4. **Projection of \(\begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ -3 \end{pmatrix}\) onto \(\mathbf{b}\):**
   The projection of \(\begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ -3 \end{pmatrix}\) onto \(\mathbf{b}\) is given by:
   \[
   \operatorname{proj}_{\mathbf{b}} \begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ -3 \end{pmatrix} = \frac{\begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ -3 \end{pmatrix} \cdot \begin{pmatrix} -2 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}}{\begin{pmatrix} -2 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix} \cdot \begin{pmatrix} -2 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}} \begin{pmatrix} -2 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}
   \]
   First, compute the dot products:
   \[
   \begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ -3 \end{pmatrix} \cdot \begin{pmatrix} -2 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix} = 3 \cdot (-2) + (-3) \cdot 1 = -6 - 3 = -9
   \]
   \[
   \begin{pmatrix} -2 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix} \cdot \begin{pmatrix} -2 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix} = (-2) \cdot (-2) + 1 \cdot 1 = 4 + 1 = 5
   \]
   So, we get:
   \[
   \operatorname{proj}_{\mathbf{b}} \begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ -3 \end{pmatrix} = \frac{-9}{5} \begin{pmatrix} -2 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} \frac{18}{5} \\ -\frac{9}{5} \end{pmatrix}
   \]
