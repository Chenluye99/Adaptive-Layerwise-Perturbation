Let's define the variables first. Suppose Monsieur Dupont initially took \( f \) francs and \( c \) centimes with him to the restaurant. The total amount of money he initially had is \( 100f + c \) centimes (since 1 franc = 100 centimes).

After his dinner, the amount of money he has left is \(\frac{1}{5} \times (100f + c)\) of the initial amount, which is \(\frac{100f + c}{5}\).

Let's call the amount of money he has left \( n \) francs and \( m \) centimes. Therefore, the total amount of money he has left in centimes is \( 100n + m \).

According to the problem, the centimes he has left are equal to the francs he initially had, so we get:
\[ m = f. \]

And the francs he has left are five times less than the initial centimes he had, so we get:
\[ n = \frac{c}{5}. \]

Also, the money he has left (\( 100n + m \)) should be equal to \(\frac{100f + c}{5}\). Substituting \( m = f \) and \( n = \frac{c}{5} \) into the equation, we get:
\[ 100 \cdot \frac{c}{5} + f = \frac{100f + c}{5}. \]

Simplifying this, we obtain:
\[ 20c + f = \frac{100f + c}{5}. \]

Multiplying both sides by 5, we get:
\[ 100c + 5f = 100f + c. \]

Rearranging terms, we get:
\[ 100c - c = 100f - 5f, \]
\[ 99c = 95f, \]
\[ 99c = 95f. \]

This equation tells us that \( 99c \) is a multiple of 95 and \( 95f \) is a multiple of 99. To solve this, we can find integer solutions by iterating possible values for \( c \) and solving for \( f \):

\[ f = \frac{99c}{95}. \]

Since \( f \) and \( c \) are integers, \( \frac{99c}{95} \) must also be an integer, meaning \( 99c \) must be divisible by 95. The smallest positive integer value for \( c \) that makes \( \frac{99c}{95} \) an integer is 95 (because 95 is a prime factor of 99 * 95). Substituting \( c = 95 \), we get:
\[ f = \frac{99 \cdot 95}{95} = 99. \]
