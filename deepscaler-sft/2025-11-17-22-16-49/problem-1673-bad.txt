The grid provided in the problem consists of a 3 by 3 array of squares, where each square is 3 units wide and 1 unit tall. We can think about forming rectangles by choosing two horizontal lines and two vertical lines from the grid's intersections. 

1. **Grid Structure:**
    - There are 4 horizontal lines (since there are 3 squares in height and each square is 1 unit tall, plus the top and bottom lines beyond the squares).
    - There are 4 vertical lines (since there are 3 squares in width and each square is 3 units wide, plus the lines beyond the squares).

2. **Counting Rectangles:**
    - To form a rectangle, we need to choose 2 out of these 4 horizontal lines and 2 out of these 4 vertical lines. Each selection of 2 lines out of 4 in both directions will give us a unique combination of opposite sides for the rectangle.
    - The number of ways to choose 2 lines from 4 lines is given by the combination formula \( \binom{4}{2} \).

Let's compute it step by step:

\[
\binom{4}{2} = \frac{4!}{2!(4-2)!} = \frac{4!}{2!2!} = \frac{4 \cdot 3}{2 \cdot 1} = 6
\]

So, the total number of rectangles is:

\[
\binom{4}{2} \times \binom{4}{2} = 6 \times 6 = 36
\]
