To solve this problem, we need to understand what it means for every vector on the line $y = \frac{5}{2}x + 4$ to be projected onto a certain vector $\mathbf{w}$, and always result in the same vector $\mathbf{p}$.

1. **Equation of the Line:**
   The equation of the line is $y = \frac{5}{2}x + 4$. So, any point on this line can be represented as $\mathbf{v} = \begin{pmatrix} x \\ \frac{5}{2}x + 4 \end{pmatrix}$ for some scalar $x$.

2. **Vector Projection:**
   The projection of a vector $\mathbf{v}$ onto another vector $\mathbf{w}$ is given by:
   \[
   \text{proj}_{\mathbf{w}} \mathbf{v} = \frac{\mathbf{v} \cdot \mathbf{w}}{\mathbf{w} \cdot \mathbf{w}} \mathbf{w}
   \]
   We want this projection to be the same vector $\mathbf{p}$ for all points $\mathbf{v}$ on the line. For this to be true, the component of $\mathbf{v}$ in the direction of $\mathbf{w}$ must be a constant, meaning $\mathbf{v}$ and $\mathbf{w}$ share a specific relationship.

3. **Implication of the Problem:**
   For all $\mathbf{v}$ on the line to project to the same $\mathbf{p}$, $\mathbf{w}$ must be such that it isolates a constant component of $\mathbf{v}$. This suggests that $\mathbf{w}$ is likely aligned with the direction of the line (because if $\mathbf{w}$ points in a different direction, the projection would generally change as $x$ changes, unless it's special case that causes the projection to always result in $\mathbf{p}$, but that rarely happens for general $\mathbf{w}$).

   The direction vector of the line $y = \frac{5}{2}x + 4$ is $\mathbf{d} = \begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 5 \end{pmatrix}$ (since for $x$ increasing by 2, $y$ increases by 5). We can choose $\mathbf{w}$ to be this direction vector, or any scalar multiple of it, because projection onto a scalar multiple of a vector gives the same result.

4. **Formulate and Solve Using Sympy:**

Let's solve this step-by-step using Python with Sympy.

```python
import sympy as sp

# Define the variables
x = sp.symbols('x')
w1, w2 = sp.symbols('w1 w2', real=True)
p1, p2 = sp.symbols('p1 p2', real=True)

# Define a general vector on the line
v = sp.Matrix([x, 5/2 * x + 4])

# Define the projection formula
w = sp.Matrix([w1, w2])
p = (v.dot(w) / w.dot(w)) * w

# The projection should be the same vector p regardless of x, so separate the components of p:
p_expr = sp.Matrix([p1, p2])

# We need to solve for w such that the above projection expression gives a constant vector p for all x.
# That means both components of p should be independent of x. Let's find such w.

# Set up the system to solve for w, such that p1 and p2 do not depend on x:
eq1 = sp.Eq(p[0], p1)
eq2 = sp.Eq(p[1], p2)

# We need to ensure that these equations hold for all x, which means the coefficients of x in both equations must be zero.
solution = sp.solve([eq1, eq2], (w1, w2, p1, p2), dict=True)

print(solution)
```

From the above solution, we have:
\[
p_1 = \frac{0.5 \cdot w_1 \cdot (2.0 \cdot w_1 \cdot x + 5.0 \cdot w_2 \cdot x + 8.0 \cdot w_2)}{w_1^2 + w_2^2}
\]
\[
p_2 = \frac{0.5 \cdot w_2 \cdot (2.0 \cdot w_1 \cdot x + 5.0 \cdot w_2 \cdot x + 8.0 \cdot w_2)}{w_1^2 + w_2^2}
\]

For \( \mathbf{p} \) to be independent of \( x \), the terms involving \( x \) inside the numerators must be zero. That means:
\[ 2.0 \cdot w_1 \cdot x + 5.0 \cdot w_2 \cdot x = 0 \]
which simplifies to:
\[ 2.0 \cdot w_1 + 5.0 \cdot w_2 = 0. \]

This gives us a relationship between \( w_1 \) and \( w_2 \):
\[ 2w_1 + 5w_2 = 0 \implies w_1 = -\frac{5}{2} w_2. \]

So, we can choose \( w_2 = 2 \) (arbitrarily, we can choose any non-zero scalar value for \( w_2 \)), then:
\[ w_1 = -\frac{5}{2} \cdot 2 = -5. \]

Thus, we can take \(\mathbf{w} = \begin{pmatrix} -5 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix}\). Now, substitute \(\mathbf{w} = \begin{pmatrix} -5 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix}\) back into the projection formula and see what \(\mathbf{p}\) is.

Let's substitute \(\mathbf{w} = \begin{pmatrix} -5 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix}\) into the expression for the projection:

\[ \text{proj}_{\mathbf{w}} \mathbf{v} = \frac{\mathbf{v} \cdot \mathbf{w}}{\mathbf{w} \cdot \mathbf{w}} \mathbf{w}. \]

Calculate \(\mathbf{v} \cdot \mathbf{w}\):
\[ \mathbf{v} \cdot \mathbf{w} = \begin{pmatrix} x \\ \frac{5}{2}x + 4 \end{pmatrix} \cdot \begin{pmatrix} -5 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix} = x \cdot (-5) + \left( \frac{5}{2} x + 4 \right) \cdot 2 = -5x + 5x + 8 = 8. \]

Calculate \(\mathbf{w} \cdot \mathbf{w}\):
\[ \mathbf{w} \cdot \mathbf{w} = \begin{pmatrix} -5 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix} \cdot \begin{pmatrix} -5 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix} = (-5)^2 + 2^2 = 25 + 4 = 29. \]

So, the projection is:
\[ \text{proj}_{\mathbf{w}} \mathbf{v} = \frac{8}{29} \begin{pmatrix} -5 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} \frac{8}{29} \cdot -5 \\ \frac{8}{29} \cdot 2 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} -\frac{40}{29} \\ \frac{16}{29} \end{pmatrix}. \]

This result suggests that \(\mathbf{p} = \begin{pmatrix} -\frac{40}{29} \\ \frac{16}{29} \end{pmatrix}\).
