To solve this problem, we need to determine the equation of the line in slope-intercept form \( y = mx + b \), where \( m \) is the slope and \( b \) is the y-intercept.

1. **Identify the given information:**
    - The slope of the line, \( m = -2 \).
    - The x-intercept of the line is \((5, 0)\), which means the line passes through the point \((5, 0)\).

2. **Formulate the equation of the line:**
    - Substitute the slope \( m = -2 \) into the equation \( y = mx + b \), so the equation becomes \( y = -2x + b \).
    - Use the x-intercept point \((5, 0)\) to solve for the y-intercept \( b \). Substitute \( x = 5 \) and \( y = 0 \) into the equation:
      \[
      0 = -2(5) + b \implies 0 = -10 + b \implies b = 10
      \]
    - So, the y-intercept is \( b = 10 \), and the y-intercept point of the line is \((0, 10)\).

Let's implement this reasoning in Python to verify our solution.
```python
# Given values
x_intercept = 5
y_intercept_point = (0, 0)
slope = -2

# Using the equation y = mx + b and substituting the x-intercept (5, 0) to solve for b:
x, y = x_intercept, y_intercept_point[1]
b = y - slope * x

# The y-intercept point will be (0, b)
y_intercept = (0, b)
print(y_intercept)
```
Code execution result: (0, 10)

Thus, the y-intercept point of the line is \(\boxed{(0, 10)}\). This confirms that our solution is accurate.
