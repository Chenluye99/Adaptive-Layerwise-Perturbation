First, let's set up the geometric constraints and relationships among the circles.

1. **First Circle**: The first circle has a radius of \( s \) and is tangent to both the \( x \)-axis and the \( y \)-axis. Therefore, its center is at \(( s, s )\).

2. **Second Circle**: The second circle is tangent to the first circle and the \( x \)-axis. Since it is tangent to the \( x \)-axis, its center has a \( y \)-coordinate of \( s' \) (its radius), and because it is tangent to the first circle, the distance between the centers of the first and second circles should be the sum of their radii, i.e., \( s' + s \). The center of the second circle is at \(( s'' , s'' )\) but since it is tangent to the \( x \)-axis, its center is \(( s'', s'' ) = ( s' , s' )\) and because it is tangent to the first circle, the distance between the centers is \( s' + s \):
   \[
   \sqrt{(s'' - s)^2 + (s'' - s)^2} = s' + s \implies \sqrt{2(s')^2} = s' + s \implies s' \sqrt{2} = s' + s
   \]
   Solving for \( s' \), we get:
   \[
   s' \sqrt{2} - s' = s \implies s' (\sqrt{2} - 1) = s \implies s' = \frac{s}{\sqrt{2} - 1} = s (\sqrt{2} + 1)
   \]

3. **Third Circle**: The third circle is tangent to the first circle and the \( y \)-axis. Similarly, its center has an \( x \)-coordinate of \( s''' \) (its radius), and because it is tangent to the first circle, the distance between the centers of the first and third circles should be the sum of their radii, i.e., \( s''' + s \). The center of the third circle is at \(( s''' , s''' )\) but since it is tangent to the \( y \)-axis, its center is \(( s''' , s''' ) = ( s''' , s''' )\) and because it is tangent to the first circle, the distance between the centers is \( s''' + s \):
   \[
   \sqrt{(s''' - s)^2 + (s''' - s)^2} = s''' + s \implies \sqrt{2(s''')^2} = s''' + s \implies s''' \sqrt{2} = s''' + s
   \]
   Solving for \( s''' \), we get:
   \[
   s''' \sqrt{2} - s''' = s \implies s''' (\sqrt{2} - 1) = s \implies s''' = \frac{s}{\sqrt{2} - 1} = s (\sqrt{2} + 1)
   \]

4. **Fourth Circle**: The fourth circle has a radius of \( r \) and is tangent to both axes, the second circle, and the third circle. Since it is tangent to both axes, its center is at \(( r, r )\). Because it is tangent to the second circle (which has a center at \(( s' , s' ) \) and radius \( s' \)) and the third circle (which has a center at \(( s''' , s''' ) \) and radius \( s''' \)), the distance between the center of the fourth circle and the center of the second or third circle will be \( r + s' \) or \( r + s''' \):
   \[
   \text{Distance to second circle: } \sqrt{(r - s')^2 + (r - s')^2} = r + s'
   \]
   \[
   \sqrt{2(r - s')^2} = r + s' \implies \sqrt{2} (r - s') = r + s'
   \]
   Squaring both sides, we get:
   \[
   2 (r - s')^2 = (r + s')^2
   \]
   Similarly, for the third circle:
   \[
   \sqrt{(r - s''')^2 + (r - s''')^2} = r + s''
   \]
   \[
   \sqrt{2(r - s''')^2} = r + s'''
   \]
   Squaring both sides, we get:
   \[
   2 (r - s''')^2 = (r + s''')^2
   \]
   Since \( s' = s''' = s (\sqrt{2} + 1) \), we only need to solve one of these equations for \( r \) in terms of \( s \).

Let's solve \(\sqrt{2}(r - s') = r + s'\):
\[
\sqrt{2}(r - s (\sqrt{2} + 1)) = r + s (\sqrt{2} + 1)
\]
Let's define \( s' = s (\sqrt{2} + 1) \) and substitute it into the equation and solve for \( r \).

We'll use Python to get the precise solution.
```python
import sympy as sp

# Define variables
r, s = sp.symbols('r s', positive=True)

# Equation from the relationship based on distances and tangencies
s_prime = s * (sp.sqrt(2) + 1)
eq = sp.Eq(sp.sqrt(2) * (r - s_prime), r + s_prime)

# Solve the equation for r in terms of s
solution = sp.solve(eq, r)[0]
ratio = solution / s
print(ratio)
```
