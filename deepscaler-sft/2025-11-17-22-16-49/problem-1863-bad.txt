First, we start by understanding and manipulating the given parameterized lines:

1. The first line is parameterized as:
\[
\begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ -2 \end{pmatrix} + t \begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ 4 \end{pmatrix}
\]
This means that any point \((x, y)\) on this line can be written as:
\[
\begin{cases}
x = 0 + 3t = 3t, \\
y = -2 + 4t.
\end{cases}
\]
So, the direction vector of the first line is \(\begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ 4 \end{pmatrix}\).

2. The second line is parameterized as:
\[
\begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} -8 \\ 12 \end{pmatrix} + u \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix}
\]
This means that any point \((x, y)\) on this line can be written as:
\[
\begin{cases}
x = -8 + 1u = -8 + u, \\
y = 12 + 3u.
\end{cases}
\]
So, the direction vector of the second line is \(\begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix}\).

To find the angle \(\theta\) between the two lines, we use the dot product formula for the direction vectors \(\mathbf{d_1} = \begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ 4 \end{pmatrix}\) and \(\mathbf{d_2} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix}\).

The cosine of the angle \(\theta\) between two vectors \(\mathbf{d_1}\) and \(\mathbf{d_2}\) is given by:
\[
\cos \theta = \frac{\mathbf{d_1} \cdot \mathbf{d_2}}{\|\mathbf{d_1}\| \|\mathbf{d_2}\|},
\]
where \(\mathbf{d_1} \cdot \mathbf{d_2}\) is the dot product of \(\mathbf{d_1}\) and \(\mathbf{d_2}\), and \(\|\mathbf{d_1}\|\) and \(\|\mathbf{d_2}\|\) are the magnitudes (or norms) of \(\mathbf{d_1}\) and \(\mathbf{d_2}\), respectively.

Let's compute these step-by-step:

1. **Compute the dot product** \(\mathbf{d_1} \cdot \mathbf{d_2}\):
\[
\mathbf{d_1} \cdot \mathbf{d_2} = 3 \cdot 1 + 4 \cdot 3 = 3 + 12 = 15.
\]

2. **Compute the magnitudes** \(\|\mathbf{d_1}\|\) and \(\|\mathbf{d_2}\|\):
\[
\|\mathbf{d_1}\| = \sqrt{3^2 + 4^2} = \sqrt{9 + 16} = \sqrt{25} = 5,
\]
\[
\|\mathbf{d_2}\| = \sqrt{1^2 + 3^2} = \sqrt{1 + 9} = \sqrt{10}.
\]

3. **Compute \(\cos \theta\)**:
\[
\cos \theta = \frac{15}{5 \cdot \sqrt{10}} = \frac{15}{5 \sqrt{10}} = \frac{3}{\sqrt{10}} = \frac{3 \sqrt{10}}{10}.
\]

Let's confirm this by implementing it in Python.
```python
import sympy as sp

# Define the direction vectors
d1 = sp.Matrix([3, 4])
d2 = sp.Matrix([1, 3])

# Compute the dot product of d1 and d2
dot_product = d1.dot(d2)

# Compute the magnitudes of d1 and d2
magnitude_d1 = sp.sqrt(d1.dot(d1))
magnitude_d2 = sp.sqrt(d2.dot(d2))

# Compute cos(theta)
cos_theta = dot_product / (magnitude_d1 * magnitude_d2)
print(cos_theta)
```
