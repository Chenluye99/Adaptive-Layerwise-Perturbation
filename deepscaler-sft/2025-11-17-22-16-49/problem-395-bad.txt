An arithmetic sequence is defined by its first term \(a_1\) and common difference \(d\). The \(n\)-th term of an arithmetic sequence is given by:
\[ a_n = a_1 + (n-1)d \]

Given that \(a_1 = 1\), we have:
\[ a_2 = a_1 + d = 1 + d \]

The sum of the first \(n\) terms of an arithmetic sequence \(S_n\) is given by:
\[ S_n = \frac{n}{2} \left( 2a_1 + (n-1)d \right) \]

For the first 2 terms, the sum \(S_2\) is:
\[ S_2 = \frac{2}{2} \left( 2 \cdot 1 + (2-1)d \right) = 2 \cdot (1 + d) = 2 (1 + d) \]

We are given that:
\[ 2S_2 = a_2 (a_2 + 1) \]

Substitute \(S_2\) and \(a_2\):
\[ 2 \cdot 2 (1 + d) = (1 + d) ((1 + d) + 1) \]
\[ 4 (1 + d) = (1 + d) (2 + d) \]

We can divide both sides by \(1 + d\) (since \(1 + d \neq 0\) as all terms of the sequence are positive):
\[ 4 = 2 + d \]
\[ d = 2 \]

So, we have the common difference \(d = 2\). Therefore, the \(n\)-th term \(a_n\) of the arithmetic sequence is:
\[ a_n = 1 + (n-1) \cdot 2 = 2n - 1 \]

And the sum of the first \(n\) terms \(S_n\) is:
\[ S_n = \frac{n}{2} \left( 2 \cdot 1 + (n-1) \cdot 2 \right) = \frac{n}{2} \left( 2 + 2n - 2 \right) = \frac{n}{2} \cdot 2n = n^2 \]

\[ \frac{2S_n + 13}{n} = \frac{2n^2 + 13}{n} = \frac{2n^2}{n} + \frac{13}{n} = 2n + \frac{13}{n} \]
