To solve this problem, we need to determine the side length of the square using the distance between its adjacent vertices, and then compute the area of the square.

1. **Calculate the Distance Between Adjacent Vertices:**
   The distance \(d\) between two points \((x_1, y_1)\) and \((x_2, y_2)\) is given by the formula:
   \[
   d = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2}
   \]
   Here, the points are \((-1, 4)\) and \((2, -3)\). Substituting these points into the formula, we get:
   \[
   d = \sqrt{(2 - (-1))^2 + (-3 - 4)^2} = \sqrt{(2 + 1)^2 + (-7)^2} = \sqrt{3^2 + 7^2} = \sqrt{9 + 49} = \sqrt{58}
   \]
   So, the distance between the points \((-1, 4)\) and \((2, -3)\) is \(\sqrt{58}\). Since this distance is the side length of the square, the side length of the square is \(\sqrt{58}\).

2. **Calculate the Area of the Square:**
   The area \(A\) of a square with side length \(s\) is given by:
   \[
   A = s^2
   \]
   Substituting the side length \(\sqrt{58}\) into the formula, we get:
   \[
   A = (\sqrt{58})^2 = 58
   \]

Now, let's confirm this by implementing it in Python.
```python
import sympy as sp

# Coordinates of the points
x1, y1 = -1, 4
x2, y2 = 2, -3

# Calculate the distance between the points
distance = sp.sqrt((x2 - x1)**2 + (y2 - y1)**2)

# The distance is the side length of the square, so the area of the square is distance^2
area_of_square = distance ** 2
print(area_of_square)
```
