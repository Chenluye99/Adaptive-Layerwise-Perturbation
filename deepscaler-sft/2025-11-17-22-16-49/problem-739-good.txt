First, let's start by finding the volume of the sphere and the volume of the right cylinder, and then determine the volume of the space inside the sphere but outside the cylinder.

1. **Volume of the Sphere:**
   The formula for the volume of a sphere is given by:
   \[
   V_{\text{sphere}} = \frac{4}{3} \pi r^3
   \]
   Given that the radius of the sphere is 5 units, we substitute \( r = 5 \):
   \[
   V_{\text{sphere}} = \frac{4}{3} \pi (5)^3 = \frac{4}{3} \pi \cdot 125 = \frac{500}{3} \pi
   \]

2. **Volume of the Right Cylinder:**
   The right cylinder is inscribed in the sphere, so its height and base radius need to be derived based on the sphere's geometry.

   Consider the right circular cylinder of radius 3 units and height \( h \). For a cylinder inscribed in a sphere, the relationship of the dimensions can be deduced with the help of the Pythagorean theorem. If we consider a right section of the sphere that cuts through the cylinder (forming a right triangle with the sphere's diameter as the hypotenuse and the cylinder's height and diameter as the other two sides), we get:
   \[
   \left(\frac{h}{2}\right)^2 + 3^2 = 5^2
   \]
   Simplifying this,
   \[
   \left(\frac{h}{2}\right)^2 + 9 = 25 \implies \left(\frac{h}{2}\right)^2 = 16 \implies \frac{h}{2} = 4 \implies h = 8
   \]

   So, the height \( h \) of the cylinder is 8 units.

   The volume of a cylinder is given by:
   \[
   V_{\text{cylinder}} = \pi r^2 h
   \]
   Substituting the radius \( r = 3 \) and height \( h = 8 \):
   \[
   V_{\text{cylinder}} = \pi (3)^2 (8) = \pi \cdot 9 \cdot 8 = 72 \pi
   \]

3. **Volume of the Space Inside the Sphere and Outside the Cylinder:**
   The volume we are looking for is the volume of the sphere minus the volume of the cylinder:
   \[
   V_{\text{space}} = V_{\text{sphere}} - V_{\text{cylinder}} = \frac{500}{3} \pi - 72 \pi
   \]
   To simplify this expression, we need a common denominator:
   \[
   V_{\text{space}} = \frac{500}{3} \pi - \frac{216}{3} \pi = \frac{500 - 216}{3} \pi = \frac{284}{3} \pi
   \]

   Therefore, we have:
   \[
   V_{\text{space}} = W \pi
   \]
   By comparison, it is clear that:
   \[
   W = \frac{284}{3}
   \]

```python
# Calculate the volume of the space inside the sphere and outside the cylinder

# Get W such that V_space = W * pi

```
