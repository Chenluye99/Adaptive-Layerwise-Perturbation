First, let's begin by recalling some properties of quadratic equations. For a quadratic equation of the form \(ax^2 + bx + c = 0\), if the roots are \( \alpha \) and \( \beta \), then:

- The sum of the roots, \( \alpha + \beta = -\frac{b}{a} \).
- The product of the roots, \( \alpha \beta = \frac{c}{a} \).

For the given quadratic equation \( ax^2 + 5x - 3 = 0 \):

- The sum of the roots, \( \alpha + \beta = -\frac{5}{a} \).
- The product of the roots, \( \alpha \beta = -\frac{3}{a} \).

The absolute value of the difference of the roots of a quadratic equation \( ax^2 + bx + c = 0 \) is given by:

\[ |\alpha - \beta| = \frac{\sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{|a|} \]

For the given equation \( ax^2 + 5x - 3 = 0 \), we have \( b = 5 \) and \( c = -3 \), so:

\[ |\alpha - \beta| = \frac{\sqrt{5^2 - 4(a)(-3)}}{|a|} = \frac{\sqrt{25 + 12a}}{|a|} = \frac{\sqrt{25 + 12a}}{a} \]

We are given that the absolute value of the difference of the two roots is \(\frac{\sqrt{61}}{3}\). Therefore, we can set up the following equation:

\[ \frac{\sqrt{25 + 12a}}{a} = \frac{\sqrt{61}}{3} \]

By cross-multiplying, we get:

\[ 3 \sqrt{25 + 12a} = a \sqrt{61} \]

Square both sides to eliminate the square roots:

\[ 9 (25 + 12a) = a^2 \cdot 61 \]

This expands to:

\[ 225 + 108a = 61a^2 \]

Rearrange the equation to standard quadratic form:

\[ 61a^2 - 108a - 225 = 0 \]

We can solve this quadratic equation for \( a \) using the quadratic formula \( a = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a} \), where \( a = 61 \), \( b = -108 \), and \( c = -225 \):

\[ a = \frac{-(-108) \pm \sqrt{(-108)^2 - 4(61)(-225)}}{2(61)} \]
\[ a = \frac{108 \pm \sqrt{11664 + 54450}}{122} \]
\[ a = \frac{108 \pm \sqrt{66114}}{122} \]
\[ a = \frac{108 \pm 257.17}{122} \]

This gives us two potential solutions:

\[ a = \frac{108 + 257.17}{122} \approx \frac{365.17}{122} \approx 3.0014 \]
\[ a = \frac{108 - 257.17}{122} \approx \frac{-149.17}{122} \approx -1.22 \]

Since \( a \) is given to be positive, we discard the negative solution, and we take:

\[ a \approx 3.0014 \]

```python
import sympy as sp
```
