To solve this problem, we need to find the greatest common divisor (GCD) of \(n^3 + 2^2 = n^3 + 4\) and \(n + 2\). To do this, we will use the Euclidean algorithm, which tells us that \(\gcd(a, b) = \gcd(b, a \mod b)\).

Let's define:
\[ a = n^3 + 4 \]
\[ b = n + 2 \]

We want to find \(\gcd(n^3 + 4, n + 2)\). According to the Euclidean algorithm, we can compute this as follows:

1. Compute \( n^3 + 4 \mod (n + 2) \).
2. Replace \( a \) with \( b \) and \( b \) with the result from step 1, then repeat until \( b \) becomes 0. The last non-zero remainder is the GCD.

To compute \( n^3 + 4 \mod (n + 2) \), we perform polynomial division or we can substitute \( n = -2 + k \) where \( k \) is a variable, so that \( n + 2 = k \), and then substitute this into \( n^3 + 4 \):

\[ n = k - 2 \]
\[ n^3 + 4 = (k - 2)^3 + 4 \]

We will then find the remainder when \((k - 2)^3 + 4\) is divided by \(k\):

\[ (k - 2)^3 + 4 = (k - 2)^3 + 4 \]
