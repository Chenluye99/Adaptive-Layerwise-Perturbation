First, let's start by visualizing the problem.

We have the following curves:
1. \( y = x^2 \)
2. \( y = \frac{1}{4}x^2 \)
3. The horizontal line \( y = 1 \)

To find the points of intersection, we need to determine where these curves and the line intersect.

- The curve \( y = x^2 \) intersects \( y = 1 \) at \( x = \pm 1 \).
- The curve \( y = \frac{1}{4}x^2 \) intersects \( y = 1 \) at \( x = \pm 2 \).

Now, to find the region enclosed by these curves and the line \( y = 1 \), we will set up an integral to compute the area between the curves and the line \( y = 1 \).

We need to determine the intersection points of \( y = x^2 \) and \( y = \frac{1}{4}x^2 \) to ensure that we integrate from the correct bounds.

Setting \( x^2 = \frac{1}{4}x^2 \), it's clear that the only valid intersection within our bounds will be from -1 to 1 because for \( x \) outside of this range, \( \frac{1}{4}x^2 \) will exceed 1 and we will integrate up to \( y = 1 \).

So, the area will be split into two parts to be integrated properly:
1. From \( x = -2 \) to \( x = -1 \), the upper curve is \( y = 1 \) and the lower curve is \( y = \frac{1}{4}x^2 \).
2. From \( x = -1 \) to \( x = 1 \), the upper curve is \( y = 1 \) and the lower curve is \( y = x^2 \).
3. From \( x = 1 \) to \( x = 2 \), the upper curve is \( y = 1 \) and the lower curve is \( y = \frac{1}{4}x^2 \).

However, due to the symmetry of the functions about the y-axis, we can integrate from 0 to 1 and then multiply by 2 to get the total area.

Let's compute this step by step by integrating the differences of the functions over the appropriate intervals:

1. Integrate from -1 to 1 between \( y = 1 \) and \( y = x^2 \).
2. Integrate from -2 to -1 and from 1 to 2 between \( y = 1 \) and \( y = \frac{1}{4}x^2 \).

We will compute this using Python to ensure accuracy.
```python
import sympy as sp

# Define the variables
x = sp.symbols('x')

# Define the functions
y1 = 1
y2 = x**2
y3 = (1/4) * x**2

# Compute the area from -1 to 1 between y = 1 and y = x^2
area_1 = sp.integrate(y1 - y2, (x, -1, 1))

# Compute the area from -2 to -1 and from 1 to 2 between y = 1 and y = (1/4)x^2
area_2 = sp.integrate(y1 - y3, (x, -2, -1)) + sp.integrate(y1 - y3, (x, 1, 2))

# Total area is the sum of area_1 and area_2
total_area = area_1 + area_2
print(total_area)
```

Thus, the area of the plane region enclosed by the curves \( y = x^2 \), \( y = \frac{1}{4}x^2 \), and the line \( y = 1 \) is \(\boxed{2.16666666666667}\). This value can also be expressed as the fraction \(\frac{13}{6}\).
