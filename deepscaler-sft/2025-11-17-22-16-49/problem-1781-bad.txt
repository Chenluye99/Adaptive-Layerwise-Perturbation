To solve the inequality problem, we need to split it into two separate inequalities and solve them step-by-step:

1. First, we solve the inequality \( 5x - 1 < (x + 1)^2 \).
2. Then, we solve the inequality \( (x + 1)^2 < 7x - 3 \).
3. Finally, we find the intersection of the solutions to these two inequalities.

Let's start by solving each inequality separately.

### Step 1: Solve \( 5x - 1 < (x + 1)^2 \)

First, rewrite the inequality:
\[ 5x - 1 < (x + 1)^2. \]
Expand and simplify:
\[ 5x - 1 < x^2 + 2x + 1, \]
\[ 0 < x^2 + 2x + 1 - 5x + 1, \]
\[ 0 < x^2 - 3x + 2, \]
\[ x^2 - 3x + 2 > 0. \]
Factorize the quadratic expression:
\[ (x - 1)(x - 2) > 0. \]
The critical points are \( x = 1 \) and \( x = 2 \). The expression \((x - 1)(x - 2) > 0\) is true when \( x \) is in the intervals \( (-\infty, 1) \cup (2, \infty) \).

### Step 2: Solve \( (x + 1)^2 < 7x - 3 \)

First, rewrite the inequality:
\[ (x + 1)^2 < 7x - 3. \]
Expand and simplify:
\[ x^2 + 2x + 1 < 7x - 3, \]
\[ x^2 + 2x + 1 - 7x + 3 < 0, \]
\[ x^2 - 5x + 4 < 0. \]
Factorize the quadratic expression:
\[ (x - 1)(x - 4) < 0. \]
The critical points are \( x = 1 \) and \( x = 4 \). The expression \((x - 1)(x - 4) < 0\) is true when \( x \) is in the interval \( (1, 4) \).

### Step 3: Find the Intersection of the Solutions

We need to find the intersection of the solutions from the two inequalities:
1. From \( 5x - 1 < (x + 1)^2 \), we have \( x \in (-\infty, 1) \cup (2, \infty) \).
2. From \( (x + 1)^2 < 7x - 3 \), we have \( x \in (1, 4) \).

The intersection of these solutions is:
\[ (2, \infty) \cap (1, 4) = (2, 4). \]

So, the solution to the original compound inequality \( 5x - 1 < (x + 1)^2 < 7x - 3 \) is:
\[ x \in (2, 4). \]
