First, let's break down the problem to understand the structure of the T-shaped figure.

1. **Form the T-shaped Figure:**
    - Place a 2 inch by 6 inch rectangle vertically.
    - Place a 3 inch by 2 inch rectangle horizontally on top of the vertical rectangle at its midpoint (which means the 3 inch side of the horizontal rectangle will be aligned with the middle of the 6 inch side of the vertical rectangle).

2. **Visualize the Dimensions of the T:**
    - The vertical part of the T is 2 inches wide and 6 inches tall.
    - The horizontal part of the T is 3 inches wide and 2 inches tall. It is placed on top of the vertical part of the T so that its full height of 2 inches is aligned with the middle of the vertical rectangle's height (3 inches from the top and 3 inches from the bottom of the 6 inch side of the vertical rectangle).

3. **Calculate the Perimeter:**
    - The perimeter of the T-shape will be the sum of all distinct outer edges.
    - The vertical portion contributes 2 inches on each of its two vertical sides (but we need to adjust for the overlap where the horizontal piece is placed, so only 4 inches of its top and bottom edges will contribute to the perimeter).
    - The horizontal portion contributes its full dimensions on its sides and top, but we need to exclude the portion that overlaps with the vertical portion on the top.

Let's now map this out properly to find the total perimeter.
