To determine the value of $y$ that makes the vectors $\begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ -3 \\ -4 \end{pmatrix}$ and $\begin{pmatrix} -2 \\ y \\ -1 \end{pmatrix}$ orthogonal, we need to use the property that the dot product of two orthogonal vectors is zero.

The dot product of the vectors $\begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ -3 \\ -4 \end{pmatrix}$ and $\begin{pmatrix} -2 \\ y \\ -1 \end{pmatrix}$ is calculated as follows:

\[ 1 \cdot (-2) + (-3) \cdot y + (-4) \cdot (-1) = 0 \]

This simplifies to:

\[ -2 - 3y + 4 = 0 \]

Combining like terms, we get:

\[ 2 - 3y = 0 \]

Now, solve for $y$:

\[ 2 = 3y \]
\[ y = \frac{2}{3} \]

Let's confirm this by implementing it in Python.
```python
from sympy import symbols, Eq, solve

# Define the variable
y = symbols('y')

# Define the equation for the dot product to be zero
equation = Eq(1 * (-2) + (-3) * y + (-4) * (-1), 0)

# Solve the equation for y
solution = solve(equation, y)[0]
print(solution)
```
Code execution result: 2/3

Thus, the value of $y$ that makes the vectors $\begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ -3 \\ -4 \end{pmatrix}$ and $\begin{pmatrix} -2 \\ y \\ -1 \end{pmatrix}$ orthogonal is \(\boxed{\frac{2}{3}}\). This solution has been confirmed by solving the equation directly.
