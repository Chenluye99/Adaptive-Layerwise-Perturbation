To solve the given problem, we need to use the formula for the projection of vector \(\mathbf{v}\) onto vector \(\mathbf{w}\):

\[
\text{proj}_{\mathbf{w}} \mathbf{v} = \frac{\mathbf{v} \cdot \mathbf{w}}{\mathbf{w} \cdot \mathbf{w}} \mathbf{w}
\]

Given:
\[
\mathbf{v} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ y \end{pmatrix}, \quad \mathbf{w} = \begin{pmatrix} 9 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix}, \quad \text{proj}_{\mathbf{w}} \mathbf{v} = \begin{pmatrix} -6 \\ -2 \end{pmatrix}
\]

First, let's define the dot products:

\[
\mathbf{v} \cdot \mathbf{w} = 1 \cdot 9 + y \cdot 3 = 9 + 3y
\]

\[
\mathbf{w} \cdot \mathbf{w} = 9 \cdot 9 + 3 \cdot 3 = 81 + 9 = 90
\]

Substitute these into the projection formula:

\[
\text{proj}_{\mathbf{w}} \mathbf{v} = \frac{9 + 3y}{90} \mathbf{w} = \frac{9 + 3y}{90} \begin{pmatrix} 9 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix}
\]

This should be equal to \(\begin{pmatrix} -6 \\ -2 \end{pmatrix}\), so we can write:

\[
\frac{9 + 3y}{90} \begin{pmatrix} 9 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} -6 \\ -2 \end{pmatrix}
\]

This gives us two equations by comparing the components:

\[
\frac{9 + 3y}{90} \cdot 9 = -6
\]

\[
\frac{9 + 3y}{90} \cdot 3 = -2
\]

We can solve either of these equations for \( y \). Let's start with the first equation:

\[
\frac{9 + 3y}{90} \cdot 9 = -6
\]

Simplify:

\[
\frac{9 (9 + 3y)}{90} = -6 \implies \frac{9 + 3y}{10} = -6 \implies 9 + 3y = -60 \implies 3y = -69 \implies y = -23
\]

We can verify this by plugging \( y = -23 \) back into the second equation to ensure it holds true:

\[
\frac{9 + 3(-23)}{90} \cdot 3 = \frac{9 - 69}{90} \cdot 3 = \frac{-60}{90} \cdot 3 = -\frac{2}{3} \cdot 3 = -2
\]

Since both equations are satisfied with \( y = -23 \), the solution is correct. Let's confirm this by coding it in Python to ensure accuracy.

# Define the variables

# Set up the equations by equating the projection to the given result
eq1 = sp.Eq(proj_v_on_w[0], proj_expected[0])

# Solve for y
