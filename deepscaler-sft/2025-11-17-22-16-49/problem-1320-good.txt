To solve the problem of finding the focus of the parabola given by the equation \( y = 4x^2 - 3 \), we can follow these steps:

1. **Understand the Equation:**
    The given equation is \( y = 4x^2 - 3 \). This is a standard form of a parabola that opens upwards or downwards. We can compare it with the standard form of a parabola \( y = a(x - h)^2 + k \), where \((h, k)\) is the vertex of the parabola.

2. **Identify Vertex:**
    By comparing \( y = 4x^2 - 3 \) with \( y = a(x - h)^2 + k \), we can see that the vertex \((h, k)\) of this parabola is \((0, -3)\) because \( h = 0 \) and \( k = -3 \).

3. **Find the Focus:**
    For the standard form \( y = a(x - h)^2 + k \), the value of \(a\) is 4. The focus of a parabola \( y = a(x - h)^2 + k \) is given by \((h, k + \frac{1}{4a}) \). Here, \( a = 4 \), so \(\frac{1}{4a} = \frac{1}{4 \cdot 4} = \frac{1}{16} \).

    Therefore, the focus of the parabola is \((0, -3 + \frac{1}{16}) = (0, -\frac{48}{16} + \frac{1}{16}) = (0, -\frac{47}{16})\).
