First, let's start by understanding the problem and breaking it down step-by-step.

1. **Understand the Problem:**
    - When two dice are rolled, each die can show a number from 1 to 6.
    - We need to find the probability that the sum of the numbers shown on the two dice is even.

2. **Determine When the Sum is Even:**
    - The sum of two numbers is even if both numbers are either even or both are odd.
    - The even numbers on a die are 2, 4, 6, and the odd numbers are 1, 3, 5.

3. **Enumerate All Possible Outcomes:**
    - There are 6 faces on each die, so there are \(6 \times 6 = 36\) possible outcomes when two dice are rolled.
    - We'll now list out all possible pairs of outcomes and count how many of these pairs have an even sum.

4. **Count Favorable Outcomes:**
    - We iterate through all pairs \((i, j)\) where \(i\) and \(j\) are the outcomes of the first and second die respectively, and check if the sum \(i + j\) is even.
    - We then count the number of such pairs.

5. **Calculate Probability:**
    - The probability is the ratio of the number of favorable outcomes to the total number of outcomes, i.e., \(\frac{\text{Number of favorable outcomes}}{36}\).

Let's implement this step-by-step process in Python to ensure accuracy.
```python
# Initialize a counter for favorable outcomes
favorable_outcomes = 0

# Loop through all possible outcomes of rolling two dice
for i in range(1, 7):
    for j in range(1, 7):
        if (i + j) % 2 == 0:
            favorable_outcomes += 1

# Total outcomes when rolling two dice
total_outcomes = 6 * 6

# Calculate the probability as a fraction
probability = favorable_outcomes / total_outcomes

print(((favorable_outcomes, total_outcomes, probability)))
```
Code execution result: (18, 36, 0.5)

From the code, we have:
- Number of favorable outcomes: 18
- Total number of outcomes: 36
- Probability: \( \frac{18}{36} = 0.5 \)

So, the probability that the sum of the numbers shown on the top faces of the two dice is even is \(\frac{1}{2}\).

Thus, the final answer is:
\(\boxed{\frac{1}{2}}\)
