To solve this problem, we need to apply the principles of conservation of momentum and kinematics.

1. **Initial Setup:**
    - The firecracker is thrown vertically upwards with an initial velocity of 20 m/s.
    - After 3 seconds, its velocity just before the explosion is given by \(v = v_0 - g \cdot t = 20 - 10 \cdot 3 = 20 - 30 = -10 \, \text{m/s}\). The negative sign indicates the direction is downward.
    - At the moment of explosion, the firecracker's position and velocity are needed, but for this problem, we only need its momentum before the explosion.

2. **Momentum Before the Explosion:**
    - Let the mass of the firecracker be \(m\). Therefore, the mass of the smaller fragment after the explosion is \(\frac{m}{3}\) and the mass of the larger fragment is \(\frac{2m}{3}\).
    - The momentum of the firecracker before the explosion is \( m \cdot (-10) = -10m \) (directed downwards).

3. **Conservation of Momentum:**
    - After the explosion, the smaller fragment flies horizontally with a speed of 16 m/s. Let its mass be \(\frac{m}{3}\), and its velocity be \((0, 16) \, \text{m/s}\) (since it flies horizontally).
    - Let the velocity of the larger fragment be \((v_x, v_y)\) with a mass of \(\frac{2m}{3}\).
    - The total momentum after the explosion must equal the total momentum before the explosion. Since the firecracker was moving vertically before the explosion, its momentum in the horizontal direction was zero.
    - Therefore, the total momentum in the vertical direction after the explosion must be \(-10m\), and the total momentum in the horizontal direction must be zero (since there was no horizontal component before the explosion).

    Mathematically, we can express this as follows:
    - **Horizontal Direction (initially there was no horizontal component):**
      \[
      \frac{m}{3} \cdot 0 + \frac{2m}{3} \cdot v_x = 0 \implies v_x = 0
      \]
    - **Vertical Direction:**
      \[
      \frac{m}{3} \cdot 0 + \frac{2m}{3} \cdot v_y = -10m \implies \frac{2m}{3} \cdot v_y = -10m \implies v_y = -\frac{10m}{\frac{2m}{3}} = -10 \cdot \frac{3}{2} = -15 \, \text{m/s}
      \]

    So, the velocity of the larger fragment is \((0, -15)\) in the vertical direction and \(0\) in the horizontal direction, which means it is purely moving downwards with a speed of 15 m/s in the vertical direction, but this is not complete because we know the correct approach should consider both directions to get the magnitude of the speed after accounting for internal explosion energy (kinetic and other possible releases).

To account for the correct dynamics and speeds in all directions, we must compute the speed magnitude based on the emerging vector resultant ways:
1. We know the smaller fragment has a speed of 16 m/s and is flying horizontally.
2. We need to compute the resultant speed of the second (larger) fragment combining its vertical and horizontal speeds correctly.

Let's write Python code to carefully solve for the complete vector components and resultant speed magnitude of the larger fragment.

```python
import sympy as sp

# Define the variables
m1, m2 = sp.Rational(1, 3), sp.Rational(2, 3)
v1 = 16  # Speed of the smaller fragment immediately after the explosion
v1x, v1y = 0, v1  # Velocity components of the smaller fragment (horizontal and vertical)

# Let v2x and v2y be the velocity components of the larger fragment after the explosion. 
# The total momentum must be conserved in both the x and y directions.
# The initial momentum of the firecracker before the explosion is -10m in the y-direction, and 0 in the x-direction.

# Setting up the equations based on momentum conservation:
# In the x-direction: m1 * v1x + m2 * v2x = 0
# In the y-direction: m1 * v1y + m2 * v2y = -10

# Solve for v2x and v2y
v2x, v2y = sp.symbols('v2x v2y')
eq_x = m1 * v1x + m2 * v2x
eq_y = m1 * v1y + m2 * v2y + 10

sol = sp.solve((eq_x, eq_y), (v2x, v2y))
v2x_val = sol[v2x]
v2y_val = sol[v2y]

# Now, compute the magnitude of the speed of the larger fragment
v2 = sp.sqrt(v2x_val**2 + v2y_val**2)
print(v2)
```
Code execution result: 23

Hence, the magnitude of the speed of the second fragment immediately after the explosion is \(\boxed{23}\) m/s. This is our final answer, obtained through both reasoning and verified by executing the Python code.
