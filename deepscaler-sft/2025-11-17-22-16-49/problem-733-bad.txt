To solve this problem, let's break it down step-by-step:

1. **Determine \( m \):**
    - \( m \) is the smallest positive integer with only two positive divisors. 
    - The smallest positive integer with only two positive divisors is a prime number, and the smallest prime number is 2. So, \( m = 2 \).

2. **Determine \( n \):**
    - \( n \) is the largest integer less than 100 that has exactly three positive divisors.
    - A number has exactly three positive divisors if and only if it is the square of a prime number. This is because if \( n = p^2 \) where \( p \) is a prime, the divisors of \( n \) are \( 1, p, \) and \( p^2 \).
    - To find the largest such number less than 100, we will check the squares of prime numbers that are below 100 when squared.
    - The prime numbers and their squares to consider are: \( 2^2 = 4, 3^2 = 9, 5^2 = 25, 7^2 = 49, 11^2 = 121 \) (which is greater than 100, so we don't consider it). Thus, the largest square of a prime number that is less than 100 is \( 7^2 = 49 \). Therefore, \( n = 49 \).

3. **Find \( m + n \):**
    - Now, we just need to add \( m \) and \( n \):
      \[
      m + n = 2 + 49 = 51
      \]

Let's confirm this by implementing it in Python.
```python
import sympy

# Step 1: Determine m
m = 2  # The smallest positive integer with only two positive divisors is 2 (which is a prime number)

# Step 2: Determine n
# Find the largest integer less than 100 that has exactly three positive divisors
largest_n = 0
for i in range(2, 100):
    divisors = list(sympy.divisors(i))
    if len(divisors) == 3:
        largest_n = i

# Step 3: Calculate m + n
result = m + largest_n
print(result)
```
Code execution result: 51

Thus, the final answer is \(\boxed{51}\). This confirms that our reasoning and calculations were correct.
