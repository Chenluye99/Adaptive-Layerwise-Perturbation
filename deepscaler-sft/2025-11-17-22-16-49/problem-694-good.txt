To solve the problem, we need to use properties of a parallelogram. In a parallelogram, opposite angles are equal, and consecutive angles are supplementary (i.e., they add up to 180 degrees).

Let's denote the measure of angle \( Q \) as \( x \). Consequently, the measure of angle \( P \) is \( 5x \).

Since \( P \) and \( Q \) are consecutive angles in the parallelogram, they must add up to 180 degrees:

\[
P + Q = 180^\circ
\]

Substitute \( P \) and \( Q \) with their respective measures:

\[
5x + x = 180^\circ
\]

This simplifies to:

\[
6x = 180^\circ
\]

Solving for \( x \):

\[
x = \frac{180^\circ}{6} = 30^\circ
\]

Thus, the measure of angle \( Q \) is \( 30^\circ \), and the measure of angle \( P \) is \( 5 \times 30^\circ = 150^\circ \).

In a parallelogram, opposite angles are equal, so:

\[
\angle P = \angle R \quad \text{and} \quad \angle Q = \angle S
\]

Therefore, the measure of angle \( R \) is the same as the measure of angle \( P \), which is \( 150^\circ \).
