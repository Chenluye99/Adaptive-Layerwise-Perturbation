1. **Find the slope of the line containing the points \((-1, 2)\) and \((1, -2)\):**
    - The formula for the slope \(m\) of a line passing through two points \((x_1, y_1)\) and \((x_2, y_2)\) is given by:
      \[
      m = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}
      \]
    - Substituting \((x_1, y_1) = (-1, 2)\) and \((x_2, y_2) = (1, -2)\):
      \[
      m = \frac{-2 - 2}{1 - (-1)} = \frac{-4}{2} = -2
      \]

2. **Find the slope of the line that is perpendicular to this line:**
    - If the slope of a line is \(m\), then the slope of any line perpendicular to it is \(-\frac{1}{m}\).
    - Given that the slope \(m\) of our line is \(-2\), the slope of the line that is perpendicular to it will be:
      \[
      m_\perp = -\frac{1}{-2} = \frac{1}{2}
      \]

Let's confirm this by implementing it in Python.
```python
# Define the points
x1, y1 = -1, 2
x2, y2 = 1, -2

# Calculate the slope of the line through the points (-1, 2) and (1, -2)
slope = (y2 - y1) / (x2 - x1)

# Calculate the slope of the line that is perpendicular to it
perpendicular_slope = -1 / slope
print(perpendicular_slope)
```

Thus, the slope of the line that is perpendicular to the line containing the points \((-1, 2)\) and \((1, -2)\) is \(\frac{1}{2}\).
