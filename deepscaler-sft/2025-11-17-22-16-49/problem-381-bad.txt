To solve this problem, we need to determine the side length of the square using the distance between the given points \((-1, 4)\) and \((2, -3)\). Once we get the length of one side of the square, we can compute its area.

### Step-by-Step Solution:

1. **Calculate the Distance Between the Points:**
   The distance \(d\) between two points \((x_1, y_1)\) and \((x_2, y_2)\) is given by the formula:
   \[
   d = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2}
   \]
   Substituting \((-1, 4)\) and \((2, -3)\) into this formula, we get:
   \[
   d = \sqrt{(2 - (-1))^2 + (-3 - 4)^2} = \sqrt{(2 + 1)^2 + (-3 - 4)^2} = \sqrt{3^2 + (-7)^2} = \sqrt{9 + 49} = \sqrt{58}
   \]
   This distance \(d = \sqrt{58}\) is either the side length of the square or the length of its diagonal, depending on where the points are positioned relative to each other.

2. **Determine if the Distance is a Side Length or a Diagonal:**
   If \((-1, 4)\) and \((2, -3)\) are adjacent vertices of the square, then the distance between them is the length of the side of the square rotated or placed in such a manner that it might relate to a diagonal across from some other point forming the square.

   Since diagonals of a square relate to its side length \(s\) as \(d = s \sqrt{2}\), let’s assume the distance calculated above is actually the diagonal of the square. So, if \(d\) is the diagonal, we have:
   \[
   d = s \sqrt{2}
   \]
   Substituting \(d = \sqrt{58}\), we get:
   \[
   \sqrt{58} = s \sqrt{2}
   \]
   Solving for \(s\):
   \[
   s = \frac{\sqrt{58}}{\sqrt{2}} = \sqrt{\frac{58}{2}} = \sqrt{29}
   \]
   The side length of the square is \(s = \sqrt{29}\).

3. **Calculate the Area of the Square:**
   The area \(A\) of a square is given by the square of its side length:
   \[
   A = s^2 = (\sqrt{29})^2 = 29
   \]

Let's confirm this by coding the solution in Python to ensure accuracy.
```python
import sympy as sp

# Coordinates of the points
x1, y1 = -1, 4
x2, y2 = 2, -3

# Calculate the distance between the points
distance = sp.sqrt((x2 - x1)**2 + (y2 - y1)**2)

# The side length 's' of the square can be derived from the relationship d = s * sqrt(2)
# So, s = d / sqrt(2)
side_length = distance / sp.sqrt(2)

# Calculate the area of the square, which is s^2
area_of_square = side_length ** 2
print(area_of_square)
```
