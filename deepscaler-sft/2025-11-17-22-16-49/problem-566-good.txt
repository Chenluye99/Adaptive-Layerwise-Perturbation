#### Part 1: Find the value of the real number \( m \)

1. **Understand the Problem:**
    - Given vectors \(\overrightarrow{a} = (1, -3)\) and \(\overrightarrow{b} = (-2, m)\).
    - The vectors \(\overrightarrow{a}\) and \(\overrightarrow{a} - \overrightarrow{b}\) are perpendicular, so their dot product is zero.

2. **Calculate \(\overrightarrow{a} - \overrightarrow{b}\):**
    \[
    \overrightarrow{a} - \overrightarrow{b} = (1 - (-2), -3 - m) = (3, -3 - m)
    \]

3. **Set Up the Perpendicular Condition:**
    - The dot product of \(\overrightarrow{a}\) and \(\overrightarrow{a} - \overrightarrow{b}\) is:
      \[
      \overrightarrow{a} \cdot (\overrightarrow{a} - \overrightarrow{b}) = (1, -3) \cdot (3, -3 - m) = 1 \cdot 3 + (-3) \cdot (-3 - m) = 3 + 9 + 3m = 12 + 3m
      \]
    - Since \(\overrightarrow{a}\) is perpendicular to \(\overrightarrow{a} - \overrightarrow{b}\), the dot product equals 0:
      \[
      12 + 3m = 0 \implies 3m = -12 \implies m = -4
      \]

#### Part 2: Find the value of the real number \( k \) when \( k\overrightarrow{a} + \overrightarrow{b} \) is parallel to \(\overrightarrow{a} - \overrightarrow{b} \)

1. **Understand the Problem:**
    - Vectors are parallel if one is a scalar multiple of the other. That is, there exists some scalar \(\lambda\) such that:
      \[
      k\overrightarrow{a} + \overrightarrow{b} = \lambda (\overrightarrow{a} - \overrightarrow{b})
      \]
    - Substitute \(\overrightarrow{a} = (1, -3)\) and \(\overrightarrow{b} = (-2, -4)\) into the equation:
      \[
      k(1, -3) + (-2, -4) = \lambda (3, -3 - (-4)) \implies (k - 2, -3k - 4) = \lambda (3, 1)
      \]
    - This gives us the system of equations:
      \[
      k - 2 = 3\lambda
      \]
      \[
      -3k - 4 = \lambda
      \]

2. **Set Up and Solve the System of Equations:**
    - Substitute \(\lambda = -3k - 4\) into the first equation:
      \[
      k - 2 = 3(-3k - 4) \implies k - 2 = -9k - 12 \implies k + 9k = -12 + 2 \implies 10k = -10 \implies k = -1
      \]
