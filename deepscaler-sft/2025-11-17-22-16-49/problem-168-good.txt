To solve this problem, we need to know how the wall is divided between Matilda and Ellie. For simplicity, let's assume the entire wall is divided into equal sections between Matilda and Ellie, and let's assume there are no other sections or people involved in painting the wall.

1. Let's say that the entire wall is divided into \(N\) sections.
2. Matilda and Ellie each get an equal share of these sections, so each of them gets \(\frac{N}{2}\) sections.

3. Matilda paints half of her section red, so she paints \(\frac{1}{2} \times \frac{N}{2} = \frac{N}{4}\) sections of the wall red.
4. Ellie paints one third of her section red, so she paints \(\frac{1}{3} \times \frac{N}{2} = \frac{N}{6}\) sections of the wall red.

5. The total number of sections painted red is the sum of the sections painted red by Matilda and Ellie, which is:
   \[
   \frac{N}{4} + \frac{N}{6}
   \]

6. To find the common denominator for \(\frac{N}{4}\) and \(\frac{N}{6}\), we note that the least common multiple of 4 and 6 is 12. So, we rewrite the fractions with this common denominator:
   \[
   \frac{N}{4} = \frac{3N}{12}, \quad \frac{N}{6} = \frac{2N}{12}
   \]
   Adding these together, we get:
   \[
   \frac{3N}{12} + \frac{2N}{12} = \frac{5N}{12}
   \]

7. The fraction of the entire wall that is painted red is \(\frac{\frac{5N}{12}}{N} = \frac{5}{12}\).
