To solve this problem, let's start by using some properties and formulas related to the isosceles trapezoid and its circumscribed circle. 

1. **Properties of Circumscribed Trapezoid:**
    - For a trapezoid to be circumscribed around a circle, the sum of its opposite sides must be equal. So, if the lengths of the bases are \(a\) and \(b\) (with \(a\) being the longer base), and the lengths of the legs are \(c\), then \(a + b = 2c\).
    - Here, the longer base \(a = 16\).

2. **Base Angle:**
    - One of the base angles is \(\arcsin(0.8)\). So, \(\sin(\theta) = 0.8\), and we can find \(\cos(\theta)\) using the identity \(\cos^2(\theta) + \sin^2(\theta) = 1\):
      \[
      \cos(\theta) = \sqrt{1 - \sin^2(\theta)} = \sqrt{1 - 0.8^2} = \sqrt{1 - 0.64} = \sqrt{0.36} = 0.6
      \]

3. **Find the Length of the Legs:**
    - Drop perpendiculars from the endpoints of the shorter base to the longer base. Let the length of the shorter base be \(b\), and the legs of the trapezoid be \(c\). The height of the trapezoid is \(h = c \cdot \sin(\theta) = c \cdot 0.8\).
    - The horizontal distance between the legs, which is the length of the longer base minus the length of the shorter base, is split into three parts: two segments that are equal (each of length \(c \cdot \cos(\theta) = c \cdot 0.6\)), and the shorter base. So, we have:
      \[
      16 - b = 2 \cdot (c \cdot 0.6) \implies 16 - b = 1.2c
      \]
    - Also, using the property of the circumscribed trapezoid, we know:
      \[
      16 + b = 2c
      \]

4. **Solve for \(b\) and \(c\):**
    - We now have a system of equations:
      \[
      \begin{cases}
      16 + b = 2c \\
      16 - b = 1.2c
      \end{cases}
      \]
    - Adding these two equations:
      \[
      (16 + b) + (16 - b) = 2c + 1.2c \implies 32 = 3.2c \implies c = \frac{32}{3.2} = 10
      \]
    - Substitute \(c = 10\) back into \(16 + b = 2c\):
      \[
      16 + b = 2 \cdot 10 \implies 16 + b = 20 \implies b = 4
      \]

5. **Find the Height of the Trapezoid:**
    - Now that we have \(c = 10\) and \(\sin(\theta) = 0.8\), the height \(h\) of the trapezoid is:
      \[
      h = c \cdot \sin(\theta) = 10 \cdot 0.8 = 8
      \]

6. **Find the Area of the Trapezoid:**
    - The area \(A\) of a trapezoid is given by:
      \[
      A = \frac{1}{2} \cdot ( \text{base}_1 + \text{base}_2 ) \cdot \text{height}
      \]
      Substituting the values we have:
      \[
      A = \frac{1}{2} \cdot (16 + 4) \cdot 8 = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 20 \cdot 8 = 10 \cdot 8 = 80
      \]
