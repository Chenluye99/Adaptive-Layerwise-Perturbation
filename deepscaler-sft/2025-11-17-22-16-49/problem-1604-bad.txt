A moving particle starts from the origin on a number line. In the first second, it moves 1 unit in the positive direction. In the second second, it moves 2 units in the negative direction. In the third second, it moves 3 units in the positive direction, and in the fourth second, it moves 4 units in the negative direction. This pattern of movement continues, with odd-numbered seconds involving movement in the positive direction and even-numbered seconds involving movement in the negative direction. After n seconds, when it stops, its distance from the origin is 2016 units. Find the value of n.
Let's represent the movement of the particle mathematically step by step. 

1. **Pattern Recognition:**
   - In the 1st second, it moves 1 unit in the positive direction.
   - In the 2nd second, it moves 2 units in the negative direction.
   - In the 3rd second, it moves 3 units in the positive direction.
   - In the 4th second, it moves 4 units in the negative direction.
   - And so on...

2. **Formulating Movement in Terms of \( n \):**
   - After 1 second, the position is \( 1 \).
   - After 2 seconds, the position is \( 1 - 2 = -1 \).
   - After 3 seconds, the position is \( -1 + 3 = 2 \).
   - After 4 seconds, the position is \( 2 - 4 = -2 \).

   We can see that pattern continues, and the net movement after every two seconds can be computed in terms of the absolute units moved in each direction:
   - After 2k seconds (k full cycles of 2 seconds):
     - It moves (1 + 3 + 5 + ... + (2k-1)) units in the positive direction.
     - It moves (2 + 4 + 6 + ... + 2k) units in the negative direction.
   - The sum of the first \( k \) odd numbers is \( k^2 \).
   - The sum of the first \( k \) even numbers is \( k(k+1) \).

   Therefore, after 2k seconds, the net movement is \( k^2 - k(k+1) = k^2 - k^2 - k = -k \).

3. **Generalizing for Any \( n \):**
   - After 2k seconds, it is at position \(-k\).
   - After 2k + 1 seconds, it moves (2k+1) units in the positive direction, so the position becomes \(-k + (2k+1) = k + 1\).

So, after \( n \) seconds, the position of the particle will depend on whether \( n \) is even or odd. We need to determine \( n \) such that after \( n \) seconds, the distance from the origin is 2016 units.

4. **Formulating the Equation:**
   - If \( n = 2k \) (an even number), then the position after \( 2k \) seconds is \(-k \).
   - If \( n = 2k + 1 \) (an odd number), then the position after \( 2k + 1 \) seconds is \( k + 1 \).

Since the distance from the origin is 2016 units, we have two cases to consider:

### Case 1: \( n = 2k \)
In this case, the position after \( 2k \) seconds is \(-k \), and the distance from the origin is \( | - k | = k \). We want this to be 2016, so:
\[ k = 2016 \]
This means \( n = 2 \cdot 2016 = 4032 \).

### Case 2: \( n = 2k + 1 \)
In this case, the position after \( 2k + 1 \) seconds is \( k + 1 \), and the distance from the origin is \( | k + 1 | = k + 1 \). We want this to be 2016, so:
\[ k + 1 = 2016 \]
This means \( k = 2015 \), and thus \( n = 2 \cdot 2015 + 1 = 4031 \).

# Function to simulate the movement of the particle and find the correct n such that its distance from the origin is 2016 units
def find_n_for_distance(target_distance):
    position = 0  # Start at the origin
    seconds = 0   # Start at 0 seconds
    
    while True:
        seconds += 1
        if seconds % 2 == 1:  # Move in the positive direction
            position += seconds
        else:  # Move in the negative direction
            position -= seconds
        
        if abs(position) == target_distance:
            return seconds

# Find the value of n such that the distance from the origin is 2016 units
