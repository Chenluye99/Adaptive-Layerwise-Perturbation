To find the coefficient of the \(x^2 y^4\) term in the expansion of \((x + y)^6\), we can use the binomial theorem. The binomial theorem states that:

\[
(x + y)^n = \sum_{k=0}^{n} \binom{n}{k} x^{n-k} y^k
\]

For \((x + y)^6\), the general term in the expansion is given by:

\[
\binom{6}{k} x^{6-k} y^k
\]

We want the coefficient of the \(x^2 y^4\) term. This means we need the term where the exponent of \(x\) is 2 and the exponent of \(y\) is 4. So, we set up:

\[
6 - k = 2 \quad \text{and} \quad k = 4
\]

Solving for \(k\), we get:

\[
6 - k = 2 \implies k = 4
\]

So, we substitute \(k = 4\) into the binomial coefficient formula:

\[
\binom{6}{4} x^{6-4} y^4 = \binom{6}{4} x^2 y^4
\]

The coefficient of the \(x^2 y^4\) term is \(\binom{6}{4}\). We know that:

\[
\binom{6}{4} = \frac{6!}{4!(6-4)!} = \frac{6!}{4! \cdot 2!} = \frac{6 \cdot 5 \cdot 4 \cdot 3 \cdot 2 \cdot 1}{4 \cdot 3 \cdot 2 \cdot 1 \cdot 2 \cdot 1} = \frac{6 \cdot 5}{2 \cdot 1} = 15
\]

Thus, the coefficient is 15. Let's confirm this using Python to ensure the accuracy of our result.
```python
import math

# Calculate the binomial coefficient for (x + y)^6 where we need the term with x^2 * y^4
n = 6
k = 4

# Compute the binomial coefficient using math.comb which is available in Python 3.8+
binomial_coefficient = math.comb(n, k)
print(binomial_coefficient)
```
Code execution result: 15

Thus, the coefficient of the \(x^2 y^4\) term in the expansion of \((x + y)^6\) is \(\boxed{15}\). This confirms our previous reasoning and calculations.
