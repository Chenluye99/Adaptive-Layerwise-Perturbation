First, let's start by recalling the formula for the volume of a cone:

\[ V = \frac{1}{3} \pi r^2 h \]

where \( r \) is the radius and \( h \) is the height of the cone.

For cone \( C \):
- Radius \( r_C = 16.4 \)
- Height \( h_C = 30.5 \)

For cone \( D \):
- Radius \( r_D = 30.5 \)
- Height \( h_D = 16.4 \)

We will plug these values into the volume formula to get the volumes of cones \( C \) and \( D \), and then find the ratio of the volume of cone \( C \) to the volume of cone \( D \).

1. Compute the volume of cone \( C \):

\[ V_C = \frac{1}{3} \pi (16.4)^2 (30.5) \]

2. Compute the volume of cone \( D \):

\[ V_D = \frac{1}{3} \pi (30.5)^2 (16.4) \]

3. Find the ratio of the volume of cone \( C \) to the volume of cone \( D \):

\[ \text{Ratio} = \frac{V_C}{V_D} = \frac{\frac{1}{3} \pi (16.4)^2 (30.5)}{\frac{1}{3} \pi (30.5)^2 (16.4)} = \frac{(16.4)^2 (30.5)}{(30.5)^2 (16.4)} = \frac{(16.4)}{(30.5)} \]
