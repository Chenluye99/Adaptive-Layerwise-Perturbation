To solve this problem, we need to find values of \( n \) and \( k \) such that the binomial coefficients \(\binom{n}{k}\), \(\binom{n}{k+1}\), and \(\binom{n}{k+2}\) are in the ratio 1:2:3. That is, we want:

\[
\frac{\binom{n}{k+1}}{\binom{n}{k}} = 2 \quad \text{and} \quad \frac{\binom{n}{k+2}}{\binom{n}{k+1}} = \frac{3}{2}
\]

First, recall the definition of the binomial coefficient:

\[
\binom{n}{k} = \frac{n!}{k!(n-k)!}
\]

So, we have:

\[
\binom{n}{k+1} = \frac{n!}{(k+1)!(n-k-1)!} \quad \text{and} \quad \binom{n}{k+2} = \frac{n!}{(k+2)!(n-k-2)!}
\]

Then, the ratios can be written as:

\[
\frac{\binom{n}{k+1}}{\binom{n}{k}} = \frac{\frac{n!}{(k+1)!(n-k-1)!}}{\frac{n!}{k!(n-k)!}} = \frac{k! (n-k)!}{(k+1)! (n-k-1)!} = \frac{(n-k)}{(k+1)} = 2
\]

And,

\[
\frac{\binom{n}{k+2}}{\binom{n}{k+1}} = \frac{\frac{n!}{(k+2)!(n-k-2)!}}{\frac{n!}{(k+1)!(n-k-1)!}} = \frac{(k+1)! (n-k-1)!}{(k+2)! (n-k-2)!} = \frac{(n-k-1)}{(k+2)} = \frac{3}{2}
\]

This gives us two equations:

1. \(\frac{n - k}{k + 1} = 2 \implies n - k = 2(k + 1) \implies n - k = 2k + 2 \implies n = 3k + 2 \)
2. \(\frac{n - k - 1}{k + 2} = \frac{3}{2} \implies 2(n - k - 1) = 3(k + 2) \implies 2n - 2k - 2 = 3k + 6 \implies 2n = 5k + 8 \implies n = \frac{5k + 8}{2} \)

We can now substitute \( n = 3k + 2 \) into the second equation to solve for \( k \) and \( n \):

\[
3k + 2 = \frac{5k + 8}{2}
\]

Multiply both sides by 2:

\[
2(3k + 2) = 5k + 8 \implies 6k + 4 = 5k + 8 \implies k = 4
\]

Substitute \( k = 4 \) back into \( n = 3k + 2 \):

\[
n = 3(4) + 2 = 12 + 2 = 14
\]

So, one solution is \( n = 14 \) and \( k = 4 \), giving \( n + k = 14 + 4 = 18 \).
