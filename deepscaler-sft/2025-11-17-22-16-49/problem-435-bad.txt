1. **Properties of the Isosceles Triangle:**
    - Let's assume \(AB = AC\) (since \( \triangle ABC \) is isosceles with apex \(A\)).
    - Let \(BC = a\), \(AB = AC = b\).
    - The incenter \(I\) is the center of the inscribed circle of the triangle, and it is also the intersection point of the angle bisectors of the triangle.
    - The distance from the incenter to any side of the triangle is the inradius \(r\) of the triangle. We are given that this distance is 2, so \(r = 2\).

2. **Using the Formula for Inradius:**
    - The inradius \(r\) of a triangle is given by the formula:
      \[
      r = \frac{A}{s}
      \]
      where \(A\) is the area of the triangle and \(s\) is the semi-perimeter of the triangle.
    - For \(\triangle ABC\), the semi-perimeter \(s\) is:
      \[
      s = \frac{a + b + b}{2} = \frac{a + 2b}{2}
      \]
    - The area \(A\) of \(\triangle ABC\) can also be expressed using the formula involving the inradius and the semi-perimeter:
      \[
      A = r \cdot s = 2 \cdot \frac{a + 2b}{2} = a + 2b
      \]
    - We can also express the area of \(\triangle ABC\) using Heron's formula or by considering that the height from \(A\) to \(BC\) (let's call it \(h\)) and the properties of an isosceles triangle. However, for simplicity, let's use another useful property here.
