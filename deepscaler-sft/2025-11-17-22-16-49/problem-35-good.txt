### Step-by-Step Solution:

1. **Find the Trigonometric Functions from Point \( P(-4, 3) \):**
   - The coordinates of the point \( P(-4, 3) \) give us the values for the sides of the right triangle formed with the origin, so we can find \(\cos a\) and \(\sin a\).
   - The distance \( r \) from the origin to the point \( P(-4, 3) \) is given by:
     \[
     r = \sqrt{(-4)^2 + 3^2} = \sqrt{16 + 9} = \sqrt{25} = 5
     \]
   - The definitions of \(\cos a\) and \(\sin a\) based on the point \( P(-4, 3) \) are:
     \[
     \cos a = \frac{-4}{5} = -\frac{4}{5}, \quad \sin a = \frac{3}{5}
     \]

2. **Use Trigonometric Identities:**
   - We need to evaluate the expression:
     \[
     \frac{\cos \left( \frac{\pi}{2} + a \right) \sin \left( -\pi - a \right)}{\cos \left( \frac{11\pi}{2} - a \right) \sin \left( \frac{9\pi}{2} + a \right)}
     \]
   - Use trigonometric identities to simplify the terms:
     - \(\cos \left( \frac{\pi}{2} + a \right) = -\sin a\)
     - \(\sin \left( -\pi - a \right) = \sin (-\pi - a) = -\sin (\pi + a) = \sin a\)
     - \(\cos \left( \frac{11\pi}{2} - a \right) = \cos \left( 5\pi + \frac{\pi}{2} - a \right) = \cos \left( \pi + \frac{\pi}{2} - a \right) = -\cos \left( \frac{\pi}{2} - a \right) = -\sin a\)
     - \(\sin \left( \frac{9\pi}{2} + a \right) = \sin \left( 4\pi + \frac{\pi}{2} + a \right) = \sin \left( \frac{\pi}{2} + a \right) = \cos a\)

3. **Substitute and Simplify:**
   - Substitute these identities into the expression:
     \[
     \frac{\cos \left( \frac{\pi}{2} + a \right) \sin \left( -\pi - a \right)}{\cos \left( \frac{11\pi}{2} - a \right) \sin \left( \frac{9\pi}{2} + a \right)} = \frac{(-\sin a) \cdot (\sin a)}{(-\sin a) \cdot (\cos a)} = \frac{(-\sin^2 a)}{(-\sin a \cdot \cos a)} = \frac{\sin^2 a}{\sin a \cdot \cos a} = \frac{\sin a}{\cos a} = \tan a
     \]
   - We know that \(\tan a = \frac{\sin a}{\cos a} = \frac{\frac{3}{5}}{-\frac{4}{5}} = -\frac{3}{4}\).
