First, we need to find the coordinates of the incenter \( I \) of triangle \( ABC \). The coordinates of the incenter of a triangle with vertices \((x_1, y_1)\), \((x_2, y_2)\), and \((x_3, y_3)\) and side lengths \(a\), \(b\), and \(c\) opposite to those vertices, respectively, are given by:

\[
I = \left( \frac{ax_1 + bx_2 + cx_3}{a + b + c}, \frac{ay_1 + by_2 + cy_3}{a + b + c} \right)
\]

Given that \( A = (x_1, y_1) \), \( B = (x_2, y_2) = (0, 0) \), and \( C = (x_3, y_3) = (7, 0) \), and the side lengths are \( a = 7 \), \( b = 9 \), and \( c = 4 \), we can place point \( A \) at coordinates that satisfy the triangle constraints. Since we know the lengths of \( AB \), \( AC \), and \( BC \), we can find point \( A \) by solving the intersection of circles with radii 4 and 9 centered at \( B \) and \( C \) respectively.

To find point \( A \):

1. Set up the equations for the circles with centers at \( B \) and \( C \) and radii 4 and 9 respectively:
   - Circle centered at \( B \) with radius 4: \((x - 0)^2 + y^2 = 4^2 \Rightarrow x^2 + y^2 = 16\)
   - Circle centered at \( C \) with radius 9: \((x - 7)^2 + y^2 = 9^2 \Rightarrow (x - 7)^2 + y^2 = 81\)

2. Solve these two equations simultaneously to get the coordinates of point \( A \).

Let's solve for \( A \):

\[
\begin{cases}
x^2 + y^2 = 16 \\
(x - 7)^2 + y^2 = 81
\end{cases}
\]

Subtract the first equation from the second:

\[
(x - 7)^2 + y^2 - (x^2 + y^2) = 81 - 16 \Rightarrow (x - 7)^2 - x^2 = 65 \Rightarrow x^2 - 14x + 49 - x^2 = 65 \Rightarrow -14x + 49 = 65 \Rightarrow -14x = 16 \Rightarrow x = -\frac{8}{7}
\]

Substitute \( x = -\frac{8}{7} \) back into \( x^2 + y^2 = 16 \):

\[
\left( -\frac{8}{7} \right)^2 + y^2 = 16 \Rightarrow \frac{64}{49} + y^2 = 16 \Rightarrow y^2 = 16 - \frac{64}{49} = \frac{784 - 64}{49} = \frac{720}{49} \Rightarrow y = \frac{12 \sqrt{5}}{7}
\]

So, the coordinates of \( A \) are \( \left( -\frac{8}{7}, \frac{12 \sqrt{5}}{7} \right) \).

Next, we find the incenter \( I \) using the formula mentioned earlier:

\[
I = \left( \frac{7 \cdot 0 + 9 \cdot 7 + 4 \cdot \left( - \frac{8}{7} \right)}{7 + 9 + 4}, \frac{7 \cdot 0 + 9 \cdot 0 + 4 \cdot \frac{12 \sqrt{5}}{7}}{7 + 9 + 4} \right) = \left( \frac{0 + 63 - \frac{32}{7}}{20}, \frac{0 + 0 + \frac{48 \sqrt{5}}{7}}{20} \right)

Simplify the coordinates of \( I \):

\[

\]

Now, we express \(\overrightarrow{I}\) as a linear combination of \(\overrightarrow{A}\), \(\overrightarrow{B}\), and \(\overrightarrow{C}\):

\[
\overrightarrow{I} = x \overrightarrow{A} + y \overrightarrow{B} + z \overrightarrow{C}
\]

This means:

\[
\left( \frac{409}{140}, \frac{12 \sqrt{5}}{35} \right) = x \left( - \frac{8}{7}, \frac{12 \sqrt{5}}{7} \right) + y (0, 0) + z (7, 0)

This gives us two equations, one for the \(x\)-coordinate and one for the \(y\)-coordinate:

\[
\frac{409}{140} = x \left( - \frac{8}{7} \right) + z \cdot 7

\[
