To solve the problem, let's start by understanding the context and break it down into steps:

1. **Problem Understanding**:
    - We have 10 pieces of art, out of which 3 are by Escher.
    - We need to find the probability that all 3 pieces by Escher are placed consecutively when the 10 pieces of art are arranged randomly.

2. **Formulate a Strategy**:
    - We need to count how many arrangements of the 10 pieces have the 3 Escher pieces consecutive.
    - We then need to divide this by the total number of ways to arrange 10 pieces of art.

3. **Mathematical Formulation**:
    - Treat the 3 Escher pieces as a single "super piece". This means we now have 8 other pieces plus this "super piece", giving us a total of 9 items to arrange.
    - The number of ways to arrange these 9 items is \(9!\).
    - Within the "super piece" of 3 Escher pieces, the 3 Escher pieces can be ordered among themselves in \(3!\) ways.
    - Therefore, the number of ways to arrange the 10 pieces such that the 3 Escher pieces are consecutive is \(9! \times 3!\).
    - The total number of ways to arrange 10 pieces of art is simply \(10!\).

4. **Compute the Probability**:
    - The probability is given by the ratio of the number of favorable outcomes to the total number of outcomes: \(\frac{9! \times 3!}{10!}\).

Let's now implement this step-by-step in Python to ensure the accuracy of our result.
```python
import math

# Calculate the factorials
factorial_9 = math.factorial(9)
factorial_3 = math.factorial(3)
factorial_10 = math.factorial(10)

# Number of ways to arrange such that the 3 Escher pieces are consecutive
num_consecutive_ways = factorial_9 * factorial_3

# Total number of ways to arrange 10 pieces of art
total_ways = factorial_10

# Calculate the probability
probability = num_consecutive_ways / total_ways
print(probability)
```
Code execution result: 0.6

So, the probability that all three pieces by Escher will be placed consecutively when the 10 pieces of art are hung in a row in a random order is \(\boxed{0.6}\). This corresponds to \(\frac{3}{5}\) when expressed as a fraction.
