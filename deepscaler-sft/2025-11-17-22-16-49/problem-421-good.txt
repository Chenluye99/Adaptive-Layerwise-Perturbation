### Step-by-Step Solution:

1. **Set Up the Variables:**
   - Let \( R \) be the revenues in the previous year.
   - Let \( E \) be the expenses in the previous year.

2. **Formulate the Expressions for the Current Year (2006):**
   - Revenues in 2006: \( R_{2006} = R \cdot (1 + 0.25) = 1.25R \)
   - Expenses in 2006: \( E_{2006} = E \cdot (1 + 0.15) = 1.15E \)
   - Profit in 2006: \( \text{Profit}_{2006} = R_{2006} - E_{2006} = 1.25R - 1.15E \)
   - Profit in the previous year: \( \text{Profit}_{\text{prev}} = R - E \)
   - The problem states that the company's profit in 2006 increased by 40% compared to the previous year:
     \[
     \text{Profit}_{2006} = \text{Profit}_{\text{prev}} \cdot (1 + 0.40) = 1.40 (R - E)
     \]
   - So we have the equation:
     \[
     1.25R - 1.15E = 1.40 (R - E)
     \]

3. **Set Up and Solve the Equation:**
   - Expand the right-hand side:
     \[
     1.25R - 1.15E = 1.40R - 1.40E
     \]
   - Rearrange the equation to isolate terms involving \( R \) and \( E \):
     \[
     1.25R - 1.40R = -1.40E + 1.15E
     \]
     \[
     -0.15R = -0.25E
     \]
     \[
     0.15R = 0.25E
     \]
     \[
     \frac{R}{E} = \frac{0.25}{0.15} = \frac{25}{15} = \frac{5}{3}
     \]
   - Therefore:
     \[
     R = \frac{5}{3} E
     \]

     \[
     R_{2006} = 1.25 \cdot \frac{5}{3} E = \frac{1.25 \cdot 5}{3} E = \frac{6.25}{3} E

     \[
