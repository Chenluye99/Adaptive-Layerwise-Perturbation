First, let's start by understanding the problem and using mathematical reasoning before implementing it in Python.

1. **Area of the Larger Square:**
    - We are given that the area of the larger square is 60. If the side length of the larger square is \( s \), then:
      \[
      s^2 = 60 \implies s = \sqrt{60}
      \]

2. **Coords and Midpoints:**
    - Place the larger square in the coordinate system such that its vertices are at \((\pm s/2, \pm s/2)\). For simplicity, let's assume the side length \( s = \sqrt{60} \), so the vertices of the larger square are at \((\pm \frac{\sqrt{60}}{2}, \pm \frac{\sqrt{60}}{2})\).
    - The midpoints \(A, B, C, D\) of the sides of the larger square will have coordinates:
      - \( A \) is the midpoint of the side from \((-\frac{\sqrt{60}}{2}, \frac{\sqrt{60}}{2})\) to \((\frac{\sqrt{60}}{2}, \frac{\sqrt{60}}{2})\), so \( A = (0, \frac{\sqrt{60}}{2}) \).
      - \( B \) is the midpoint of the side from \((\frac{\sqrt{60}}{2}, \frac{\sqrt{60}}{2})\) to \((\frac{\sqrt{60}}{2}, -\frac{\sqrt{60}}{2})\), so \( B = (\frac{\sqrt{60}}{2}, 0) \).
      - \( C \) is the midpoint of the side from \((\frac{\sqrt{60}}{2}, -\frac{\sqrt{60}}{2})\) to \((- \frac{\sqrt{60}}{2}, -\frac{\sqrt{60}}{2})\), so \( C = (0, -\frac{\sqrt{60}}{2}) \).
      - \( D \) is the midpoint of the side from \((- \frac{\sqrt{60}}{2}, -\frac{\sqrt{60}}{2})\) to \((- \frac{\sqrt{60}}{2}, \frac{\sqrt{60}}{2})\), so \( D = (-\frac{\sqrt{60}}{2}, 0) \).

3. **Forming the Smaller Square:**
    - The points \( A, B, C, D \) form the vertices of the smaller square. To find the side length of the smaller square, observe that the distance between \( A \) and \( B \) (for example) is the diagonal of the smaller square.
    - The distance between \( A (0, \frac{\sqrt{60}}{2}) \) and \( B (\frac{\sqrt{60}}{2}, 0) \) can be computed using the distance formula:
      \[
      \text{Distance} = \sqrt{\left( \frac{\sqrt{60}}{2} - 0 \right)^2 + \left( 0 - \frac{\sqrt{60}}{2} \right)^2} = \sqrt{\left( \frac{\sqrt{60}}{2} \right)^2 + \left( \frac{\sqrt{60}}{2} \right)^2} = \sqrt{2 \cdot \left( \frac{\sqrt{60}}{2} \right)^2} = \sqrt{2 \cdot \frac{60}{4}} = \sqrt{30}
      \]
    - This distance is the diagonal of the smaller square. If the side length of the smaller square is \( s_{\text{small}} \), then the relationship between the side length and the diagonal of a square is given by:
      \[
      \text{Diagonal} = s_{\text{small}} \cdot \sqrt{2}
      \]
      So,
      \[
      s_{\text{small}} \cdot \sqrt{2} = \sqrt{30} \implies s_{\text{small}} = \frac{\sqrt{30}}{\sqrt{2}} = \sqrt{\frac{30}{2}} = \sqrt{15}
      \]

4. **Area of the Smaller Square:**
    - The area of the smaller square is given by:
      \[
      \text{Area}_{\text{small}} = s_{\text{small}}^2 = (\sqrt{15})^2 = 15
      \]
