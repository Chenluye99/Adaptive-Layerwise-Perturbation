To solve the problem of expressing the repeating decimal \(0.\overline{5}\) as a common fraction, we can follow these steps:

1. **Set Up the Equation:**
    Let \( x = 0.\overline{5} \).
    This means that \( x = 0.55555\ldots \).

2. **Manipulate the Equation:**
    Multiply both sides of the equation by 10 to shift the decimal point one place to the right:
    \[
    10x = 5.55555\ldots
    \]
    Now, we have two equations:
    \[
    x = 0.55555\ldots \quad \text{(1)}
    \]
    \[
    10x = 5.55555\ldots \quad \text{(2)}
    \]
    Subtract equation (1) from equation (2):
    \[
    10x - x = 5.55555\ldots - 0.55555\ldots
    \]
    \[
    9x = 5
    \]
    So,
    \[
    x = \frac{5}{9}
    \]

3. **Conclusion:**
    Therefore, the repeating decimal \(0.\overline{5}\) is equal to the fraction \(\frac{5}{9}\). To ensure this is in the simplest form, we need to confirm that 5 and 9 have no common factors other than 1 (i.e., they are coprime).

Since 5 and 9 have no common factors other than 1, \(\frac{5}{9}\) is already in its simplest form.

