To solve this problem, let's start by understanding the concept of vector projection. The projection of a vector $\mathbf{v}$ onto another vector $\mathbf{w}$ is given by:

\[
\text{proj}_{\mathbf{w}} \mathbf{v} = \frac{\mathbf{v} \cdot \mathbf{w}}{\mathbf{w} \cdot \mathbf{w}} \mathbf{w}
\]

However, we only need the scalar projection (i.e., the magnitude of the projection along $\mathbf{w}$) for this problem:

\[
\frac{\mathbf{v} \cdot \mathbf{w}}{\|\mathbf{w}\|}
\]

Given that the projection of $\begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix}$ onto $\mathbf{w}$ is $\begin{pmatrix} -9/10 \\ 3/10 \end{pmatrix}$, we can use this information to determine $\mathbf{w}$. Let's denote $\mathbf{w} = \begin{pmatrix} w_1 \\ w_2 \end{pmatrix}$. The projection formula for $\begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix}$ onto $\mathbf{w}$ is:

\[
\frac{\begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix} \cdot \begin{pmatrix} w_1 \\ w_2 \end{pmatrix}}{\begin{pmatrix} w_1 \\ w_2 \end{pmatrix} \cdot \begin{pmatrix} w_1 \\ w_2 \end{pmatrix}} \begin{pmatrix} w_1 \\ w_2 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} -9/10 \\ 3/10 \end{pmatrix}
\]

This means:

\[
\frac{0 \cdot w_1 + 3 \cdot w_2}{w_1^2 + w_2^2} \begin{pmatrix} w_1 \\ w_2 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} -9/10 \\ 3/10 \end{pmatrix}
\]

Simplifying the scalar projection part, we get:

\[
\frac{3 w_2}{w_1^2 + w_2^2} \begin{pmatrix} w_1 \\ w_2 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} -9/10 \\ 3/10 \end{pmatrix}
\]

This implies that:

\[
\frac{3 w_2}{w_1^2 + w_2^2} w_1 = -9/10 \quad \text{and} \quad \frac{3 w_2}{w_1^2 + w_2^2} w_2 = 3/10
\]

We can solve these equations to get the relationship between $w_1$ and $w_2$. Let's start by solving the second equation:

\[
\frac{3 w_2^2}{w_1^2 + w_2^2} = \frac{3}{10}
\]

This simplifies to:

\[
3 w_2^2 = \frac{3}{10} (w_1^2 + w_2^2)
\]

\[
3 w_2^2 = \frac{3}{10} w_1^2 + \frac{3}{10} w_2^2
\]

\[
3 w_2^2 - \frac{3}{10} w_2^2 = \frac{3}{10} w_1^2
\]

\[
\frac{30}{10} w_2^2 - \frac{3}{10} w_2^2 = \frac{3}{10} w_1^2
\]

\[
\frac{27}{10} w_2^2 = \frac{3}{10} w_1^2
\]

\[
27 w_2^2 = 3 w_1^2
\]

\[
9 w_2^2 = w_1^2
\]

\[
w_1 = \pm 3 w_2
\]

We can substitute $w_1 = 3 w_2$ (we can consider both $+$ and $-$, but let's start with $w_1 = 3 w_2$ for simplicity, and we can check consistency later) into the first equation:

\[
\frac{3 w_2}{(3 w_2)^2 + w_2^2} \cdot 3 w_2 = - \frac{9}{10}
\]

This becomes:

\[
\frac{3 w_2}{9 w_2^2 + w_2^2} \cdot 3 w_2 = - \frac{9}{10}
\]

\[
\frac{3 w_2}{10 w_2^2} \cdot 3 w_2 = - \frac{9}{10}
\]

\[
\frac{9 w_2^2}{10 w_2^2} = - \frac{9}{10}
\]

\[
\frac{9}{10} = - \frac{9}{10}
\]

It is clear that this equation is not correct as it stands, which suggests that we may have made an oversight or need to consider the negative case ($w_1 = -3 w_2$). Let's test $w_1 = -3 w_2$:

\[
\frac{3 w_2}{(-3 w_2)^2 + w_2^2} \cdot (-3 w_2) = - \frac{9}{10}
\]

This becomes:

\[
\frac{3 w_2}{9 w_2^2 + w_2^2} \cdot (-3 w_2) = - \frac{9}{10}
\]

\[
\frac{3 w_2}{10 w_2^2} \cdot (-3 w_2) = - \frac{9}{10}

\[
\frac{-9 w_2^2}{10 w_2^2} = - \frac{9}{10}

\[
- \frac{9}{10} = - \frac{9}{10}
\]

This is consistent. So, we have $\mathbf{w} = \begin{pmatrix} -3 w_2 \\ w_2 \end{pmatrix}$. To simplify further, we can take $w_2 = 1$, giving us $\mathbf{w} = \begin{pmatrix} -3 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}$.

Next, we need to find the projection of $\begin{pmatrix} 4 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}$ onto $\mathbf{w} = \begin{pmatrix} -3 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}$. The projection formula is:

\[
\text{proj}_{\mathbf{w}} \begin{pmatrix} 4 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix} = \frac{\begin{pmatrix} 4 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix} \cdot \begin{pmatrix} -3 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}}{\begin{pmatrix} -3 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix} \cdot \begin{pmatrix} -3 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}} \begin{pmatrix} -3 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}
\]

First, compute the dot products:

\[
\begin{pmatrix} 4 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix} \cdot \begin{pmatrix} -3 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix} = 4 \cdot (-3) + 1 \cdot 1 = -12 + 1 = -11
\]

\[
\begin{pmatrix} -3 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix} \cdot \begin{pmatrix} -3 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix} = (-3)^2 + 1^2 = 9 + 1 = 10
\]

So, the scalar projection is:

\[
\frac{-11}{10}
\]

Hence, the projection of $\begin{pmatrix} 4 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}$ onto $\mathbf{w}$ is:

\[
\frac{-11}{10} \begin{pmatrix} -3 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} \frac{-11}{10} \cdot -3 \\ \frac{-11}{10} \cdot 1 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} \frac{33}{10} \\ -\frac{11}{10} \end{pmatrix}
\]
