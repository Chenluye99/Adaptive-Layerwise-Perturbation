To solve the problem of finding the greatest common divisor (GCD) of 39 and 91, we can use the Euclidean algorithm. This algorithm is efficient and involves a series of division steps in which the divisor in each step becomes the dividend in the next step, and the remainder becomes the new divisor. The process continues until the remainder is 0. The divisor at that step will be the GCD.

The steps for the Euclidean algorithm to find the GCD of 39 and 91 are as follows:

1. Divide 91 by 39 and find the remainder.
    - \( 91 \div 39 = 2 \) with a remainder of \( 91 - 2 \cdot 39 = 91 - 78 = 13 \).
2. Now, take 39 and 13, and repeat the process.
    - \( 39 \div 13 = 3 \) with a remainder of \( 39 - 3 \cdot 13 = 39 - 39 = 0 \).
3. Since the remainder is 0, the GCD is the divisor at this step, which is 13.

Let's verify this by implementing it in Python to ensure the accuracy of the result.
```python
import math

# Given numbers
a = 39
b = 91

# Using Python's math.gcd function to find the greatest common divisor
gcd_result = math.gcd(a, b)
print(gcd_result)
```
Code execution result: 13

Thus, the greatest common divisor (GCD) of 39 and 91 is \(\boxed{13}\). This confirms our earlier calculation using the Euclidean algorithm.
