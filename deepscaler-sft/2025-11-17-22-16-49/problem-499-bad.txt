To solve this problem, we need to find how many positive integers less than 200 are divisible by 2, 3, and 5. A number that is divisible by 2, 3, and 5 must be divisible by their least common multiple (LCM). 

First, let's determine the LCM of 2, 3, and 5. 

- The prime factorization of 2 is \(2\).
- The prime factorization of 3 is \(3\).
- The prime factorization of 5 is \(5\).

Since 2, 3, and 5 are all prime numbers and do not share any common factors other than 1, their LCM is simply their product:
\[ \text{LCM}(2, 3, 5) = 2 \times 3 \times 5 = 30 \]

So, we need to find how many numbers less than 200 are divisible by 30. We can do this by iterating through all numbers less than 200 and checking if they are divisible by 30. If a number is divisible by 30, we will count it.

Let's write some Python code to get the accurate result.
```python
# Initialize a counter
count = 0

# Check each number less than 200 to see if it is divisible by 30
for num in range(1, 200):
    if num % 30 == 0:
        count += 1

print(count)
```
Code execution result: 6

Thus, the number of positive integers less than 200 that are divisible by 2, 3, and 5 is \(\boxed{6}\). This is our final answer.
