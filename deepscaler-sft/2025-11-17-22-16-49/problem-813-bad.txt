To solve this problem, we need to find the common tangent line of the parabolas \(P_1: y = x^2 + \frac{101}{100}\) and \(P_2: x = y^2 + \frac{45}{4}\) that has a rational slope. We will then express this tangent line in the form \(ax + by = c\) with positive integers \(a, b, c\) and \(\gcd(a, b, c) = 1\), and find \(a + b + c\).

### Step-by-Step Solution

1. **Equation of the Tangent Line to \(P_1\):**
   For the parabola \(P_1: y = x^2 + \frac{101}{100}\), the tangent line at a point \((x_0, y_0)\) (where \(y_0 = x_0^2 + \frac{101}{100}\)) has the form:
   \[
   y - y_0 = m(x - x_0)
   \]
   where the slope \(m\) is given by \(m = \frac{dy}{dx}\bigg|_{x = x_0} = 2x_0\). So, the tangent line equation becomes:
   \[
   y - \left(x_0^2 + \frac{101}{100}\right) = 2x_0 (x - x_0)
   \]
   Simplifying this, we get:
   \[
   y = 2x_0 x - 2x_0^2 + x_0^2 + \frac{101}{100} = 2x_0 x - x_0^2 + \frac{101}{100}
   \]
   So, the equation of the tangent line to \(P_1\) is:
   \[
   y = 2x_0 x - x_0^2 + \frac{101}{100}
   \]

2. **Equation of the Tangent Line to \(P_2\):**
   For the parabola \(P_2: x = y^2 + \frac{45}{4}\), the tangent line at a point \((x_0, y_0)\) (where \(x_0 = y_0^2 + \frac{45}{4}\)) has the form:
   \[
   x - x_0 = m(y - y_0)
   \]
   where the slope \(m\) is given by \(m = \frac{dx}{dy}\bigg|_{y = y_0} = \frac{1}{2y_0}\). So, the tangent line equation becomes:
   \[
   x - \left(y_0^2 + \frac{45}{4}\right) = \frac{1}{2y_0} (y - y_0)
   \]
   Simplifying this, we get:
   \[
   x = \frac{1}{2y_0} y - \frac{1}{2y_0} y_0 + y_0^2 + \frac{45}{4} = \frac{1}{2y_0} y - \frac{1}{2} + y_0^2 + \frac{45}{4}
   \]
   So, the equation of the tangent line to \(P_2\) is:
   \[
   x = \frac{1}{2y_0} y + y_0^2 - \frac{1}{2} + \frac{45}{4}
   \]
   or equivalently:
   \[
   x = \frac{1}{2y_0} y + y_0^2 + \frac{43}{4}
   \]
   Rewriting this in the form \(ax + by = c\):
   \[
   -x + \frac{1}{2y_0} y = - y_0^2 - \frac{43}{4}
   \]
   Multiplying through by \(-2 y_0\) to clear the fraction and make coefficients integers (if possible):
   \[
   2 y_0 x - y = 2 y_0 y_0^2 + \frac{43}{2} y_0
   \]
   \[
   2 y_0 x - y = 2 y_0^3 + \frac{43}{2} y_0
   \]
   For simplicity, let's consider the tangent line in its more standard form, comparing it with the tangent line of \(P_1\).

3. **Find the Common Tangent Line:**
   The tangent line to both parabolas must have the same equation with a rational slope. Let's assume the tangent line has slope \(m\) and express it in the form \(y = mx + c\). We substitute this into both parabolas and solve for the conditions where the quadratic equations formed have a double root (indicating a tangent line).

   Substituting \( y = mx + c \) into \( P_1 \):
   \[
   mx + c = x^2 + \frac{101}{100}
   \]
   \[
   x^2 - mx + \left(c - \frac{101}{100}\right) = 0
   \]
   For this to be a tangent, the discriminant must be zero:
   \[
   m^2 - 4 \left(c - \frac{101}{100}\right) = 0 \implies m^2 = 4 \left(c - \frac{101}{100}\right) \implies c = \frac{m^2}{4} + \frac{101}{100}
   \]

   Substituting \( y = mx + c \) into \( P_2 \) (solving for \(x\)):
   \[
   x = (mx + c)^2 + \frac{45}{4}
   \]
   \[
   x = m^2 x^2 + 2mcx + c^2 + \frac{45}{4}
   \]
   Rearranging, we get:
   \[
   m^2 x^2 + (2mc - 1)x + \left(c^2 + \frac{45}{4}\right) = 0
   \]
   For this to be a tangent, the discriminant must be zero:
   \[
   (2mc - 1)^2 - 4 m^2 \left(c^2 + \frac{45}{4}\right) = 0
   \]
   Substituting \( c = \frac{m^2}{4} + \frac{101}{100} \) into the above equation and solving for \( m \) will give us the correct slope. We seek a rational slope, so we check feasible rational values for \( m \).
