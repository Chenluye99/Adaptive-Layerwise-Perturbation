First, let's solve the problem step by step.

1. **Understand the Problem:**
    - A standard deck of cards has 52 cards, divided into 4 suits: hearts, diamonds, clubs, and spades. Each suit has 13 cards.
    - When Max picks two cards without replacement, the first card can be any of the 52 cards, and the second card can be any of the remaining 51 cards.
    - We need to find the probability that these two cards are of different suits.

2. **Formulate the Solution:**
    - First, we will calculate the total number of ways to pick 2 cards from a 52-card deck. This is given by the combination formula \( \binom{52}{2} \), but since we are picking cards one at a time without replacement, it's the same as \( 52 \times 51 \).
    - Next, we will calculate the number of ways to pick 2 cards such that they are of different suits.
        - For the first card, there are 52 choices.
        - For the second card, there are 39 choices that will ensure it is of a different suit than the first card (since there are 13 cards in each of the 3 other suits, and 3 suits * 13 cards per suit = 39 choices).
    - The probability is then given by the ratio of the number of ways to pick 2 cards of different suits to the total number of ways to pick 2 cards from the deck.

3. **Translate to Code:**
    - We will implement this step-by-step in Python to ensure accuracy.
Let's write the Python code to get the precise answer.
```python
import sympy as sp

# Total number of ways to pick 2 cards out of 52 without replacement
total_ways = 52 * 51

# Number of ways to pick 2 cards such that they are of different suits
ways_different_suits = 0

for i in range(52):
    for j in range(i + 1, 52):
        card1 = i
        card2 = j
        suit1 = card1 // 13
        suit2 = card2 // 13
        if suit1 != suit2:
            ways_different_suits += 1

# Probability that the two cards are of different suits
probability_different_suits = ways_different_suits / total_ways
print(probability_different_suits)
```
