First, let's start with the problem. We begin with a 2-inch by 2-inch square.

1. **Calculate the original area:**
   The area of the original 2-inch by 2-inch square is:
   \[
   \text{Original Area} = 2 \times 2 = 4 \text{ square inches}
   \]

2. **Calculate the new area:**
   The area is increased by 21 square inches, so the new area is:
   \[
   \text{New Area} = \text{Original Area} + 21 = 4 + 21 = 25 \text{ square inches}
   \]

3. **Determine the side length of the new square:**
   Let the side length of the new square be \( s \). Then, the area of the new square is:
   \[
   s^2 = 25
   \]
   To find \( s \), we take the square root of 25:
   \[
   s = \sqrt{25} = 5
   \]

So, the side length of the new square is 5 inches. Let's verify this by implementing it in Python to ensure accuracy.
```python
import sympy as sp

# Original side length of the square
original_side_length = 2

# Original area of the square
original_area = original_side_length ** 2

# New area after increasing by 21 square inches
new_area = original_area + 21

# Find the side length of the new square by taking the square root of the new area
new_side_length = sp.sqrt(new_area)
print(new_side_length)
```
Code execution result: 5

Thus, the side length of the new square is \(\boxed{5}\) inches. This confirms that our solution is correct.
