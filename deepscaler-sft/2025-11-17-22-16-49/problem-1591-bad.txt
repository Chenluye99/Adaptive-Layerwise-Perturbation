First, let's start by determining the side lengths and areas of both squares.

1. **Larger Square:**
    - Let's assume the side length of the larger square is \( s \).
    - The diagonal of the larger square is the diameter of the circle. If the side length of the larger square is \( s \), then its diagonal can be found using the Pythagorean theorem: \[ \text{Diagonal} = s \sqrt{2} \]
    - This diagonal is also the diameter of the circle. If we denote the radius of the circle as \( R \), then the diameter of the circle is \( 2R \), and so we have: \[ s \sqrt{2} = 2R \]
    - Solving for \( R \), we get: \[ R = \frac{s \sqrt{2}}{2} = \frac{s}{\sqrt{2}} = \frac{s \sqrt{2}}{2} \]
    - The area of the larger square is: \[ \text{Area}_{\text{large}} = s^2 \]

2. **Smaller Square:**
    - The smaller square has one side that coincides with one side of the larger square and two of its vertices on the circle. Let the side length of the smaller square be \( t \).
    - The vertices of the smaller square that are on the circle will determine a diagonal which is equal to the side length of the larger square, i.e., \( s \).
    - The relationship between the side length \( t \) of the smaller square and the circle's geometry can be found using its diagonal, which connects opposite vertices of the smaller square and is given by \( t \sqrt{2} \). Since this diagonal is a chord of the circle and it is the same as the side length of the larger square, we get: \[ t \sqrt{2} = s \]
    - Solving for \( t \), we get: \[ t = \frac{s}{\sqrt{2}} = \frac{s \sqrt{2}}{2} \]
    - The area of the smaller square is: \[ \text{Area}_{\text{small}} = t^2 = \left( \frac{s \sqrt{2}}{2} \right)^2 = \frac{2 s^2}{4} = \frac{s^2}{2} \]

3. **Find the Percentage:**
    - To find what percent of the area of the larger square is the area of the smaller square, we need: \[ \frac{\text{Area}_{\text{small}}}{\text{Area}_{\text{large}}} \times 100 = \frac{\frac{s^2}{2}}{s^2} \times 100 = \frac{1}{2} \times 100 = 50 \% \]

Now, let's confirm this using Python.
```python
import sympy as sp

# Let's define side lengths of the larger and smaller squares
s = sp.symbols('s', positive=True)

# Calculate the side length of the smaller square
t = s / sp.sqrt(2)

# Calculate the area of the larger and smaller squares
area_large = s ** 2
area_small = t ** 2

# Calculate the percentage of the area of the smaller square with respect to the larger square
percentage_area = (area_small / area_large) * 100
print(percentage_area)
```
Code execution result: 50

Thus, the area of the smaller square is \(\boxed{50}\) percent of the area of the larger square. This confirms our analytical solution.
