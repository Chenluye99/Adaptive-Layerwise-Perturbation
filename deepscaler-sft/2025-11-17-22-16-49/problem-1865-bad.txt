To solve this problem, we need to determine the radius of the circle whose center is on the x-axis and which passes through the points \((0, 4)\) and \((1, 3)\).

1. **Circle Equation:**
   The general equation of a circle with center \((h, 0)\) on the x-axis and radius \(r\) is:
   \[
   (x - h)^2 + (y - 0)^2 = r^2
   \]
   which simplifies to:
   \[
   (x - h)^2 + y^2 = r^2
   \]

2. **Substitute Points into the Circle Equation:**
   Since the points \((0, 4)\) and \((1, 3)\) lie on the circle, they must satisfy the above equation. Substituting \((0, 4)\) into the equation, we get:
   \[
   (0 - h)^2 + 4^2 = r^2 \implies h^2 + 16 = r^2
   \]
   Substituting \((1, 3)\) into the equation, we get:
   \[
   (1 - h)^2 + 3^2 = r^2 \implies (1 - h)^2 + 9 = r^2
   \]

3. **Set Up and Solve the Equations:**
   We now have two equations:
   \[
   h^2 + 16 = r^2
   \]
   \[
   (1 - h)^2 + 9 = r^2
   \]
   We can set these two expressions for \(r^2\) equal to each other:
   \[
   h^2 + 16 = (1 - h)^2 + 9
   \]
   Let's solve this equation step-by-step:

   \[
   h^2 + 16 = (1 - h)^2 + 9
   \]
   Expanding the right-hand side:
   \[
   h^2 + 16 = 1 - 2h + h^2 + 9
   \]
   Simplifying further:
   \[
   h^2 + 16 = h^2 - 2h + 10
   \]
   Cancel out \(h^2\) from both sides:
   \[
   16 = -2h + 10
   \]
   Rearrange to solve for \(h\):
   \[
   16 - 10 = -2h \implies 6 = -2h \implies h = -3
   \]

4. **Find the Radius:**
   Now substitute \(h = -3\) back into one of the original equations to find \(r\). We use \( h^2 + 16 = r^2 \):
   \[
   (-3)^2 + 16 = r^2 \implies 9 + 16 = r^2 \implies 25 = r^2 \implies r = \sqrt{25} = 5
   \]
