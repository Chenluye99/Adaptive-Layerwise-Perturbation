To solve this problem, we need to select 3 volunteers out of the 7 to work on Saturday, and then we need to select 3 more volunteers out of the remaining 4 to work on Sunday. The last volunteer will not work over the weekend.

1. First, we choose 3 volunteers out of 7 for Saturday. The number of ways to choose 3 out of 7 is given by the combination formula \( \binom{7}{3} \).
2. After selecting 3 volunteers for Saturday, we have 4 volunteers left, and we need to choose 3 out of these 4 for Sunday. The number of ways to choose 3 out of 4 is given by the combination formula \( \binom{4}{3} \).
3. The order in which we choose the groups for Saturday and Sunday doesn't matter (i.e., the selection of volunteers for Saturday and Sunday are independent events, and we need to consider all possible ways to divide the 3 chosen for Saturday and 3 chosen for Sunday).

The formula to find the total number of different arrangements is:
\[
\binom{7}{3} \times \binom{4}{3}
\]

Let's compute this step-by-step using Python to ensure accuracy.
```python
import math

# Calculate the combinations
comb_7_choose_3 = math.comb(7, 3)
comb_4_choose_3 = math.comb(4, 3)

# Get the total number of arrangements
total_arrangements = comb_7_choose_3 * comb_4_choose_3
print(total_arrangements)
```
Code execution result: 140

Thus, the total number of different arrangements is \(\boxed{140}\). This concludes that there are 140 ways to arrange 3 volunteers for Saturday and 3 volunteers for Sunday from a group of 7 volunteers.
